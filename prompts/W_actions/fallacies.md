Fallacy catalog to check:

- **Appeal to Authority**: relying on an expert's opinion as absolute proof without sufficient evidence.
- **Slippery Slope**: arguing that a minor action will inevitably lead to a disastrous chain of events.
- **No True Scotsman**: redefining a term to exclude counterexamples.
- **Straw Man**: misrepresenting an opponent's argument to make it easier to attack.
- **Special Pleading**: applying a rule to others while claiming an exemption for oneself.
- **False Dichotomy**: presenting only two options when more exist.
- **Appeal to Popularity**: arguing that something is true just because many people believe it.
- **False Analogy**: assuming that because two things are alike in one way, they are alike in others.
- **Ad Hominem**: attacking the person making an argument rather than the argument itself.
- **Poisoning the Well**: discrediting someone before they have a chance to speak.
- **Sunk Cost**: continuing an endeavor because of previously invested time or resources.
- **Appeal to Emotion**: manipulating feelings to win an argument rather than using logic.
- **Argument from Anecdote**: using personal experience instead of sound, representative data.
- **Red Herring**: introducing irrelevant information to distract from the topic.
- **Is-Ought Fallacy**: inferring that how things are is how they should be.
- **Appeal to Tradition**: arguing something is right because it has been done that way for a long time.
- **Hasty Generalization**: making a broad claim based on a very small sample size.
- **Affirming the Consequent**: a formal fallacy that assumes the cause based on the result.
- **Undistributed Middle**: assuming two things are related just because they share one trait.
- **Tu Quoque**: deflecting criticism by accusing the critic of the same flaw.
- **Begging the Question**: assuming the truth of the conclusion in the premises.
- **Non Sequitur**: a conclusion that does not logically follow from the premises.
- **Equivocation**: using ambiguous language to conceal the truth.
- **Appeal to Ignorance**: arguing that something is true simply because it hasn't been proven false.
- **Composition**: inferring that what is true for the parts is true for the whole.
- **Genetic Fallacy**: judging an idea based on its origin rather than its merits.
- **Argument from Silence**: drawing a conclusion from a lack of information.
- **Moving the Goalposts / Gambler's Fallacy**: changing the criteria for success after the initial criteria have been met.
- **Whataboutism**: avoiding a critique by pointing to someone else's faults.
- **Personal Incredulity**: claiming something is false just because you find it hard to believe.
