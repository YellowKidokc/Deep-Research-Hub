Rate this document as an editor for an educated, philosophically inclined reader would.

- First decide the document's parts from its own structure (headings, or the opening / middle / end when it has none) and name each part with the paragraph range it covers.
- Pick the rubric that fits each part (an opening is scored on hook and pacing; an argument on persuasiveness and logical clarity; an ending on payoff, rigor and readability) and state it in `basis`.
- The overall `rubric` lists 4–6 dimensions for the whole piece, for example Hook & Engagement, Tone & Authority, Structural Logic, Pacing & Accessibility, Methodological Rigor, Formatting & Readability.
- Raise 1–3 issues per part — the ones that cost the most points. Quote the words that show the problem.
- Watch for: leaps the text has not earned, jargon dropped on the reader before it is introduced, defensive tone, walls of text that should be lists, appendix material at the climax, punchlines that were never set up.
