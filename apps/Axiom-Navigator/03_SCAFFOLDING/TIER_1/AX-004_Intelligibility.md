## Objections & Responses

### Objection 1: "Quantum mechanics is fundamentally random/unintelligible"

**Response:**
Quantum mechanics is highly intelligible—it's one of the most precisely tested theories in physics. "Random" doesn't mean "unintelligible." Probability distributions are intelligible structures. The Schrödinger equation is deterministic; only measurement outcomes are probabilistic.

**Status:** ✅ Resolved (confuses randomness with unintelligibility)

---

### Objection 2: "Chaos theory shows some things are unpredictable"

**Response:**
Unpredictable ≠ unintelligible. Chaotic systems follow deterministic rules (strange attractors, Lyapunov exponents). We understand chaos mathematically. Sensitive dependence on initial conditions doesn't make the system unintelligible.

**Status:** ✅ Resolved

---

### Objection 3: "Gödel's incompleteness shows limits to intelligibility"

**Response:**
Gödel shows formal systems can't prove all truths about themselves. But:

1. Incompleteness is itself an intelligible result
2. Physics may not be a formal system
3. Unprovability ≠ unintelligibility

**Status:** ✅ Resolved

---

### Objection 4: "We don't understand consciousness/quantum measurement"

**Response:**
"Don't yet understand" ≠ "fundamentally unintelligible." Hard problems are still problems—they admit investigation, models, progress. If consciousness were truly unintelligible, we couldn't even formulate the hard problem.

**Status:** ✅ Resolved

---

### Objection 5: "This is anthropocentric—assumes human minds can understand reality"

**Response:**
No. Intelligibility is ontological, not epistemological. Reality has structure whether or not humans (or any minds) exist. The electron's charge is -e and follows Maxwell's equations regardless of observers.

**Status:** ✅ Resolved
