## I. The Problem with Bottom-Up Theories

### 1.1 The Endless Debate

For 60 years, scholars have debated American decline:
- Conservatives blame the 1960s counterculture
- Progressives blame capitalism and racism
- Libertarians blame government expansion
- Communitarians blame individualism

**They are all wrong—and all right.**

Each identifies a *symptom* and mistakes it for the *cause*. This is like debating whether the fever or the cough is killing the patient, while ignoring the underlying infection.

### 1.2 Why Bottom-Up Fails

Bottom-up theories:
1. **Start with observations** (divorce rates, trust surveys, crime statistics)
2. **Propose causal mechanisms** (cultural shifts, policy changes, technology)
3. **Argue about which mechanism is primary**

This method *cannot resolve the debate* because:
- Multiple symptoms can have the same root cause
- Correlation does not establish causation
- The mechanism level is too shallow

**You cannot diagnose a system failure by cataloging its symptoms.**

### 1.3 The Alternative: Top-Down Derivation

Mature physics works differently:
1. **Establish axioms** (principles that cannot be violated without contradiction)
2. **Derive predictions** (what must happen if the axioms are true)
3. **Confirm empirically** (do observations match predictions?)

Einstein did not observe time dilation and then propose relativity. He derived time dilation from axioms (constancy of light speed, equivalence principle) and then sought confirmation.

**Theophysics applies this method to social systems for the first time.**
