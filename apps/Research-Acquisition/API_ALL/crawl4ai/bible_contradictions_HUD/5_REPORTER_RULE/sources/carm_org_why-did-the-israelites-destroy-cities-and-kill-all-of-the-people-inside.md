# Why did the Israelites destroy cities and kill all of the people inside?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
November 28, 2008 
3 min read 
The command by God to kill all inside a city is seen by many to be immoral and a demonstration that the Bible is not true. But, we must examine the issue in light of its context – its biblical context – not in light of present-day, non-Christian assumptions. If we want to see if it is moral or not, we must know which morals are in question.
[Deuteronomy 2:33-34](https://ref.ly/Deut%202.33-34;nasb95?t=biblia), “And the Lord our God delivered him over to us; and we defeated him with his sons and all his people. So we captured all his cities at that time, and utterly destroyed the men, women and children of every city. We left no survivor.”
First of all, the context of this verse is dealing with the wickedness of the Amorite king Sihon of Hesbon, a city of the Amorites ([Num. 21:25](https://ref.ly/Num.%2021.25;nasb95?t=biblia)). They were wicked people ([Gen. 15:16](https://ref.ly/Gen.%2015.16;nasb95?t=biblia); [2 Kings 21:11](https://ref.ly/2%20Kings%2021.11;nasb95?t=biblia)). When the Israelites wanted to pass through their land during their exodus from Egypt, the Amorites refused them safe passage and attacked the Israelites. However, they were soundly defeated by Israel ([Num. 21:21-31](https://ref.ly/Num.%2021.21-31;nasb95?t=biblia)).
