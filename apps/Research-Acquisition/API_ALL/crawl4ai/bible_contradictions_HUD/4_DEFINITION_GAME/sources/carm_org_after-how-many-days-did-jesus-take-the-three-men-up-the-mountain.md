# After how many days did Jesus take the three men up the mountain?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 02, 2008 
3 min read 
After how many days did Jesus take the three men up the mountain? ![Jesus took three men](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/church-07-2-300x225.jpg) [Matthew 17:1](https://ref.ly/Matt%2017.1;nasb95?t=biblia); [Mark 9:2](https://ref.ly/Mark%209.2;nasb95?t=biblia) and [Luke 9:28](https://ref.ly/Luke%209.28;nasb95?t=biblia)
In the Greek in both [Matthew 17:1](https://ref.ly/Matt%2017.1;nasb95?t=biblia) and [Mark 9:2](https://ref.ly/Mark%209.2;nasb95?t=biblia), it says, “And after six days…” The word “after” in Greek is _meta_. According to the Enhanced Strong’s Lexicon meta means, “with, after, or behind.” In [Luke 9:28](https://ref.ly/Luke%209.28;nasb95?t=biblia) it says something different. It says “And some eight days after these sayings . . .” (NASB). The Greek word “some” is _hosei_ which means “about” or “nearly.” Other translations render it the same way.
[Luke 9:28](https://ref.ly/Luke%209.28;nasb95?t=biblia) is an approximation evidenced by it saying “about eight days after…” [Matthew 17:1](https://ref.ly/Matt%2017.1;nasb95?t=biblia) and [Mark 9:2](https://ref.ly/Mark%209.2;nasb95?t=biblia) are more precise. They say “after six days.” Logically, eight days **is** after six days, so there is no logical contradiction. But, the key lies in Luke saying “about eight days later.” Luke was giving an approximation. Matthew and Mark were more precise.
