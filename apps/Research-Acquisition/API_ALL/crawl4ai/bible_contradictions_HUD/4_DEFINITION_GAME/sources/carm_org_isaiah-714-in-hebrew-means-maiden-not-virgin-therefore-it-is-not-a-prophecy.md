# Isaiah 7:14, in Hebrew means maiden, not virgin. Therefore, it is not a prophecy.
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 01, 2008 
3 min read 
[Isaiah 7:14](https://ref.ly/Isa%207.14;nasb95?t=biblia), in Hebrew, means maiden, not virgin. Critics of the Bible will, therefore, say it is not a prophecy. But, it is a prophecy because of how the Jews translated it in the Septuagint. [Isaiah 7:14](https://ref.ly/Isa%207.14;nasb95?t=biblia) says that a virgin will bear a son. The Hebrew word for virgin, which is almah. According to the Strong’s Concordance, it means, “virgin, young woman 1a) of marriageable age 1b) maid or newly married.” Therefore, the word almah does not always mean virgin. The word “occurs elsewhere in the Old Testament only in [Genesis 24:43](https://ref.ly/Gen%2024.43;nasb95?t=biblia) (maiden); [Exodus 2:8](https://ref.ly/Exod%202.8;nasb95?t=biblia) (girl); [Psalm 68:25](https://ref.ly/Ps%2068.25;nasb95?t=biblia) (maidens); [Proverbs 30:19](https://ref.ly/Prov%2030.19;nasb95?t=biblia) (maiden); [Song of Songs 1:3](https://ref.ly/Song%201.3;nasb95?t=biblia) (maidens); 6:8 (virgins).” (Walvoord, John F., and Roy B. Zuck, The Bible Knowledge Commentary, Wheaton, IL: Scripture Press Publications, 1985.) Additionally, there is a Hebrew word for virgin:  bethulah. If [Isaiah 7:14](https://ref.ly/Isa%207.14;nasb95?t=biblia) was meant to mean virgin instead of a young maiden, then why wasn’t the word used here?
“Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign: Behold, a virgin will be with child and bear a son, and she will call His name Immanuel,” ([Isaiah 7:14](https://ref.ly/Isa%207.14;nasb95?t=biblia)).
The Septuagint, LXX, is a translation of the Hebrew scriptures into Greek. This translation was made around 200 B.C. by 70 Hebrew scholars. In [Isaiah 7:14](https://ref.ly/Isa%207.14;nasb95?t=biblia), they translated the word almah into the Greek word parthenos, which means virgin. According to A Greek-English Lexicon of the New Testament and Other Early Christian Literature,(Bauer, Walter, F. Wilbur Gingrich, and Frederick W. Danker, _A Greek-English Lexicon of the New Testament and Other Early Christian Literature_ , Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1979.) parthenos means virgin. This word is used in the New Testament of the Virgin Mary ([Matt. 1:23](https://ref.ly/Matt.%201.23;nasb95?t=biblia); [Luke 1:27](https://ref.ly/Luke%201.27;nasb95?t=biblia)) and of the ten virgins in the parable ([Matt. 25:1](https://ref.ly/Matt.%2025.1;nasb95?t=biblia), [7](https://ref.ly/Matt%2025.7;nasb95?t=biblia), [11](https://ref.ly/Matt%2025.11;nasb95?t=biblia)). If the Hebrews translated the Hebrew word almah into the Greek word for virgin, then they understood what the Hebrew text meant here.
