## You Said 'Sunrise' This Morning

You checked the weather today and noted the 'sunrise' time. You know the sun does not literally rise — the earth rotates. But you used observational language: describing what things look like from the human vantage point rather than what is technically happening at the cosmological level. This is not error. It is standard human communication.
Ten alleged contradictions treat biblical observational or poetic language as if it were making scientific claims. The Bible describes the sun 'rising' and 'setting,' mentions the 'four corners of the earth,' and speaks of God's 'hand' or 'eyes.' The critic reads these as competing cosmological or anatomical assertions. They are nothing of the kind.

## Poetic Language in Ancient Literature

When the Psalms say 'the earth is set on pillars,' this is poetic language in a hymn — the same genre that describes God as having wings (Psalm 91:4), which no one reads as claiming God is a bird. When Joshua 10 describes the sun 'standing still,' it describes the event from the observer's perspective. Even modern astronomers use geocentric language in everyday speech because it describes the appearance.
'God dwells in thick darkness' (1 Kings 8:12) and 'God dwells in unapproachable light' (1 Timothy 6:16) are not contradictions — they are different metaphorical descriptions of divine transcendence. Darkness represents mystery and hiddenness. Light represents holiness and purity. Both describe aspects of the same divine reality that exceeds human comprehension.
Anthropomorphic language — ascribing human features to God — is a communication necessity, not an error. When the text says God 'rested' on the seventh day, it does not mean God was tired (Isaiah 40:28 explicitly says God does not grow weary). 'Rested' means 'ceased creative activity.' The text uses a human metaphor because its audience is human. Ten 'contradictions' resolve by applying this principle.

## The Standard Applied Consistently

If we held all ancient literature to the standard the critic applies to the Bible — treating every metaphor, idiom, and observational description as a literal scientific claim — no ancient text would survive. Homer contradicts himself. Herodotus contradicts himself. The Vedas, the Quran, and the Tao Te Ching all use phenomenological language that does not map to modern scientific descriptions. The Bible is held to a standard that would invalidate every text in human history.
