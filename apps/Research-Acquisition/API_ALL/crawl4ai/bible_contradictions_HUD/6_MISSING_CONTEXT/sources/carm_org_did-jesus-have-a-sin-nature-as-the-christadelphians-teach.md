# Did Jesus have a sin nature as the Christadelphians teach?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
January 12, 2002 
14 min read 
Jesus did not have a sin nature. However, that has not stopped the [Christadelphians](https://carm.org/minor-groups-issues/christadelphianism/) from teaching He did. This is not surprising considering they deny the doctrine of the Trinity and the deity of Jesus. As with all non-Christian cults that deny the true doctrine of God, other doctrines necessarily become incorrect as well. In this case, their error is that Jesus has a sin nature.
One of the main verses they use to support their erroneous doctrine is [Rom. 8:3-4](https://ref.ly/Rom.%208.3-4;nasb95?t=biblia) which says, “For what the Law could not do, weak as it was through the flesh, God did: sending His own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh and as an offering for sin, He condemned sin in the flesh.” _(All scripture quotes are from the NASB.)_ They teach that the “likeness of sinful flesh” means that Jesus had a sinful nature. But it doesn’t. The key to understanding this verse is the word “likeness.” If this word were omitted then the text would say “…sending His own Son in sinful flesh…” If that is what the verse said then the Christadelphians would have a valid argument. But the text says that Jesus came in the “likeness” of sinful flesh, not that He came in sinful flesh. In other words, men are sinners. Jesus appeared as a man. Therefore, Jesus appeared in the likeness of a sinner, though He was not a sinner.
Another verse they use is [Heb. 2:14](https://ref.ly/Heb.%202.14;nasb95?t=biblia) which says, “Since then the children share in flesh and blood, He Himself likewise also partook of the same, that through death He might render powerless him who had the power of death, that is, the devil.” This verse can easily be explained in the same manner as [Rom. 8:3-4](https://ref.ly/Rom.%208.3-4;nasb95?t=biblia) above. Jesus partook of flesh and blood. But it does not here say that He had a sin nature.
