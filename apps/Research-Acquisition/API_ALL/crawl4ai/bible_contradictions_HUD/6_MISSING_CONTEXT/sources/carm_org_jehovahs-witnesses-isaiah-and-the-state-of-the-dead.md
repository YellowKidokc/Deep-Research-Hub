# Jehovah’s Witnesses, Isaiah, and the state of the dead
by [Luke Wayne](https://carm.org/luke-wayne)
June 30, 2017 
8 min read 
Jehovah’s Witnesses teach that man has no soul or spirit that continues to exist after death. They insist that when a man dies, he ceases to exist. Nothing of his person endures. Only his rotting flesh remains. Future [resurrection](https://carm.org/dictionary/resurrection/) is not the spirit of a man returning to the fullness of bodily life, but rather God re-creating an identical person out of nothing from only His memory of the way that person once was. One of the texts to which they turn in defense of this belief is in the Book of Isaiah:
“Lo, for my own welfare I had great bitterness; It is You who has kept my soul from the pit of destruction, For You have cast all my sins behind Your back. For Sheol cannot thank You, Death cannot praise You; Those who go down to the pit cannot hope for Your faithfulness,” ([Isaiah 38:17-18](https://ref.ly/Isa%2038.17-18;nasb95?t=biblia)).
On the surface and read in isolation, this appears to support the idea that we cease to exist when we die. Read in the fuller context, however, Isaiah gives us a very different picture.
