# What does the Eastern Orthodox Church say about sacraments?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
April 03, 2025 
7 min read 
The Eastern Orthodox Church teaches that sacraments are essential to the Christian life. These sacraments are also called “Holy Mysteries.” The sacraments are not just symbols used in rituals. Rather, they are grace-filled encounters with God. So, participating in them imparts grace to a believer. Therefore, they are central to salvation, sanctification, and union with Christ. There are seven sacraments: Baptism, Confirmation, the Eucharist, Confession, Anointing the sick, Marriage, and Ordination.![What does the Eastern Orthodox Church say about sacraments?](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/eastern-orthodox-cross02-230x300.png)
If we define a sacrament according to the Eastern Orthodox position as a physical means through which grace is imparted to the believer, then such a teaching is not found in the Bible.
Obtaining grace becomes ritualistically connected to the person participating in the ceremony that can only be officiated by a priest who is supposed to have authority. But since there is no New Testament priesthood in the form of the Eastern Orthodox system, the EO view of the sacraments is disproven. (See the article [Is there a New Testament priesthood?](https://carm.org/world-religions/is-there-a-new-testament-priesthood/))
