# Why did God put the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil in the Garden?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
January 08, 2011 
3 min read 
God put two trees in the Garden of Eden, the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil ([Gen. 2:9](https://ref.ly/Gen.%202.9;nasb95?t=biblia)). Why did He do this? Wasn’t putting those trees there a temptation to sin that would not have happened if God had not placed them there? We can’t speak for God, but we can offer some possible answers. First, their existence was consistent with His divine nature and holy purpose. God has all knowledge. He knows the responsibility that anyone must have regarding that knowledge that He reveals to them. But since Adam and Eve cannot know all things, they could not also possess the wisdom that goes along with it. So, God placed the two trees in the garden that signifying the need to have wisdom and avoid sin. [ ![knowledge good and evil](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/01/two-trees-01.jpg) ](https://carm.org/about-god/why-did-god-put-the-tree-of-the-knowledge-of-good-and-evil-in-the-garden-of-eden/attachment/two-trees-01/)
Second, whether or not there was a tree, there would not mean that righteousness did not exist. After all, it is based on God’s character. He is the creator, and He is, therefore, the standard of all moral perfection. So, it would seem logical that the trees were there because they represented the moral purity, revelation, and obligation found in the character of God. He could know right from wrong since Adam was made in God’s image ([Gen. 1:26](https://ref.ly/Gen.%201.26;nasb95?t=biblia)). Therefore God put the trees there as a physical manifestation of the spiritual purity of God’s knowledge of righteousness and sin, life and death.
Third, Adam and Eve would know, and as their knowledge increased, the responsibility to act properly would also increase. God gave them a rule regarding the knowledge of good and evil because that is the nature of moral truth found in God. There is a consequence for breaking it. Remember, morality is based on God’s character. Therefore, going against God’s character means incurring judgment. After all, if God did not judge the sinner, then He would be approving of evil. He cannot do that, so He must judge the sinner.
