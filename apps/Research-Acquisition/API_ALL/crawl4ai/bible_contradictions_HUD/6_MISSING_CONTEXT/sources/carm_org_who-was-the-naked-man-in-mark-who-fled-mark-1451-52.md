# Who was the naked man in Mark who fled, Mark 14:51-52
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
June 05, 2024 
3 min read 
In [Mark 14:51-52](https://ref.ly/Mark%2014.51-52;nasb95?t=biblia), there is a reference to someone who ran away naked after being seized by a large crowd ([Matt. 26:47](https://ref.ly/Matt.%2026.47;nasb95?t=biblia); Luke 47-53). The large crowd appeared to be a Roman cohort that worked with the chief priests and Pharisees ([John 18:3-11](https://ref.ly/John%2018.3-11;nasb95?t=biblia)). So, who was this naked man in Mark? It doesn’t say. Therefore, we can’t say who it was for sure. Though the other Gospels contain the account of Jesus’ arrest, this particular event is found only in Mark. This might be significant. Let’s take a look. [ ![naked man in Mark](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/man-running-01-136x150.jpg) ](https://carm.org/bible-difficulties/who-was-the-naked-man-in-mark-who-fled-mark-1451-52/attachment/man-running-01/)
“A young man was following Him, wearing nothing but a linen sheet over his naked body; and they *seized him. 52 But he pulled free of the linen sheet and escaped naked” ([Mark 14:51-52](https://ref.ly/Mark%2014.51-52;nasb95?t=biblia)).
The most common theory is that the naked man in Mark’s gospel was Mark himself, the son of Mary, “the mother of John is also called Mark” ([Acts 12:12](https://ref.ly/Acts%2012.12;nasb95?t=biblia)). This is because it appears that it was John Mark’s home where the Last Supper may have taken place.
