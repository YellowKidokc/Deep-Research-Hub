# Annihilationism and Luke 16, the rich man and Lazarus
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
October 11, 2018 
16 min read 
Luke 16:19-31 (the full text is at the end of this article) is often used to support the idea of conscious torment after death. It is the account of Lazarus and the Rich Man. In it, both died, and both were alive after their deaths. The rich man was in torment in flame, and Lazarus, the poor man, was comforted. Jesus ‘ message is problematic to most annihilationists because the vast majority of them hold to the doctrine of soul sleep. But soul sleep is a poorly defined doctrine among annihilationists. What exactly is it? See the article, [Soul sleep problems and questions](https://carm.org/annihilationism/soul-sleep-problems-and-questions/).
Since most conditionalists affirm soul sleep and since they all deny eternal conscious torment, then this section of Scripture cannot be interpreted to support either one. They must explain it in such a way as to agree with their assumptions.
So, what we gather from the quotes is that story, which they insist is a parable (I don’t believe it is a parable), is not about judgment and punishment. Instead, it is “to show how to love the poor and needy in our midst.”
