# What does the Bible teach about Michael the archangel?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
January 16, 2023 
4 min read 
The word ‘Michael’ occurs 15 times in the Bible and refers to people ten times, with the remaining five referring to Michael the archangel: [Daniel 10:13](https://ref.ly/Dan%2010.13;nasb95?t=biblia), [21](https://ref.ly/Daniel%2010.21;nasb95?t=biblia); [12:1](https://ref.ly/Daniel%2012.1;nasb95?t=biblia); [Jude 9](https://ref.ly/Jude%209;nasb95?t=biblia), [Rev. 12:7](https://ref.ly/Rev.%2012.7;nasb95?t=biblia). In the NASB, these five references, Michael is called the ‘chief prince’ in [Dan. 10:13](https://ref.ly/Dan.%2010.13;nasb95?t=biblia), a ‘prince’ in [Dan. 10:21](https://ref.ly/Dan.%2010.21;nasb95?t=biblia), the ‘great prince’ in [Dan. 12:1](https://ref.ly/Dan.%2012.1;nasb95?t=biblia), ‘archangel’ in [Jude 9](https://ref.ly/Jude%209;nasb95?t=biblia), and simply ‘Michael’ in [Rev. 12:7](https://ref.ly/Rev.%2012.7;nasb95?t=biblia). So, we know that he is an archangel. The word ‘archangel’ only occurs twice in the New Testament, [1 Thess. 4:16](https://ref.ly/1%20Thess.%204.16;nasb95?t=biblia) and [Jude 9](https://ref.ly/Jude%209;nasb95?t=biblia).
We don’t know the precise hierarchical arrangement of angelic majesties and which are more powerful than others. But, an archangel seems to be a chief angel ([Dan. 10:13](https://ref.ly/Dan.%2010.13;nasb95?t=biblia)), who is in charge of other angels and appears to have more power and authority. Furthermore, the archangel Michael seems to be a warrior angel since he is referenced as the one doing battle ([Dan. 10:13](https://ref.ly/Dan.%2010.13;nasb95?t=biblia), [21](https://ref.ly/Dan%2010.21;nasb95?t=biblia)) and is also the one who disputed with the devil over the body of Moses ([Jude 9](https://ref.ly/Jude%209;nasb95?t=biblia)). No other angel in the Bible is referenced as an archangel. The only words spoken by Michael recorded in the Bible are found in [Jude 9](https://ref.ly/Jude%209;nasb95?t=biblia) when he disputed with the devil over the body of Moses and said, “the Lord rebuke you!”
Some religions (Seventh-day Adventists and the Jehovah’s Witnesses) refer to Michael, the archangel in the Old Testament, as Jesus. But this is not true. Let me explain why.
