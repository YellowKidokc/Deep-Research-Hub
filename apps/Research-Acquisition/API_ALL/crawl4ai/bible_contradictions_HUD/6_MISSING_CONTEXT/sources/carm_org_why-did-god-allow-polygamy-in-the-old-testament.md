# Why did God allow polygamy in the Old Testament?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
May 02, 2017 
4 min read 
God allowed polygamy (as well as divorce) in the Old Testament because of the hardness of people’s hearts ([Matt. 19:8](https://ref.ly/Matt.%2019.8;nasb95?t=biblia)). God never approved of polygamy nor did he intend it to be the norm. In fact, he specifically said not to have more than one wife because, in part, they can turn a person’s heart away from God. [Deuteronomy 17:17](https://ref.ly/Deut%2017.17;nasb95?t=biblia), “He shall not multiply wives for himself, or else his heart will turn away; nor shall he greatly increase silver and gold for himself.” Nevertheless, God permitted polygamy, just as he permits people to do other sinful acts.
The standard that God established in the Garden of Eden was monogamy, one man and one woman. [Genesis 1:27](https://ref.ly/Gen%201.27;nasb95?t=biblia), “God created man in His own image, in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them.” God did not create multiple people, but only one man and one woman, and from them, in that bond of marriage, was the means to have families and fill the earth. God goes on to say in [Genesis 2:24](https://ref.ly/Gen%202.24;nasb95?t=biblia), “For this reason, a man shall leave his father and his mother, and be joined to his wife; and they shall become one flesh.” This principle is in the New Testament as well.
So, polygamy was permitted by God in the Old Testament but it was not his design. It often leads people astray and caused dissension within households where multiple wives are present. It did not properly represent the relationship between Christ and his bride the church ([Ephesians 5:25-27](https://ref.ly/Eph%205.25-27;nasb95?t=biblia)). He does not have multiple brides, he is one bride.
