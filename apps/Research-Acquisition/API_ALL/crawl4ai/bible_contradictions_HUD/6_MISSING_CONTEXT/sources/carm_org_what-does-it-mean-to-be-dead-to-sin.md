# What does it mean to be dead to sin?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
October 26, 2010 
4 min read 
The phrase “dead to sin” only occurs once in the Bible. Roman 6:11 says, “Even so consider yourselves to be dead to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus.” As Christians, we are dead to sin through our union with Christ. In other words, We are to consider ourselves as being free from the bondage of sin.  ![Dead to sin](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2009/01/person-bondage-sin.jpg) Jesus is the one who bore our sins in his body on the cross ([1 Peter 2:24](https://ref.ly/1%20Pet%202.24;nasb95?t=biblia)), and we died with him when he was crucified ( [Romans 6:6](https://ref.ly/Rom%206.6;nasb95?t=biblia):8). So, the phrase “dead to sin” means that the Christian who is trusted in the work of Christ on the cross is no longer a slave of sin and is freed from it.
Consider these verses.
The last verse, [Romans 7:4](https://ref.ly/Rom%207.4;nasb95?t=biblia) is particularly interesting and relevant to answer the question. Because of our union with Christ and because we were “in him” (a phrase used regarding representation, see [1 Cor. 15:22](https://ref.ly/1%20Cor.%2015.22;nasb95?t=biblia) and Federal Headship), it is said that when Jesus died on the cross, we died with him. The Bible says that you are free from the law when you have died. Therefore, because we have died with Christ, we have died to the law. What is interesting is that the law reveals our sin ([Romans 7:7](https://ref.ly/Rom%207.7;nasb95?t=biblia)). But, if the law no longer has power over us because we died to it with Christ, then we are said to be dead to sin.
