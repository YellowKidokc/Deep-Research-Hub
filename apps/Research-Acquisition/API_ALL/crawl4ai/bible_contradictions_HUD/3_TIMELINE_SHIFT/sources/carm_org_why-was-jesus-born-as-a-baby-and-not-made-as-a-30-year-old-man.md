# Why was Jesus born as a baby and not made as a 30-year-old man?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 21, 2010 
3 min read 
The reason that Jesus was born as a baby and not made as a mature 30-year-old man is that in order for Him to be the Messiah and fulfill prophecy, he needed to be born under the law. According to [Galatians 4:4](https://ref.ly/Gal%204.4;nasb95?t=biblia), Jesus was “born of a woman, born under the law,” indicating His submission to the Mosaic Law from birth. This submission necessitated His circumcision on the eighth day, a ritual He could not have partaken in had He appeared as a 30-year-old man. By undergoing this rite, Jesus fully identified with the Jewish people, fulfilling the law’s requirements from infancy.
Also, if He were not born, then Jesus would not be both divine and human because there would not be a true incarnation of the Word, which was God ([John 1:1](https://ref.ly/John%201.1;nasb95?t=biblia)), and was made flesh ([John 1:14](https://ref.ly/John%201.14;nasb95?t=biblia)) in the one person of Jesus. Also, if He were “made 30 years old,” then he would not _really_ be 30 years old. He would only _appear_ to be 30 years of age. This is important because in order for a priest to be eligible for His priestly work he had to be 30 years of age ([Num. 4:3](https://ref.ly/Num.%204.3;nasb95?t=biblia)). So Jesus needed to be born under the law, be circumcised, be in subjection to His parents, and grow to the age of 30 before He was eligible to become the high priest after the order of Melchizedek and perform the atoning work ([Heb. 6:20](https://ref.ly/Heb.%206.20;nasb95?t=biblia); [7:25](https://ref.ly/Heb%207.25;nasb95?t=biblia)). ![personhood](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/person-jesus-single-line.png)
The incarnation of Jesus as a newborn rather than appearing as a fully grown man holds profound theological significance within Christian doctrine. This deliberate choice fulfilled specific prophecies and adhered to the requirements of Jewish law, underscoring the authenticity of His human experience.
