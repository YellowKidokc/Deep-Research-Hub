## Precision Standards Are Not Universal

Modern Western culture treats numerical precision as a marker of reliability. Ancient Near Eastern culture did not share this assumption. Round numbers were standard convention, not deception. When the Bible reports '400 years' in one place and '430 years' in another, it is using a round number and a more precise number for the same period — exactly as you might say 'about an hour' and 'sixty-three minutes' without contradicting yourself.

## Representative Cases

The Egyptian captivity lasted 430 years according to Exodus 12:40 and 'about 400 years' according to Genesis 15:13 and Acts 7:6. The flood lasted 40 days of rain (Genesis 7:12) within a larger water event lasting 150 days (Genesis 7:24). Elijah's drought lasted '3 years' (1 Kings 18:1) or '3 years and 6 months' (Luke 4:25 / James 5:17). In each case, the round figure and the precise figure refer to the same event with different levels of precision.
The molten sea in Solomon's temple held 2,000 baths (1 Kings 7:26) or 3,000 baths (2 Chronicles 4:5). This likely represents capacity vs. operational fill level, or approximation at different precision levels. Either way, the discrepancy is exactly the kind that appears when two records describe the same physical object using different measurement approaches — not evidence of competing historical traditions about whether the object existed.

## The Standard

Demanding modern numerical precision from ancient texts and then declaring 'contradiction' when the text uses ancient numerical conventions is anachronistic. It would be like criticizing a medieval cartographer for not using GPS coordinates. The information is accurate within the conventions of its time. Thirteen alleged contradictions resolve by simply acknowledging this — a principle that any student of ancient history already takes for granted.
