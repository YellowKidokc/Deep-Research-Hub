**Adrian Bejan** is a [Romanian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian-American "Romanian-American") professor who has made contributions to modern thermodynamics and developed the [constructal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#Constructal_law). He is J. A. Jones [Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Professor "Distinguished Professor") of [Mechanical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_Engineering "Mechanical Engineering") at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University")[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Ephrat-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Duke_webpage-2) and author of the books _Design in Nature_ ,[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-3) _The Physics of Life_ [[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_PoL-4)_, Freedom and Evolution_ [[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-:1-5) and _Time And Beauty_.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-:2-6) He is an Honorary Member [[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-ASMEhonorary-7) of the [American Society of Mechanical Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Mechanical_Engineers "American Society of Mechanical Engineers") and was awarded the [Benjamin Franklin Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin_Medal_\(Franklin_Institute\) "Benjamin Franklin Medal \(Franklin Institute\)") and the [ASME Medal](https://www.asme.org/about-asme/media-inquiries/press-releases/duke-university-professor-adrian-bejan-receives-asme-medal). 
## Early life and education
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adrian_Bejan&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")]
Bejan was born in [Galaţi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gala%C5%A3i "Galaţi"), a city on the [Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube "Danube") in [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania "Romania"). His mother, Marioara Bejan (1914–1998), was a pharmacist.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Ephrat-1)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) His father, Dr. Anghel Bejan (1910–1976), was a veterinarian.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) Bejan showed an early talent in drawing, and his parents enrolled him in art school. He also excelled in basketball, which earned him a position on the [Romanian national basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_national_basketball_team "Romanian national basketball team").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Basket-9)
At age 19 Bejan won a scholarship to the United States and entered [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) In 1972 he was awarded BS and MS degrees as a member of the Honors Course in Mechanical Engineering.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Duke_webpage-2)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) He graduated in 1975 with a PhD from MIT a thesis titled "Improved thermal design of the cryogenic cooling system for a superconducting synchronous generator". His advisor was Joseph L. Smith Jr.,[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-10) a disciple of Prof. Joseph H. Keenan. 
## Career
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adrian_Bejan&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")]
From 1976 to 1978 Bejan was a [Miller research fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Research_Fellows "Miller Research Fellows") in at the University of California Berkeley working with [Chang-Lin Tien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang-Lin_Tien "Chang-Lin Tien").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) In 1978 he moved to Colorado and joined the faculty of the Department of Mechanical Engineering at the University of Colorado in Boulder. In 1982 Bejan published his first book, _Entropy Generation Through Heat and Fluid Flow_. The book is aimed at practical applications of the [second law of thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_law_of_thermodynamics "Second law of thermodynamics"), and presented his ideas on [irreversibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreversibility "Irreversibility"), availability and [exergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exergy "Exergy") analysis in a form for engineers.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) In 1984 he published the first edition of _Convection Heat Transfer'_. In an era when researchers did heat transfer calculations using numerical methods on supercomputers, the book emphasized new research methods such as intersection of asymptotes, heatlines, and scale analysis to solve problems.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8)
Bejan was appointed [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_\(highest_academic_rank\) "Professor \(highest academic rank\)") at Duke University in 1984. In 1988 he published the first edition of his textbook _Advanced Engineering Thermodynamics_. The book combined thermodynamics theory with engineering heat transfer and [fluid mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_mechanics "Fluid mechanics"), and introduced entropy generation minimization as a method of optimization.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) In 1996 the ASME awarded him the Worcester Reed Warner Medal for "originality, challenges to orthodoxy, and impact on thermodynamics and heat transfer, which were made through his first three books".[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-11)
In 1989 Bejan was appointed the J. A. Jones Distinguished Professor of Mechanical Engineering. In 1988 and 1989, his peers named two dimensionless groups _[Bejan number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bejan_number "Bejan number") (Be),_ in two different fields: for the pressure difference group, in heat transfer by forced convection, and for the dimensionless ratio of fluid friction irreversibility divided by heat transfer irreversibility, in thermodynamics.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Duke_webpage-2) From 1992 to 1996 he published four more books, _Convection in Porous Media_ , _Heat Transfer_ , _Thermal Design and Optimization_ and _Entropy Generation Minimization_.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8)
## Constructal law
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adrian_Bejan&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Constructal law")]
In 1995[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) while reviewing entropy generation minimization for a symposium paper and writing another paper on the cooling of electronic components, Bejan formulated the **constructal law**.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-:0-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan1997-13) The constructal law is an organizing design principle by which natural phenomena as well as human designed systems will _evolve_ in a way that facilitates the flow of energy and material passing through it.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-14)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-ref3-15) As an example, for electronic components too small for [convective cooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection_\(heat_transfer\) "Convection \(heat transfer\)"), they must be designed for efficient conduction. The 1995 paper provides a method for efficiently designing conductive paths, from smaller paths leading to larger ones. The similarity of the solution to the branching structures seen in multiple inanimate and living things led to his statement of what he calls a new law of nature: "For a finite-size system to persist in time (to live), it must evolve in such a way that it provides easier access to the imposed (global) currents that flow through it."[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-:0-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan1997-13) To emphasize the coming together of paths he called the theory _constructal_ from the Latin verb "to construct", the opposite time direction of _fractal_ from the Latin "to break".[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-:0-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan1997-13)
Bejan incorporated his constructal law into the second edition of his textbook, _Advanced Engineering Thermodynamics_ (1997).[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) He continued thermodynamics and its constructal law and implications.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) In 2004, he published _Porous and Complex Flow Structures in Modern Technologies_.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Bejan_60-8) The same year, he and [Sylvie Lorente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvie_Lorente "Sylvie Lorente") were awarded the Edward F. Obert Award by the ASME for their paper "Thermodynamic Formulation of the Constructal Law"[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Duke_webpage-2) In 2008 he published _Design with Constructal Theory_.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-16)
### Awards for Constructal Law
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adrian_Bejan&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Awards for Constructal Law")]
In 2011 the [American Society of Mechanical Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Mechanical_Engineers "American Society of Mechanical Engineers") presented him with an honorary membership. He was cited for "an extraordinary record of creative work, including the unification of thermodynamics and heat transfer; the conceptual development of design as a science that unites all fields; legendary contributions to engineering education; and, since 1996, the discovery and continued development of the constructal law."[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-ASMEhonorary-7)
Bejan has also written books for the general audience. In 2012 he published _Design in Nature: How the Constructal Law Governs Evolution in Biology, Technology, and Social Organization_ and in 2016 _The Physics of Life: The Evolution of Everything_.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-Ephrat-1) Bejan's books for the general audience continued with _Freedom and Evolution, Hierarchy in Nature, Society and Science_ (2020),[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-:1-5) and _Time and Beauty, Why Time Flies and Beauty Never Dies_ (2022).[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-:2-6) He credits these books for his award of the Ralph Coats Roe Medal from the ASME in 2017.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-17) He was cited for "permanent contributions to the public appreciation of the pivotal role of engineering in an advanced society through outstanding accomplishments as an engineering scientist and educator, renowned communicator and prolific writer".[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-18)
In November 2017 the [Franklin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Institute "Franklin Institute") of [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") announced that Bejan would be awarded the 2018 Benjamin Franklin Medal in Mechanical Engineering.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-19) He was cited for "his pioneering interdisciplinary contributions in thermodynamics and convection heat transfer that have improved the performance of engineering systems, and for constructal theory, which predicts natural design and its evolution in engineering, scientific, and social systems."[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-20)
On 27 June 2019, in Berlin, the Humboldt Foundation awarded Bejan the Humboldt Research Award for lifetime achievement. He was cited for "his pioneering contributions to modern thermodynamics and "Constructal Law" – a law of physics that predicts natural design and its evolution in biology, geophysics, climate change, technology, social organization, evolutionary design and development, wealth and sustainability".[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-21)
On 30 December 2019, in Ankara, the Turkish Academy of Sciences (TÜBA) awarded Bejan the TÜBA International Academy Prize in the category of Basic and Engineering Sciences "for his remarkable number of creative works such as combining thermodynamics and heat transfer in the field of thermodynamics, developing design as a science that brings together all fields, and putting forth "Constructal Theory".[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-22)
On 20 February 2020, in Durham, the French government awarded Bejan the title of Knight of the French Order of Academic Palms.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-23)
On 18 July 2021, the International Association for Green Energy (IAGE) gave Bejan the IAGE Lifetime Achievement Award “For revolutionary contributions to thermal sciences through entropy generation minimization and the original development of a new law in physics, the constructal law, for predicting natural design and its evolution as climate, social ecosystems, and sustainability.”[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-24)
In September 2023, peers from many countries reviewed Bejan's scholarly legacy on the occasion of his 75th birthday.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-25)
In April 2024, Duke University honored Bejan for excellence in teaching and research.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-26)
On 26 August 2024, Adrian Bejan was named the 2024 recipient of The American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) Medal.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-:3-27) The award, established in 1920, is the highest award that the Society can bestow and recognizes eminently distinguished engineering achievement. Bejan is honored for “unprecedented creativity, breadth, and permanent impact on engineering; for developments in the new science of energy, motion, form, and evolution; and for building bridges to design in biological, geophysical, and sociological systems. Bejan is credited with several groundbreaking developments. He unified thermodynamics with heat transfer, fluid dynamics, and the science of form (i.e., flow configuration, image, design), as a counterweight to the doctrine of reductionism; discovered, taught, and applied the Constructal Law of evolution in nature; and brought together biologists, physicists, engineers, sociologists, philosophers, economists, managers, and athletes with creative books for the public, including Design in Nature (2012), The Physics of Life (2016), Freedom and Evolution (2020), and Time and Beauty (2022). His influential work and prolific publication record have earned him 18 honorary doctorates from 11 countries. He holds a position among the top 0.01% of most-cited and impactful scientists, is the sixth most impactful scholar in mechanical engineering worldwide, and the 11th across all engineering disciplines, according to the citations impact database in PLOS Biology.” 
At a public ceremony on 9 October 2024 in Bucharest, the Romanian Basketball Federation conferred upon Prof. Bejan the Title of Excellence: "For promoting the role of sport in achieving excellence in academia, and his remarkable contributions to combining science and sport, embodying in his career as physicist and basketball player an exceptional synergy between the laws of physics and the dynamics of sport. Through his innovative studies and the applicability of constructal theory in the field of movement, he opened new horizons for understanding and optimizing performance in sport in general, and in basketball in particular. The Romanian Basketball Federation recognizes his academic excellence and the impact he had on the evolution of basketball from a scientific and educational perspective."[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bejan#cite_note-28)
