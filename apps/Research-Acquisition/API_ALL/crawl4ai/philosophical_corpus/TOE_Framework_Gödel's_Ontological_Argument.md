Logical argument for the existence of God
**Gödel's ontological proof** is a [formal argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_proof "Formal proof") by the mathematician [Kurt Gödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_G%C3%B6del "Kurt Gödel") (1906–1978) for the [existence of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existence_of_God "Existence of God"). The argument is in a line of development that goes back to [Anselm of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anselm_of_Canterbury "Anselm of Canterbury") (1033–1109). St. Anselm's [ontological argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontological_argument "Ontological argument"), in its most succinct form, is as follows: "God, by definition, is that for which no greater can be conceived. God exists in the understanding. If God exists in the understanding, we could imagine Him to be greater by existing in [reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality "Reality"). Therefore, God must exist." A more elaborate version was given by [Gottfried Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Leibniz "Gottfried Leibniz") (1646–1716); this is the version that Gödel studied and attempted to clarify with his ontological argument. 
The argument uses [modal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_logic "Modal logic"), which deals with statements about what is _necessarily_ true or _possibly_ true. From the axioms that a property can only be positive if not-having-it is not positive, and that properties implied by a positive property must all also be themselves positive, it concludes that (since positive properties [do not involve contradiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_contradiction "Principle of contradiction")) for any positive property, there is possibly a being that instantiates it. It defines God as the being instantiating all positive properties. After defining what it means for a property to be "the essence" of something (the one property that necessarily implies all its other properties), it concludes that God's instantiation of all positive properties must be the essence of God. After defining a property of "necessary existence" and taking it as an axiom that it is positive, the argument concludes that, since God must have this property, God must exist necessarily. 
## History
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]
Gödel left a fourteen-point outline of his philosophical beliefs in his papers.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-1) Points relevant to the ontological proof include:      4. There are other worlds and rational beings of a different and higher kind.     5. The world in which we live is not the only one in which we shall live or have lived.     13. There is a scientific (exact) philosophy and theology, which deals with concepts of the highest abstractness; and this is also most highly fruitful for science.     14. Religions are, for the most part, bad—but religion is not.
The first version of the ontological proof in Gödel's papers is dated "around 1941". Gödel is not known to have told anyone about his work on the proof until 1970, when he thought he was dying. In February, he allowed [Dana Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Scott "Dana Scott") to copy out a version of the proof, which circulated privately. In August 1970, Gödel told [Oskar Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Morgenstern "Oskar Morgenstern") that he was "satisfied" with the proof, but Morgenstern recorded in his diary entry for 29 August 1970, that Gödel would not publish because he was afraid that others might think "that he actually believes in God, whereas he is only engaged in a logical investigation (that is, in showing that such a proof with classical assumptions (completeness, etc.) correspondingly axiomatized, is possible)."[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-2) Gödel died January 14, 1978. Another version, slightly different from Scott's, was found in his papers. It was finally published, together with Scott's version, in 1987.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-3)
In letters to his mother, who was not a churchgoer and had raised Kurt and his brother as [freethinkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freethinker "Freethinker"),[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-4) Gödel argued at length for a belief in an afterlife.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-5) He did the same in an interview with a skeptical [Hao Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hao_Wang_\(academic\) "Hao Wang \(academic\)"), who said: "I expressed my doubts as G spoke [...] Gödel smiled as he replied to my questions, obviously aware that his answers were not convincing me."[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-6) Wang reports that Gödel's wife, Adele, two days after Gödel's death, told Wang that "Gödel, although he did not go to church, was religious and read the Bible in bed every Sunday morning."[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-7) In an unmailed answer to a questionnaire, Gödel described his religion as "baptized Lutheran (but not member of any religious congregation). My belief is _[theistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theism "Theism")_ , not [pantheistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheism "Pantheism"), following [Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz "Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz") rather than [Spinoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinoza "Spinoza")."[[note 1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-8)
## Outline
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Outline")]
The proof[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-11) uses [modal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_logic "Modal logic"), which distinguishes between [_necessary_ truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_truth "Logical truth") and [_contingent_ truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contingency_\(philosophy\) "Contingency \(philosophy\)"). In the most common semantics for modal logic, many "[possible worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_logic#Semantics "Modal logic")" are considered. A [truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth "Truth") is _necessary_ if it is true in all possible worlds. By contrast, if a statement happens to be true in our world, but is false in another world, then it is a _contingent_ truth. A statement that is true in some world (not necessarily our own) is called a _[possible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logically_possible "Logically possible")_ truth. 
Furthermore, the proof uses [higher-order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-order_logic "Higher-order logic") (modal) logic because the definition of God employs an explicit quantification over properties.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-12)
First, Gödel axiomatizes the notion of a "positive property":[[note 2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-13) for each property _φ_ , either _φ_ or its [negation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negation "Negation") ¬ _φ_ must be positive, but not both (axiom 2). If a positive property _φ_ implies a property _ψ_ in each possible world, then _ψ_ is positive, too (axiom 1).[[note 3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-14) Gödel then argues that each positive property is "possibly exemplified", i.e. applies at least to some object in some world (theorem 1). Defining an object to be Godlike if it has all positive properties (definition 1),[[note 4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-15) and requiring that property to be positive itself (axiom 3),[[note 5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-16) Gödel shows that in _some_ possible world a Godlike object exists (theorem 2), called "God" in the following.[[note 6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-17) Gödel proceeds to prove that a Godlike object exists in _every_ possible world. 
To this end, he defines _essences_ : if _x_ is an object in some world, then a property _φ_ is said to be an essence of _x_ if _φ_(_x_) is true in that world and if _φ_ necessarily entails all other properties that _x_ has in that world (definition 2). Requiring positive properties being positive in every possible world (axiom 4), Gödel can show that Godlikeness is an essence of a Godlike object (theorem 3). Now, _x_ is said to _exist necessarily_ if, for every essence _φ_ of _x_ , there is an element _y_ with property _φ_ in every possible world (definition 3). Axiom 5 requires necessary existence to be a positive property. 
Hence, it must follow from Godlikeness. Moreover, Godlikeness is an essence of God, since it entails all positive properties, and any non-positive property is the negation of some positive property, so God cannot have any non-positive properties. Since necessary existence is also a positive property (axiom 5), it must be a property of every Godlike object, as every Godlike object has all the positive properties (definition 1). Since any Godlike object is necessarily existent, it follows that any Godlike object in one world is a Godlike object in all worlds, by the definition of necessary existence. Given the existence of a Godlike object in one world, proven above, we may conclude that there is a Godlike object in every possible world, as required (theorem 4). Besides axiom 1-5 and definition 1–3, a few other axioms from modal logic[_[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_] were tacitly used in the proof. 
From these hypotheses, it is also possible to prove that there is only one God in each world by Leibniz's law, the [identity of indiscernibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_of_indiscernibles "Identity of indiscernibles"): two or more objects are identical (the same) if they have all their properties in common, and so, there would only be one object in each world that possesses property G. Gödel did not attempt to do so however, as he purposely limited his proof to the issue of existence, rather than uniqueness.

## Criticism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Criticism")]
Most criticism of Gödel's proof is aimed at its axioms: as with any proof in any logical system, if the axioms the proof depends on are doubted, then the conclusions can be doubted. It is particularly applicable to Gödel's proof – because it rests on five axioms, some of which are considered questionable. A proof does not necessitate that the conclusion be correct, but rather that by accepting the axioms, the conclusion follows logically. 
Many philosophers have called the axioms into question. The first layer of criticism is simply that there are no arguments presented that give reasons why the axioms are true. A second layer is that these particular axioms lead to unwelcome conclusions. This line of thought was argued by [Jordan Howard Sobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Howard_Sobel "Jordan Howard Sobel"),[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-18) showing that if the axioms are accepted, they lead to a "[modal collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_collapse "Modal collapse")" where every statement that is true is necessarily true, i.e. the sets of necessary, of contingent, and of possible truths all coincide (provided there are [accessible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accessibility_relation "Accessibility relation") worlds at all).[[note 7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-19) According to [Robert Koons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Koons "Robert Koons"),[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-Koons.2005-10): 9  Sobel suggested in a 2005 conference paper[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] that Gödel might have welcomed modal collapse.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-20)
There are suggested amendments to the proof, presented by [C. Anthony Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Anthony_Anderson "C. Anthony Anderson"),[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-21) but argued to be refutable by Anderson and Michael Gettings.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-22) Sobel's proof of modal collapse has been questioned by Koons,[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-Koons.2005-10)[[note 8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-23) but a counter-defence by Sobel has been given.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
Gödel's proof has also been questioned by [Graham Oppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Oppy "Graham Oppy"),[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-24) asking whether many other almost-gods would also be "proven" through Gödel's axioms. This counter-argument has been questioned by Gettings,[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-25) who agrees that the axioms might be questioned, but disagrees that Oppy's particular counter-example can be shown from Gödel's axioms. 
Religious scholar [Fr. Robert J. Spitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Spitzer_\(priest\) "Robert Spitzer \(priest\)") accepted Gödel's proof, calling it "an improvement over the Anselmian Ontological Argument (which does not work)."[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-26)
There are, however, many more criticisms, most of them focusing on the question of whether these axioms must be rejected to avoid odd conclusions. The broader criticism is that even if the axioms cannot be shown to be false, that does not mean that they are true. Hilbert's famous [remark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_geometry#Logical_synthesis "Synthetic geometry") about interchangeability of the primitives' names applies to those in Gödel's ontological axioms ("positive", "god-like", "essence") as well as to those in Hilbert's geometry axioms ("point", "line", "plane"). According to [André Fuhrmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Fuhrmann "André Fuhrmann") (2005) it remains to show that the dazzling notion prescribed by traditions and often believed to be essentially mysterious satisfies Gödel's axioms. This is not a mathematical, but a theological task.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-27): 364–366  It is this task which decides which religion's god has been proven to exist. 
## Computationally verified versions
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Computationally verified versions")]
[Christoph Benzmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Benzm%C3%BCller "Christoph Benzmüller") and Bruno Woltzenlogel-Paleo formalized Gödel's proof to a level that is suitable for [automated theorem proving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_theorem_proving "Automated theorem proving") or at least computational verification via [proof assistants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_assistant "Proof assistant").[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-28) The effort made headlines in German newspapers. According to the authors of this effort, they were inspired by [Melvin Fitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Fitting "Melvin Fitting")'s book.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-29)
In 2014, they computationally verified Gödel's proof (in the [above](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#Symbolic_notation) version).[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-Benzmuller.Woltzenlogel.2014-30): 97 [[note 9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-31) They also proved that this version's axioms are consistent,[[note 10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-32) but imply modal collapse,[[note 11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-33) thus confirming Sobel's 1987 argument. In the same paper, they suspected Gödel's original version of the axioms[[note 12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-Godel_vs_Scott-35) to be inconsistent, as they failed to prove their consistency.[[note 13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-36)
In 2016, they gave an automated proof that the original version implies  ◊ ◻ ⊥ {\displaystyle \Diamond \Box \bot } ![{\\displaystyle \\Diamond \\Box \\bot }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/0b28367d8891f73204207213ba369c000048f34d), i.e., is inconsistent in every modal logic with a reflexive or symmetric [accessibility relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accessibility_relation "Accessibility relation").[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-Benzmuller.Woltzenlogel.2016-37): 940 lf  Moreover, they gave an argument that this version is inconsistent in every logic at all,[[note 14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-38) but failed to duplicate it by automated provers.[[note 15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-39) However, they were able to verify Melvin Fitting's reformulation of the argument and guarantee its consistency.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-40)
In 2025, Benzmüller and [Dana Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Scott "Dana Scott") provided the proofs with [Isabelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelle_\(proof_assistant\) "Isabelle \(proof assistant\)") for Scott's variant of Gödel’s modal ontological argument.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_note-scott-41)

## Notes
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Notes")]
  1. **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_ref-8 "Jump up")** Gödel's answer to a special questionnaire sent him by the sociologist Burke Grandjean. This answer is quoted directly in Wang 1987, p. 18, and indirectly in Wang 1996, p. 112. It's also quoted directly in Dawson 1997, p. 6, who cites Wang 1987. The Grandjean questionnaire is perhaps the most extended autobiographical item in Gödel's papers. Gödel filled it out in pencil and wrote a cover letter, but he never returned it. "Theistic" is italicized in both Wang 1987 and Wang 1996. It is possible that this italicization is Wang's and not Gödel's. The quote follows Wang 1987, with two corrections taken from Wang 1996. Wang 1987 reads "Baptist Lutheran" where Wang 1996 has "baptized Lutheran". "Baptist Lutheran" makes no sense, especially in context, and was presumably a typo or mistranscription. Wang 1987 has "rel. cong.", which in Wang 1996 is expanded to "religious congregation".
  2. **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_ref-13 "Jump up")** It assumes that it is possible to single out _positive_ properties from among all properties. Gödel comments that "Positive means positive in the [moral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality "Morality") [aesthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetics "Aesthetics") sense (independently of the accidental structure of the world)... It may also mean pure _attribution_ as opposed to _privation_ (or containing privation)." (Gödel 1995), see also manuscript in (Gawlick 2012).
  3. **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_ref-14 "Jump up")** As a profane example, if the property of being green is positive, that of not being red is, too (by axiom 1), hence that of being red is negative (by axiom 2). More generally, at most one color can be considered positive.
  4. **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_ref-15 "Jump up")** Continuing the color example, a godlike object must have the unique color that is considered positive, or no color at all; both alternatives may seem counter-intuitive.
  5. **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_ref-16 "Jump up")** If one considers the [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order "Partial order") ⪯ {\displaystyle \preceq } ![{\\displaystyle \\preceq }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/63dfe475e1377b3b4e936a3aa8fb1d7177dcdbc3) defined by  φ ⪯ ψ {\displaystyle \varphi \preceq \psi } ![{\\displaystyle \\varphi \\preceq \\psi }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/05b3ebca277d5cdf6147502bfd25b2ad7bdb8e14) if  ◻ ∀ y ( φ ( y ) → ψ ( y ) ) {\displaystyle \square \forall y(\varphi (y)\to \psi (y))} ![{\\displaystyle \\square \\forall y\(\\varphi \(y\)\\to \\psi \(y\)\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/99455e2271f0dd738a32ca99ca5fc81d8bc41d4d), then Axioms 1-3 can be summarized by saying that positive properties form an [ultrafilter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter "Ultrafilter") on this ordering. Definition 1 and Axiom 4 are needed to establish the _Godlike_ property as principal element of the ultrafilter.
  6. **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_ref-17 "Jump up")** By removing all modal operators from axioms, definitions, proofs, and theorems, a modified version of theorem 2 is obtained saying "∃ _x_ _G_(_x_)", i.e. "There exists an object which has all positive, but no negative properties". Nothing more than axioms 1-3, definition 1, and theorems 1-2 needs to be considered for this result.
  7. **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_ref-19 "Jump up")** Formally,  p ⇒ ◻ p {\displaystyle p\Rightarrow \Box p} ![{\\displaystyle p\\Rightarrow \\Box p}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/a89ac7db0b0a0f232f37245a82956032df777459) for all _p_ implies  ◊ p ⇒ p {\displaystyle \Diamond p\Rightarrow p} ![{\\displaystyle \\Diamond p\\Rightarrow p}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/6c35ced4643941f21417c44719ffe9c424659a9b) for all _p_ by [indirect proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_proof "Indirect proof"), and  ◻ p ⇒ ◊ p {\displaystyle \Box p\Rightarrow \Diamond p} ![{\\displaystyle \\Box p\\Rightarrow \\Diamond p}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/85bcdbc5e618cdf43ada7bf7e9443303b7145fd1) holds for all _p_ whenever there are accessible worlds.
  8. **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_ontological_proof#cite_ref-23 "Jump up")** Since Sobel's proof of modal collapse uses [lambda abstraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambda_abstraction "Lambda abstraction"), but Gödel's proof does not, Koons suggests to forbid this property-construction operation as the "most conservative" measure, before "rejecting or emending ... axioms (as Anderson does)".
