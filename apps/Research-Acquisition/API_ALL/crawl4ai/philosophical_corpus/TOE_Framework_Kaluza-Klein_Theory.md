In [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics"), **Kaluza–Klein theory** (**KK theory**) is an attempt at creating a [unified field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_field_theory "Unified field theory") of [gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation "Gravitation") and [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism "Electromagnetism") based on the idea of a [fifth dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-dimensional_space#Physics "Five-dimensional space") of space beyond the conventional four-dimensional spacetime of general relativity. According to this proposal, there are three dimensions of space and one of time but with an additional spatial dimension in the shape of a tiny circle. The Kaluza-Klein theory is named after [Theodor Kaluza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Kaluza "Theodor Kaluza") and [Oskar Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Klein "Oskar Klein"). It is [not supported by experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#Empirical_tests), but is a precursor to [supergravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergravity "Supergravity") and modern [string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_theory "String theory") (in eleven-dimensional spacetime).[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:2-1)
## Kaluza hypothesis
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Kaluza hypothesis")]
In his 1921 article,[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-kal-2) Kaluza established all the elements of the classical five-dimensional theory: the [Kaluza–Klein metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_metric "Kaluza–Klein metric"), the [Kaluza–Klein–Einstein field equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein%E2%80%93Einstein_field_equations "Kaluza–Klein–Einstein field equations"), the equations of motion, the stress–energy tensor, and the cylinder condition. With no [free parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_parameter "Free parameter"), it merely extends general relativity to five dimensions. ## Field equations from the Kaluza hypothesis
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Field equations from the Kaluza hypothesis")]
The [Kaluza–Klein–Einstein field equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein%E2%80%93Einstein_field_equations "Kaluza–Klein–Einstein field equations") of the five-dimensional theory were never adequately provided by Kaluza or Klein because they ignored the [scalar field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_field "Scalar field"). The full Kaluza field equations are generally attributed to Thiry,[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-thry-3) who obtained vacuum field equations, although Kaluza[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-kal-2) originally provided a stress–energy tensor for his theory, and Thiry included a stress–energy tensor in his thesis. But as described by Gonner,[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-gon-4) several independent groups worked on the field equations in the 1940s and earlier. Thiry is perhaps best known only because an English translation was provided by Applequist, Chodos, & Freund in their review book.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-5) Applequist et al. also provided an English translation of Kaluza's article. Translations of the three (1946, 1947, 1948) Jordan articles can be found on the [ResearchGate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ResearchGate "ResearchGate") and [Academia.edu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia.edu "Academia.edu") archives.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-jor1-6)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-jor2-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-jor3-8) The first correct English-language Kaluza field equations, including the scalar field, were provided by Williams.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-LLW-9)
To obtain the 5D Kaluza–Klein–Einstein field equations, the 5D [Kaluza–Klein–Christoffel symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein%E2%80%93Christoffel_symbol "Kaluza–Klein–Christoffel symbol") Γ ~ b c a {\displaystyle {\widetilde {\Gamma }}_{bc}^{a}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\widetilde {\\Gamma }}_{bc}^{a}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/397e7bce431189d6cbf35febfadb6ad9653faadb) are calculated from the 5D Kaluza–Klein metric  g ~ a b {\displaystyle {\widetilde {g}}_{ab}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\widetilde {g}}_{ab}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/6385a8c6c8614189b81d4dc61a317a23356b78cd), and the 5D [Kaluza–Klein–Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein%E2%80%93Ricci_tensor "Kaluza–Klein–Ricci tensor") R ~ a b {\displaystyle {\widetilde {R}}_{ab}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\widetilde {R}}_{ab}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/c7135160330a3cc9274407baec584720429d88d4) is calculated from the 5D [connections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connection_\(mathematics\) "Connection \(mathematics\)"). 
The classic results of Thiry and other authors presume the cylinder condition:       ∂ g ~ a b ∂ x 5 = 0. {\displaystyle {\frac {\partial {\widetilde {g}}_{ab}}{\partial x^{5}}}=0.} ![{\\displaystyle {\\frac {\\partial {\\widetilde {g}}_{ab}}{\\partial x^{5}}}=0.}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b0b8915a8464a57ca1446e9a32210fb196ed4767)
Without this assumption, the field equations become much more complex, providing many more degrees of freedom that can be identified with various new fields. Paul Wesson and colleagues have pursued relaxation of the cylinder condition to gain extra terms that can be identified with the matter fields,[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-10) for which Kaluza[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-kal-2) otherwise inserted a stress–energy tensor by hand. 
It has been an objection to the original Kaluza hypothesis to invoke the fifth dimension only to negate its dynamics. But Thiry argued[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-gon-4) that the interpretation of the Lorentz force law in terms of a five-dimensional [geodesic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic "Geodesic") militates strongly for a fifth dimension irrespective of the cylinder condition. ## Quantum interpretation of Klein
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Quantum interpretation of Klein")]
Kaluza's original hypothesis was purely classical and extended discoveries of general relativity. By the time of Klein's contribution, the discoveries of Heisenberg, Schrödinger, and [Louis de Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Broglie "Louis de Broglie") were receiving a lot of attention. Klein's _[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_\(journal\) "Nature \(journal\)")_ article[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-KN-16) suggested that the fifth dimension is closed and periodic, and that the identification of electric charge with motion in the fifth dimension can be interpreted as standing waves of wavelength  λ 5 {\displaystyle \lambda ^{5}} ![{\\displaystyle \\lambda ^{5}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/4f5f793c459aab62ee2a4434b7e99a5f892aa89e), much like the electrons around a nucleus in the [Bohr model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_model "Bohr model") of the atom. The quantization of electric charge could then be nicely understood in terms of integer multiples of fifth-dimensional momentum. Combining the previous Kaluza result for  U 5 {\displaystyle U^{5}} ![{\\displaystyle U^{5}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/ea7ab7383e4d1d4d76befb8cb2771c736b10e5e4) in terms of electric charge, and a [de Broglie relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Broglie_relation "De Broglie relation") for momentum  p 5 = h / λ 5 {\displaystyle p^{5}=h/\lambda ^{5}} ![{\\displaystyle p^{5}=h/\\lambda ^{5}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/93da4d1e2c5fd8263fffdd8767e63a59bb6afafe), Klein obtained[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-KN-16) an expression for the 0th mode of such waves:       m U 5 = c q G 1 / 2 = h λ 5 ⇒ λ 5 ∼ h G 1 / 2 c q , {\displaystyle mU^{5}={\frac {cq}{G^{1/2}}}={\frac {h}{\lambda ^{5}}}\quad \Rightarrow \quad \lambda ^{5}\sim {\frac {hG^{1/2}}{cq}},} ![{\\displaystyle mU^{5}={\\frac {cq}{G^{1/2}}}={\\frac {h}{\\lambda ^{5}}}\\quad \\Rightarrow \\quad \\lambda ^{5}\\sim {\\frac {hG^{1/2}}{cq}},}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/953b906da19007c4d71940ccbc218b6ce1307797)
where  h {\displaystyle h} ![{\\displaystyle h}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b26be3e694314bc90c3215047e4a2010c6ee184a) is the [Planck constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_constant "Planck constant"). Klein found that  λ 5 ∼ 10 − 30 {\displaystyle \lambda ^{5}\sim 10^{-30}} ![{\\displaystyle \\lambda ^{5}\\sim 10^{-30}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/9e211e32b3b1865418a45d2c78960bb106f8e5f0) cm, and thereby an explanation for the cylinder condition in this small value. 
Klein's _[Zeitschrift für Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeitschrift_f%C3%BCr_Physik "Zeitschrift für Physik")_ article of the same year,[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-KZ-17) gave a more detailed treatment that explicitly invoked the techniques of Schrödinger and de Broglie. It recapitulated much of the classical theory of Kaluza described above, and then departed into Klein's quantum interpretation. Klein solved a Schrödinger-like wave equation using an expansion in terms of fifth-dimensional waves resonating in the closed, compact fifth dimension. 
## Group theory interpretation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Group theory interpretation")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Kaluza_Klein_compactification.svg/330px-Kaluza_Klein_compactification.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kaluza_Klein_compactification.svg)The space _M_ × _C_ is compactified over the compact set _C_ , and after Kaluza–Klein decomposition one has an [effective field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_field_theory "Effective field theory") over _M_.
In 1926, Oskar Klein proposed that the fourth spatial dimension is curled up in a circle of a very small [radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radius "Radius"), so that a [particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle "Elementary particle") moving a short distance along that axis would return to where it began. The distance a particle can travel before reaching its initial position is said to be the size of the dimension. This extra dimension is a [compact set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set "Compact set"), and construction of this compact dimension is referred to as [compactification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactification_\(physics\) "Compactification \(physics\)"). 
In modern geometry, the extra fifth dimension can be understood to be the [circle group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_group "Circle group") [U(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U\(1\) "U\(1\)"), as [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism "Electromagnetism") can essentially be formulated as a [gauge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_theory "Gauge theory") on a [fiber bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_bundle "Fiber bundle"), the [circle bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_bundle "Circle bundle"), with [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group "Gauge group") U(1). In Kaluza–Klein theory this group suggests that gauge symmetry is the symmetry of circular compact dimensions. Once this geometrical interpretation is understood, it is relatively straightforward to replace U(1) by a general [Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group "Lie group"). Such generalizations are often called [Yang–Mills theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang%E2%80%93Mills_theory "Yang–Mills theory"). If a distinction is drawn, then it is that Yang–Mills theories occur on a flat spacetime, whereas Kaluza–Klein treats the more general case of curved spacetime. The base space of Kaluza–Klein theory need not be four-dimensional spacetime; it can be any ([pseudo-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-Riemannian_manifold "Pseudo-Riemannian manifold"))[Riemannian manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_manifold "Riemannian manifold"), or even a [supersymmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersymmetry "Supersymmetry") manifold or [orbifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbifold "Orbifold") or even a [noncommutative space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_space "Noncommutative space"). 
The construction can be outlined, roughly, as follows.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-18) One starts by considering a [principal fiber bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_fiber_bundle "Principal fiber bundle") _P_ with [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group "Gauge group") _G_ over a [manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold "Manifold") M. Given a [connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connection_\(fibred_manifold\) "Connection \(fibred manifold\)") on the bundle, and a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_tensor "Metric tensor") on the base manifold, and a gauge invariant metric on the tangent of each fiber, one can construct a [bundle metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_\(vector_bundle\) "Metric \(vector bundle\)") defined on the entire bundle. Computing the [scalar curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_curvature "Scalar curvature") of this bundle metric, one finds that it is constant on each fiber: this is the "Kaluza miracle". One did not have to explicitly impose a cylinder condition, or to compactify: by assumption, the gauge group is already compact. Next, one takes this scalar curvature as the [Lagrangian density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_density "Lagrangian density"), and, from this, constructs the [Einstein–Hilbert action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein%E2%80%93Hilbert_action "Einstein–Hilbert action") for the bundle, as a whole. The equations of motion, the [Euler–Lagrange equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler%E2%80%93Lagrange_equations "Euler–Lagrange equations"), can be then obtained by considering where the action is [stationary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_state "Stationary state") with respect to variations of either the metric on the base manifold, or of the gauge connection. Variations with respect to the base metric gives the [Einstein field equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_field_equations "Einstein field equations") on the base manifold, with the [energy–momentum tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy%E2%80%93momentum_tensor "Energy–momentum tensor") given by the [curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curvature_form "Curvature form") ([field strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_strength "Field strength")) of the gauge connection. On the flip side, the action is stationary against variations of the gauge connection precisely when the gauge connection solves the [Yang–Mills equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang%E2%80%93Mills_theory "Yang–Mills theory"). Thus, by applying a single idea: the [principle of least action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_least_action "Principle of least action"), to a single quantity: the scalar curvature on the bundle (as a whole), one obtains simultaneously all of the needed field equations, for both the spacetime and the gauge field. 
As an approach to the unification of the forces, it is straightforward to apply the Kaluza–Klein theory in an attempt to unify gravity with the [strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_force "Strong force") and [electroweak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroweak "Electroweak") forces by using the symmetry group of the [Standard Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model "Standard Model"), [SU(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU\(3\) "SU\(3\)") × [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU\(2\) "SU\(2\)") × [U(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U\(1\) "U\(1\)"). However, an attempt to convert this interesting geometrical construction into a bona-fide model of reality flounders on a number of issues, including the fact that the [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion "Fermion") must be introduced in an artificial way (in nonsupersymmetric models). Nonetheless, KK remains an important [touchstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchstone_\(metaphor\) "Touchstone \(metaphor\)") in theoretical physics and is often embedded in more sophisticated theories. It is studied in its own right as an object of geometric interest in [K-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-theory "K-theory"). 
Even in the absence of a completely satisfying theoretical physics framework, the idea of exploring extra, compactified, dimensions is of considerable interest in the [experimental physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics") and [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics") communities. A variety of predictions, with real experimental consequences, can be made (in the case of [large extra dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_extra_dimension "Large extra dimension") and [warped models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warped_model "Warped model")). For example, on the simplest of principles, one might expect to have [standing waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_wave "Standing wave") in the extra compactified dimension(s). If a spatial extra dimension is of radius _R_ , the invariant [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass "Mass") of such standing waves would be _M_ _n_ = _nh_ /_Rc_ with _n_ an [integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer "Integer"), _h_ being the [Planck constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_constant "Planck constant") and _c_ the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light "Speed of light"). This set of possible mass values is often called the **Kaluza–Klein tower**. Similarly, in [Thermal quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_quantum_field_theory "Thermal quantum field theory") a compactification of the euclidean time dimension leads to the [Matsubara frequencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsubara_frequency "Matsubara frequency") and thus to a discretized thermal energy spectrum. 
However, Klein's approach to a quantum theory is flawed[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] and, for example, leads to a calculated electron mass in the order of magnitude of the [Planck mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_mass "Planck mass").[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-19)
Examples of experimental pursuits include work by the [CDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collider_Detector_at_Fermilab "Collider Detector at Fermilab") collaboration, which has re-analyzed [particle collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_collider "Particle collider") data for the signature of effects associated with large extra dimensions/[warped models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warped_model "Warped model").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
[Robert Brandenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Brandenberger "Robert Brandenberger") and [Cumrun Vafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumrun_Vafa "Cumrun Vafa") have speculated that in the early universe, [cosmic inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_inflation "Cosmic inflation") causes three of the space dimensions to expand to cosmological size while the remaining dimensions of space remained microscopic.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-20)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Space–time–matter theory")]
One particular variant of Kaluza–Klein theory is **space–time–matter theory** or **induced matter theory** , chiefly promulgated by [Paul Wesson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_S._Wesson "Paul S. Wesson") and other members of the Space–Time–Matter Consortium.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-21) In this version of the theory, it is noted that solutions to the equation       R ~ a b = 0 {\displaystyle {\widetilde {R}}_{ab}=0} ![{\\displaystyle {\\widetilde {R}}_{ab}=0}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/8b90a084bf71238eb96e6e068fa8479b362f3459)
may be re-expressed so that in four dimensions, these solutions satisfy [Einstein's equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein%27s_equation "Einstein's equation")      G μ ν = 8 π T μ ν {\displaystyle G_{\mu \nu }=8\pi T_{\mu \nu }\,} ![{\\displaystyle G_{\\mu \\nu }=8\\pi T_{\\mu \\nu }\\,}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/72115d9e9788d737de9ad926484ec54c5b516442)
with the precise form of the _T_ _μν_ following from the [Ricci-flat condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci-flat_condition "Ricci-flat condition") on the five-dimensional space. In other words, the cylinder condition of the previous development is dropped, and the stress–energy now comes from the derivatives of the 5D metric with respect to the fifth coordinate. Because the [energy–momentum tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy%E2%80%93momentum_tensor "Energy–momentum tensor") is normally understood to be due to concentrations of matter in four-dimensional space, the above result is interpreted as saying that four-dimensional matter is induced from geometry in five-dimensional space. 
In particular, the [soliton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soliton "Soliton") solutions of  R ~ a b = 0 {\displaystyle {\widetilde {R}}_{ab}=0} ![{\\displaystyle {\\widetilde {R}}_{ab}=0}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/8b90a084bf71238eb96e6e068fa8479b362f3459) can be shown to contain the [Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedmann%E2%80%93Lema%C3%AEtre%E2%80%93Robertson%E2%80%93Walker_metric "Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric") in both radiation-dominated (early universe) and matter-dominated (later universe) forms. The general equations can be shown to be sufficiently consistent with classical [tests of general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tests_of_general_relativity "Tests of general relativity") to be acceptable on physical principles, while still leaving considerable freedom to also provide interesting [cosmological models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_model "Cosmological model"). 
## Geometric interpretation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Geometric interpretation")]
The Kaluza–Klein theory has a particularly elegant presentation in terms of geometry. In a certain sense, it looks just like ordinary gravity in [free space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_space "Free space"), except that it is phrased in five dimensions instead of four. 
### Einstein equations
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Einstein equations")]
The equations governing ordinary gravity in free space can be obtained from an [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_\(physics\) "Action \(physics\)"), by applying the [variational principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_principle "Variational principle") to a certain [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_\(physics\) "Action \(physics\)"). Let _M_ be a ([pseudo-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-Riemannian_manifold "Pseudo-Riemannian manifold"))[Riemannian manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_manifold "Riemannian manifold"), which may be taken as the [spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetime "Spacetime") of [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity"). If _g_ is the [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_\(mathematics\) "Metric \(mathematics\)") on this manifold, one defines the [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_\(physics\) "Action \(physics\)") _S_(_g_) as       S ( g ) = ∫ M R ( g ) vol ⁡ ( g ) , {\displaystyle S(g)=\int _{M}R(g)\operatorname {vol} (g),} ![{\\displaystyle S\(g\)=\\int _{M}R\(g\)\\operatorname {vol} \(g\),}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/3f75968159c42d9c6f8faed5598069b20f8bb0f7)
where _R_(_g_) is the [scalar curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_curvature "Scalar curvature"), and vol(_g_) is the [volume element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_element "Volume element"). By applying the [variational principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_principle "Variational principle") to the action       δ S ( g ) δ g = 0 , {\displaystyle {\frac {\delta S(g)}{\delta g}}=0,} ![{\\displaystyle {\\frac {\\delta S\(g\)}{\\delta g}}=0,}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/071ef8b50c8d510c8629327128e833ad0c0aeb3c)
one obtains precisely the [Einstein equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_equation "Einstein equation") for free space:       R i j − 1 2 g i j R = 0 , {\displaystyle R_{ij}-{\frac {1}{2}}g_{ij}R=0,} ![{\\displaystyle R_{ij}-{\\frac {1}{2}}g_{ij}R=0,}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/d1f851657b183b6e91c84b433376e8a014110c8a)
where _R_ _ij_ is the [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor "Ricci tensor"). 
### Maxwell equations
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Maxwell equations")]
By contrast, the [Maxwell equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_equation "Maxwell equation") describing [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism "Electromagnetism") can be understood to be the [Hodge equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Rham_cohomology "De Rham cohomology") of a [principal  U ⁡ ( 1 ) {\displaystyle \operatorname {U} (1)} ![{\\displaystyle \\operatorname {U} \(1\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/39823af285de3e72d7d50f96fe5b8ba024017af2)-bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_U\(1\)-bundle "Principal U\(1\)-bundle") or [circle bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_bundle "Circle bundle") π : P → M {\displaystyle \pi :P\to M} ![{\\displaystyle \\pi :P\\to M}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/9bc921dc155d2c06ba6ae891c0ae2c117b9ddbe9) with fiber [ U ⁡ ( 1 ) {\displaystyle \operatorname {U} (1)} ![{\\displaystyle \\operatorname {U} \(1\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/39823af285de3e72d7d50f96fe5b8ba024017af2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_U\(1\)-bundle "Principal U\(1\)-bundle"). That is, the [electromagnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field "Electromagnetic field") F {\displaystyle F} ![{\\displaystyle F}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/545fd099af8541605f7ee55f08225526be88ce57) is a [harmonic 2-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_form "Harmonic form") in the space  Ω 2 ( M ) {\displaystyle \Omega ^{2}(M)} ![{\\displaystyle \\Omega ^{2}\(M\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/d13fb94a8c7d243906c16c59d738f4e0636ff7fa) of differentiable [2-forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-form "2-form") on the manifold  M {\displaystyle M} ![{\\displaystyle M}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f82cade9898ced02fdd08712e5f0c0151758a0dd). In the absence of charges and currents, the free-field Maxwell equations are       d F = 0 and d ⋆ F = 0 , {\displaystyle \mathrm {d} F=0\quad {\text{and}}\quad \mathrm {d} {\star }F=0,} ![{\\displaystyle \\mathrm {d} F=0\\quad {\\text{and}}\\quad \\mathrm {d} {\\star }F=0,}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/3cc9d5510d6aa2ccd5484991a3457cfed91cc96c)
where  ⋆ {\displaystyle \star } ![{\\displaystyle \\star }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/bd316a21eeb5079a850f223b1d096a06bfa788c0) is the [Hodge star operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_star_operator "Hodge star operator"). 
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Kaluza–Klein geometry")]
To build the Kaluza–Klein theory, one picks an invariant metric on the circle  S 1 {\displaystyle S^{1}} ![{\\displaystyle S^{1}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/60796c8d0c03cf575637d3202463b214d9635880) that is the fiber of the U(1)-bundle of electromagnetism. In this discussion, an _invariant metric_ is simply one that is invariant under rotations of the circle. Suppose that this metric gives the circle a total length  Λ {\displaystyle \Lambda } ![{\\displaystyle \\Lambda }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/0ac0a4a98a414e3480335f9ba652d12571ec6733). One then considers metrics  g ^ {\displaystyle {\widehat {g}}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\widehat {g}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f03ff4b434f75ad5db89ea97ae9f4336927e6d9f) on the bundle  P {\displaystyle P} ![{\\displaystyle P}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b4dc73bf40314945ff376bd363916a738548d40a) that are consistent with both the fiber metric, and the metric on the underlying manifold  M {\displaystyle M} ![{\\displaystyle M}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f82cade9898ced02fdd08712e5f0c0151758a0dd). The consistency conditions are: 
  * The projection of  g ^ {\displaystyle {\widehat {g}}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\widehat {g}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f03ff4b434f75ad5db89ea97ae9f4336927e6d9f) to the [vertical subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_bundle "Vertical bundle") Vert p ⁡ P ⊂ T p P {\displaystyle \operatorname {Vert} _{p}P\subset T_{p}P} ![{\\displaystyle \\operatorname {Vert} _{p}P\\subset T_{p}P}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/441b2ffd286f6c54c4954e9a8066ec685915efc8) needs to agree with metric on the fiber over a point in the manifold  M {\displaystyle M} ![{\\displaystyle M}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f82cade9898ced02fdd08712e5f0c0151758a0dd).
  * The projection of  g ^ {\displaystyle {\widehat {g}}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\widehat {g}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f03ff4b434f75ad5db89ea97ae9f4336927e6d9f) to the [horizontal subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_bundle "Horizontal bundle") Hor p ⁡ P ⊂ T p P {\displaystyle \operatorname {Hor} _{p}P\subset T_{p}P} ![{\\displaystyle \\operatorname {Hor} _{p}P\\subset T_{p}P}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/93ce081e39598b6bb685c3e679b3baca11650760) of the [tangent space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_space "Tangent space") at point  p ∈ P {\displaystyle p\in P} ![{\\displaystyle p\\in P}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f4dc1da33d78a2bdae06c9edff726feb126dae34) must be isomorphic to the metric  g {\displaystyle g} ![{\\displaystyle g}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/d3556280e66fe2c0d0140df20935a6f057381d77) on  M {\displaystyle M} ![{\\displaystyle M}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f82cade9898ced02fdd08712e5f0c0151758a0dd) at  π ( P ) {\displaystyle \pi (P)} ![{\\displaystyle \\pi \(P\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5d62d005e6548967443d8bd98d1204c6ec683fd7).


The Kaluza–Klein action for such a metric is given by       S ( g ^ ) = ∫ P R ( g ^ ) vol ⁡ ( g ^ ) . {\displaystyle S({\widehat {g}})=\int _{P}R({\widehat {g}})\operatorname {vol} ({\widehat {g}}).} ![{\\displaystyle S\({\\widehat {g}}\)=\\int _{P}R\({\\widehat {g}}\)\\operatorname {vol} \({\\widehat {g}}\).}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/42f1d0a76d6699623cefa8bb43dafd57646d33be)
The scalar curvature, written in components, then expands to       R ( g ^ ) = π ∗ ( R ( g ) − Λ 2 2 | F | 2 ) , {\displaystyle R({\widehat {g}})=\pi ^{*}\left(R(g)-{\frac {\Lambda ^{2}}{2}}|F|^{2}\right),} ![{\\displaystyle R\({\\widehat {g}}\)=\\pi ^{*}\\left\(R\(g\)-{\\frac {\\Lambda ^{2}}{2}}|F|^{2}\\right\),}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/98eef8e59a548bc2d6f8e2b978ee39d1edee2a38)
where  π ∗ {\displaystyle \pi ^{*}} ![{\\displaystyle \\pi ^{*}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f44ad69ec033a9a86437b2edaf620ea0b2c3f494) is the [pullback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullback_\(differential_geometry\) "Pullback \(differential geometry\)") of the fiber bundle projection  π : P → M {\displaystyle \pi :P\to M} ![{\\displaystyle \\pi :P\\to M}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/9bc921dc155d2c06ba6ae891c0ae2c117b9ddbe9). The connection  A {\displaystyle A} ![{\\displaystyle A}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/7daff47fa58cdfd29dc333def748ff5fa4c923e3) on the fiber bundle is related to the electromagnetic field strength as       π ∗ F = d A . {\displaystyle \pi ^{*}F=dA.} ![{\\displaystyle \\pi ^{*}F=dA.}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/4caaa7d177374a5e3ebdfa5e90bd0e38b34e16ff)
That there always exists such a connection, even for fiber bundles of arbitrarily complex topology, is a result from [homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_\(mathematics\) "Homology \(mathematics\)") and specifically, [K-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-theory "K-theory"). Applying [Fubini's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fubini%27s_theorem "Fubini's theorem") and integrating on the fiber, one gets       S ( g ^ ) = Λ ∫ M ( R ( g ) − 1 Λ 2 | F | 2 ) vol ⁡ ( g ) . {\displaystyle S({\widehat {g}})=\Lambda \int _{M}\left(R(g)-{\frac {1}{\Lambda ^{2}}}|F|^{2}\right)\operatorname {vol} (g).} ![{\\displaystyle S\({\\widehat {g}}\)=\\Lambda \\int _{M}\\left\(R\(g\)-{\\frac {1}{\\Lambda ^{2}}}|F|^{2}\\right\)\\operatorname {vol} \(g\).}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/a51de203d3b4fa409b891d9762c3309b7a4b57f1)
Varying the action with respect to the component  A {\displaystyle A} ![{\\displaystyle A}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/7daff47fa58cdfd29dc333def748ff5fa4c923e3), one regains the Maxwell equations. Applying the variational principle to the base metric  g {\displaystyle g} ![{\\displaystyle g}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/d3556280e66fe2c0d0140df20935a6f057381d77), one gets the Einstein equations       R i j − 1 2 g i j R = 1 Λ 2 T i j {\displaystyle R_{ij}-{\frac {1}{2}}g_{ij}R={\frac {1}{\Lambda ^{2}}}T_{ij}} ![{\\displaystyle R_{ij}-{\\frac {1}{2}}g_{ij}R={\\frac {1}{\\Lambda ^{2}}}T_{ij}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/33ef911d401e8c81837de53b56160a94a112265a)
with the [electromagnetic stress–energy tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_stress%E2%80%93energy_tensor "Electromagnetic stress–energy tensor") being given by       T i j = F i k F j l g k l − 1 4 g i j | F | 2 . {\displaystyle T^{ij}=F^{ik}F^{jl}g_{kl}-{\frac {1}{4}}g^{ij}|F|^{2}.} ![{\\displaystyle T^{ij}=F^{ik}F^{jl}g_{kl}-{\\frac {1}{4}}g^{ij}|F|^{2}.}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/32acdc19b3192734541fb5efe66c16567a6068fd)
The original theory identifies  Λ {\displaystyle \Lambda } ![{\\displaystyle \\Lambda }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/0ac0a4a98a414e3480335f9ba652d12571ec6733) with the fiber metric  g 55 {\displaystyle g_{55}} ![{\\displaystyle g_{55}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/9301606a8c2b3641347b4acd022ee81cdf5aab8b) and allows  Λ {\displaystyle \Lambda } ![{\\displaystyle \\Lambda }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/0ac0a4a98a414e3480335f9ba652d12571ec6733) to vary from fiber to fiber. In this case, the coupling between gravity and the electromagnetic field is not constant, but has its own dynamical field, the [radion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radion_\(physics\) "Radion \(physics\)"). 
### Generalizations
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Generalizations")]
In the above, the size of the loop  Λ {\displaystyle \Lambda } ![{\\displaystyle \\Lambda }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/0ac0a4a98a414e3480335f9ba652d12571ec6733) acts as a coupling constant between the gravitational field and the electromagnetic field. If the base manifold is four-dimensional, the Kaluza–Klein manifold _P_ is five-dimensional. The fifth dimension is a [compact space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_space "Compact space") and is called the **compact dimension**. The technique of introducing compact dimensions to obtain a higher-dimensional manifold is referred to as [compactification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactification_\(physics\) "Compactification \(physics\)"). Compactification does not produce group actions on [chiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirality_\(physics\) "Chirality \(physics\)") [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermions "Fermions") except in very specific cases: the dimension of the total space must be 2 mod 8, and the G-index of the Dirac operator of the compact space must be nonzero.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-22)
The above development generalizes in a more-or-less straightforward fashion to general [principal _G_ -bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_G-bundle "Principal G-bundle") for some arbitrary [Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group "Lie group") _G_ taking the place of [U(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U\(1\) "U\(1\)"). In such a case, the theory is often referred to as a [Yang–Mills theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang%E2%80%93Mills_theory "Yang–Mills theory") and is sometimes taken to be synonymous. If the underlying manifold is [supersymmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersymmetric "Supersymmetric"), the resulting theory is a super-symmetric Yang–Mills theory. 
## History
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: History")]
[Theodor Kaluza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Kaluza "Theodor Kaluza") proposed the possibility of unifying gravitation with electromagnetism in a paper he sent to [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") in 1919.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:0-23): 329–30  ([Gunnar Nordström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunnar_Nordstr%C3%B6m "Gunnar Nordström") published a similar idea in 1914. But in that case, a fifth component was added to the electromagnetic vector potential, a scalar representing the Newtonian gravitational potential, and writing the Maxwell equations in five dimensions.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-nrd-24)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:0-23): 329  But this had to be abandoned because it could not account for [gravitational lensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_lens "Gravitational lens").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:2-1)) Einstein wrote back, saying he liked the idea "enormously" but was not entirely convinced. But two years later, Einstein finally agreed to present Kaluza's paper to the [Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences "Prussian Academy of Sciences") in Berlin.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:2-1)[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:1-25): 197  Kaluza published it in the same year.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-kal-2) Kaluza presented a purely classical extension of [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity") to five dimensions of space and time, with a metric [tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor "Tensor") of 15 components. Correspondingly, the five-dimensional framework produced four-dimensional [Einstein field equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_field_equations "Einstein field equations") of gravitation, [Maxwell's equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell%27s_equations "Maxwell's equations") of electromagnetism, and an equation for the scalar field representing a hypothetical particle called the [dilaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilaton "Dilaton"). Kaluza also introduced the "cylinder condition" hypothesis, that no component of the five-dimensional metric depends on the fifth dimension. Without this restriction, terms are introduced that involve derivatives of the fields with respect to the fifth coordinate, and this extra degree of freedom makes the mathematics of the fully variable 5D relativity enormously complex. Standard 4D physics seems to manifest this "cylinder condition" and, along with it, simpler mathematics. 
In 1926, [Oskar Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Klein "Oskar Klein") extended Kaluza's five-dimensional classical field theory by suggesting that the fifth dimension of space had to do with quantization.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-KZ-17)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-KN-16)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:0-23): 331–2  Klein introduced the hypothesis that the fifth dimension was curled up and microscopic, to explain the cylinder condition. Klein suggested that the geometry of the extra fifth dimension could take the form of a circle, with the radius of −30 cm. More precisely, the radius of the circular dimension is 23 times the [Planck length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_units "Planck units"), which in turn is of the order of −33 cm.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-KN-16) Klein also made a contribution to the classical theory by providing a properly normalized 5D metric.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-KZ-17) Klein argued that solutions to the [Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schr%C3%B6dinger_equation "Schrödinger equation") in five dimensions could be interpreted as [waves or particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave%E2%80%93particle_duality "Wave–particle duality") moving under the influence of electromagnetism and gravity in four-dimensional spacetime.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:2-1) However, in the original papers by Kaluza and Klein, it was not clear whether the hypothetical fifth dimension was merely a mathematical artifice or physically real.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:2-1) Einstein, [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli"), [Peter Bergmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bergmann "Peter Bergmann"), and [Valentine Bargmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine_Bargmann "Valentine Bargmann") explored the Kaluza-Klein theory further during the 1930s and early 1940s.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:2-1)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:0-23): 334–6, 347  (At this time, Einstein and a number of other physicists and mathematicians were exploring various possibilities of formulating unified field theories.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:12-26)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:0-23): 341–51 ) 
In the 1940s, the classical theory was completed, and the full field equations including the scalar field were obtained by three independent research groups:[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-gon-4) Yves Thiry,[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-27)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-thry-3)[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-28) working in France on his dissertation under [André Lichnerowicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Lichnerowicz "André Lichnerowicz"); [Pascual Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascual_Jordan "Pascual Jordan"), Günther Ludwig, and Claus Müller in Germany,[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-jor1-6)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-jor2-7)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-29)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-jor3-8)[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-30) with critical input from Pauli and [Markus Fierz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_Fierz "Markus Fierz"); and [Paul Scherrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Scherrer "Paul Scherrer")[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-31)[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-32)[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-33) working alone in Switzerland. Jordan's work led to the scalar–tensor theory of [Brans–Dicke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brans%E2%80%93Dicke_theory "Brans–Dicke theory");[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-34) [Carl H. Brans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_H._Brans "Carl H. Brans") and [Robert H. Dicke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Dicke "Robert H. Dicke") were apparently unaware of Thiry or Scherrer. The curvature tensors for the complete Kaluza equations were evaluated using [tensor-algebra software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_software "Tensor software") in 2015,[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-LLW-9) verifying results of J. A. Ferrari[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-fri-35) and R. Coquereaux & G. Esposito-Farese.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-coq-36) The 5D covariant form of the energy–momentum source terms is treated by L. L. Williams.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-LLW2-37)
However, most theoretical physicists who were initially interested in the Kaluza-Klein theory ultimately lost interest because it was in sharp disagreement with experimental results.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:1-25): 197  All other attempts to generalize [Riemannian geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_geometry "Riemannian geometry") in order to unify electromagnetism and gravitation have also failed.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:14-38): 1135  But the basic idea of unification of the fundamental forces using higher dimensions of space was revived during the 1970s with the arrival of string theory and supergravity.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-:2-1)
## Empirical tests
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Empirical tests")]
The Kaluza–Klein theory's prediction of [electron mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_mass "Electron mass") is off by a factor of about 1018.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-39)
No experimental or observational signs of extra dimensions have been officially reported. Many theoretical search techniques for detecting Kaluza–Klein resonances have been proposed using the mass couplings of such resonances with the [top quark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_quark "Top quark"). An analysis of results from the [Large Hadron Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Hadron_Collider "Large Hadron Collider") (LHC) in December 2010 severely constrains theories with [large extra dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_extra_dimensions "Large extra dimensions").[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-arxiv.org_1-40)
Observations of the gravitational-wave event [GW170817](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GW170817 "GW170817") refuted the hypothesis that gravity is leaking into higher dimensions as in [brane theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brane_theory "Brane theory"). [Gravitational waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_wave "Gravitational wave") propagate in (3+1)-dimensional spacetime.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-arxiv.org_2-41)[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory#cite_note-42)
