From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Neural oscillation in the 25–140Hz range
Not to be confused with [gamma rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_rays "Gamma rays").
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Eeg_gamma.svg/500px-Eeg_gamma.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Eeg_gamma.svg)Gamma waves
A **gamma wave** or **gamma rhythm** is a pattern of [neural oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_oscillation "Neural oscillation") in humans with a frequency between 30 and 100 [Hz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz"), the 40 Hz point being of particular interest.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid30040729-1) Gamma waves with frequencies between 30 and 70 [hertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz") may be classified as [low gamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_oscillation#Overview "Neural oscillation"), and those between 70 and 150 hertz as [high gamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_oscillation#Overview "Neural oscillation"). Gamma rhythms are correlated with [large-scale brain network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large-scale_brain_network "Large-scale brain network") activity and [cognitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition") phenomena such as [working memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_memory "Working memory"), [attention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention "Attention"), and [perceptual grouping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principles_of_grouping "Principles of grouping"), and can be increased in amplitude via [meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditation "Meditation")[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid15534199-2) or [neurostimulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurostimulation "Neurostimulation").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid30040729-1)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid29493598-3) Altered gamma activity has been observed in many [mood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_disorder "Mood disorder") and [cognitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_disorder "Cognitive disorder") disorders such as [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer%27s_disease "Alzheimer's disease"),[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid18607528-4) [epilepsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epilepsy "Epilepsy"),[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-Hughes-5) and [schizophrenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizophrenia "Schizophrenia").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid21556334-6)
## Discovery
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Discovery")]
Gamma waves can be detected by [electroencephalography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroencephalography "Electroencephalography") or [magnetoencephalography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetoencephalography "Magnetoencephalography"). One of the earliest reports of gamma wave activity was recorded from the [visual cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_cortex "Visual cortex") of awake monkeys.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid14282370-7) Subsequently, significant research activity has concentrated on gamma activity in visual cortex.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-8)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-11)
Gamma activity has also been detected and studied across [premotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premotor_cortex "Premotor cortex"), [parietal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parietal_lobe "Parietal lobe"), [temporal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporal_lobe "Temporal lobe"), and [frontal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontal_lobe "Frontal lobe") cortical regions.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-Kort16-12) Gamma waves constitute a common class of oscillatory activity in neurons belonging to the [cortico-basal ganglia-thalamo-cortical loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortico-basal_ganglia-thalamo-cortical_loop "Cortico-basal ganglia-thalamo-cortical loop").[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid25460069-13) Typically, this activity is understood to reflect [feedforward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedforward_neural_network "Feedforward neural network") connections between distinct brain regions, in contrast to [alpha wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_wave "Alpha wave") feedback across the same regions.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid25205811-14) Gamma oscillations have also been shown to correlate with the firing of single neurons, mostly inhibitory neurons, during all states of the wake-sleep cycle.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-15) Gamma wave activity is most prominent during alert, attentive wakefulness.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid25460069-13) However, the mechanisms and substrates by which gamma activity may help to generate different states of consciousness remain unknown. 
### Controversy
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Controversy")]
Some researchers contest the validity or meaningfulness of gamma wave activity detected by [scalp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalp "Scalp") [EEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EEG "EEG"), because the frequency band of gamma waves overlaps with the [electromyographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromyography "Electromyography") (EMG) frequency band. Thus, gamma signal recordings could be contaminated by muscle activity.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid23596409-16) Studies utilizing muscle paralysis techniques have confirmed that scalp EEG recordings do contain significant EMG signal,[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid17574912-17)[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid18329954-18) and these signals can be traced to local motor dynamics such as [saccade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saccade "Saccade") rate[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid18466752-19) or other motor actions involving the head. Advances in signal processing and separation, such as the application of [independent component analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_component_analysis "Independent component analysis") or other techniques based on [spatial filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_filtering "Spatial filtering"), have been proposed to reduce the presence of EMG artifacts.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid23596409-16)
In at least some EEG textbooks, users are instructed to put an electrode on an eyelid to catch these, as well as 1 on the heart, & a pair on the sides of the neck, to catch muscle-signal from the body below the neck. 
## Function
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Function")]
### Conscious perception
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Conscious perception")]
[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Development-of-grouped-icEEG-for-the-study-of-cognitive-processing-Video1.ogv "Play video")[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Development-of-grouped-icEEG-for-the-study-of-cognitive-processing-Video1.ogv)[Electrocorticographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrocorticography "Electrocorticography") movie showing changes in high-frequency broadband gamma activity in specific cortical regions when visual stimuli are presented during a face-/place-naming task
Gamma waves may participate in the formation of coherent, unified [perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perception "Perception"), also known as the problem of combination in the [binding problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binding_problem "Binding problem"), due to their apparent synchronization of neural firing rates across distinct brain regions.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-buzsaki-20)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pollack-21)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-singer-22) [[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-gold-23) e.g. two neurons oscillate synchronously (though they are not directly connected) when a single external object stimulates their respective receptive fields. Subsequent experiments by many others demonstrated this phenomenon in a wide range of visual cognition. In particular, [Francis Crick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick "Francis Crick") and [Christof Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christof_Koch "Christof Koch") in 1990[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-24) argued that there is a significant relation between the binding problem and the problem of visual consciousness and, as a result, that synchronous 40 Hz oscillations may be causally implicated in visual awareness as well as in visual binding. Later the same authors expressed skepticism over the idea that [[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-25)
A number of experiments conducted by [Rodolfo Llinás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Llin%C3%A1s "Rodolfo Llinás") supports a hypothesis that the basis for consciousness in awake states and dreaming is 
A lead article by [Andreas K. Engel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_K._Engel "Andreas K. Engel") _et al_. in the journal _Consciousness and Cognition_ (1999) that argues for temporal synchrony as the basis for consciousness, defines the gamma wave hypothesis thus:[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-26)     The hypothesis is that synchronization of neuronal discharges can serve for the integration of distributed neurons into cell assemblies and that this process may underlie the selection of perceptually and behaviorally relevant information.
### Attention
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Attention")]
The suggested mechanism is that gamma waves relate to neural consciousness via the mechanism for conscious attention: 
> The proposed answer lies in a wave that, originating in the thalamus, sweeps the brain from front to back, 40 times per second, drawing different neuronal circuits into synch with the [precept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precept "Precept") [sic], and thereby bringing the precept [sic] into the attentional foreground. If the thalamus is damaged even a little bit, this wave stops, conscious awarenesses do not form, and the patient slips into profound coma.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pollack-21)
Thus the claim is that when all these neuronal clusters oscillate together during these transient periods of synchronized firing, they help bring up memories and associations from the visual percept to other notions.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-27) This brings a [distributed matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Distributed_matrix&action=edit&redlink=1 "Distributed matrix \(page does not exist\)") of cognitive processes together to generate a coherent, concerted cognitive act, such as perception. This has led to theories that gamma waves are associated with solving the [binding problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binding_problem "Binding problem").[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-buzsaki-20)
Gamma waves are observed as [neural synchrony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_oscillations "Neural oscillations") from visual cues in both conscious and [subliminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconscious_mind "Unconscious mind") stimuli.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-28)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-29)[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-30)[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-31) This research also sheds light on how neural synchrony may explain [stochastic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_resonance "Stochastic resonance") in the nervous system.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-32)
## Clinical relevance
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Clinical relevance")]
### Mood disorders
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Mood disorders")]
Altered gamma wave activity is associated with [mood disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_disorder "Mood disorder") such as [major depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_depression "Major depression") or [bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder "Bipolar disorder") and may be a potential [biomarker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomarker "Biomarker") to differentiate between unipolar and bipolar disorders. For example, human subjects with high depression scores exhibit differential gamma signaling when performing emotional, spatial, or arithmetic tasks. Increased gamma signaling is also observed in brain regions that participate in the [default mode network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Default_mode_network "Default mode network"), which is normally suppressed during tasks requiring significant attention. Rodent models of depression-like behaviors also exhibit deficient gamma rhythms.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid30181587-33)
### Schizophrenia
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Schizophrenia")]
Decreased gamma-wave activity is observed in [schizophrenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizophrenia "Schizophrenia"). Specifically, the amplitude of gamma oscillations is reduced, as is the synchrony of different brain regions involved in tasks such as [visual oddball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oddball_paradigm "Oddball paradigm") and [Gestalt perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestalt_psychology#Properties "Gestalt psychology"). People with schizophrenia perform worse on these behavioral tasks, which relate to perception and continuous recognition memory.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-BowerSciNews-34) The neurobiological basis of gamma dysfunction in schizophrenia is thought to lie with [GABAergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GABAergic "GABAergic") [interneurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interneuron "Interneuron") involved in known brain wave rhythm-generating networks.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid20087360-35) [Antipsychotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipsychotic "Antipsychotic") treatment, which diminishes some behavioral symptoms of schizophrenia, does not restore gamma synchrony to normal levels.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-BowerSciNews-34)
### Epilepsy
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Epilepsy")]
Gamma oscillations are observed in the majority of [seizures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizures "Seizures")[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-Hughes-5) and may contribute to their onset in [epilepsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epilepsy "Epilepsy"). Visual stimuli such as large, high-contrast gratings that are known to trigger seizures in [photosensitive epilepsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosensitive_epilepsy "Photosensitive epilepsy") also drive gamma oscillations in visual cortex.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid28486114-36) During a focal seizure event, maximal gamma rhythm synchrony of [interneurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interneuron "Interneuron") is always observed in the seizure onset zone, and synchrony propagates from the onset zone over the whole epileptogenic zone.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid28839247-37)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Alzheimer's disease")]
Enhanced gamma band power and lagged gamma responses have been observed in patients with [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer%27s_disease "Alzheimer's disease") (AD).[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid18607528-4)[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid26937378-38) Interestingly, the [tg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgenic_mouse "Transgenic mouse") APP-PS1 mouse model of AD exhibits decreased gamma oscillation power in the lateral [entorhinal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entorhinal_cortex "Entorhinal cortex"), which transmits various sensory inputs to the [hippocampus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocampus "Hippocampus") and thus participates in memory processes analogous to those affected by human AD.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid27833535-39) Decreased hippocampal slow gamma power has also been observed in the 3xTg mouse model of AD.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid30121451-40)
Gamma stimulation may have therapeutic potential for AD and other [neurodegenerative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurodegeneration "Neurodegeneration") diseases. [Optogenetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optogenetics "Optogenetics") stimulation of fast-spiking [interneurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interneuron "Interneuron") in the gamma-wave frequency range was first demonstrated in mice in 2009.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-41) Entrainment or synchronization of hippocampal gamma oscillations and spiking to 40 Hz via non-invasive stimuli in the gamma-frequency band, such as flashing lights or pulses of sound,[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid29493598-3) reduces [amyloid beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amyloid_beta "Amyloid beta") load and activates [microglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microglia "Microglia") in the well-established 5XFAD mouse model of AD.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-42) Subsequent human clinical trials of gamma band stimulation have shown mild cognitive improvements in AD patients who have been exposed to light, sound, or tactile stimuli in the 40 Hz range.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid30040729-1) However, the precise molecular and cellular mechanisms by which gamma band stimulation ameliorates AD pathology is unknown. 
### Fragile X syndrome
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Fragile X syndrome")]
Hypersensitivity and memory deficits due to [Fragile X syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragile_X_syndrome "Fragile X syndrome") may be linked to gamma rhythm abnormalities in the [sensory cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensory_cortex "Sensory cortex") and [hippocampus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocampus "Hippocampus"). For example, decreased synchrony of gamma oscillations has been observed in the [auditory cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditory_cortex "Auditory cortex") of FXS patients. The FMR1 knockout rat model of FXS exhibits an increased ratio of slow (~25–50 Hz) to fast (~55–100 Hz) gamma waves.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid30121451-40)
## Other functions
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Other functions")]
### Meditation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Meditation")]
High-amplitude gamma wave synchrony can be self-induced via [meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditation "Meditation"). Long-term practitioners of meditation such as [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people "Tibetan people") [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist "Buddhist") monks exhibit both increased gamma-band activity at baseline as well as significant increases in gamma synchrony during meditation, as determined by scalp EEG.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-pmid15534199-2) fMRI on the same monks revealed greater activation of right [insular cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_cortex "Insular cortex") and [caudate nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudate_nucleus "Caudate nucleus") during meditation.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-dalailama-43) The neurobiological mechanisms of gamma synchrony induction are thus highly [plastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroplasticity "Neuroplasticity").[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-44) This evidence may support the hypothesis that one's sense of consciousness, stress management ability, and focus, often said to be enhanced after meditation, are all underpinned by gamma activity. At the 2005 annual meeting of the [Society for Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Neuroscience#Annual_meeting "Society for Neuroscience"), the current [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama "14th Dalai Lama") commented that if neuroscience could propose a way to induce the psychological and biological benefits of meditation without intensive practice, he "would be an enthusiastic volunteer."[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-45)
### Death
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gamma_wave&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Death")]
Elevated gamma activity has also been observed in moments preceding [death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death "Death").[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave#cite_note-46)
