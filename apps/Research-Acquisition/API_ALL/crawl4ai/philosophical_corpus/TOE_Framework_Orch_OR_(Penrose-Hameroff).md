Theory of a quantum origin of consciousness
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Roger_Penrose_9671.JPG/250px-Roger_Penrose_9671.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Roger_Penrose_9671.JPG)
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Stuart_Hameroff_TASC2008.JPG/250px-Stuart_Hameroff_TASC2008.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Stuart_Hameroff_TASC2008.JPG)
The founders of the theory: [Roger Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Penrose "Roger Penrose") (left) and [Stuart Hameroff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Hameroff "Stuart Hameroff")
**Orchestrated objective reduction** (**Orch OR**) is a controversial theory postulating that [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness "Consciousness") originates at the [quantum level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mind "Quantum mind") inside [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurons "Neurons") (rather than being a product of [neural connections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_pathway "Neural pathway")). The mechanism is held to be a [quantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics "Quantum physics") process called [objective reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objective_reduction "Objective reduction") that is orchestrated by cellular structures called [microtubules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtubule "Microtubule"). It is proposed that the theory may answer the [hard problem of consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_problem_of_consciousness "Hard problem of consciousness") and provide a mechanism for [free will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_will "Free will").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-frontiers-1) The hypothesis was put forward in the 1990s by physicist [Roger Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Penrose "Roger Penrose") and [anesthesiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anesthesiologist "Anesthesiologist") [Stuart Hameroff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Hameroff "Stuart Hameroff"); it combines [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology"), [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience "Neuroscience"), [pharmacology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology "Pharmacology"), [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy"), [quantum information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information_theory "Quantum information theory"), and [quantum gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_gravity "Quantum gravity").[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-H&PvsReimers2014-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Penrose2014-3)
While some other theories assert that consciousness emerges as the complexity of the [computations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computation "Computation") performed by [cerebral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex") [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron "Neuron") increases,[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-McCulloch1943-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hodgkin1952-5) Orch OR posits that consciousness is based on [non-computable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computability_theory "Computability theory") [quantum processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computing "Quantum computing") performed by [qubits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubits "Qubits") formed collectively on cellular microtubules, a process significantly amplified in the neurons. The qubits are based on oscillating [dipoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipoles "Dipoles") forming [superposed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_superposition "Quantum superposition") resonance rings in helical pathways throughout lattices of microtubules. The oscillations are either electric, due to charge separation from [London forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_forces "London forces"), or magnetic, due to [electron spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_spin "Electron spin")—and possibly also due to [nuclear spins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_spin "Nuclear spin") (that can remain isolated for longer periods) that occur in [gigahertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigahertz "Gigahertz"), [megahertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megahertz "Megahertz"), and [kilohertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilohertz "Kilohertz") frequency ranges.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-H&PvsReimers2014-2)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-HameroffVs7Others2014-6) Orchestration refers to the hypothetical process by which connective proteins, such as [microtubule-associated proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtubule-associated_protein "Microtubule-associated protein"), influence or orchestrate qubit [state reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave-function_collapse "Wave-function collapse") by modifying the spacetime-separation of their superimposed states.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Penrose-Hameroff2014-7) The latter is based on [Penrose's objective-collapse theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_interpretation "Penrose interpretation") for interpreting quantum mechanics, which postulates the existence of an objective threshold governing the collapse of [quantum states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_state "Quantum state"), related to the difference of the [spacetime curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetime_curvature "Spacetime curvature") of these states in the universe's [fine-scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_scale "Planck scale") structure.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-8)
Orchestrated objective reduction has been criticized from its inception by mathematicians, philosophers,[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Boolos_1990-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-martin_1993-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-lewis_1969-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-putnam_1995-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-13) and scientists.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Tegmark2000-14)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Koch2006-15)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hepp2012-16) These criticisms focus on three issues: Penrose's interpretation of [Gödel's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del%27s_incompleteness_theorems "Gödel's incompleteness theorems"); Penrose's [abductive reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abductive_reasoning "Abductive reasoning"), linking non-computability to quantum events; and the brain's unsuitability to host the quantum phenomena required by the theory, since it is considered too "warm, wet and noisy" to avoid [decoherence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoherence "Decoherence"). 
## Background
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Orchestrated_objective_reduction&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")]
Further information: [Penrose–Lucas argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose%E2%80%93Lucas_argument "Penrose–Lucas argument")
In 1931, mathematician and logician [Kurt Gödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_G%C3%B6del "Kurt Gödel") proved that any [effectively generated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_procedure "Effective procedure") theory capable of proving basic arithmetic cannot be both [consistent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistency "Consistency") and [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completeness_\(logic\) "Completeness \(logic\)"). In other words, a mathematically sound theory lacks the means to prove itself.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-17) In his first book concerning consciousness, _[The Emperor's New Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emperor%27s_New_Mind "The Emperor's New Mind")_ (1989), [Roger Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Penrose "Roger Penrose") argued that equivalent statements to "Gödel-type propositions" had recently been put forward.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Penrose1989-18)
Partially in response to Gödel's argument, the [Penrose–Lucas argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose%E2%80%93Lucas_argument "Penrose–Lucas argument") leaves the question of the physical basis of non-[computable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computable_function "Computable function") behavior open. Most physical laws are computable, and thus algorithmic. However, Penrose determined that [wave function collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_function_collapse "Wave function collapse") was a prime candidate for a non-computable process. In [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"), particles are treated differently from the objects of [classical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_mechanics "Classical mechanics"). Particles are described by [wave functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_function "Wave function") that evolve according to the [Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schr%C3%B6dinger_equation "Schrödinger equation"). Non-stationary wave functions are [linear combinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_combination "Linear combination") of the [eigenstates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenstate "Eigenstate") of the system, a phenomenon described by the [superposition principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superposition_principle "Superposition principle"). When a quantum system interacts with a classical system—i.e., when an [observable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observable "Observable") is measured—the system appears to [collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_function_collapse "Wave function collapse") to a random eigenstate of that observable from a classical vantage point. 
If collapse is truly random, then no process or algorithm can deterministically predict its outcome. This provided Penrose with a candidate for the physical basis of the non-computable process that he hypothesized to exist in the brain. However, he disliked the random nature of environmentally induced collapse, as randomness was not a promising basis for mathematical understanding. Penrose proposed that isolated systems may still undergo a new form of wave function collapse, which he called objective reduction (OR).[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Penrose-Hameroff2014-7)
Penrose sought to reconcile [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity") and quantum theory using his own ideas about the possible structure of [spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetime "Spacetime").[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Penrose1989-18)[_[page needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources "Wikipedia:Citing sources")_][[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Penrose1994-19) He suggested that at the [Planck scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_scale "Planck scale"), curved spacetime is not continuous, but discrete. He further postulated that each separated [quantum superposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_superposition "Quantum superposition") has its own piece of [spacetime curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetime_curvature "Spacetime curvature"), a blister in spacetime. Penrose suggests that gravity exerts a force on these spacetime blisters, which become unstable above the Planck scale of  10 − 35 m {\displaystyle 10^{-35}{\text{m}}} ![{\\displaystyle 10^{-35}{\\text{m}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5ad2753b625fbbd2287f914526662802e35534f4) and collapse to just one of the possible states. The rough threshold for OR is given by Penrose's indeterminacy principle:           τ ≈ ℏ / E G {\displaystyle \tau \approx \hbar /E_{G}} ![{\\displaystyle \\tau \\approx \\hbar /E_{G}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/56ceb346e9efc60d6bedf3f99021a58bc011de00)     where:     
  * τ {\displaystyle \tau } ![{\\displaystyle \\tau }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/38a7dcde9730ef0853809fefc18d88771f95206c) is the time until OR occurs,
  * E G {\displaystyle E_{G}} ![{\\displaystyle E_{G}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/8d16c71d8a8fae7eaf44f35514c17ad5747a7370) is the gravitational self-energy or the degree of spacetime separation given by the superpositioned mass, and
  * ℏ {\displaystyle \hbar } ![{\\displaystyle \\hbar }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/de68de3a92517953436c93b5a76461d49160cc41) is the [reduced Planck constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_Planck_constant "Reduced Planck constant").


Thus, the greater the mass–energy of the object, the faster it will undergo OR and vice versa. [Mesoscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoscopic_physics "Mesoscopic physics") objects could collapse on a timescale relevant to neural processing.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Penrose-Hameroff2014-7)[_[additional citation(s) needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
An essential feature of Penrose's theory is that the choice of states when objective reduction occurs is selected neither randomly (as are choices following wave function collapse) nor algorithmically. Rather, states are selected by a "non-computable" influence embedded in the [Planck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck "Planck") scale of spacetime geometry. Penrose claimed that such information is [Platonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism "Platonism"), representing pure mathematical truths, which relates to Penrose's ideas concerning the three worlds: the physical, the mental, and the Platonic mathematical world. In _[Shadows of the Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadows_of_the_Mind "Shadows of the Mind")_ (1994), Penrose briefly indicates that this Platonic world could also include aesthetic and ethical values, but he does not commit to this further hypothesis.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Penrose1994-19)
The Penrose–Lucas argument has been criticized by mathematicians,[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-laforte_1998-20)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-solomon_1996-21)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-krajewski_2007-22) computer scientists,[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-putnam_1995-12) and philosophers,[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-mindpapers-23)[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-lucas_criticisms-24)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Boolos_1990-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-martin_1993-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-lewis_1969-11) and the consensus among experts in these fields is that the argument fails,[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-25)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-26)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Burgess-27) with different authors attacking various aspects of it.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Burgess-27)[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-28) [Marvin Minsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Minsky "Marvin Minsky") has argued that because humans can believe false ideas to be true, human mathematical understanding need not be consistent, and consciousness may easily have a deterministic basis.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-29) [Solomon Feferman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Feferman "Solomon Feferman") has argued that mathematicians do not progress by mechanistic search through proofs, but by trial-and-error reasoning, insight, and inspiration, and that machines do not share this approach with humans.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-solomon_1996-21)
## Orch OR
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Orchestrated_objective_reduction&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Orch OR")]
Penrose outlined a predecessor to Orch OR in _The Emperor's New Mind_ , coming to the problem from a mathematical viewpoint and in particular Gödel's theorem, but it lacked a detailed proposal for how quantum processes could be implemented in the brain. [Stuart Hameroff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Hameroff "Stuart Hameroff") separately worked in cancer research and [anesthesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anesthesia "Anesthesia"), which gave him an interest in brain processes. Hameroff read Penrose's book and suggested to him that [microtubules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtubule "Microtubule") within neurons were suitable candidate sites for quantum processing, and ultimately for consciousness.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hameroff1982-30)[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hameroff1987-31) Throughout the 1990s, the two collaborated on the Orch OR theory, which Penrose published in _[Shadows of the Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadows_of_the_Mind "Shadows of the Mind")_ (1994).[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Penrose1994-19)
Hameroff's contribution to the theory derived from his study of the neural [cytoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoskeleton "Cytoskeleton"), and particularly on microtubules.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hameroff1987-31) As neuroscience has progressed, the role of the cytoskeleton and microtubules has assumed greater importance. In addition to providing structural support, microtubule functions include [axoplasmic transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axoplasmic_transport "Axoplasmic transport") and control of the cell's movement, growth, and shape.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hameroff1987-31)
Orch OR combines the Penrose–Lucas argument with Hameroff's hypothesis on quantum processing in microtubules. It proposes that when condensates in the brain undergo an objective wave function reduction, their collapse connects noncomputational decision-making to experiences embedded in spacetime's fundamental geometry. The theory further proposes that the microtubules both influence and are influenced by the conventional activity at the synapses between neurons. 
### Microtubule computation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Orchestrated_objective_reduction&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Microtubule computation")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Microtubule_diagram.jpg/330px-Microtubule_diagram.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Microtubule_diagram.jpg)**A:** An [axon terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axon_terminal "Axon terminal") releases [neurotransmitters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") through a synapse, which are received by microtubules in a neuron's [dendritic spine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendritic_spine "Dendritic spine").  
**B:** Simulated microtubule tubulins switch states.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-frontiers-1)
Hameroff proposed that microtubules were suitable candidates for quantum processing.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hameroff1987-31) Microtubules are made up of [tubulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubulin "Tubulin") protein subunits. The tubulin protein [dimers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimer_\(biochemistry\) "Dimer \(biochemistry\)") of the microtubules have [hydrophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophobic "Hydrophobic") pockets that may contain delocalized [π electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%A0_electron "Π electron"). Tubulin has other, smaller non-polar regions, for example eight [tryptophans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryptophan "Tryptophan") per tubulin, which contain π electron-rich [indole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indole "Indole") rings distributed throughout tubulin with separations of roughly 2 nm. Hameroff claims that this is close enough for the tubulin π electrons to become [quantum entangled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_entanglement "Quantum entanglement").[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hameroff_2007-32) During entanglement, particle states become inseparably correlated. 
Hameroff originally suggested in the fringe _[Journal of Cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Cosmology "Journal of Cosmology")_ that the tubulin-subunit electrons would form a [Bose–Einstein condensate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose%E2%80%93Einstein_condensate "Bose–Einstein condensate").[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Penrose-Hameroff2011-33) He then proposed a [Frohlich condensate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frohlich_condensate "Frohlich condensate"), a hypothetical coherent oscillation of dipolar molecules. However, this too was rejected by Reimers's group.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Reimers2009-34) Hameroff and Penrose contested the conclusion, noting that Reimers's microtubule model was oversimplified.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-35)
Hameroff then proposed that condensates in microtubules in one [neuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron "Neuron") can link with microtubule condensates in other neurons and [glial cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glial_cell "Glial cell") via the [gap junctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gap_junctions "Gap junctions") of [electrical synapses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_synapse "Electrical synapse").[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hameroff2006a-36)[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hameroff2009-37) He proposed that the gap between the cells is sufficiently small that quantum objects can [tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_tunneling "Quantum tunneling") across it, allowing them to extend across a large area of the brain. He further postulated that the action of this large-scale quantum activity is the source of 40 Hz [gamma waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_wave "Gamma wave"), building upon the much less controversial theory that gap junctions are related to gamma oscillation.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Bennett&Zukin2004-38)
## Experimental results
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Orchestrated_objective_reduction&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Experimental results")]
### Superradiance
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Orchestrated_objective_reduction&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Superradiance")]
In a study Hameroff was part of, [Jack Tuszyński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Tuszy%C5%84ski "Jack Tuszyński") of the [University of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alberta "University of Alberta") demonstrated that anesthetics hasten the duration of a process called delayed luminescence, in which microtubules and tubulins re-emit trapped light. Tuszyński suspects that the phenomenon has a quantum origin, with [superradiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superradiance "Superradiance") being investigated as one possibility (in a 2024 study, superradiance was confirmed to occur in networks of [tryptophans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryptophan "Tryptophan"), which are found in microtubules).[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:0-39)[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:1-40) Tuszyński told _New Scientist_ that "We're not at the level of interpreting this physiologically, saying 'Yeah, this is where consciousness begins,' but it may."[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-41)
The 2024 study, called "Ultraviolet Superradiance from Mega-Networks of Tryptophan in Biological Architectures" and published in _[The Journal of Physical Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Journal_of_Physical_Chemistry "The Journal of Physical Chemistry")_ , confirmed superradiance in networks of tryptophans.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:0-39)[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:1-40) Large networks of tryptophans are a warm and noisy environment, in which quantum effects typically are not expected to take place.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:0-39) The results of the study were theoretically predicted and then experimentally confirmed by the researchers.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:0-39)[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:1-40) [Majed Chergui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majed_Chergui "Majed Chergui"), who led the experimental team, stated that "It's a beautiful result. It took very precise and careful application of standard protein spectroscopy methods, but guided by the theoretical predictions of our collaborators, we were able to confirm a stunning signature of superradiance in a micron-scale biological system."[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:0-39) [Marlan Scully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlan_Scully "Marlan Scully"), a physicist known for his work in the field of theoretical quantum optics, said, "We will certainly be examining closely the implications for quantum effects in living systems for years to come."[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:0-39) The study states that "by analyzing the coupling with the electromagnetic field of mega-networks of Trp present in these biologically relevant architectures, we find the emergence of collective quantum optical effects, namely, superradiant and subradiant eigenmodes. ... our work demonstrates that collective and cooperative UV excitations in mega-networks of Trp support robust quantum states in protein aggregates, with observed consequences even under thermal equilibrium conditions."[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:1-40)
### Microtubule quantum vibration theory of anesthetic action
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Orchestrated_objective_reduction&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Microtubule quantum vibration theory of anesthetic action")]
In an experiment, [Gregory D. Scholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_D._Scholes "Gregory D. Scholes") and Aarat Kalra of [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") used lasers to excite molecules within tubulins, causing a prolonged excitation to diffuse through microtubules farther than expected, which did not occur when repeated under anesthesia.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-42) However, diffusion results have to be interpreted carefully, since even classical diffusion can be very complex due to the wide range of length scales in the fluid-filled extracellular space.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-43)
At high concentrations (~5 [MAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_alveolar_concentration "Minimum alveolar concentration")), the anesthetic gas [halothane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halothane "Halothane") causes reversible depolymerization of microtubules.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-44) This cannot be the mechanism of anesthetic action, however, because human anesthesia is performed at 1 [MAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_alveolar_concentration "Minimum alveolar concentration"). (Neither Penrose or Hameroff claim that depolymerization is the mechanism of action for Orch OR.)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-45)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-46) At ~1 MAC halothane, reported minor changes in tubulin protein expression (~1.3-fold) in primary cortical neurons after exposure to halothane and isoflurane are not evidence that tubulin directly interacts with general anesthetics, but rather shows that the proteins controlling tubulin production are possible anesthetic targets.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-47) Further proteomic study reports 0.5 mM [14C]halothane binding to tubulin monomers alongside three dozens of other proteins.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Pan2007-48) In addition, modulation of microtubule stability has been reported during [anthracene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthracene "Anthracene") general anesthesia of tadpoles.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:3-49) The study, called "Direct Modulation of Microtubule Stability Contributes to Anthracene General Anesthesia" claims to provide "strong evidence that destabilization of neuronal microtubules provides a path to achieving general anesthesia".[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-:3-49)
Computer modeling of tubulin's atomic structure[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-50) found that anesthetic gas molecules bind adjacent to amino acid aromatic rings of non-polar [π-electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi_electron "Pi electron") and that collective quantum dipole oscillations among all π-electron resonance rings in each tubulin showed a spectrum with a common mode peak at 613 [T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tera- "Tera-") [Hz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hz "Hz").[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Craddock2017-51) Simulated presence of eight different anesthetic gases abolished the 613 THz peak, whereas the presence of two different nonanesthetic gases did not affect the 613 THz peak, from which it was speculated that this 613 THz peak in microtubules could be related to consciousness and anesthetic action.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Craddock2017-51)
Another study that Hameroff was a part of claims to show that "anesthetic molecules can impair π-resonance energy transfer and exciton hopping in 'quantum channels' of tryptophan rings in tubulin, and thus account for selective action of anesthetics on consciousness and memory".[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-52)
In a study published in August 2024, an undergraduate group led by a [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College "Wellesley College") professor found that rats given [epothilone B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epothilone "Epothilone"), a drug that binds to microtubules, took over a minute longer to fall unconscious when exposed to an anesthetic gas.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-53)
## Criticism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Orchestrated_objective_reduction&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Criticism")]
Orch OR has been criticized both by physicists[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Tegmark2000-14)[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-McKemmish2009-54)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Reimers2009-34)[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Reimers2013-55)[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Villatoro2015-56) and [neuroscientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscientist "Neuroscientist"),[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Baars2012-57)[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Georgiev2017-58)[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Litt2006-59) who consider it to be a poor model of brain [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology"). It has also been critiqued for lacking [explanatory power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explanatory_power "Explanatory power"): the philosopher [Patricia Churchland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Churchland "Patricia Churchland") wrote, "Pixie dust in the synapses is about as explanatorily powerful as quantum coherence in the microtubules."[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-60)
[David Chalmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Chalmers "David Chalmers") has argued against quantum consciousness, discussing instead how quantum mechanics may relate to [dualistic consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind%E2%80%93body_dualism "Mind–body dualism").[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Chalmers2003-61) He has expressed skepticism that any new physics can resolve the [hard problem of consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_problem_of_consciousness "Hard problem of consciousness")[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Chalmers1995-62)[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Chalmers1997-63)[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-David_Chalmers_1996-64) and argued that quantum theories of consciousness suffer from the same weakness as more conventional theories. Just as he has argued that there is no particular reason why specific macroscopic physical features in the brain should give rise to consciousness, he also holds that there is no particular reason why a specific quantum feature, such as the EM field in the brain, should give rise to consciousness.[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-David_Chalmers_1996-64)
### Decoherence in living organisms
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Orchestrated_objective_reduction&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Decoherence in living organisms")]
In 2000, [Max Tegmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Tegmark "Max Tegmark") claimed that any quantum coherent system in the brain would undergo effective [wave function collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_function_collapse "Wave function collapse") due to environmental interaction long before it could influence neural processes (the "warm, wet and noisy" argument, as it later came to be known).[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Tegmark2000-14) He determined the decoherence timescale of microtubule entanglement at brain temperatures to be on the order of [femtoseconds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femtosecond "Femtosecond"), far too brief for neural processing. [Christof Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christof_Koch "Christof Koch") and [Klaus Hepp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Hepp "Klaus Hepp") also agreed that [quantum coherence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_\(physics\)#Quantum_coherence "Coherence \(physics\)") does not play, or does not need to play, any major role in [neurophysiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurophysiology "Neurophysiology").[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Koch2006-15)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hepp2012-16) Koch and Hepp concluded that "The empirical demonstration of slowly decoherent and controllable quantum bits in neurons connected by electrical or chemical synapses, or the discovery of an efficient quantum algorithm for computations performed by the brain, would do much to bring these speculations from the 'far-out' to the mere 'very unlikely'".[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Koch2006-15)
In response to Tegmark's claims, Hagan, Tuszynski, and Hameroff claimed that he did not address the Orch OR model but instead a model of his own construction. This involved superpositions of quanta separated by 24 nm rather than the much smaller separations stipulated for Orch OR. As a result, Hameroff's group claimed a decoherence time seven orders of magnitude greater than Tegmark's, although still far below 25ms. Hameroff's group also suggested that the [Debye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debye_force "Debye force") layer of [counterions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterion "Counterion") could screen thermal fluctuations, and that the surrounding [actin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actin "Actin") gel might enhance the ordering of water, further screening noise. They also suggested that incoherent metabolic energy could further order water, and finally that the configuration of the microtubule lattice might be suitable for [quantum error correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_error_correction "Quantum error correction"), a means of resisting quantum decoherence.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hagan2002-65)[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Hameroff2006b-66)
In 2009, Reimers et al. and McKemmish et al. published critical assessments. Earlier versions of the theory had required tubulin-electrons to form either [Bose–Einsteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose%E2%80%93Einstein_condensate "Bose–Einstein condensate") or [Frohlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Fr%C3%B6hlich "Herbert Fröhlich") condensates, and the Reimers group noted the lack of empirical evidence that such could occur. Additionally, they calculated that microtubules could only support weak 8 MHz coherence. McKemmish et al. argued that [aromatic molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromaticity "Aromaticity") cannot switch states, because they are delocalized, and that changes in tubulin protein-conformation driven by [GTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanosine_triphosphate "Guanosine triphosphate") conversion would result in a prohibitive energy requirement.[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-McKemmish2009-54)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Reimers2009-34)[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-Reimers2013-55)
In 2022, a group of Italian physicists conducted several experiments that failed to observe spontaneous radiation emissions predicted by the [Diósi–Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Di%C3%B3si%E2%80%93Penrose_model "Diósi–Penrose model") collapse model, but that "Penrose's original collapse model, unlike Diósi's, did not predict spontaneous radiation, so has not been ruled out."[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-67)[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-68)

While some of the studies mentioned above purport to show superradiance and an influence of anesthetics on decreasing excitation diffusion through microtubules, those studies were performed under artificial conditions that failed to include proteins associated with microtubules like [ferritin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferritin "Ferritin"),[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-69) which quenches microtubule superradiance.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-70) Evidence published prior to those studies establishes that ferritin interacts with microtubules in vivo and is essential for microtubule stability and function.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-71) For instance, those studies overlooked that: 
  * Studies of biophotons in the human body fail to find any evidence of [ultraviolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet "Ultraviolet") (UV) [biophotons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophoton "Biophoton").[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-72) In contrast, at least one of the studies cited above that is relied on as evidence of microtubule superradiance in support of Orch-OR relies on earlier studies of UV biophotons measured in single-celled organisms like _E. coli_ and respiratory deficient yeast as the basis for its contention that such biophotons are present in cells.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-73)[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-74) That study also used [UV-vis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UV-vis "UV-vis") equipment with a light source that can generate 1020 photons per second, which is not representative of neurons' environment.
  * Ferritin in the human body absorbs UV from external sources at least in the skin and in the cornea, where the levels of UV photons are much higher than measured biophoton levels of UV even in _E. coli_ and yeast.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-75)[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-76) Endogenous ferritin in neurons would absorb UV biophotons that might be emitted from chemical processes (at levels that are too low to measure), and those UV biophotons would not even reach microtubules to cause superradiance or energy transport.
  * Ferritin contains tryptophan residues, the same material in microtubules that is supposed to cause microtubule superradiance.[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-77) According to one of the studies cited above, microtubule superradiance is based on special configurations of tryptophan residues. The failure of that study to consider additional ferritin tryptophan residues in the vicinity of microtubule tryptophan residues means that the study is not relevant to cellular environments that include ferritin (which is basically every cell). As noted above, ferritin perturbs tubulin in the vicinity of tryptophan residues, which invalidates an _a priori_ assumption of that study.
  * Ferritin has stronger ionic interaction with microtubules than the anesthetics that were used in one of the studies cited above and has electrical and magnetic properties that those anesthetics lack.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-78)[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-79)[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction#cite_note-80) Even if anesthetics interact with microtubules, ferritin has stronger interactions with microtubules, which may explain why ferritin is able to quench microtubule fluorescence.


In summary, experiments trying to demonstrate microtubule superradiance involved unrealistic levels of UV light and artificial environments, and excluded cellular substances that would prevent microtubule superradiance and energy transport.
