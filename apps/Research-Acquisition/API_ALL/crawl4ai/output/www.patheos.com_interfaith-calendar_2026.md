# Looking for a Multi-Faith Religion Events Calendar for 2026?
Below is a comprehensive list of religious observances and cultural awareness moments for 2026.
This multi-faith religion calendar is a practical resource, highlighting key holidays and events from diverse traditions. It serves as a tool for fostering respect and understanding, encouraging recognition and celebration of faiths and cultures throughout the year.
Explore our 2026 calendar to honor significant dates and promote greater awareness of global beliefs and traditions.
## January 2026 Religious Holidays Calendar
January is a time to celebrate the many ways we communicate and connect. It highlights the significance of Braille and honors diverse traditions.
  * **January 1:** _Catholic/Christian: Solemnity of Mary, Mother of God_  
- Celebrated on January 1.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/blogs/formedbyaflame/2023/12/solemnity-of-mary-mother-of-god-new-years-day-january-1/)  
  

  * **January 1:** _Eastern Orthodox: Circumcision of Our Lord_  
- Commemorates the circumcision of Jesus eight days after his birth, in accordance with Jewish law.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.goarch.org/chapel/saints?contentid=702)  
  

  * **January 3:** _Buddhist: Mahayana New Year_  
- The beginning of the new year observed by Mahayana Buddhist communities.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.%D8%AB%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%81%D8%A9buddhism.com/mahayana-new-year)  
  

  * **January 4:** _UN: World Braille Day_  
- Celebrates the importance of Braille.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.un.org/en/observances/braille-day)  
  

  * **January 5:** _Sikh: Guru Gobind Singh Jayanti_  
- Birth anniversary of Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth and final human Sikh Guru, founder of the Khalsa.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.sikhnet.com/pages/sikh-gurpurab-calendar)  
  

  * **January 6:** _Christian: Epiphany (Three Kings' Day)_  
- Revelation of Christ to the Gentiles.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/blogs/faithandcriticalthinking/2024/01/epiphany-three-kings-day/)  
  

  * **January 6:** _Eastern Orthodox: Theophany (Epiphany)_  
- Celebrates the baptism of Jesus Christ in the Jordan River and the revelation of the Holy Trinity.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.goarch.org/theophany)  
  

  * **January 7:** _Eastern Orthodox/Coptic: Christmas (Julian)_  
- Nativity of Christ on Julian calendar.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/blogs/hopejoylife/2024/01/celebrating-orthodox-christmas-day-and-its-importance/)  
  

  * **January 13:** _Sikh/Hindu: Lohri_  
- A winter folk festival marking the end of winter and celebrating the harvest season.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.sikhiwiki.org/index.php/Lohri)  
  

  * **January 14:** _Hindu: Makar Sankranti_  
- Solar festival marking sun's northward journey.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/blogs/hindu2/2014/11/the-significance-of-makar-sankranti-festival-life-is-movement/)  
  

  * **January 14:** _Sikh: Maghi_  
- Honors the 40 Muktas (martyrs) who sacrificed their lives defending Guru Gobind Singh at the Battle of Muktsar.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.sikhnet.com/pages/sikh-gurpurab-calendar)  
  

  * **January 14–17:** _Hindu: Pongal_  
- Tamil harvest festival (multi-day).  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/answers/what-are-the-main-hindu-holidays)  
  

  * **January 16:** _Muslim: Lailat al-Miraj_  
- Commemorates the Prophet Muhammad's Night Journey from Mecca to Jerusalem and ascension to heaven.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/library/islam/ritual-worship-devotion-symbolism/sacred-time)  
  

  * **January 18:** _Bahá'í: World Religion Day_  
- Promotes interfaith understanding and the essential unity of all world religions.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.bahai.us/world-religion-day/)  
  

  * **January 19:** _U.S.: Martin Luther King Jr. Day_  
- U.S. federal holiday (3rd Monday in January).  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/blogs/religionprof/2026/01/martin-luther-king-day-2026.html)  
  

  * **January 27:** _UN: International Holocaust Remembrance Day_  
- Remembers victims of the Holocaust.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/answers/what-is-yom-hashoah)

  * **February 15:** _Hindu: Maha Shivaratri_

  * **February 15:** _Buddhist: Parinirvana (Nirvana) Day_

  * **February 17:** _Buddhist: Lunar New Year_

## March 2026 Inclusive Religious Calendar
March is Women's History Month, highlighting significant observances from various faiths and cultures.
  * **March 2–3:** _Jewish: Purim_  
- Festival of Esther.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/answers/what-is-the-jewish-holiday-purim)  
  

  * **March 3:** _Buddhist: Magha Puja (Sangha Day)_  
- Commemorates the spontaneous gathering of 1,250 enlightened disciples who came to hear the Buddha teach.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.timeanddate.com/holidays/buddhist/magha-puja)  
  

  * **March 3:** _Hindu: Holika Dahan_  
- Bonfire night preceding Holi.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/answers/what-is-holi-and-why-is-it-celebrated)  
  

  * **March 4:** _Hindu: Holi_  
- Festival of Colors.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/answers/what-is-holi-and-why-is-it-celebrated)  
  

  * **March 4–6:** _Sikh: Hola Mohalla_  
- Martial displays, kirtan, poetry, langar.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.sikhdharma.org/the-sikh-festival-of-hola-mohalla/)  
  

  * **March 19:** _Hindu: Chaitra Navratri Begins / Ugadi / Gudi Padwa_  
- Nine nights of worship honoring the goddess Durga; also marks the Hindu New Year in some regions.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/blogs/ancientrootmodernfruits/2024/04/ringing-in-renewal/)  
  

  * **March 20:** _Muslim: Eid al-Fitr (Shawwal 1)_  
- Festival marking end of Ramadan.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/answers/what-is-eid-al-fitr)  
  

  * **March 20:** _Pagan: Ostara (Spring Equinox)_  
- Celebration of balance and renewal.  
[_**Read more**_](https://www.patheos.com/blogs/panmankey/2018/03/witchs-guide-ostara/)
