Origins 
## Influences
![a model of Jerusalem \(c. first century CE\), with the temple in the foreground Source: http://www.flickr.com/photos/secagle/4075315762/](http://www.patheos.com/~/media/patheos-legacy-images/christianity/christianity_anno2_13.jpg)Christianity originated in Jewish Palestine, a province of the Roman Empire. The first Christians were Jews who attended temple, read the Jewish [scriptures](http://www.patheos.com/library/taoism/origins/scriptures), kept the [Sabbath](http://www.patheos.com/library/judaism/ritual-worship-devotion-symbolism/sacred-time), and adhered to Jewish dietary and religious [practices](http://www.patheos.com/library/judaism/ritual-worship-devotion-symbolism/worship-and-devotion-in-daily-life). In the decades following the death of Jesus, Jewish Christians quickly spread to other Mediterranean provinces of Rome and began converting Gentiles.
Two or three centuries passed before a religion completely separate from Judaism took shape, a religion that we now recognize as Christianity. The Jewish, Greek, and Roman cultures of the first two centuries of the Common [Era](http://www.patheos.com/library/glossary/common-era) had deep and lasting influence on the new faith during this formative time.
Jesus, his [disciples](http://www.patheos.com/library/glossary/disciple), and the first Christians were all Jews who kept Jewish laws and customs and studied the Jewish [scriptures](http://www.patheos.com/library/judaism/origins/scriptures). Christianity preserves the Jewish scriptures in the [Old Testament](http://www.patheos.com/library/glossary/old-testament), incorporating the essential Jewish view of God as the God of history.
![Jesus' return “in the day of the Lord” Source: http://www.flickr.com/photos/36829113@N05/3392966849/](http://www.patheos.com/~/media/patheos-legacy-images/Christianity/Christianity_Anno2_14.jpg)Like Judaism, Christianity teaches that creation is the act of a single God, and throughout history this God has intervened, showing divine power through mighty deeds. God's role in history will culminate in the future in "the day of the Lord," when evil will be conquered and a new world will arise. In that new world, God will reign as a king of peace and righteousness (Isaiah 2:12; Joel 1:15; Zephaniah 1:7; Malachi 3:17). The first Christians believed that Jesus was the [Messiah](http://www.patheos.com/library/glossary/messiah-x2588) (in Greek, _Christos_ , or Christ) whose death and resurrection was a sign that God's promised "day of the Lord" was coming soon.
![Roman empire at its greatest extent, 117 CE](http://www.patheos.com/~/media/patheos-legacy-images/Christianity/Christianity_Anno2_15.jpg?w=25)
Early Christian [missionaries](http://www.patheos.com/library/glossary/missionary) carried this belief into the major population centers around the Mediterranean. The message found a receptive audience in the non-Jewish communities of the Roman Empire, including Greek-speaking Gentiles who were trained in classical philosophy. This brought Greek intellectual culture into the heart of Christianity.
Christian leaders explored the core beliefs of Christianity in accordance with their intellectual training, launching theological controversies over the use of philosophical terms such as person and substance to define and understand the nature of God. The Christological controversies over the nature of the [incarnation](http://www.patheos.com/library/glossary/incarnation) of God in Jesus and the [Trinitarian](http://www.patheos.com/library/christianity/beliefs/ultimate-reality-and-divine-beings) controversy over the relationship of God, Jesus, and the Holy Spirit were particularly sharp and of continuing significance. Both the conclusions formulated and some of the controversies associated with them persist to the present day.
![sculpture of an early Christian martyr, San Sebastiano Source: http://www.flickr.com/photos/raenef/3922327359/](http://www.patheos.com/~/media/patheos-legacy-images/Christianity/Christianity_Anno2_16.jpg?w=25)The politics of the Roman Empire had two significant and lasting effects on Christianity. In the first case, the fledgling Christian communities suffered official persecution by the Romans for almost three centuries, leaving a lasting legacy of [martyrdom](http://www.patheos.com/library/glossary/martyr). In the second case, early Christian communities responded to questions of communal organization by adopting the hierarchical model of Roman political organization. This is most apparent in the roles and relationships of [clergy](http://www.patheos.com/library/christianity/ethics-morality-community/leadershipclergy), where bishops have authority over priests, and archbishops or popes have authority over everyone.
