# If God tortures people for eternity, what does that say about His morality?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
October 31, 2011 
3 min read 
If God tortures people for eternity, what does that say about His morality? Atheists usually ask this question. However, the first problem is that they have no objective moral standard by which they might say God’s judgment is right or wrong. They may not like it. They may disapprove of it. But these objections and preferences are irrelevant as to whether or not God is right or wrong for damning people eternally. The only way they can rationally assert that something is morally right or wrong is to appeal to moral standards outside of themselves that have universal application. If they appeal to what society says, it only appeals to the popular opinion. Since popular opinions change, it is not a good foundation for determining moral truth.
If God punishes anyone for their sin, it is because God is holy ([1 Pet. 1:16](https://ref.ly/1%20Pet.%201.16;nasb95?t=biblia)) and righteous ([Psalm 7:11](https://ref.ly/Ps%207.11;nasb95?t=biblia); [Neh. 9:32-33](https://ref.ly/Neh.%209.32-33;nasb95?t=biblia); [2 Thess. 1:6](https://ref.ly/2%20Thess.%201.6;nasb95?t=biblia) ). If He does not punish the sinner if he does not punish the sinner, then he is approving of evil. Because God is holy, he must deal with sin. ![If God tortures people for eternity, what does that say about His morality?](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/12/God-01.jpg)
Sin is breaking the law of God ([1 John 3:4](https://ref.ly/1%20John%203.4;nasb95?t=biblia)). So when a person sins, he is sinning against God. The only way to undo the damage against the infinitely holy God is if he provides a way out. He is the one who did that in the sacrifice of himself, Jesus, who is God in the flesh ([John 1:1](https://ref.ly/John%201.1;nasb95?t=biblia), [14](https://ref.ly/John%201.14;nasb95?t=biblia)). Jesus is God and is of infinite value. Therefore the sacrifice on the cross he offered is sufficient to pay for the sins of everyone who ever lived.
