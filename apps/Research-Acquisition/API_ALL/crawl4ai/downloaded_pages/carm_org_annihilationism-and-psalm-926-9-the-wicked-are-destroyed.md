# Annihilationism and Psalm 92:6-9, the wicked are destroyed
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
October 11, 2018 
13 min read 
Psalm 92 is about praise and thanksgiving, where God’s people look forward to the destruction of the enemies (vv. 7-9) and the triumph of the righteous (vv. 10-14). It is not didactic teaching about soul-sleep or some future Day of Judgment that results in annihilation. It is an imprecatory Psalm. But, conditionalists use it nonetheless. The following are extractions from the Psalm that they use for their support.
An imprecatory Psalm is a psalm that has curses against an enemy. There are many imprecations in the Psalms: 5:10; 17:13; 28:4; 35:4-6; 40:14; 55:15; 59:5; 71:13; 79:6; 94:1-2; 109:6-9; 129:5-6; 137:9; 139:19; 140:10-11. Here is an example of some of them.
In [Psalm 92:7](https://ref.ly/Ps%2092.7;nasb95?t=biblia),[9](https://ref.ly/Psalm%2092.9;nasb95?t=biblia), when the psalmist wishes that the wicked might be destroyed forevermore (v. 7) and that they would perish (v. 9), he is not pronouncing a doctrinal statement on the condition of the soul after their physical death. Nor is he providing an eschatological statement on the final condition of the wicked being nonexistence. Instead, he is cursing the wicked, as so many imprecatory Psalms do: [Psalms 35:4-6](https://ref.ly/Ps%2035.4-6;nasb95?t=biblia); [40:14](https://ref.ly/Psalms%2040.14;nasb95?t=biblia); [79:6](https://ref.ly/Psalms%2079.6;nasb95?t=biblia); [94:1-2](https://ref.ly/Psalms%2094.1-2;nasb95?t=biblia); [109:6-9](https://ref.ly/Psalms%20109.6-9;nasb95?t=biblia); [137:9](https://ref.ly/Psalms%20137.9;nasb95?t=biblia), etc. Therefore, in its context, Psalm 92 is not about soul sleep or annihilationism. It is a Psalm of praise and thanksgiving to God that also speaks of the destruction of the wicked. But, this destruction is not declarative. It is imprecatory.
