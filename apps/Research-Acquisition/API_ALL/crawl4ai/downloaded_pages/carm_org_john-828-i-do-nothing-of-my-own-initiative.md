# John 8:28, “I do nothing of my own initiative.”
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 15, 2008 
3 min read 
“Jesus therefore said, ‘When you lift up the Son of Man, then you will know that I am He, and I do nothing on My own initiative, but I speak these things as the Father taught Me,'” ([John 8:28](https://ref.ly/John%208.28;nasb95?t=biblia)).
Why would Jesus say in [John 8:28](https://ref.ly/John%208.28;nasb95?t=biblia), “I do nothing of myself?” If he is God, can’t he anything he wants?
Basic Christian theology teaches that [Jesus is both divine and human](https://carm.org/doctrine-and-theology/jesus-two-natures-god-and-man/) at the same time. In other words, Jesus has two natures: God and man. We find support for this in the verses that say that “the Word was God and was with God,” ([John 1:1](https://ref.ly/John%201.1;nasb95?t=biblia)) and that “the Word became flesh and dwelt among us,” ([John 1:14](https://ref.ly/John%201.14;nasb95?t=biblia)). Also, [Col. 2:9](https://ref.ly/Col.%202.9;nasb95?t=biblia) says that in Jesus “dwells all the fullness of the Godhead and bodily form.” Therefore, Jesus is both divine and human in one person. ![John 8:28, "I do nothing of my own initiative."](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2009/07/Jesus-23.jpg)
