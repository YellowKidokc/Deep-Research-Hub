# Did Jesus abolish the Law or not?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 02, 2008 
3 min read 
Jesus seems to say that He did  _not_ do away with the law, but Paul implies that Jesus  _did_ abolish the law. Is this a contradiction? Let’s consider the verses:
In [Matthew 5:17](https://ref.ly/Matt%205.17;nasb95?t=biblia), Jesus speaks about the Old Testament principles and authority of rule and revelation. When Jesus said that He came to fulfill the law, He came to establish it and demonstrate how it pointed to Him and how He would live it perfectly. ![Did Jesus abolish the Law or not?](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2009/07/Jesus-23.jpg)
In [Eph. 2:14-15](https://ref.ly/Eph.%202.14-15;nasb95?t=biblia), Paul is speaking about how the Gentiles, who were called the uncircumcision (v. 11), were separated from Christ (v. 12) but have now been brought near (to God) by the blood of Christ (v. 13). Jesus removed the requirement of having to follow the Law to please God, established justification by faith, and thereby united both Jew and Gentile into one group in Christ. This is when Paul says in verse 15 that Jesus abolished in His flesh the enmity, which is the law of commandments in ordinances. The Law was that which separated Jew from Gentile because the Gentiles did not keep it. The Jews held to that Law, in part, as a recognition of God’s call to separate a people for Himself. But since it has been fulfilled in Christ, it is fulfilled and is no longer to be adhered to in regard to its priestly, ceremonial, and legal theocratic specifics. However, the moral law (don’t lie, don’t murder, honor your parents, etc.) is still in effect.
