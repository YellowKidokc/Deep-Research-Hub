# Did early Christians change the words to Luke 3:22?
by [Luke Wayne](https://carm.org/luke-wayne)
May 07, 2021 
18 min read 
![Jesus Baptism: "Well pleased" or "begotten you"?](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Jesus-baptism-300x278.png)Critics of the New Testament often traffic in conspiracy theories about how early scribes allegedly altered the words of the Scriptures to suit the whims of their theological positions. One of the more interesting examples of this involves the words that the voice from heaven said during Jesus’ baptism. According to these critics, Luke’s version of this event originally had the voice saying “You are My beloved Son, today I have begotten You.” Orthodox scribes are said to have been increasingly uncomfortable with this language, and eventually they changed the words to match what is found in Mark, “You are My beloved Son, in You I am well-pleased.” This charge is demonstrably false, but looking at the _reasons_ for the claim can be a helpful case study in how to properly understand and deal with [textual variants](https://carm.org/the-bible/what-is-a-textual-variant/).
The verse at the center of the claim reads as follows:
[Luke 3:22](https://ref.ly/Luke%203.22;nasb95?t=biblia), “and the Holy Spirit descended upon Him in bodily form like a dove, and a voice came out of heaven, ‘You are My beloved Son, **in You I am well-pleased**.'”
