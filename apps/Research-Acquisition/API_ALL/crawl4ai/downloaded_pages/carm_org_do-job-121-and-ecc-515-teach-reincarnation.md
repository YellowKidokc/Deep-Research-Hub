# Do Job 1:21 and Ecc. 5:15 teach reincarnation?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 01, 2008 
4 min read 
Do [Job 1:21](https://ref.ly/Job%201.21;nasb95?t=biblia) and [Ecc. 5:15](https://ref.ly/Eccles.%205.15;nasb95?t=biblia) teach reincarnation? [Job 1:21](https://ref.ly/Job%201.21;nasb95?t=biblia) and [Ecc. 5:15](https://ref.ly/Eccles.%205.15;nasb95?t=biblia)
Does [Job. 1:21](https://ref.ly/Job.%201.21;nasb95?t=biblia) teach reincarnation? No, it does not. Job says that he would return to his mother’s womb when he died, but this cannot be reincarnation for two reasons. First of all, the Bible teaches us that “it is appointed for men to die once and after this comes judgment,” ([Heb. 9:27](https://ref.ly/Heb.%209.27;nasb95?t=biblia)). This soundly refutes reincarnation. Therefore, [Job. 1:21](https://ref.ly/Job.%201.21;nasb95?t=biblia) cannot be referring to reincarnation. Second, if we take [Job. 1:21](https://ref.ly/Job.%201.21;nasb95?t=biblia) literally, then does it mean that Job will be reincarnated into his **own** mother’s womb? If so, we have a problem. Since Job was old enough to have seven sons and three daughters ([Job 1:2](https://ref.ly/Job%201.2;nasb95?t=biblia)), he was probably advanced in years, and his mother, if she were still alive, would be well past childbearing years.
Likewise [Ecclesiastes 5:15](https://ref.ly/Eccles%205.15;nasb95?t=biblia). The context deals with evil under the sun and not having a way to support children due to bad investments. This is when the writer says, “As he had come naked from his mother’s womb, so will he return as he came. He will take nothing from the fruit of his labor that he can carry in his hand.” The writer is not speaking about reincarnation but about coming into the world with nothing and returning to the earth. So Ecclesiastes does not teach reincarnation.
