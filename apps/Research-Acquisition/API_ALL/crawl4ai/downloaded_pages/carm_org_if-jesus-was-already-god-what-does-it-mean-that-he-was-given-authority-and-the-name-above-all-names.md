# If Jesus was God, what does it mean that He was “given” authority and the name above all names?
by [Luke Wayne](https://carm.org/luke-wayne)
January 26, 2018 
20 min read 
The Bible clearly identifies [Jesus as God](https://carm.org/about-jesus/if-jesus-sits-at-gods-right-hand-doesnt-that-make-him-lower-than-god-the-father/) and [specifically as YHWH](https://carm.org/jehovahs-witnesses/does-the-bible-identify-jesus-as-jehovah/), the one true God of the Old Testament. Jesus [affirmed His own deity](https://carm.org/islam/did-jesus-actually-say-he-is-god/) and so did the New Testament writers _(For example, see our articles on the deity of Christ in the writings of Paul, John, Mark, James, and Jude.)_ and the [early Christian writers](https://carm.org/doctrine-and-theology/the-apostolic-fathers-and-the-deity-of-christ/) after them. It is abundantly clear that Jesus is and has eternally been God. Yet, there are startling phrases occasionally used about Jesus that, to our modern eyes, can seem out of place with this eternal reality. These passages speak of Jesus being given authority by God and given the name above all names at the time of His resurrection. But as God, did He not already have all authority? Did He not already possess the name above all names? What could these statements mean? As it turns out, these passages are steeped in Messianic tradition and, when considered in context, present no contradiction to the broader claims of Scripture about Jesus’ deity.
We will here examine four of the major passages that present us with this conundrum: Matthew 28:18, Philippians 2:9-10, Hebrews 1:2-4, and Romans 1:3-4.
