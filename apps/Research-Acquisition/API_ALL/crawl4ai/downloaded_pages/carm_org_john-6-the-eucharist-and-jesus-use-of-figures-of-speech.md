# John 6, the Eucharist, and Jesus’ use of Figures of Speech
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
October 13, 2025 
6 min read 
The Roman Catholics and Eastern Orthodox often use John 6 as proof that the communion supper, the Eucharist, is the actual body and blood of Jesus. Each church essentially says that the essence of Christ’s body replaces the essence of the bread, and the essence of Christ’s blood replaces the essence of the wine. The RC affirms transubstantiation. The EO affirms the Real Presence. But both affirm that the bread and wine truly become the body and blood of Christ. Here are frequently cited verses for their position.
John 6:53–54, “So Jesus said to them, ‘Truly, truly, I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no life in yourselves. 54 He who eats My flesh and drinks My blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day.’“
Are the words of Christ in John 6 literal or figurative? Was the bread actually His (sacrificed) body and the wine actually His (sacrificed) blood? If yes, then Jesus was asking the disciples to violate Levitical law, where it says to the Jews, “You are not to eat the blood of any flesh” (Lev. 17:14). Not only that, there is the logical problem of the essence of something replacing the essence of something else, yet not displaying the necessary properties of that essence. This is a serious issue. See the article [The logical problem of the Eucharist](https://carm.org/the-logical-problem-of-the-eucharist).
