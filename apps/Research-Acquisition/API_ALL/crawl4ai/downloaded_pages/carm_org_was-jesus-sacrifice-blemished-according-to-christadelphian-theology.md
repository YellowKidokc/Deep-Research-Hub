# Was Jesus’ sacrifice blemished according to Christadelphian theology?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
January 03, 2002 
10 min read 
Without realizing it, Christadelphians present Jesus as unacceptable sacrifice with blemish and defect. According to Christadelphian theology, [Jesus](https://carm.org/doctrine-and-theology/jesus-christ/) had a sinful and fallen nature. _(The Christadelphians: What They Believe and Preach, p. 82.)_. He, therefore, needed to be redeemed just like anyone else. _(Christadelphian Answers, p. 24.)_ But, say the Christadelphians, Jesus never sinned, so His sacrifice on the cross was pure and acceptable to God.
The Old Testament sacrifices were to be without blemish or defect. Of course, the blood of animals cannot remove our sin, but these sacrifices were representative of the sacrifice of Christ. [Deut. 17:1](https://ref.ly/Deut.%2017.1;nasb95?t=biblia) says, “You shall not sacrifice to the Lord your God an ox or a sheep which has a blemish or any defect, for that is a detestable thing to the Lord your God” (NASB). (See also [Ezekiel 43:22-23](https://ref.ly/Ezek%2043.22-23;nasb95?t=biblia), [25](https://ref.ly/Ezekiel%2043.25;nasb95?t=biblia); [45:18](https://ref.ly/Ezekiel%2045.18;nasb95?t=biblia), [23](https://ref.ly/Ezekiel%2045.23;nasb95?t=biblia) for the same theme.).
The words “blemish” and “defect” are “mowm” and “ra”, respectively. According to the Strong’s Concordance “Mowm” means “1) blemish, spot, defect 1a) of physical defect 1b) of moral stain.” Obviously, the sacrifice can have no physical defect.
