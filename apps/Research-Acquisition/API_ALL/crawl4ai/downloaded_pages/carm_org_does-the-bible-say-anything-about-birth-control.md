# Does the Bible say anything about birth control and contraception?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 11, 2008 
4 min read 
The Bible does not address the issue of birth control and contraception, so it is difficult to say whether or not it is right or wrong. But since it doesn’t condemn it outright, we do not either.
Biblically, having children is a blessing from God and a fulfillment of the command of God to multiply and fill the earth ([Gen. 1:28](https://ref.ly/Gen.%201.28;nasb95?t=biblia)). If anything, we are told to have children and never told to avoid having them. But that alone isn’t enough to settle the issue. A generic principle of having children is to fulfil God’s command to His people as a whole, and not always for individuals. Take Jesus, for example. He did not marry or have children. He did not fulfill the command to do so. Therefore, since Jesus never sinned, we can conclude that the command to have children is a generic one for mankind and not necessarily intended to each and every individual. Therefore, it would seem logical that birth control and contraception would be permissible to some degree.
If a couple decides to practice birth control, they must take special care that the method used is not abortive. Birth control is not acceptable if it causes a fertilized egg, fetus, or baby to be aborted. This is because we are told not to murder ([Exodus 20:13](https://ref.ly/Exod%2020.13;nasb95?t=biblia)). Remember, the life in the womb is human by nature, and we do not have the right to destroy it as a means of birth control arbitrarily. Nevertheless, we see birth control as biblically permissible and sometimes even wise — given certain circumstances.
