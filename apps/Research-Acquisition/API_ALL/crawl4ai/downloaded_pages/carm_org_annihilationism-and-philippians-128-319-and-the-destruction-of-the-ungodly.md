# Annihilationism and Philippians 1:28, 3:19, and the destruction of the ungodly
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
October 11, 2018 
3 min read 
Annihilationism and [Philippians 1:28](https://ref.ly/Phil%201.28;nasb95?t=biblia). Does it support the idea that we cease to exist or not? The conditionalists say it does. But logically, it does not prove their position.
[Philippians 1:28](https://ref.ly/Phil%201.28;nasb95?t=biblia) and [3:19](https://ref.ly/Philippians%203.19;nasb95?t=biblia) are sometimes used by annihilationists to support their idea that when a person is judged by God, he will stop existing. They presuppose that the word “destruction” means they will not exist. In both of these two verses, the word “destruction” is apoleia. I have done an analysis of every single occurrence of the Greek word apoloeia, ἀπώλεια, in the entire New Testament. You can see it here: [Word study on apoleia, destroy](https://carm.org/annihilationism/word-study-on-apoleia-destroy/). I quote from that article.
“The Greek word apoleia is used in the New Testament to designate destruction of persons, objects, and institutions. When it comes to persons such as Judas Iscariot ([John 17:12](https://ref.ly/John%2017.12;nasb95?t=biblia)) and the Antichrist ([2 Thess. 2:3](https://ref.ly/2%20Thess.%202.3;nasb95?t=biblia)), the meaning of destruction is open. In other words, the contexts do not necessitate nonexistence or continued existence. The word is used of spiritual death ([Matt. 7:13](https://ref.ly/Matt.%207.13;nasb95?t=biblia); [Rom. 9:22](https://ref.ly/Rom.%209.22;nasb95?t=biblia); [Heb. 10:39](https://ref.ly/Heb.%2010.39;nasb95?t=biblia); [Rev. 17:8](https://ref.ly/Rev.%2017.8;nasb95?t=biblia), [11](https://ref.ly/Rev%2017.11;nasb95?t=biblia)) but again, exactly what the nature of this spiritual death is, whether it be existence or nonexistence, is not stated.”
