# How many trees are listed in the Garden of Eden?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
February 15, 2025 
3 min read 
The exact number of trees in the Garden of Eden is not listed, but there are many ([Gen. 3:8](https://ref.ly/Gen.%203.8;nasb95?t=biblia)). Two trees are specifically named: The tree of Life ([Gen. 2:9](https://ref.ly/Gen.%202.9;nasb95?t=biblia)) and the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil ([Gen. 2:9](https://ref.ly/Gen.%202.9;nasb95?t=biblia)). A third tree is indirectly named, the fig tree, since Adam and Eve covered themselves with fig leaves ([Gen. 3:7](https://ref.ly/Gen.%203.7;nasb95?t=biblia)). Let’s take a look at what the Scripture says. ![How many trees are listed in the Garden of Eden?](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/trees-01.jpg)
The book of Revelation mentions trees that parallel some of them mentioned in the garden of Eden. In [Revelation 2:7](https://ref.ly/Rev%202.7;nasb95?t=biblia), “the tree of life is in the paradise of God.” The tree of life, which bears 12 kinds of fruit, is also mentioned in [Rev. 22:2](https://ref.ly/Rev.%2022.2;nasb95?t=biblia), [14](https://ref.ly/Rev%2022.14;nasb95?t=biblia), [19](https://ref.ly/Rev%2022.19;nasb95?t=biblia); [22:2](https://ref.ly/Rev%2022.2;nasb95?t=biblia), [14](https://ref.ly/Rev%2022.14;nasb95?t=biblia), [19](https://ref.ly/Rev%2022.19;nasb95?t=biblia). The fig tree is mentioned in [Rev. 6:13](https://ref.ly/Rev.%206.13;nasb95?t=biblia), “…as a fig tree casts its unripe figs when shaken by a great wind.”
In [Matthew 21:19](https://ref.ly/Matt%2021.19;nasb95?t=biblia), Jesus encounters a fig tree that is not bearing fruit. He cursed it, and it once the tree withered. This is most probably about the nation of Israel as a technological judgment that the nation of Israel did not bear appropriate fruit. This analogy is paralleled in [Luke 13:8](https://ref.ly/Luke%2013.8;nasb95?t=biblia) when Jesus speaks of the fig tree planted in the vineyard and did not bear fruit. However, the vine keeper nurtured for one more year so that it would bear fruit in the hopes that it would not be cut down.
