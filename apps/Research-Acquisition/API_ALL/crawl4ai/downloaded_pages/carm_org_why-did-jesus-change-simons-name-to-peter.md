# Why did Jesus change Simon’s name to Peter?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
June 11, 2024 
11 min read 
Some Catholics say Jesus changed Peter’s name from Simon because he was the first pope, the leader of the Church. Furthermore, they claim that Peter is the rock on which the true Church was built ([Matt. 16:18](https://ref.ly/Matt.%2016.18;nasb95?t=biblia)). The true church is supposed to be the Catholic Church. First, nothing in scripture says that Peter’s name was changed for this reason. Therefore, it is conjecture. Second, he wasn’t the only apostle whose name was changed. Paul was first called Saul ([Acts 13:9](https://ref.ly/Acts%2013.9;nasb95?t=biblia)) but became known as Paul. Third, Peter is the apostle to the Jews ([Gal. 2:7](https://ref.ly/Gal.%202.7;nasb95?t=biblia)). Paul is the apostle to the Gentiles ([Romans 11:13](https://ref.ly/Rom%2011.13;nasb95?t=biblia); [Gal. 2:7](https://ref.ly/Gal.%202.7;nasb95?t=biblia); [Acts 9:15](https://ref.ly/Acts%209.15;nasb95?t=biblia)). If anything, the name change of both Peter and Paul reflects their assignments as apostles to the Jews (Peter) and the Gentiles (Paul). It is not about being a pope. Finally, to be consistent, Peter is not the “pope” to the Gentiles – because that is not his apostolic assignment. He is the apostle to the Jews. Of course, this doesn’t mean that Peter can’t teach the Gentiles ([Acts 15:7-9](https://ref.ly/Acts%2015.7-9;nasb95?t=biblia)) or that Paul can’t teach the Jews. So, it makes sense to say that when Jesus changed Peter’s name from Simon, it did not mean that it was because he was the pope. It designates Peter’s apostolic assignment to the Jews.[ ![Jesus changed Peter's name](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Jesus-09sqs.jpg) ](https://carm.org/attachment/jesus-09sqs/)
Catholics also use [Matt. 16:18](https://ref.ly/Matt.%2016.18;nasb95?t=biblia) to claim that Peter is the head of the Church. He isn’t. The word “Peter,” is ‘petros’ in the Greek. The word “rock” is ‘petra.’ Petros is masculine and means a small rock. Petra is feminine and means a large rock. The feminine “petra” occurs four times in the Greek New Testament: [Matthew 16:18](https://ref.ly/Matt%2016.18;nasb95?t=biblia), [27:60](https://ref.ly/Matthew%2027.60;nasb95?t=biblia), [1 Cor. 10:4](https://ref.ly/1%20Cor.%2010.4;nasb95?t=biblia), [1 Pet. 2:8](https://ref.ly/1%20Pet.%202.8;nasb95?t=biblia). Aside from the text in question ([Matt. 16:18](https://ref.ly/Matt.%2016.18;nasb95?t=biblia)), we see that in each of the other occurrences, petra refers to a large immovable mass of rock in which a tomb is carved out ([Matt. 27:60](https://ref.ly/Matt.%2027.60;nasb95?t=biblia)). It is also used about Christ ([1 Cor. 10:4](https://ref.ly/1%20Cor.%2010.4;nasb95?t=biblia), [1 Pet. 2:8](https://ref.ly/1%20Pet.%202.8;nasb95?t=biblia)). Note that in [1 Peter 2:8](https://ref.ly/1%20Pet%202.8;nasb95?t=biblia), Peter refers to Jesus as “a stone of stumbling and a rock [petra] of offense.” So, Peter references to Jesus as ‘petra’. Should we? So [Matt. 16:18](https://ref.ly/Matt.%2016.18;nasb95?t=biblia) does not support the idea that Jesus changed Peter’s name from Simon’s because he is the pope.
