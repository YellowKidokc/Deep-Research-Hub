1 Now when Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea in the days of Herod the king, behold, there came wise men from the east to Jerusalem,
2 Saying, Where is he that is born King of the Jews? for we have seen his star in the east, and are come to worship him.
Jesus was born in Bethlehem during the reign of Herod the Great. [[1](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#1n)]
During this time wise men [[2](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#2n)] from the east came saying,
> Where is the baby who will become the king of the Jews? 
> We have seen his star in the east and have come to worship him.
3 When Herod the king had heard these things, he was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him.
When Herod heard about the wise men, it troubled him, along with everyone else in Jerusalem.
4 And when he had gathered all the chief priests and scribes of the people together, he demanded of them where Christ should be born.
He asked the priests and scribes where Christ would be born.
5 And they said unto him, In Bethlehem of Judea: for thus it is written by the prophet,
6 And thou Bethlehem, in the land of Judah, art not the least among the princes of Juda: for out of thee shall come a Governor, that shall rule my people Israel.
They told him it would be in Bethlehem of Judea, according to the prophet. [[3](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#3n)]
7 Then Herod, when he had privily called the wise men, enquired of them diligently what time the star appeared.
Herod called the wise men and asked when the star appeared.
8 And he sent them to Bethlehem, and said, Go and search diligently for the young child; and when ye have found him, bring me word again, that I may come and worship him also.
He sent the wise men to Bethlehem, saying to them, 
> Go and find the child. When you have found him, tell me where he is so I can come and worship him.
9 When they had heard the king, they departed; and, lo, the star, which they saw in the east, went before them, till it came and stood over where the young child was. 
10 When they saw the star, they rejoiced with exceeding great joy.
So the wise men left Jerusalem, and the star that they saw in the east went before them, until it stood over where the child was. [[4](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#4n)]
11 And when they were come into the house, they saw the young child with Mary his mother, and fell down, and worshipped him: and when they had opened their treasures, they presented unto him gifts; gold, and frankincense and myrrh.
The wise men came to the house [[5](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#5n)] where they saw the child with Mary, his mother.
12 And being warned of God in a dream that they should not return to Herod, they departed into their own country another way.
The wise men were warned in a dream [[6](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#6n)] not to return to Herod, so they left for their own country by another way.

### Notes
  1. _Jesus was born in Bethlehem ... in the days of Herod the king._ ([v.1a](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#1)) 
The Herod that is referred to here is [Herod the Great](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_the_Great), as is made clear in [verse 22](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#2), which refers to Herod's son, [Archalaus](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_Archelaus). Since Herod the Great died in 4 BCE, this dates Jesus's birth to no later than that year. But the gospel of Luke ([2:2](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/lk/2.html#2)) says that Cyrenius ([Quirinius](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirinius)) was governor of Syria when Mary was pregnant with Jesus. Since Quirinius was appointed governor in 6 CE we know that Jesus was born sometime before 4 BCE and sometime after 6 CE -- which, if true, would indeed make it a miraculous birth.
[When was Jesus born?](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/contra/jesus_born.html)
  2. _There came wise men from the east._ ([v.1b](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#1)) 
The Revised English Bible translates this as, "After his birth astrologers from the east arrived in Jerusalem...." If this translation is correct, then Matthew's gospel seems to approve of astrology.
[Is astrology condemned by the bible?](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/contra/astrology.html)
[Strange Flesh: What about the magi?](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/SF/possibly.html#magi)
[Queer Epiphany: Three kings or three queens?](http://qspirit.net/queer-epiphany/)
  3. _In Bethlehem of Judea_ ([v.5](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#5)) 
Matthew claims that Jesus's birth in Bethlehem fulfils a prophecy from [Micah 5:2](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mic/5.html#2). But this is unlikely since:
     * "Bethlehem Ephratah" in [Micah 5:2](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mic/5.html#2) refers not to a town, but to a clan: the clan of Bethlehem, who was the son of Caleb's second wife, Ephrathah ([2:50-52](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/1chr/2.html#50), [4:4](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/1chr/4.html#4)).
     * The prophecy (if that is what it is) does not refer to the Messiah, but rather to a military leader, as can be seen from verse [5:6](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mic/5.html#6). This leader is supposed to defeat the Assyrians, which, of course, Jesus never did.
Whatever the correct interpretation of Micah 5:2 may be, the author of Matthew thought that it required the Messiah to be born in Bethlehem. So he found a way to make that happen, as did the author of Luke, probably for the same reason. They achieved it in different ways, however. Matthew's gospel has Joseph and Mary living in a house in Bethlehem when Mary becomes pregnant; Luke has them living in Nazareth and traveling to Bethlehem because of a census. Neither Mark nor John mention anything about a birth in Bethlehem, although the author of the gospel of John was aware that some expected the Messiah to be born there ([John 7:41-42](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/jn/7.html#42)).
  4. _The star, which they saw in the east, went before them, till it came and stood over where the young child was._ ([v.9](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#9)) 
If the star "went before them," leading them to Bethlehem, then it couldn't have been a star or any other astronomical object or event. But Matthew couldn't have known that. Everyone at the time thought that stars were just little points of light a short distance above the earth. It'd be no problem to have one hover above a particular place for a while.
  5. _And when they were come into the house_ ([v.11](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#11)) 
[Was Jesus born in a house or a manger?](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/contra/manger.html)
[Where did Joseph and Mary live before the birth of Jesus?](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/contra/before_birth.html)
  6. _Being warned of God in a dream_ ([v.12](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#12)) 
[Dreams in the Bible](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/topics/dreams.html#wise)
  7. _The angel of the Lord appeareth to Joseph in a dream_ ([v.13](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#13)) 
[Dreams in the Bible](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/topics/dreams.html#Joseph-2nd)
  8. _He took the young child and his mother by night, and departed into Egypt._ ([v.14](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#14)) 
According to Matthew, Mary, and the baby Jesus left for Egypt soon after Jesus' birth, yet Luke ([2:39](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/lk/2.html#39)) says they went directly to Nazareth.  
[Did Jesus, Mary, and Joseph go to Egypt or Nazareth?](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/contra/egypt.html)
  9. _Out of Egypt have I called my son_ ([v.15](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#15)) 
Matthew claims that the flight of Jesus's family to Egypt is a fulfillment of [Hosea 11:1](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/hos/11.html#1).
But [Hosea 11:1](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/hos/11.html#1) is not a prophecy. It is a reference to the Hebrew exodus from Egypt and has nothing to do with Jesus. Matthew tries to hide this fact by quoting only the last part of the verse ("Out of Egypt I have called my son"). Here is the complete verse.
It is also strange that God told Joseph and his family to walk five hundred miles (the didstance from Bethlehem to Egypt) to avoid the slaughter in Bethlehem. How could such a poor family afford to make such a journey? Why not just suggest that they go to a nearby village until the danger is over?
> When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt. [Hosea 11:1](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/hos/11.html#1)
  10. _Herod ... slew all the children that were in Bethlehem ... from two years old and under._ ([v.16](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#16)) 
Herod kills all infants in and around Bethlehem that are two years old and under. Such a massacre would certainly have been noted by contemporary historians. Yet not even Josephus, who documented Herod's life in detail, mentioned this event.
And notice that the killing of children was done to fulfill a prophecy. So God allowed the mass slaughter of all the infants in Bethlehem _to fulfill a prophecy_!
  11. _Then was fulfilled that which was spoken by Jeremy._ ([v.17](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#17)) 
Matthew quotes [Jeremiah 31:15](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/jer/31.html#15), claiming that it was a prophecy of King Herod's alleged slaughter of the children in and around Bethlehem after the birth of Jesus. But this verse refers to the Babylonian captivity, as is clear by reading the next two verses (16 and 17), and, thus, has nothing to do with Herod's massacre.
  12. _An angel of the Lord appeareth in a dream to Joseph._ ([v.19](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#19)) 
[Dreams in the Bible](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/topics/dreams.html#Joseph-3rd)
  13. _Being warned of God in a dream_ ([v.22](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#22)) 
[Dreams in the Bible](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/topics/dreams.html#Joseph-4th)
  14. _He shall be called a Nazarene._ ([v.23](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/mt/2.html#23)) 
Matthew claims this was a fulfillment of prophecy, yet such a prophecy is not found anywhere in the Old Testament.
