# Revelation 14:1 and King James Onlyism
by [Luke Wayne](https://carm.org/luke-wayne)
October 31, 2018 
4 min read 
Discussions with King James Onlyists often center on instances where the KJV contains a phrase that is not present in a modern translation. Anywhere a King James Only advocate finds a verse that is longer in the KJV than in a more recent translation, the King James Onlyist will accuse the translator of “deleting” or “removing” something, often attributing the worst possible intent. Yet, there are also places where valuable clauses are present in modern translations but are lacking in the KJV. One notable example is in [Revelation 14:1](https://ref.ly/Rev%2014.1;nasb95?t=biblia).
The verse under discussion here traditionally reads in the KJV:
“And I looked, and, lo, a Lamb stood on the mount Sion, and with him an hundred forty and four thousand, having his Father’s name written in their foreheads,” ([Revelation 14:1, KJV](https://ref.ly/Rev%2014.1;kjv1900?t=biblia)).
