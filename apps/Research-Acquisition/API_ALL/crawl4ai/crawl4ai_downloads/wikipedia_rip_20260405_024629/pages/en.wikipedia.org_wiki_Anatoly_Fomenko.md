**Anatoly Timofeevich Fomenko** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Анато́лий Тимофе́евич Фоме́нко; born 13 March 1945 in [Stalino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donetsk "Donetsk"), [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR "USSR")) is a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR "USSR") and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia") professor of [Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University"). He is well-known as a [topologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology "Topology") and member of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences"). He is a painter and illustrator of original artworks inspired by [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological "Topological") objects and structures. 
Fomenko is also widely known as a [conspiracy theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theorist "Conspiracy theorist"). He originated a fictitious and [pseudoscientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscientific "Pseudoscientific") history called [New Chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_chronology_\(Fomenko\) "New chronology \(Fomenko\)"), based on works of Russian-Soviet writer [Nikolai Alexandrovich Morozov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Alexandrovich_Morozov "Nikolai Alexandrovich Morozov").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Fomenko#cite_note-1)
## Biography
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatoly_Fomenko&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Biography")]
Fomenko is the son of Timothy Grigorievich Fomenko (Russian: Тимофей Григорьевич Фоме́нко), an industrial engineer, and Valentina Polikarpovna (née Markova) (Russian: Валентина Поликарповна Маркова), a [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology "Philology") and teacher of Russian language and literature. His parents would later co-author his works on New Chronology in 1983 and 1996. Born in what is now Donetsk, he was raised and schooled in [Magadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadan "Magadan"). In 1959, his family returned to Eastern [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine") and settled in the city of [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk "Luhansk"), where Fomenko attended Secondary School No. 26. During secondary school, Fomenko participated in many competitions relating to mathematics and won several medals as a result. Also in 1959, the magazine _[Pionerskaya Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pionerskaya_Pravda "Pionerskaya Pravda")_ (Russian: Пионерская правда, _Pioneer Truth_) published his first known science fiction story, "The Mystery of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way "Milky Way")". 
Fomenko graduated from the Mechanics and Mathematics Department of [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University") in 1967. In 1969, he began studying [differential geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_geometry "Differential geometry") in the same department. In 1970, he defended his thesis "Classification of totally [geodesic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic "Geodesic") manifolds realizing nontrivial cycles in Riemannian homogeneous spaces", and in 1972 defended his doctoral thesis, "The decision of the multidimensional plateau problems on [Riemannian manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_manifold "Riemannian manifold")." In December 1981, he became a professor of Higher Geometry and Topology, and in 1992, was appointed as head of Differential Geometry and Applications in the Department of Mathematics and Mechanics at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University"). 
## Mathematical work
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatoly_Fomenko&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Mathematical work")]
Fomenko developed the theory of topological invariants of an integrable [Hamiltonian system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_system "Hamiltonian system"). He is the author of 180 scientific publications and 26 textbooks (including monographs) on mathematics. Fomenko is a specialist in geometry and topology, [variational calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_calculus "Variational calculus"), [symplectic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology "Symplectic topology"), [Hamiltonian geometry and mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_mechanics "Hamiltonian mechanics"), and [computational geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_geometry "Computational geometry"). 
Fomenko has served as the editor of several Russian-language mathematics journals and is a member of many councils overseeing dissertations in his field. In 1996, he won the [State Prize of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Prize_of_the_Russian_Federation "State Prize of the Russian Federation") for excellence in mathematics. He is a full member (Academician) of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") (1994), the International Higher Education Academy of Sciences (1993), and the Russian Academy of Technological Sciences (2009). He is also a member of the Russian Academy of Natural Sciences (1991). 
Fomenko is the author of extensive writings in his original fields of mathematics, and is also known for his original drawings inspired by [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological "Topological") objects and structures.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Fomenko#cite_note-2)
## Historical revisionism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatoly_Fomenko&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Historical revisionism")]
Fomenko is one of the authors of a concept that manipulates historical chronology called New Chronology. Fomenko claims that he has discovered new empirico-statistical methods and their application to determine that many historical events do not correspond with the dates on which they are supposed to have occurred. He asserts from this that all of ancient history (including the history of [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece"), [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire"), and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt")) is merely a reflection of events that occurred in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages"), and that all of Chinese and Arab history are fabrications of 17th- and 18th-century [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus "Society of Jesus"). Fomenko is the author and sometimes co-author of several books on the analysis of historical chronicles, as well as a chronology of antiquity and the Middle Ages. 
He also claims that [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus") lived in the 12th century A.D. and was crucified on [Joshua's Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua%27s_Hill "Joshua's Hill"); that the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War "Trojan War") and the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades") were the same historical event; and that [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan "Genghis Khan") and the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire") were actually Russians, that the lands west of the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies "Thirteen Colonies") that now constitute the [American West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_West "American West") and [Middle West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States "Midwestern United States") were a far eastern part of "Siberian-American Empire" prior to its disintegration in 1775, and many other claims that contradict conventional [historiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography "Historiography"). As well as disputing [written chronologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_source "Primary source"), Fomenko also disputes scientific dating techniques such as [dendrochronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrochronology "Dendrochronology") and [radiocarbon dating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon_dating "Radiocarbon dating") (see [Radiocarbon dating § Carbon exchange reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon_dating#Carbon_exchange_reservoir "Radiocarbon dating") for an examination of the latter criticism). His books include _Empirico-statistical Analysis of Narrative Material and Its Applications_ , and _History: Fiction or Science?_. 
Most Russian scientists and worldwide historians consider Fomenko's historical works to be either [pseudoscientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience "Pseudoscience") or antiscientific.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Fomenko#cite_note-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Fomenko#cite_note-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Fomenko#cite_note-5)
