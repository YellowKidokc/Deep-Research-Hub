The **Chinese people** , or simply **Chinese** , are people or ethnic groups identified with [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China "Greater China"), usually through ethnicity, nationality, citizenship, [social construct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_construct "Social construct")[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-1) or other affiliation.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-2)
People of Chinese ethnicity or ancestry who live outside [Greater China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China "Greater China") may consider themselves [overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese "Overseas Chinese").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-4) In some areas throughout the world, [ethnic enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_enclave "Ethnic enclave") known as [Chinatowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown "Chinatown") are home to populations of overseas Chinese. 
## Terminology
The term _Zhōngguó zhī rén_ ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters "Traditional Chinese characters"): 中國之人; lit. 'people of China'; [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_language "Manchu language"): ᡩᡠᠯᡳᠮᠪᠠᡳ  
ᡤᡠᡵᡠᠨ ‍ᡳ  
ᠨᡳᠶᠠᠯᠮᠠ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Manchu "Romanization of Manchu"):  _Dulimbai gurun-i niyalma_) was used by the Qing government to refer to all traditionally native subjects of the empire, including Han, Manchu, and Mongols.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-5) In modern [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"), there is a distinction between _Huaren_ and _Zhongguoren_. The former term refers to ethnic Chinese, and is often used for those who reside overseas or those who are non-citizens. The latter term refers to people who have lived in China or are Chinese citizens.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-6)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-8) The term _Zhongguoren_ has a more political or ideological aspect in its use, and while many in China may use _Zhongguoren_ to mean the Chinese ethnicity, some in Taiwan would refuse to be called _Zhongguoren_.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-chang-9)
People from Taiwan, which is officially called the Republic of China (ROC), may also be referred to as "Chinese" in various contexts, though they are usually referred to as "[Taiwanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_people "Taiwanese people")". The territory of Taiwan is [disputed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Taiwan "Political status of Taiwan") and the ROC has [limited recognition of its sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Taiwan "Foreign relations of Taiwan"). 
## Ethnicity
Further information: [Ethnic minorities in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China "Ethnic minorities in China"), [List of ethnic groups in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_in_China "List of ethnic groups in China"), and [Unrecognized ethnic groups in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrecognized_ethnic_groups_in_China "Unrecognized ethnic groups in China")
A number of ethnic groups as well as other racial minorities of China are referred to as Chinese people.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-MWD-10) _[Zhonghua minzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhonghua_minzu "Zhonghua minzu")_ ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters "Simplified Chinese characters"): 中华民族), the "Chinese nation", is a [supra-ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supra-ethnic "Supra-ethnic") concept which includes all 56 ethnic groups living in China that are officially recognized by the government of the People's Republic of China. It includes established ethnic groups who have lived within the borders of premodern China.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-11) The term _zhonghua minzu_ was used during the Republic of China from 1911 to 1949 to refer to [five primary ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Races_Under_One_Union "Five Races Under One Union")[[a]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-12) in China.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-13) The term _zhongguo renmin_ ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 中国人民), "Chinese people", was the government's preferred term during the early communist era; _zhonghua minzu_ is more common in recent decades.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-14)

The [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese "Han Chinese"), alternatively Han people,[[b]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Han_people-15) Chinese people,[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-16) ethnic Chinese,[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-17) or Chinese,[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-18) are an [East Asian ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_people "East Asian people") native to [Greater China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China "Greater China").[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-19) With a global population of over 1.4 billion, the Han Chinese are the [world's largest ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_contemporary_ethnic_groups "List of contemporary ethnic groups"), making up about 17% of the [world population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population "World population"). The Han Chinese represent 91.11% of the population in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") and 97% of the population in [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan").[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Han_Chinese_%E4%B8%AD%E8%8F%AF%E6%B0%91%E5%9C%8B%E5%9C%8B%E6%83%85%E7%B0%A1%E4%BB%8B2016-20)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Han_Chinese_Yearbook2014-21) They are a significant [diasporic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese "Overseas Chinese") in Southeast Asian countries such as [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"),[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-22) [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"), the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"). 
The Han Chinese have exerted a primary formative influence in [Chinese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_culture "Chinese culture") and [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China "History of China").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-23)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Han_Chinese_hack-24)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Han_Chinese_MA-25) Originating from [Zhongyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongyuan "Zhongyuan"), the Han Chinese trace their ancestry and culture to the [Huaxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaxia "Huaxia") people, a confederation of agricultural tribes that lived along the middle and lower reaches of the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River "Yellow River")[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-26)[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-27)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Han_Chinese_CH-28)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-29) in the north central plains of China.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Han_Chinese_CA-30)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-31)
Han Chinese people and culture later spread southwards in the Chinese mainland, driven by large and sustained waves of migration during successive periods of Chinese history, for example the [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty "Qin dynasty") (221–206 BC) and [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty") (202 BC – 220 AD) dynasties, leading to a demographic and economic tilt towards the south, and the absorption of various [non-Han ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Chinese_history#List_of_ethnic_groups "Ethnic groups in Chinese history") over the centuries at various points in Chinese history.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Han_Chinese_CH-28)[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-32)[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-33) The Han Chinese became the main inhabitants of the fertile lowland areas and cities of Southern China by the time of the Tang and Song dynasties,[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Han_Chinese_PK-34) with minority tribes occupying the highlands. 
### Ethnic minorities
The [Chinese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_China "Government of China") recognizes 56 ethnic groups in China. Other ethnic groups in China include the [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people "Zhuang people"), [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people "Hui people"), [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people "Manchu people"), [Uyghurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghurs "Uyghurs"), and [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people "Miao people"), who make up the five largest [ethnic minorities in mainland China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China "Ethnic minorities in China"), with populations of approximately 10 million or more. In addition, the [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people "Yi people"), [Tujia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_people "Tujia people"), [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people "Tibetan people") and [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols_in_China "Mongols in China") each have populations between five and ten million. Ethnic minorities may consider themselves _Zhongguoren._
## Diaspora
These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese "Overseas Chinese").[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Overseas_Chinese&action=edit)]
[Overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese "Overseas Chinese") people or the Chinese diaspora are a [diaspora people of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora "Diaspora") Chinese origin who reside outside [Greater China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China "Greater China") ([mainland China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_China "Mainland China"), [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong"), [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau "Macau"), and [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan")).[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-35) As of 2011, there were over 40.3 million overseas Chinese.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Overseas_Chinese_Poston_and_Wong-36) As of 2023, there were 10.5 million people living outside mainland China who were born in mainland China, corresponding to 0.7 percent of China's population.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people#cite_note-Overseas_Chinese_:1-37) Overall, China has a low percent of its population [living overseas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_by_immigrant_and_emigrant_population "List of sovereign states by immigrant and emigrant population").
