From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Historical term for northern and central Asia
Not to be confused with [Tatarstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatarstan "Tatarstan"), [Strait of Tartary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Tartary "Strait of Tartary"), or [Tărtăria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C4%83li%C8%99tea "Săliștea").
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/1806_Cary_Map_of_Tartary_or_Central_Asia_-_Geographicus_-_Tartary-cary-1806.jpg/330px-1806_Cary_Map_of_Tartary_or_Central_Asia_-_Geographicus_-_Tartary-cary-1806.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1806_Cary_Map_of_Tartary_or_Central_Asia_-_Geographicus_-_Tartary-cary-1806.jpg) Cary's Map of independent Tartary (in yellow) and [Chinese Tartary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Tartary "Chinese Tartary") (in violet), in 1808.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-1)
**Tartary** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin"): _Tartaria_ ; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language"): _Tartarie_ ; [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language "German language"): _Tartarei_ ; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Тартария, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian "Romanization of Russian"):  _Tartariya_) or **Tatary** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Татария, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian "Romanization of Russian"):  _Tatariya_) was a [blanket term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypernymy_and_hyponymy "Hypernymy and hyponymy") used in [Western European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe "Western Europe") literature and [cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography "Cartography") for a vast part of [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia") bounded by the [Caspian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Sea "Caspian Sea"), the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains "Ural Mountains"), the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean"), and the northern borders of [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), and [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), at a time when this region was largely unknown to European geographers. 
The active use of the [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponymy "Toponymy") (place name) can be traced from the 13th to the 19th centuries. In European sources, Tartary became the most common name for [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") that had no connection with the real polities or ethnic groups of the region; until the 19th century, European knowledge of the area remained extremely scarce and fragmentary. In modern English-speaking tradition, the region formerly known as Tartary is usually called [Inner Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Asia "Inner Asia") or [Central Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"). Much of this area consists of arid plains, the main nomadic population of which in the past was engaged in [animal husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry "Animal husbandry").[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEConnell2016-2)
Ignorance surrounding Tartary's use as a place name has spawned [pseudohistoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory "Pseudohistory") [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory "Conspiracy theory") including ideas of a "hidden past" and "mud floods". Such theories assert that Tartary (or the "[Tartarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire "Tartarian Empire")") was a lost civilization with advanced technology and culture. This ignores the well-documented [history of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Asia "History of Asia"), to which Tartary refers.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-3) In the present day, the Tartary region spans from central [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") to northern [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan "Kazakhstan"), as well as areas in present [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia "Mongolia"), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), and the [Russian Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Far_East "Russian Far East") in "[Chinese Tartary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Tartary "Chinese Tartary")". 
## Geography and history
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tartary&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Geography and history")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Giovanni_Botero_-_Tartaria_map_and_description.jpg/250px-Giovanni_Botero_-_Tartaria_map_and_description.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Giovanni_Botero_-_Tartaria_map_and_description.jpg)Tartaria map and description by [Giovanni Botero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Botero "Giovanni Botero") from his "Relationi universali" ([Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia "Brescia"), 1599).
Knowledge of [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria "Manchuria"), [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia"), and [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") in Europe before the 18th century was limited. The entire area was known simply as "Tartary" and its inhabitants as "Tartars".[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEElliott2000625%E2%80%93626-4) In the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period "Early modern period"), as understanding of the geography increased, Europeans began to subdivide Tartary into sections with prefixes denoting the name of the ruling power or the geographical location. Thus, Siberia was _Great Tartary_ or _Russian Tartary_ , the [Crimean Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate "Crimean Khanate") was _Little Tartary_ , Manchuria was _[Chinese Tartary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Tartary "Chinese Tartary")_ , and western Central Asia (before becoming [Russian Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Central_Asia "Russian Central Asia")) was known as _Independent Tartary_.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEElliott2000625%E2%80%93626-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVermeulen201888-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMatochkina2016542-6) But by the 17th century, largely under the influence of Catholic missionary writings, the word "Tartar" came to refer to the [Manchu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people "Manchu people") and "Tartary" to the lands they ruled.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDong202082%E2%80%9383-7)
European opinions of the area were often unfavorable, and reflected the legacy of the [Mongol invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion "Mongol invasion") that originated from the region. The term originated in the wake of the widespread devastation spread by the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire"). The adding of an extra "r" to "Tatar" was suggestive of [Tartarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarus "Tartarus"), a [Hell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell "Hell")-like realm in [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology "Greek mythology").[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEElliott2000625%E2%80%93626-4) In the 18th century, conceptions of Siberia or Tartary and its inhabitants as "barbarous" by [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment")-era writers tied into contemporary concepts of [civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization "Civilization"), savagery, and [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism "Racism").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWolff2006448-8)
But some Europeans saw Tartary as a possible source of spiritual knowledge lacking in contemporary European society. [Theosophist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theosophist "Theosophist") writer [Tallapragada Subba Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallapragada_Subba_Row "Tallapragada Subba Row") quotes [Emanuel Swedenborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Swedenborg "Emanuel Swedenborg") as having advised, "Seek for the Lost Word among the [hierophants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierophant "Hierophant") of Tartary, China, and Tibet."[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-9)
### Decline
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tartary&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Decline")]
The use of "Tartary" declined as the region became more known to European geographers, but was still used long into the 19th century.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMatochkina2016542-6) Ethnographical data collected by [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit "Jesuit") missionaries in China contributed to the replacement of "[Chinese Tartary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Tartary "Chinese Tartary")" with [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria "Manchuria") in European geography by the early 18th century.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEElliott2000625%E2%80%93626-4) Egor Meyendorff's and [Alexander von Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt "Alexander von Humboldt")'s voyages into this region gave rise to the term [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") in the early 19th century as well as supplementary terms such as [Inner Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Asia "Inner Asia"),[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMatochkina2016542-6) and Russian eastward expansionism led to the term "[Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia")" being coined for the Asian half of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVermeulen201888-5)
By the 20th century, the use of Tartary as a term for Siberia and Central Asia had waned.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] However, it lent the title to [Peter Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Fleming_\(writer\) "Peter Fleming \(writer\)")'s 1936 book _[News from Tartary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_from_Tartary "News from Tartary")_ , which detailed his travels in Central Asia. 
## Tartaria conspiracy theory
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tartary&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Tartaria conspiracy theory")]
Main article: [Tartarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire "Tartarian Empire")
Misinterpretations of Tartary as an empire distinct from the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire"), rather than as an archaic name for Central Asia, gave rise to a conspiracy theory alleging the existence of an advanced "Tartarian Empire".[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary#cite_note-10)
