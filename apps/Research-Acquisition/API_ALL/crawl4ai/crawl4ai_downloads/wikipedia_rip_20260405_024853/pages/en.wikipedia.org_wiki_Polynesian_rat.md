Polynesian rat native range in Southeast Asia (in red) (note: the [IUCN Red List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN_Red_List "IUCN Red List") instead considers it introduced throughout all of its [Pacific islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_islands "Pacific islands") range, as well as [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei"), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), and the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"))   
The **Polynesian rat** , **Pacific rat** or **little rat** (_**Rattus exulans**_), or **_kiore_** , is the third most widespread species of [rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rat "Rat") in the world behind the [brown rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_rat "Brown rat") and [black rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_rat "Black rat"). Contrary to its vernacular name, the Polynesian rat originated in Southeast Asia, and like its relatives has become widespread, migrating to most of [Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia "Polynesia"), including [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand"), [Easter Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Island "Easter Island"), and [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii "Hawaii"). It shares high adaptability with other rat species extending to many environments, from grasslands to forests. It is also closely associated with humans, who provide easy access to food. It has become a major [pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest_\(organism\) "Pest \(organism\)") in most areas of its distribution. 
## Description
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polynesian_rat&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Description")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/PolynesianRatNZ.jpg/250px-PolynesianRatNZ.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:PolynesianRatNZ.jpg)1870 illustration of Polynesian rats, referred to as _Mus novæ zelandiæ_
The Polynesian rat is similar in appearance to other rats, such as the [black rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_rat "Black rat") and the [brown rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_rat "Brown rat"). It has large, round ears, a pointed snout, black/brown hair with a lighter belly, and comparatively small feet. It has a thin, long body, reaching up to 15 cm (5.9 in) in length from the nose to the base of the tail, making it slightly smaller than other human-associated rats. Where it exists on smaller islands, it tends to be smaller still – 11.5 cm (4.5 in). It is commonly distinguished by a dark upper edge of the hind foot near the ankle; the rest of its foot is pale.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-3)
## Distribution and habitat
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polynesian_rat&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Distribution and habitat")]
The Polynesian rat is widespread throughout the [Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean") and [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"). [Mitochondrial DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_DNA "Mitochondrial DNA") analysis suggests that the species originated on the island of [Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flores "Flores").[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-4) The [IUCN Red List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN_Red_List "IUCN Red List") considers it native to [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), all of [mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia "Mainland Southeast Asia"), and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), but introduced to all of its Pacific range (including the island of [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea")), the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei"), and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), and of uncertain origin in [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-iucn-1) It cannot swim over long distances, so is considered to be a significant marker of the [human migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_migration "Human migration") across the Pacific, as the [Polynesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia "Polynesia") accidentally or deliberately introduced it to the islands they settled. The species has been implicated in many of the [extinctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction "Extinction") that occurred in the Pacific amongst the native birds and insects; these species had evolved in the absence of [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal "Mammal") and were [unable to cope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_tameness "Island tameness") with the predation pressures posed by the rat. This rat also may have played a role in the complete [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation "Deforestation") of [Easter Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Island "Easter Island") by eating the nuts of the local palm tree _[Paschalococos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paschalococos "Paschalococos")_ , thus preventing regrowth of the forest.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-6)
Although remains of the Polynesian rat in New Zealand were dated to over 2,000 years old during the 1990s,[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-7) which was much earlier than the accepted dates for Polynesian migrations to New Zealand, this finding has been challenged by later research showing the rat was introduced to both the country's main islands circa 1280.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-8)
## Behaviour
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polynesian_rat&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Behaviour")]
Polynesian rats are [nocturnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnal "Nocturnal") like most rodents, and are adept climbers, often nesting in trees. In winter, when food is scarce, they commonly strip bark for consumption and satisfy themselves with plant stems. They have common rat characteristics regarding reproduction: they are [polyestrous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyestrous "Polyestrous"), with gestations of 21–24 days; their litter size is affected by food and other resources (6–11 pups); and weaning takes around another month at 28 days. They diverge only in that they do not breed year round, instead being restricted to spring and summer. 
### Diet
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polynesian_rat&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Diet")]
_R. exulans_ is an [omnivorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnivore "Omnivore") species, eating seeds, fruit, leaves, bark, insects, earthworms, spiders, lizards, and avian eggs and hatchlings. Polynesian rats have been observed to often take pieces of food back to a safe place to properly shell a seed or otherwise prepare certain foods. This not only protects them from predators, but also from rain and other rats. These "husking stations" are often found among trees, near the roots, in fissures of the trunk, and even in the top branches. In New Zealand, for instance, such stations are found under rock piles and fronds shed by [nīkau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C4%ABkau "Nīkau") palms. 
## Rat control and bird conservation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polynesian_rat&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Rat control and bird conservation")]
### New Zealand
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polynesian_rat&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: New Zealand")]
In New Zealand and its offshore islands, many bird species evolved in the absence of terrestrial mammalian predators, so developed no behavioral defenses to rats. The introduction by the Māori of the Polynesian rat into New Zealand resulted in the eradication of several species of terrestrial and small seabirds. 
Subsequent elimination of rats from islands has resulted in substantial increases in populations of certain seabirds and endemic terrestrial birds, as well as species of insects such as the [Little Barrier Island giant wētā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deinacrida_heteracantha "Deinacrida heteracantha"). As part of its program to restore these populations, such as the critically endangered [kākāpō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%81k%C4%81p%C5%8D "Kākāpō"), the [New Zealand Department of Conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Department_of_Conservation "New Zealand Department of Conservation") undertakes programs to eliminate the Polynesian rat on most offshore islands in its jurisdiction, and other conservation groups have adopted similar programs in other reserves seeking to be predator- and rat-free.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-9)
However, two islands in the [Hen and Chickens group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hen_and_Chicken_Islands "Hen and Chicken Islands"), Mauitaha and Araara, have now been set aside as sanctuaries for the Polynesian rat.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-NZ_Herald_10649358-10)
### Rest of the Pacific
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polynesian_rat&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Rest of the Pacific")]
[NZAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NZAID "NZAID") has funded rat eradication programs in the [Phoenix Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Islands "Phoenix Islands") of [Kiribati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiribati "Kiribati") in order to protect the bird species of the [Phoenix Islands Protected Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Islands_Protected_Area "Phoenix Islands Protected Area").[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-PI12-11)
Between July and November 2011, a partnership of the Pitcairn Islands Government and the [Royal Society for the Protection of Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_for_the_Protection_of_Birds "Royal Society for the Protection of Birds") implemented a poison baiting programme on [Henderson Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson_Island_\(Pitcairn_Islands\) "Henderson Island \(Pitcairn Islands\)") aimed at eradicating the Polynesian rat.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-12) Mortality was massive, but of the 50,000 to 100,000 population, 60 to 80 individuals survived and the population has now fully recovered.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_rat#cite_note-13)
