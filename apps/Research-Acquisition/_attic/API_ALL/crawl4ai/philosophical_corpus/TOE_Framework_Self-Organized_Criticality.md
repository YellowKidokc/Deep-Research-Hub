**Self-organized criticality** (**SOC**) is a property of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_system "Dynamical system") that have a [critical point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_phenomena "Critical phenomena") as an [attractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attractor "Attractor"). Their macroscopic behavior thus displays the spatial or temporal [scale-invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_invariance "Scale invariance") characteristic of the [critical point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_point_\(physics\) "Critical point \(physics\)") of a [phase transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_transition "Phase transition"), but without the need to tune control parameters to a precise value, because the system, effectively, tunes itself as it evolves towards criticality. 
The concept was put forward by [Per Bak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Bak "Per Bak"), [Chao Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Tang "Chao Tang") and [Kurt Wiesenfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Wiesenfeld "Kurt Wiesenfeld") ("BTW") in a paper[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Bak1987-1) following an earlier paper[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Katz1986-2) by Jonathan I. Katz published in 1987 in _[Physical Review Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Review_Letters "Physical Review Letters")_ , and is considered to be one of the mechanisms by which [complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity "Complexity")[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Bak1995-3) arises in nature. Its concepts have been applied across fields as diverse as [geophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysics "Geophysics"),[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-SmalleyTurcotteSolla85-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-SmythNashMoum2019-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-6) [physical cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology "Physical cosmology"), [evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology "Evolutionary biology") and [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology "Ecology"), [bio-inspired computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bio-inspired_computing "Bio-inspired computing") and [optimization (mathematics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_\(mathematics\) "Optimization \(mathematics\)"), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics"), [quantum gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_gravity "Quantum gravity"), [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology "Sociology"), [solar physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_physics "Solar physics"), [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics "Plasma physics"), [neurobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurobiology "Neurobiology")[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-8)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-LinkenkaerHansen2001-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Beggs2003-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Chialvo2004-11) and others. 
SOC is typically observed in slowly driven [non-equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-equilibrium_thermodynamics "Non-equilibrium thermodynamics") systems with many [degrees of freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrees_of_freedom_\(physics_and_chemistry\) "Degrees of freedom \(physics and chemistry\)") and strongly [nonlinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinearity "Nonlinearity") dynamics. Many individual examples have been identified since BTW's original paper, but to date there is no known set of general characteristics that _guarantee_ a system will display SOC. 
## Overview
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Self-organized_criticality&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Overview")]
Self-organized criticality is one of a number of important discoveries made in [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics "Statistical physics") and related fields over the latter half of the 20th century, discoveries which relate particularly to the study of [complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity "Complexity") in nature. For example, the study of [cellular automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automata "Cellular automata"), from the early discoveries of [Stanislaw Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ulam "Stanislaw Ulam") and [John von Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann") through to [John Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Horton_Conway "John Horton Conway")'s [Game of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway%27s_Game_of_Life "Conway's Game of Life") and the extensive work of [Stephen Wolfram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Wolfram "Stephen Wolfram"), made it clear that complexity could be generated as an [emergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergence "Emergence") feature of extended systems with simple local interactions. Over a similar period of time, [Benoît Mandelbrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beno%C3%AEt_Mandelbrot "Benoît Mandelbrot")'s large body of work on [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractals "Fractals") showed that much complexity in nature could be described by certain ubiquitous mathematical laws, while the extensive study of [phase transitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_transition "Phase transition") carried out in the 1960s and 1970s showed how [scale invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_invariance "Scale invariance") phenomena such as [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractals "Fractals") and [power laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_law "Power law") emerged at the [critical point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_point_\(physics\) "Critical point \(physics\)") between phases. 
The term _self-organized criticality_ was first introduced in [Bak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Bak "Per Bak"), [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Tang "Chao Tang") and [Wiesenfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Wiesenfeld "Kurt Wiesenfeld")'s 1987 paper, which clearly linked together those factors: a simple [cellular automaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automaton "Cellular automaton") was shown to produce several characteristic features observed in natural complexity ([fractal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal "Fractal") geometry, [pink (1/f) noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_noise "Pink noise") and [power laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_law "Power law")) in a way that could be linked to [critical-point phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_point_\(physics\) "Critical point \(physics\)"). Crucially, however, the paper emphasized that the complexity observed emerged in a robust manner that did not depend on finely tuned details of the system: variable parameters in the model could be changed widely without affecting the emergence of critical behavior: hence, _[self-organized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized "Self-organized")_ criticality. Thus, the key result of BTW's paper was its discovery of a mechanism by which the emergence of complexity from simple local interactions could be _spontaneous_ —and therefore plausible as a source of natural complexity—rather than something that was only possible in artificial situations in which control parameters are tuned to precise critical values. An alternative view is that SOC appears when the criticality is linked to a value of zero of the control parameters.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Gabrielli2000-12)
Despite the considerable interest and research output generated from the SOC hypothesis, there remains no general agreement with regards to its mechanisms in abstract mathematical form. Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld based their hypothesis on the behavior of their sandpile model.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Bak1987-1)
## Mathematical models
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Self-organized_criticality&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Mathematical models")]
In chronological order of development: 
  * [Invasion percolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_percolation "Invasion percolation")
  * Stick-slip model of fault failure[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-TurcotteSmalleySolla85-13)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-SmalleyTurcotteSolla85-4)
  * [Bak–Tang–Wiesenfeld sandpile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_sandpile_model "Abelian sandpile model")
  * [Forest-fire model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest-fire_model "Forest-fire model")
  * Manna model[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-14)
  * [Olami–Feder–Christensen model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olami%E2%80%93Feder%E2%80%93Christensen_model "Olami–Feder–Christensen model")
  * [Bak–Sneppen model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bak%E2%80%93Sneppen_model "Bak–Sneppen model")
  * [Neural network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_network "Neural network")[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-15)
  * The rice pile[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-16) or Oslo model[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-17)


Early theoretical work included the development of a variety of alternative SOC-generating dynamics distinct from the BTW model, attempts to prove model properties analytically (including calculating the [critical exponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_exponent "Critical exponent")[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Tang1988a-18)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Tang1988b-19)), and examination of the conditions necessary for SOC to emerge. One of the important issues for the latter investigation was whether [conservation of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_energy "Conservation of energy") was required in the local dynamical exchanges of models: the answer in general is no, but with (minor) reservations, as some exchange dynamics (such as those of BTW) do require local conservation at least on average [_[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_]. 
It has been argued that the energy released in the BTW "sandpile" model should actually generate 1/f2 noise rather than 1/f noise.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Jensen1989-20) This claim was based on untested scaling assumptions, and a more rigorous analysis showed that sandpile models generally produce 1/fa spectra, with a<2.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Laurson2005-21) However, the dynamics of the accumulated stress does exhibit the 1/f noise in the BTW model.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-22) Other simulation models were proposed later that could also produce true 1/f noise.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Maslov1999-23)
In addition to the nonconservative theoretical model mentioned above [_[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_], other theoretical models for SOC have been based upon [information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theory "Information theory"),[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Dewar2003-24) [mean field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_field_theory "Mean field theory"),[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Vespignani1998-25) the [convergence of random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_of_random_variables "Convergence of random variables"),[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Kendal2015-26) and cluster formation.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Hoffmann2018b-27) A continuous model of self-organised criticality is proposed by using [tropical geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_geometry "Tropical geometry").[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-28)
Key theoretical issues yet to be resolved include the calculation of the possible [universality classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universality_class "Universality class") of SOC behavior and the question of whether it is possible to derive a general rule for determining if an arbitrary [algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithm "Algorithm") displays SOC. 
## In nature
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Self-organized_criticality&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: In nature")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Sandpile_Matemateca_19.jpg/250px-Sandpile_Matemateca_19.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sandpile_Matemateca_19.jpg)The relevance of SOC to the dynamics of real sand has been questioned.
SOC has become established as a strong candidate for explaining a number of natural phenomena, including: 
  * The magnitude of earthquakes ([Gutenberg–Richter law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutenberg%E2%80%93Richter_law "Gutenberg–Richter law")) and frequency of aftershocks ([Omori law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftershock "Aftershock")) [[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-TurcotteSmalleySolla85-13)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-SmalleyTurcotteSolla85-4)
  * Fluctuations in economic systems such as [financial markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_markets "Financial markets") (references to SOC are common in [econophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econophysics "Econophysics")) [[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-29)[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-30)
  * The evolution of proteins[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Phillips2014-31)[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Phillips2021-32)
  * Forest fires [_[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_][[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-33)
  * Neuronal avalanches in the cortex[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Beggs2003-10)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Poil2012-34)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-35)[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-36)
  * [Acoustic emission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_emission "Acoustic emission") from fracturing materials[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Caldarelli1996-37)


Despite the numerous applications of SOC to understanding natural phenomena, the universality of SOC theory has been questioned. For example, experiments with real piles of rice revealed their dynamics to be far more sensitive to parameters than originally predicted.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Frette1996-38)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Bak1987-1) Furthermore, it has been argued that 1/f scaling in EEG recordings are inconsistent with critical states,[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Bedard2006-39) and whether SOC is a fundamental property of neural systems remains an open and controversial topic.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Hesse2014-40)
## Optimization
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Self-organized_criticality&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Optimization")]
It has been found that the avalanches from an SOC process make effective patterns in a random search for optimal solutions on graphs.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality#cite_note-Hoffmann2018-41) An example of such an optimization problem is [graph coloring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_coloring "Graph coloring"). The SOC process apparently helps the optimization from getting stuck in a [local optimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_optimum "Local optimum") without the use of any [annealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulated_annealing "Simulated annealing") scheme, as suggested by previous work on [extremal optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremal_optimization "Extremal optimization").
