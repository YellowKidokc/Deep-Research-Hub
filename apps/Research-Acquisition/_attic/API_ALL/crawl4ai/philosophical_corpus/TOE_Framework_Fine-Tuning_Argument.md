**The fine-tuned universe** is the hypothesis that, because "life as we know it" could not exist if the [constants of nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_constant "Physical constant")—such as the [electron charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_charge "Electron charge") or the [gravitational constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_constant "Gravitational constant")—had been even slightly different, the universe must be tuned specifically for life.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-:0-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-:1-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-:2-4) In practice, this hypothesis is formulated in terms of [dimensionless physical constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_physical_constants "Dimensionless physical constants").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-stanford_encylopedia-5)
## History
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]
In 1913, [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") [Lawrence Joseph Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Joseph_Henderson "Lawrence Joseph Henderson") wrote _The Fitness of the Environment,_ one of the first books to explore fine tuning in the universe. Henderson discusses the importance of water and the environment to living things, pointing out that life as it exists on Earth depends entirely on Earth's very specific environmental conditions, especially the prevalence and properties of water.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-6)
In 1961, physicist [Robert H. Dicke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Dicke "Robert H. Dicke") argued that certain forces in physics, such as [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity "Gravity") and [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism "Electromagnetism"), must be perfectly fine-tuned for life to exist in the universe.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Dicke-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-8)
Astronomer [Fred Hoyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Hoyle "Fred Hoyle") argued for a fine-tuned universe: "From 1953 onward, Willy Fowler and I have always been intrigued by the remarkable relation of [...] and your fixing would have to be just where these levels are actually found to be. [...] A common sense interpretation of the facts suggests that a superintellect has monkeyed with physics, as well as with chemistry and biology, and that there are no blind forces worth speaking about in nature."[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-9) In his 1983 book _The Intelligent Universe_ ,[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-10) Hoyle wrote, "The list of anthropic properties, apparent accidents of a non-biological nature without which carbon-based and hence human life could not exist, is large and impressive."[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-11)
The desire to resolve the fine-tuning problem led to the expectation that the [Large Hadron Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Hadron_Collider "Large Hadron Collider") would produce evidence of [physics beyond the Standard Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_beyond_the_Standard_Model "Physics beyond the Standard Model"), such as [supersymmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersymmetry "Supersymmetry"),[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-rosaler-12) but by 2012 it had not produced evidence for supersymmetry at the energy scales it was able to probe.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-13) This absence leaves the fine-tuning problem unresolved, leading some physicists to suggest that the Standard Model may fundamentally require fine-tuning rather than a natural explanation.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-rosaler-12)
## Motivation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Motivation")]
Physicist [Paul Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Davies "Paul Davies") said: "There is now broad agreement among physicists and cosmologists that the Universe is in several respects 'fine-tuned' for life. But the conclusion is not so much that the Universe is fine-tuned for life; rather it is fine-tuned for the building blocks and environments that life requires".[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-14) He also said that "'[anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic_principle "Anthropic principle")' reasoning fails to distinguish between minimally [biophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophilia_hypothesis "Biophilia hypothesis") universes, in which life is permitted, but only marginally possible, and optimally biophilic universes, in which life flourishes because [biogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogenesis "Biogenesis") occurs frequently".[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Davies200-15) Among scientists who find the evidence persuasive, a variety of [natural explanations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalism_\(philosophy\) "Naturalism \(philosophy\)") have been proposed, such as the existence of [multiple universes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse "Multiverse") introducing a [survivorship bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivorship_bias "Survivorship bias") under the [anthropic principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic_principle "Anthropic principle").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-stanford_encylopedia-5)
The premise of the fine-tuned universe assertion is that a small change in several of the physical constants would make the universe radically different. [Stephen Hawking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking "Stephen Hawking") observed: "The laws of science, as we know them at present, contain many fundamental numbers, like the size of the electric charge of the electron and the ratio of the masses of the proton and the electron. ... The remarkable fact is that the values of these numbers seem to have been very finely adjusted to make possible the development of life".[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-:2-4)
For example, if the strong nuclear force were 2% stronger than it is (i.e. if the [coupling constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_constant "Coupling constant") representing its strength were 2% larger) while the other constants were left unchanged, [diprotons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diproton "Diproton") would be stable; according to Davies, hydrogen would [fuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_fusion "Hydrogen fusion") into them instead of [deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium "Deuterium") and [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium "Helium").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Davies1993-16) This would drastically alter the physics of [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star "Star"), and presumably preclude the existence of life similar to what we observe on Earth. The diproton's existence would short-circuit the slow fusion of hydrogen into deuterium. Hydrogen would fuse so easily that it is likely that all the universe's hydrogen would be consumed in the first few minutes after the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang "Big Bang").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Davies1993-16) This "diproton argument" is disputed by other physicists, who calculate that as long as the increase in strength is less than 50%, stellar fusion could occur despite the existence of stable diprotons.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-macdonald-17)
The precise formulation of the idea is made difficult by the fact that it is not yet known how many independent physical constants there are. The [Standard Model of particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model_of_particle_physics "Standard Model of particle physics") has 25 freely adjustable parameters and [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity") has one more, the [cosmological constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_constant "Cosmological constant"), which is [known to be nonzero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_expansion_of_the_universe "Accelerating expansion of the universe") but profoundly small in value. Because physicists have not developed an empirically successful theory of [quantum gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_gravity "Quantum gravity"), there is no known way to combine quantum mechanics, on which the standard model depends, and general relativity.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-The_Mystery_of_the_Cosmological_Con-18)
Without knowledge of this more complete theory suspected to underlie the standard model, it is impossible to definitively count the number of truly independent physical constants. In some candidate theories, the number of independent physical constants may be as small as one. For example, the cosmological constant may be a fundamental constant but attempts have also been made to calculate it from other constants, and according to the author of one such calculation, "the small value of the cosmological constant is telling us that a remarkably precise and totally unexpected relation exists among all the parameters of the [Standard Model of particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model_of_particle_physics "Standard Model of particle physics"), the bare cosmological constant and unknown physics".[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-The_Mystery_of_the_Cosmological_Con-18)
## Examples
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Examples")]
[Martin Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Rees "Martin Rees") formulates the fine-tuning of the universe in terms of the following six [dimensionless physical constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_physical_constant "Dimensionless physical constant").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-:0-1)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-discover_nov_2000_cover_story-19)
  * _N_ , the ratio of the electromagnetic force to the gravitational force between a pair of protons, is approximately 1036. According to Rees, if it were significantly smaller, only a small and short-lived universe could exist.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-discover_nov_2000_cover_story-19) If it were large enough, they would repel them so violently that larger atoms would never be generated.
  * _Epsilon_ (_ε_), a measure of the nuclear efficiency of [fusion from hydrogen to helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_nucleosynthesis#Hydrogen_fusion "Stellar nucleosynthesis"), is 0.007: when four nucleons fuse into helium, 0.007 (0.7%) of their mass is converted to energy. The value of _ε_ is in part determined by the strength of the [strong nuclear force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_nuclear_force "Strong nuclear force").[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-20) If _ε_ were 0.006, a proton could not bond to a neutron, and only hydrogen could exist, and complex chemistry would be impossible. According to Rees, if it were above 0.008, no hydrogen would exist, as all the hydrogen would have been fused shortly after the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang "Big Bang"). Other physicists disagree, calculating that substantial hydrogen remains as long as the strong force coupling constant increases by less than about 50%.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-macdonald-17)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-discover_nov_2000_cover_story-19)
  * _Omega_ (Ω), commonly known as the [density parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_parameter#Density_parameter "Density parameter"), is the relative importance of gravity and expansion energy in the universe. It is the ratio of the mass density of the universe to the "critical density" and is approximately 1. If gravity were too strong compared with dark energy and the initial cosmic expansion rate, the universe would have collapsed before life could have evolved. If gravity were too weak, no stars would have formed.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-discover_nov_2000_cover_story-19)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-21)
  * _Lambda_ (Λ), commonly known as the [cosmological constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_constant "Cosmological constant"), describes the ratio of the density of [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy "Dark energy") to the critical energy density of the universe, given certain reasonable assumptions such as that dark energy density is a constant. In terms of [Planck units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_units "Planck units"), and as a natural dimensionless value, Λ is on the order of −122.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-22) This is so small that it has no significant effect on cosmic structures that are smaller than a billion light-years across. A slightly larger value of the cosmological constant would have caused [space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetime "Spacetime") to expand rapidly enough that stars and other astronomical structures would not be able to form.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-discover_nov_2000_cover_story-19)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-susskind-23)
  * _Q_ , the ratio of the gravitational energy required to pull a large galaxy apart to the energy equivalent of its mass, is around 10−5. If it is too small, no stars can form. If it is too large, no stars can survive because the universe is too violent, according to Rees.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-discover_nov_2000_cover_story-19)
  * _D_ , the number of spatial [dimensions in spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionality "Dimensionality"), is 3. Rees claims that life could not exist if there were 2 or 4 spatial dimensions.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-discover_nov_2000_cover_story-19) Rees argues this does not preclude the existence of [ten-dimensional strings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_theory#Number_of_dimensions "String theory").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-:0-1)


[Max Tegmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Tegmark "Max Tegmark") argued that if there is more than one time dimension, then physical systems' behavior could not be predicted reliably from knowledge of the relevant [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equation "Partial differential equation"). In such a universe, intelligent life capable of manipulating technology could not emerge. Moreover, [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") and [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") would be unstable and could decay into particles having greater mass than themselves. This is not a problem if the particles have a sufficiently low temperature.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-tegmark-dim-24)
### Carbon and oxygen
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Carbon and oxygen")]
Further information: [Triple-alpha process § Improbability and fine-tuning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple-alpha_process#Improbability_and_fine-tuning "Triple-alpha process")
An older example is the [Hoyle state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoyle_state "Hoyle state"), the third-lowest energy state of the [carbon-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon-12 "Carbon-12") nucleus, with an energy of 7.656 MeV above the ground level.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-25) According to one calculation, if the state's energy level were lower than 7.3 or greater than 7.9 MeV, insufficient carbon would exist to support life. To explain the universe's abundance of carbon, the Hoyle state must be further tuned to a value between 7.596 and 7.716 MeV. A similar calculation, focusing on the underlying fundamental constants that give rise to various energy levels, concludes that the [strong force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_force "Strong force") must be tuned to a precision of at least 0.5%, and the electromagnetic force to a precision of at least 4%, to prevent either carbon production or oxygen production from dropping significantly.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-26)
## Explanations
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Explanations")]
Some explanations of fine-tuning are [naturalistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysical_naturalism "Metaphysical naturalism").[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-27) First, the fine-tuning might be an illusion: more fundamental physics may explain the apparent fine-tuning in physical parameters in the current understanding by constraining the values those parameters are likely to take. As [Lawrence Krauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Krauss "Lawrence Krauss") put it, "certain quantities have seemed inexplicable and fine-tuned, and once we understand them, they don't seem to be so fine-tuned. We have to have some historical perspective".[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-susskind-23) [Victor J. Stenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_J._Stenger "Victor J. Stenger") has shown that random selection of physical parameters can still produce universes capable of harboring life.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-28) Some argue it is possible that a final fundamental [theory of everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_everything "Theory of everything") will explain the underlying causes of the apparent fine-tuning in every parameter.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-29)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-susskind-23)
Still, as modern cosmology developed, various hypotheses not presuming hidden order have been proposed. One is a [multiverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse "Multiverse"), where fundamental physical constants are postulated to have different values outside of the known universe.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-30)[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-31): 3–33  On this hypothesis, separate parts of reality would have wildly different characteristics. In such scenarios, the appearance of fine-tuning is explained as a consequence of the weak [anthropic principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic_principle "Anthropic principle") and [selection bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selection_bias "Selection bias"), specifically [survivorship bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivorship_bias "Survivorship bias"). Only those universes with fundamental constants hospitable to life, such as on Earth, could contain life forms capable of observing the universe who can contemplate the question of fine-tuning.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-32) Zhi-Wei Wang and [Samuel L. Braunstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_L._Braunstein "Samuel L. Braunstein") argue that the apparent fine-tuning of fundamental constants could be due to the lack of understanding of these constants.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Sciama's_argument_on_life_in_a_rand-33)
### Multiverse
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Multiverse")]
Main article: [Multiverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse "Multiverse")
See also: [Anthropic principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic_principle "Anthropic principle")
If the universe is just one of many (possibly infinitely many) universes, each with different physical phenomena and constants, it is unsurprising that there is a universe hospitable to intelligent life. Some versions of the multiverse hypothesis therefore provide a simple explanation for any fine-tuning,[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-stanford_encylopedia-5) while the analysis of Wang and Braunstein challenges the view that this universe is unique in its ability to support life.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Sciama's_argument_on_life_in_a_rand-33)
The multiverse idea has led to considerable research into the anthropic principle and has been of particular interest to [particle physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics") because [theories of everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_everything "Theory of everything") do apparently generate large numbers of universes in which the physical constants vary widely. Although there is no evidence for the existence of a multiverse, some versions of the theory make predictions of which some researchers studying [M-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-theory "M-theory") and gravity leaks hope to see some evidence soon.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Michio_Kaku-34) According to [Laura Mersini-Houghton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Mersini-Houghton "Laura Mersini-Houghton"), the [WMAP cold spot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WMAP_cold_spot#Parallel_universe "WMAP cold spot") could provide testable empirical evidence of a [parallel universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse "Multiverse").[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-35) Variants of this approach include [Lee Smolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Smolin "Lee Smolin")'s notion of cosmological [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection "Natural selection"), the [ekpyrotic universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekpyrotic_universe "Ekpyrotic universe"), and the [bubble universe theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_inflation#Overview "Eternal inflation").[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Michio_Kaku-34): 220–221 
It has been suggested that invoking the multiverse to explain fine-tuning is a form of the [inverse gambler's fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_gambler%27s_fallacy "Inverse gambler's fallacy").[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-36)[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-37)
### Top-down cosmology
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Top-down cosmology")]
[Stephen Hawking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking "Stephen Hawking") and [Thomas Hertog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hertog "Thomas Hertog") proposed that the universe's initial conditions consisted of a [superposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_superposition "Quantum superposition") of many possible initial conditions, only a small fraction of which contributed to the conditions seen today.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-38) According to the [top-down cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top-down_cosmology "Top-down cosmology") theory, the universe's "fine-tuned" physical constants are inevitable, because the universe "selects" only those histories that led to the present conditions. In this way, top-down cosmology provides an anthropic explanation for why this universe allows matter and life without invoking the multiverse.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-39)
### Carbon chauvinism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Carbon chauvinism")]
Some forms of fine-tuning arguments about the formation of life assume that only carbon-based life forms are possible, an assumption sometimes called [carbon chauvinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_chauvinism "Carbon chauvinism").[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-stenger-40) Conceptually, [alternative biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_biochemistry "Alternative biochemistry") or other forms of life are possible.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-41)
### Simulation hypothesis
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Simulation hypothesis")]
The [simulation hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation_hypothesis "Simulation hypothesis") holds that the universe is fine-tuned simply because the more technologically advanced simulation operator(s) programmed it that way.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-42)
### No improbability
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: No improbability")]
[Graham Priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Priest "Graham Priest"), [Mark Colyvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Colyvan "Mark Colyvan"), [Jay L. Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_L._Garfield "Jay L. Garfield"), and others have argued against the presupposition that "the laws of physics or the boundary conditions of the universe could have been other than they are".[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-43)
### Theistic
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fine-tuned_universe&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Theistic")]
See also: [Teleological Argument § Fine-tuned universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleological_Argument#Fine-tuned_universe "Teleological Argument")
Some scientists, [theologians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theologian "Theologian"), and [philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher "Philosopher"), as well as certain religious groups, argue that [providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Providence "Divine Providence") or [creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_myth "Creation myth") are responsible for fine-tuning.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-44)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-45)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-46)[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-47)[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-48) Christian philosopher [Alvin Plantinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Plantinga "Alvin Plantinga") argues that random chance, applied to a single and sole universe, only raises the question as to why this universe could be so "lucky" as to have precise conditions that support life at least at some place (the Earth) and time (within millions of years of the present). 
> One reaction to these apparent enormous [coincidences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic_principle#Anthropic_'coincidences' "Anthropic principle") is to see them as substantiating the theistic claim that the universe has been created by a personal God and as offering the material for a properly restrained theistic argument – hence the fine-tuning argument. It's as if there are a large number of dials that have to be tuned to within extremely narrow limits for life to be possible in our universe. It is extremely unlikely that this should happen by chance, but much more likely that this should happen if there is such a person as God.
— Alvin Plantinga, "The Dawkins Confusion: Naturalism _ad absurdum_ "[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Plantinga-49)
[William Lane Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lane_Craig "William Lane Craig"), a philosopher and [Christian apologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_apologist "Christian apologist"), cites this fine-tuning of the universe as [evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_from_design "Argument from design") for the existence of [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God "God") or some form of [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_designer "Intelligent designer") capable of manipulating (or designing) the basic [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") that governs the universe.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Craig-50) Philosopher and theologian [Richard Swinburne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Swinburne "Richard Swinburne") reaches the design conclusion using [Bayesian probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_probability "Bayesian probability").[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-51) Scientist and theologian [Alister McGrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alister_McGrath "Alister McGrath") observed that the fine-tuning of carbon is even responsible for nature's ability to tune itself to any degree. 
> The entire biological evolutionary process depends upon the unusual chemistry of carbon, which allows it to bond to itself, as well as other elements, creating highly complex molecules that are stable over prevailing terrestrial temperatures, and are capable of conveying genetic information (especially DNA). [...] Whereas it might be argued that nature creates its own fine-tuning, this can only be done if the primordial constituents of the universe are such that an evolutionary process can be initiated. The unique chemistry of carbon is the ultimate foundation of the capacity of nature to tune itself.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-52)[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-53)
Theoretical physicist and Anglican priest [John Polkinghorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Polkinghorne "John Polkinghorne") stated: "Anthropic fine tuning is too remarkable to be dismissed as just a happy accident".[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-54)
Theologian and philosopher [Andrew Loke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Loke "Andrew Loke") argues that there are only five possible categories of hypotheses concerning fine-tuning and order: (i) chance, (ii) regularity, (iii) combinations of regularity and chance, (iv) uncaused, and (v) design, and that only design gives an exclusively logical explanation of order in the universe.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Palgrave-55) He argues that the [Kalam Cosmological Argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalam_Cosmological_Argument "Kalam Cosmological Argument") strengthens the teleological argument by answering the question "[Who designed the Designer?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_of_the_creator_of_God "Problem of the creator of God")".[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-Palgrave-55)
Creationist [Hugh Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Ross_\(astrophysicist\) "Hugh Ross \(astrophysicist\)") advances a number of fine-tuning hypotheses.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-56)[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-57) One is the existence of what Ross calls "vital poisons", which are elemental [nutrients that are harmful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutrient#Deficiencies_and_toxicity "Nutrient") in large quantities but essential for animal life in smaller quantities.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-58)
Philosopher and theologian [Robin Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Collins "Robin Collins") argues that theism entails the expectation that God would create a reality structured to allow for scientific discovery to easily happen. According to Collins, various physical constants such as the [fine-structure constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-structure_constant "Fine-structure constant") allowing for efficient energy usage, the [baryon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baryon "Baryon")-to-[photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon "Photon") ratio allowing for the [cosmic microwave background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background "Cosmic microwave background") to be discovered, and the mass of the [Higgs boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_boson "Higgs boson") allowing it to be detected are examples of the laws of physics being fine-tuned for scientific discovery.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-59)
Evolutionary biologist [Richard Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dawkins "Richard Dawkins") dismisses the theistic argument as "deeply unsatisfying" since it leaves the existence of God unexplained, with a God capable of calculating the fine-tuning at least as improbable as the fine-tuning itself.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-60) Against this claim, it has been argued that theism is a simple hypothesis, allowing theists to deny that God is at least as improbable as the fine-tuning.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-61)[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-62)[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-63)
[Douglas Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Adams "Douglas Adams") satirized the theistic argument in his 2002 book _[The Salmon of Doubt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Salmon_of_Doubt "The Salmon of Doubt")_ :[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe#cite_note-64)
> Imagine a puddle waking up one morning and thinking, "This is an interesting world I find myself in, an interesting hole I find myself in, fits me rather neatly, doesn't it? In fact, it fits me staggeringly well, must have been made to have me in it!"
