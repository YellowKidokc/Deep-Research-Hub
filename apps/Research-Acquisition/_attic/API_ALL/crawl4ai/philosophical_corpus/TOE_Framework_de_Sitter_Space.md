In [mathematical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physics "Mathematical physics"), _n_ -dimensional **de Sitter space** (often denoted dS _n_) is a maximally symmetric [Lorentzian manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold "Lorentzian manifold") with constant positive [scalar curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_curvature "Scalar curvature"). It is analogue of an [_n_ -sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-sphere "N-sphere"), with a [Lorentzian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_metric "Lorentzian metric") in place of the [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric "Riemannian metric") of the latter. 
The main application of de Sitter space is its use in [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity"), where it serves as one of the simplest mathematical models of the universe consistent with the observed [accelerating expansion of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_expansion_of_the_universe "Accelerating expansion of the universe"). More specifically, de Sitter space is the maximally symmetric [vacuum solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_solution "Vacuum solution") of [Einstein's field equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein%27s_field_equations "Einstein's field equations") in which the [cosmological constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_constant "Cosmological constant") Λ {\displaystyle \Lambda } ![{\\displaystyle \\Lambda }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/0ac0a4a98a414e3480335f9ba652d12571ec6733) is positive (corresponding to a positive vacuum energy density and negative pressure). 
De Sitter space and [anti-de Sitter space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-de_Sitter_space "Anti-de Sitter space") are named after [Willem de Sitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_de_Sitter "Willem de Sitter") (1872–1934),[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Sitter_space#cite_note-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Sitter_space#cite_note-2) professor of astronomy at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University "Leiden University") and director of the [Leiden Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_Observatory "Leiden Observatory"). Willem de Sitter and [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") worked closely together in [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden "Leiden") in the 1920s on the spacetime structure of the universe. De Sitter space was also discovered, independently, and about the same time, by [Tullio Levi-Civita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullio_Levi-Civita "Tullio Levi-Civita").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Sitter_space#cite_note-3)
## Definition
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=De_Sitter_space&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Definition")]
A de Sitter space can be defined as a [submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submanifold "Submanifold") of a generalized [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space "Minkowski space") of one higher [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension "Dimension"), including the induced metric. Take Minkowski space **R** 1,_n_ with the standard [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_tensor "Metric tensor"):  d s 2 = − d x 0 2 + ∑ i = 1 n d x i 2 . {\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dx_{0}^{2}+\sum _{i=1}^{n}dx_{i}^{2}.} ![{\\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dx_{0}^{2}+\\sum _{i=1}^{n}dx_{i}^{2}.}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/60a2f98c1a3d6da5b4142fc4c832450e1a221a1b)
The _n_ -dimensional de Sitter space is the submanifold described by the [hyperboloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperboloid "Hyperboloid") of one sheet  − x 0 2 + ∑ i = 1 n x i 2 = α 2 , {\displaystyle -x_{0}^{2}+\sum _{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}=\alpha ^{2},} ![{\\displaystyle -x_{0}^{2}+\\sum _{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}=\\alpha ^{2},}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b10cc418b2866908f82ed2999f1de4be1f7802a6) where  α {\displaystyle \alpha } ![{\\displaystyle \\alpha }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b79333175c8b3f0840bfb4ec41b8072c83ea88d3) is some nonzero constant with its dimension being that of length. The induced [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_tensor "Metric tensor") on the de Sitter space is induced from the ambient Lorentzian metric. It is [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate "Nondegenerate") and has Lorentzian signature. (If one replaces  α 2 {\displaystyle \alpha ^{2}} ![{\\displaystyle \\alpha ^{2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/9850832395fd0deec6790b4b2265df6656f08ef2) with  − α 2 {\displaystyle -\alpha ^{2}} ![{\\displaystyle -\\alpha ^{2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/2973234387b55778ce29262d829f47261068a82d) in the above definition, one obtains a [hyperboloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperboloid "Hyperboloid") of two sheets. The induced metric in this case is [positive-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definite_quadratic_form "Definite quadratic form"), and each sheet is a copy of [hyperbolic _n_ -space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_space "Hyperbolic space"). See _[Minkowski space § Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space#Geometry "Minkowski space")_.) 
The de Sitter space can also be defined as the [quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_space "Homogeneous space") O(1, _n_) / O(1, _n_ − 1) of two [indefinite orthogonal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_orthogonal_group "Indefinite orthogonal group"), which shows that it is a non-Riemannian [symmetric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_space "Symmetric space"). 
[Topologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology "Topology"), dS _n_ is **R** × _S_ _n_ −1, which is [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected "Simply connected") if _n_ ≥ 3. 
## Properties
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=De_Sitter_space&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Properties")]
The [isometry group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometry_group "Isometry group") of de Sitter space is the [Lorentz group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_group "Lorentz group") O(1, _n_). The metric therefore then has _n_(_n_ + 1)/2 independent [Killing vector fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_vector_field "Killing vector field") and is maximally symmetric. Every maximally symmetric space has constant curvature. The [Riemann curvature tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_curvature_tensor "Riemann curvature tensor") of de Sitter space is given by[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Sitter_space#cite_note-zee-p626-4)
R ρ σ μ ν = 1 α 2 ( g ρ μ g σ ν − g ρ ν g σ μ ) {\displaystyle R_{\rho \sigma \mu \nu }={1 \over \alpha ^{2}}\left(g_{\rho \mu }g_{\sigma \nu }-g_{\rho \nu }g_{\sigma \mu }\right)} ![{\\displaystyle R_{\\rho \\sigma \\mu \\nu }={1 \\over \\alpha ^{2}}\\left\(g_{\\rho \\mu }g_{\\sigma \\nu }-g_{\\rho \\nu }g_{\\sigma \\mu }\\right\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/e6d10ca765173e6a344a83940cf0f433cce38735)
(using the sign convention  R ρ σ μ ν = ∂ μ Γ ν σ ρ − ∂ ν Γ μ σ ρ + Γ μ λ ρ Γ ν σ λ − Γ ν λ ρ Γ μ σ λ {\displaystyle R^{\rho }{}_{\sigma \mu \nu }=\partial _{\mu }\Gamma _{\nu \sigma }^{\rho }-\partial _{\nu }\Gamma _{\mu \sigma }^{\rho }+\Gamma _{\mu \lambda }^{\rho }\Gamma _{\nu \sigma }^{\lambda }-\Gamma _{\nu \lambda }^{\rho }\Gamma _{\mu \sigma }^{\lambda }} ![{\\displaystyle R^{\\rho }{}_{\\sigma \\mu \\nu }=\\partial _{\\mu }\\Gamma _{\\nu \\sigma }^{\\rho }-\\partial _{\\nu }\\Gamma _{\\mu \\sigma }^{\\rho }+\\Gamma _{\\mu \\lambda }^{\\rho }\\Gamma _{\\nu \\sigma }^{\\lambda }-\\Gamma _{\\nu \\lambda }^{\\rho }\\Gamma _{\\mu \\sigma }^{\\lambda }}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/64fddf066c60e94ac0a24b7d09bd2412846f6e3f) for the Riemann curvature tensor). De Sitter space is an [Einstein manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_manifold "Einstein manifold") since the [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor "Ricci tensor") is proportional to the metric: 
R μ ν = R λ μ λ ν = n − 1 α 2 g μ ν {\displaystyle R_{\mu \nu }=R^{\lambda }{}_{\mu \lambda \nu }={\frac {n-1}{\alpha ^{2}}}g_{\mu \nu }} ![{\\displaystyle R_{\\mu \\nu }=R^{\\lambda }{}_{\\mu \\lambda \\nu }={\\frac {n-1}{\\alpha ^{2}}}g_{\\mu \\nu }}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/296aedc3591818e656648f5487365656dcf8e7dd)
This means de Sitter space is a vacuum solution of Einstein's equation with cosmological constant given by 
Λ = ( n − 1 ) ( n − 2 ) 2 α 2 . {\displaystyle \Lambda ={\frac {(n-1)(n-2)}{2\alpha ^{2}}}.} ![{\\displaystyle \\Lambda ={\\frac {\(n-1\)\(n-2\)}{2\\alpha ^{2}}}.}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/1fde480d3503cead2a43fe1b3595124e767f9a4c)
The [scalar curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_curvature "Scalar curvature") of de Sitter space is given by[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Sitter_space#cite_note-zee-p626-4)
R = n ( n − 1 ) α 2 = 2 n n − 2 Λ . {\displaystyle R={\frac {n(n-1)}{\alpha ^{2}}}={\frac {2n}{n-2}}\Lambda .} ![{\\displaystyle R={\\frac {n\(n-1\)}{\\alpha ^{2}}}={\\frac {2n}{n-2}}\\Lambda .}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/6535e0395f7ecfd03c30aad7e7cd9475f79d2f98)
For the case _n_ = 4, we have Λ = 3/_α_ 2 and _R_ = 4Λ = 12/_α_ 2.

### Static coordinates

We can introduce [static coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_spacetime "Static spacetime") ( t , r , … ) {\displaystyle (t,r,\ldots )} ![{\\displaystyle \(t,r,\\ldots \)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/16de62036356627e91764255c62e920b98b05b89) for de Sitter as follows: 
x 0 = α 2 − r 2 sinh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) x 1 = α 2 − r 2 cosh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) x i = r z i 2 ≤ i ≤ n , {\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}x_{0}&={\sqrt {\alpha ^{2}-r^{2}}}\sinh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)\\\x_{1}&={\sqrt {\alpha ^{2}-r^{2}}}\cosh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)\\\x_{i}&=rz_{i}\qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad 2\leq i\leq n,\end{aligned}}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\begin{aligned}x_{0}&={\\sqrt {\\alpha ^{2}-r^{2}}}\\sinh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)\\\\x_{1}&={\\sqrt {\\alpha ^{2}-r^{2}}}\\cosh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)\\\\x_{i}&=rz_{i}\\qquad \\qquad \\qquad \\qquad \\qquad 2\\leq i\\leq n,\\end{aligned}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/27d34cde325fc827f6aab77cae85599937a4e54b)
where  z i {\displaystyle z_{i}} ![{\\displaystyle z_{i}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5c6e920bac39ad09fff4efef16254595091a1025) gives the standard embedding the (_n_ − 2)-sphere in **R** _n_ −1. In these coordinates the de Sitter metric takes the form: 
d s 2 = − ( 1 − r 2 α 2 ) d t 2 + ( 1 − r 2 α 2 ) − 1 d r 2 + r 2 d Ω n − 2 2 . {\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\left(1-{\frac {r^{2}}{\alpha ^{2}}}\right)dt^{2}+\left(1-{\frac {r^{2}}{\alpha ^{2}}}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega _{n-2}^{2}.} ![{\\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\\left\(1-{\\frac {r^{2}}{\\alpha ^{2}}}\\right\)dt^{2}+\\left\(1-{\\frac {r^{2}}{\\alpha ^{2}}}\\right\)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\\Omega _{n-2}^{2}.}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/846e157859cf53d3bb2c1549b0b44830a0e859ee)
Note that there is a [cosmological horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_horizon "Cosmological horizon") at  r = α {\displaystyle r=\alpha } ![{\\displaystyle r=\\alpha }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/fb987f55c5c289fac8a055582f5b5f1ed6894a1f). 
### Flat slicing
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=De_Sitter_space&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Flat slicing")]
Let 
x 0 = α sinh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) + 1 2 α r 2 e 1 α t , x 1 = α cosh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) − 1 2 α r 2 e 1 α t , x i = e 1 α t y i , 2 ≤ i ≤ n {\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}x_{0}&=\alpha \sinh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)+{\frac {1}{2\alpha }}r^{2}e^{{\frac {1}{\alpha }}t},\\\x_{1}&=\alpha \cosh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)-{\frac {1}{2\alpha }}r^{2}e^{{\frac {1}{\alpha }}t},\\\x_{i}&=e^{{\frac {1}{\alpha }}t}y_{i},\qquad 2\leq i\leq n\end{aligned}}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\begin{aligned}x_{0}&=\\alpha \\sinh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)+{\\frac {1}{2\\alpha }}r^{2}e^{{\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t},\\\\x_{1}&=\\alpha \\cosh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)-{\\frac {1}{2\\alpha }}r^{2}e^{{\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t},\\\\x_{i}&=e^{{\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t}y_{i},\\qquad 2\\leq i\\leq n\\end{aligned}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/349a3603f826f690930778ef5b13171604bd0e8c)
where  r 2 = ∑ i y i 2 {\textstyle r^{2}=\sum _{i}y_{i}^{2}} ![{\\textstyle r^{2}=\\sum _{i}y_{i}^{2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/ad269bf011069128394bffbf21a32f52ae88d6d3). Then in the  ( t , y i ) {\displaystyle \left(t,y_{i}\right)} ![{\\displaystyle \\left\(t,y_{i}\\right\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/947cef8697c3651bc7d6e3b9e48603fdfc91b64d) coordinates metric reads: 
d s 2 = − d t 2 + e 2 1 α t d y 2 {\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+e^{2{\frac {1}{\alpha }}t}dy^{2}} ![{\\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+e^{2{\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t}dy^{2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/de241bd7100a85251b1062ae303df0a1b6771663)
where  d y 2 = ∑ i d y i 2 {\textstyle dy^{2}=\sum _{i}dy_{i}^{2}} ![{\\textstyle dy^{2}=\\sum _{i}dy_{i}^{2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f2e3f131c1bba62dbb0e1d0c8f060640da079b71) is the flat metric on  y i {\displaystyle y_{i}} ![{\\displaystyle y_{i}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/67d30d30b6c2dbe4d6f150d699de040937ecc95f)'s. 
Setting  ζ = ζ ∞ − α e − 1 α t {\displaystyle \zeta =\zeta _{\infty }-\alpha e^{-{\frac {1}{\alpha }}t}} ![{\\displaystyle \\zeta =\\zeta _{\\infty }-\\alpha e^{-{\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/bde1535ca0c807fb59b37ec59b60bb0bed015e68), we obtain the conformally flat metric: 
d s 2 = α 2 ( ζ ∞ − ζ ) 2 ( d y 2 − d ζ 2 ) {\displaystyle ds^{2}={\frac {\alpha ^{2}}{(\zeta _{\infty }-\zeta )^{2}}}\left(dy^{2}-d\zeta ^{2}\right)} ![{\\displaystyle ds^{2}={\\frac {\\alpha ^{2}}{\(\\zeta _{\\infty }-\\zeta \)^{2}}}\\left\(dy^{2}-d\\zeta ^{2}\\right\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5e53b06dfb2ea8474c52cffbb00b4be2d86ca2c7)
### Open slicing
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=De_Sitter_space&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Open slicing")]
Let 
x 0 = α sinh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) cosh ⁡ ξ , x 1 = α cosh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) , x i = α z i sinh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) sinh ⁡ ξ , 2 ≤ i ≤ n {\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}x_{0}&=\alpha \sinh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)\cosh \xi ,\\\x_{1}&=\alpha \cosh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right),\\\x_{i}&=\alpha z_{i}\sinh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)\sinh \xi ,\qquad 2\leq i\leq n\end{aligned}}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\begin{aligned}x_{0}&=\\alpha \\sinh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)\\cosh \\xi ,\\\\x_{1}&=\\alpha \\cosh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\),\\\\x_{i}&=\\alpha z_{i}\\sinh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)\\sinh \\xi ,\\qquad 2\\leq i\\leq n\\end{aligned}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/da32cf7fa2b73c3e5083336aa1766e27db198994)
where  ∑ i z i 2 = 1 {\textstyle \sum _{i}z_{i}^{2}=1} ![{\\textstyle \\sum _{i}z_{i}^{2}=1}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f185e0f49658021df9193388998da9db01beaca8) forming a  S n − 2 {\displaystyle S^{n-2}} ![{\\displaystyle S^{n-2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/9599199d8a358396c8f887c37aa926f026b0eb61) with the standard metric  ∑ i d z i 2 = d Ω n − 2 2 {\textstyle \sum _{i}dz_{i}^{2}=d\Omega _{n-2}^{2}} ![{\\textstyle \\sum _{i}dz_{i}^{2}=d\\Omega _{n-2}^{2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/337ee28147a28890c300700c46204f9a49d730a2). Then the metric of the de Sitter space reads 
d s 2 = − d t 2 + α 2 sinh 2 ⁡ ( 1 α t ) d H n − 1 2 , {\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+\alpha ^{2}\sinh ^{2}\left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)dH_{n-1}^{2},} ![{\\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+\\alpha ^{2}\\sinh ^{2}\\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)dH_{n-1}^{2},}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/d98268adb83c510a11971a202d348d9067b8a225)
where 
d H n − 1 2 = d ξ 2 + sinh 2 ⁡ ( ξ ) d Ω n − 2 2 {\displaystyle dH_{n-1}^{2}=d\xi ^{2}+\sinh ^{2}(\xi )d\Omega _{n-2}^{2}} ![{\\displaystyle dH_{n-1}^{2}=d\\xi ^{2}+\\sinh ^{2}\(\\xi \)d\\Omega _{n-2}^{2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f8b17a53842cde125476531343676d998a25b699)
is the standard hyperbolic metric. 
### Closed slicing
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=De_Sitter_space&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Closed slicing")]
Let 
x 0 = α sinh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) , x i = α cosh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) z i , 1 ≤ i ≤ n {\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}x_{0}&=\alpha \sinh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right),\\\x_{i}&=\alpha \cosh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)z_{i},\qquad 1\leq i\leq n\end{aligned}}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\begin{aligned}x_{0}&=\\alpha \\sinh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\),\\\\x_{i}&=\\alpha \\cosh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)z_{i},\\qquad 1\\leq i\\leq n\\end{aligned}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b9c47d0dfba421e730e34101b86abb003e06e614)
where  z i {\displaystyle z_{i}} ![{\\displaystyle z_{i}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5c6e920bac39ad09fff4efef16254595091a1025)s describe a  S n − 1 {\displaystyle S^{n-1}} ![{\\displaystyle S^{n-1}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/742bebb03fe630674b18823a59d2c75efd0066e7). Then the metric reads: 
d s 2 = − d t 2 + α 2 cosh 2 ⁡ ( 1 α t ) d Ω n − 1 2 . {\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+\alpha ^{2}\cosh ^{2}\left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)d\Omega _{n-1}^{2}.} ![{\\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+\\alpha ^{2}\\cosh ^{2}\\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)d\\Omega _{n-1}^{2}.}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/fe0e00852fb01ab49ba2a9ba942a0c0eb1c5a17b)
Changing the time variable to the conformal time via  tan ⁡ ( 1 2 η ) = tanh ⁡ ( 1 2 α t ) {\textstyle \tan \left({\frac {1}{2}}\eta \right)=\tanh \left({\frac {1}{2\alpha }}t\right)} ![{\\textstyle \\tan \\left\({\\frac {1}{2}}\\eta \\right\)=\\tanh \\left\({\\frac {1}{2\\alpha }}t\\right\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/2e5d47c90e54eb0815c32f5fef6dea3bdaf7f6cf) we obtain a metric conformally equivalent to Einstein static universe: 
d s 2 = α 2 cos 2 ⁡ η ( − d η 2 + d Ω n − 1 2 ) . {\displaystyle ds^{2}={\frac {\alpha ^{2}}{\cos ^{2}\eta }}\left(-d\eta ^{2}+d\Omega _{n-1}^{2}\right).} ![{\\displaystyle ds^{2}={\\frac {\\alpha ^{2}}{\\cos ^{2}\\eta }}\\left\(-d\\eta ^{2}+d\\Omega _{n-1}^{2}\\right\).}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/32266687ff58bb723adc898cd0baa1f92ff3e66f)
These coordinates, also known as "global coordinates" cover the maximal extension of de Sitter space, and can therefore be used to find its [Penrose diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_diagram "Penrose diagram").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Sitter_space#cite_note-5)
### dS slicing
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=De_Sitter_space&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: dS slicing")]
Let 
x 0 = α sin ⁡ ( 1 α χ ) sinh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) cosh ⁡ ξ , x 1 = α cos ⁡ ( 1 α χ ) , x 2 = α sin ⁡ ( 1 α χ ) cosh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) , x i = α z i sin ⁡ ( 1 α χ ) sinh ⁡ ( 1 α t ) sinh ⁡ ξ , 3 ≤ i ≤ n {\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}x_{0}&=\alpha \sin \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}\chi \right)\sinh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)\cosh \xi ,\\\x_{1}&=\alpha \cos \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}\chi \right),\\\x_{2}&=\alpha \sin \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}\chi \right)\cosh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right),\\\x_{i}&=\alpha z_{i}\sin \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}\chi \right)\sinh \left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)\sinh \xi ,\qquad 3\leq i\leq n\end{aligned}}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\begin{aligned}x_{0}&=\\alpha \\sin \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}\\chi \\right\)\\sinh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)\\cosh \\xi ,\\\\x_{1}&=\\alpha \\cos \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}\\chi \\right\),\\\\x_{2}&=\\alpha \\sin \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}\\chi \\right\)\\cosh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\),\\\\x_{i}&=\\alpha z_{i}\\sin \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}\\chi \\right\)\\sinh \\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)\\sinh \\xi ,\\qquad 3\\leq i\\leq n\\end{aligned}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/eaf0ea6478be4fcd7437df3ba797574578ca2435)
where  z i {\displaystyle z_{i}} ![{\\displaystyle z_{i}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5c6e920bac39ad09fff4efef16254595091a1025)s describe a  S n − 3 {\displaystyle S^{n-3}} ![{\\displaystyle S^{n-3}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/ce56b8f274f4f2bd34016bfd34d0938e314af323). Then the metric reads: 
d s 2 = d χ 2 + sin 2 ⁡ ( 1 α χ ) d s d S , α , n − 1 2 , {\displaystyle ds^{2}=d\chi ^{2}+\sin ^{2}\left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}\chi \right)ds_{dS,\alpha ,n-1}^{2},} ![{\\displaystyle ds^{2}=d\\chi ^{2}+\\sin ^{2}\\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}\\chi \\right\)ds_{dS,\\alpha ,n-1}^{2},}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b358741e354d21fa4d27ae81c5c122d2f8f43eea)
where 
d s d S , α , n − 1 2 = − d t 2 + α 2 sinh 2 ⁡ ( 1 α t ) d H n − 2 2 {\displaystyle ds_{dS,\alpha ,n-1}^{2}=-dt^{2}+\alpha ^{2}\sinh ^{2}\left({\frac {1}{\alpha }}t\right)dH_{n-2}^{2}} ![{\\displaystyle ds_{dS,\\alpha ,n-1}^{2}=-dt^{2}+\\alpha ^{2}\\sinh ^{2}\\left\({\\frac {1}{\\alpha }}t\\right\)dH_{n-2}^{2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/231af2b10c76d026a8f46fa5ecf7d4a6afd86967)
is the metric of an  n − 1 {\displaystyle n-1} ![{\\displaystyle n-1}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/fbd0b0f32b28f51962943ee9ede4fb34198a2521) dimensional de Sitter space with radius of curvature  α {\displaystyle \alpha } ![{\\displaystyle \\alpha }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b79333175c8b3f0840bfb4ec41b8072c83ea88d3) in open slicing coordinates. The hyperbolic metric is given by: 
d H n − 2 2 = d ξ 2 + sinh 2 ⁡ ( ξ ) d Ω n − 3 2 . {\displaystyle dH_{n-2}^{2}=d\xi ^{2}+\sinh ^{2}(\xi )d\Omega _{n-3}^{2}.} ![{\\displaystyle dH_{n-2}^{2}=d\\xi ^{2}+\\sinh ^{2}\(\\xi \)d\\Omega _{n-3}^{2}.}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/fdb06642a9baa8b6eedf440f4d78d2515bd44a11)
This is the analytic continuation of the open slicing coordinates under  ( t , ξ , θ , ϕ 1 , ϕ 2 , … , ϕ n − 3 ) → ( i χ , ξ , i t , θ , ϕ 1 , … , ϕ n − 4 ) {\displaystyle \left(t,\xi ,\theta ,\phi _{1},\phi _{2},\ldots ,\phi _{n-3}\right)\to \left(i\chi ,\xi ,it,\theta ,\phi _{1},\ldots ,\phi _{n-4}\right)} ![{\\displaystyle \\left\(t,\\xi ,\\theta ,\\phi _{1},\\phi _{2},\\ldots ,\\phi _{n-3}\\right\)\\to \\left\(i\\chi ,\\xi ,it,\\theta ,\\phi _{1},\\ldots ,\\phi _{n-4}\\right\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/6234f5675a2574a540a6dfdac6bc1bf58442471f) and also switching  x 0 {\displaystyle x_{0}} ![{\\displaystyle x_{0}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/86f21d0e31751534cd6584264ecf864a6aa792cf) and  x 2 {\displaystyle x_{2}} ![{\\displaystyle x_{2}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/d7af1b928f06e4c7e3e8ebfd60704656719bd766) because they change their timelike/spacelike nature.
