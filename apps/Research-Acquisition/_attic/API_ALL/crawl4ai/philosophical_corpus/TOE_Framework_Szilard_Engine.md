Relationship between the concepts of thermodynamic entropy and information entropy
Because the mathematical expressions for [information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theory "Information theory") developed by [Claude Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Shannon "Claude Shannon") and [Ralph Hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Hartley "Ralph Hartley") in the 1940s are similar to the mathematics of [statistical thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_thermodynamics "Statistical thermodynamics") worked out by [Ludwig Boltzmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Boltzmann "Ludwig Boltzmann") and [J. Willard Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Willard_Gibbs "J. Willard Gibbs") in the 1870s, in which the concept of [entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy "Entropy") is central, Shannon was persuaded to employ the same term 'entropy' for his measure of uncertainty. [Information entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_entropy "Information entropy") is often presumed to be equivalent to [physical (thermodynamic) entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_\(classical_thermodynamics\) "Entropy \(classical thermodynamics\)"). 
## Equivalence of form of the defining expressions
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Equivalence of form of the defining expressions")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Zentralfriedhof_Vienna_-_Boltzmann.JPG/250px-Zentralfriedhof_Vienna_-_Boltzmann.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Zentralfriedhof_Vienna_-_Boltzmann.JPG)Boltzmann's grave in the [Zentralfriedhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zentralfriedhof "Zentralfriedhof"), Vienna, with bust and entropy formula
The defining expression for [entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy "Entropy") in the theory of [statistical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics") established by [Ludwig Boltzmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Boltzmann "Ludwig Boltzmann") and [J. Willard Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Willard_Gibbs "J. Willard Gibbs") in the 1870s, is of the form:       S = − k B ∑ i p i ln ⁡ p i , {\displaystyle S=-k_{\text{B}}\sum _{i}p_{i}\ln p_{i},} ![{\\displaystyle S=-k_{\\text{B}}\\sum _{i}p_{i}\\ln p_{i},}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5f8d9813965de12a0f8c60af223b458d8265cf22)
where  p i {\displaystyle p_{i}} ![{\\displaystyle p_{i}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5bab39399bf5424f25d957cdc57c84a0622626d2) is the probability of the [microstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstate_\(statistical_mechanics\) "Microstate \(statistical mechanics\)") _i_ taken from an [equilibrium ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_\(mathematical_physics\) "Ensemble \(mathematical physics\)"), and  k B {\displaystyle k_{B}} ![{\\displaystyle k_{B}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/70f38f7b73e53fd7b5d9ca64bec3a1438cc0eade) is the [Boltzmann constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_constant "Boltzmann constant"). 
The defining expression for [entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_entropy "Information entropy") in the theory of [information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theory "Information theory") established by [Claude E. Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon "Claude E. Shannon") in 1948 is of the form:       H = − ∑ i p i log b ⁡ p i , {\displaystyle H=-\sum _{i}p_{i}\log _{b}p_{i},} ![{\\displaystyle H=-\\sum _{i}p_{i}\\log _{b}p_{i},}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/25ccd149289bc5f4cb8de7457aa5edefaf6ef718)
where  p i {\displaystyle p_{i}} ![{\\displaystyle p_{i}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5bab39399bf5424f25d957cdc57c84a0622626d2) is the probability of the message  m i {\displaystyle m_{i}} ![{\\displaystyle m_{i}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/95ec8e804f69706d3f5ad235f4f983220c8df7c2) taken from the message space _M_ , and _b_ is the [base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_of_a_logarithm "Base of a logarithm") of the [logarithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithm "Logarithm") used. Common values of _b_ are 2, [Euler's number _e_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_\(mathematical_constant\) "E \(mathematical constant\)"), and 10, and the unit of entropy is [shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_\(unit\) "Shannon \(unit\)") (or [bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit "Bit")) for _b_ = 2, [nat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nat_\(unit\) "Nat \(unit\)") for _b_ =  _e_ , and [hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartley_\(unit\) "Hartley \(unit\)") for _b_ = 10.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-1)
Mathematically _H_ may also be seen as an average information, taken over the message space, because when a certain message occurs with probability _p_ _i_ , the information quantity −log(_p_ _i_) (called [information content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_content "Information content") or self-information) will be obtained. 
If all the microstates are equiprobable (a [microcanonical ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcanonical_ensemble "Microcanonical ensemble")), the statistical thermodynamic entropy reduces to the form, as given by Boltzmann,       S = k B ln ⁡ W , {\displaystyle S=k_{\text{B}}\ln W,} ![{\\displaystyle S=k_{\\text{B}}\\ln W,}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f2faa4eb8445a3510b8596e237c077f738e95f76)
where _W_ is the number of microstates that corresponds to the **macroscopic** thermodynamic state. Therefore S depends on temperature. 
If all the messages are equiprobable, the information entropy reduces to the [Hartley entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartley_entropy "Hartley entropy")      H = log b ⁡ | M | , {\displaystyle H=\log _{b}|M|\ ,} ![{\\displaystyle H=\\log _{b}|M|\\ ,}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/a91887e3179b0ec76043a2cc4e13dc4eb884ec99)
where  | M | {\displaystyle |M|} ![{\\displaystyle |M|}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/061c869574b5af78907f9f2b841fe84ea876e515) is the [cardinality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinality "Cardinality") of the message space _M_. 
The logarithm in the thermodynamic definition is the [natural logarithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_logarithm "Natural logarithm"). It can be shown that the [Gibbs entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_\(statistical_thermodynamics\) "Entropy \(statistical thermodynamics\)") formula, with the natural logarithm, reproduces all of the properties of the macroscopic [classical thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_thermodynamics "Classical thermodynamics") of [Rudolf Clausius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Clausius "Rudolf Clausius"). (See article: [Entropy (statistical views)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_\(statistical_views\) "Entropy \(statistical views\)")). 
The [logarithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithm "Logarithm") can also be taken to the natural base in the case of information entropy. This is equivalent to choosing to measure information in nats instead of the usual [bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit "Bit") (or more formally, shannons). In practice, information entropy is almost always calculated using base-2 logarithms, but this distinction amounts to nothing other than a change in units. One nat is about 1.44 shannons. 
For a simple compressible system that can only perform volume work, the [first law of thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_law_of_thermodynamics "First law of thermodynamics") becomes       d E = − p d V + T d S . {\displaystyle dE=-pdV+TdS.} ![{\\displaystyle dE=-pdV+TdS.}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/c10c3b8c4f49633cde0f9a16784d84eb42a72956)
But one can equally well write this equation in terms of what physicists and chemists sometimes call the 'reduced' or dimensionless entropy, _σ_ = _S_ /_k_ , so that       d E = − p d V + k B T d σ . {\displaystyle dE=-pdV+k_{\text{B}}Td\sigma .} ![{\\displaystyle dE=-pdV+k_{\\text{B}}Td\\sigma .}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/142774d1c2ebe7c0a865a49ec8a4dd257b207feb)
Just as _S_ is conjugate to _T_ , so _σ_ is conjugate to _k_ B _T_ (the energy that is characteristic of _T_ on a molecular scale). 
Thus the definitions of entropy in statistical mechanics (The [ Gibbs entropy formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_\(statistical_thermodynamics\)#Gibbs_entropy_formula "Entropy \(statistical thermodynamics\)") S = − k B ∑ i p i log ⁡ p i {\displaystyle S=-k_{\mathrm {B} }\sum _{i}p_{i}\log p_{i}} ![{\\displaystyle S=-k_{\\mathrm {B} }\\sum _{i}p_{i}\\log p_{i}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/6f26f2f90291743460b57559472c94a4a154cf7b)) and in classical thermodynamics ( d S = δ Q rev T {\displaystyle dS={\frac {\delta Q_{\text{rev}}}{T}}} ![{\\displaystyle dS={\\frac {\\delta Q_{\\text{rev}}}{T}}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/d0da4b310ff00bb37b716ac167074324383046fb), and the [fundamental thermodynamic relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_thermodynamic_relation "Fundamental thermodynamic relation")) are equivalent for [microcanonical ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcanonical_ensemble "Microcanonical ensemble"), and statistical ensembles describing a thermodynamic system in equilibrium with a reservoir, such as the [canonical ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_ensemble "Canonical ensemble"), [grand canonical ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_canonical_ensemble "Grand canonical ensemble"), [isothermal–isobaric ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isothermal%E2%80%93isobaric_ensemble "Isothermal–isobaric ensemble"). This equivalence is commonly shown in textbooks. However, the equivalence between the thermodynamic definition of entropy and the [ Gibbs entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_\(statistical_thermodynamics\)#Gibbs_entropy_formula "Entropy \(statistical thermodynamics\)") is not general but instead an exclusive property of the [generalized Boltzmann distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_distribution#Generalized_Boltzmann_distribution "Boltzmann distribution").[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-2)
Furthermore, it has been shown that the definitions of entropy in statistical mechanics is the only entropy that is equivalent to the classical thermodynamics entropy under the following postulates:[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-Gao2022-3)
  1. The probability density function is proportional to some function of the ensemble parameters and random variables.
  2. Thermodynamic state functions are described by ensemble averages of random variables.
  3. At infinite temperature, all the microstates have the same probability.


## Theoretical relationship
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Theoretical relationship")]
Despite the foregoing, there is a difference between the two quantities. The [information entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_entropy "Information entropy") _Η_ can be calculated for _any_ [probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_distribution "Probability distribution") (if the "message" is taken to be that the event _i_ which had probability _p i_ occurred, out of the space of the events possible), while the [thermodynamic entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy "Entropy") _S_ refers to thermodynamic probabilities _p i_ specifically. The difference is more theoretical than actual, however, because any probability distribution can be approximated arbitrarily closely by some thermodynamic system.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
Moreover, a direct connection can be made between the two. If the probabilities in question are the thermodynamic probabilities _p i_: the (reduced) [Gibbs entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_entropy "Gibbs entropy") _σ_ can then be seen as simply the amount of Shannon information needed to define the detailed microscopic state of the system, given its macroscopic description. Or, in the words of [G. N. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._N._Lewis "G. N. Lewis") writing about chemical entropy in 1930, "Gain in entropy always means loss of information, and nothing more". To be more concrete, in the discrete case using base two logarithms, the reduced Gibbs entropy is equal to the average of the minimum number of yes–no questions needed to be answered in order to fully specify the [microstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstate_\(statistical_mechanics\) "Microstate \(statistical mechanics\)"), given that we know the macrostate. 
Furthermore, the prescription to find the equilibrium distributions of statistical mechanics—such as the Boltzmann distribution—by maximising the Gibbs entropy subject to appropriate constraints (the [Gibbs algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_algorithm "Gibbs algorithm")) can be seen as something not unique to thermodynamics, but as a principle of general relevance in statistical inference, if it is desired to find a [maximally uninformative probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_maximum_entropy "Principle of maximum entropy"), subject to certain constraints on its averages. (These perspectives are explored further in the article [Maximum entropy thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_entropy_thermodynamics "Maximum entropy thermodynamics").) 
The Shannon entropy in information theory is sometimes expressed in units of bits per symbol. The physical entropy may be on a "per quantity" basis (_h_) which is called "[intensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensive_and_extensive_properties "Intensive and extensive properties")" entropy instead of the usual total entropy which is called "extensive" entropy. The "shannons" of a message (_Η_) are its total "extensive" information entropy and is _h_ times the number of bits in the message. 
A direct and physically real relationship between _h_ and _S_ can be found by assigning a symbol to each microstate that occurs per mole, kilogram, volume, or particle of a homogeneous substance, then calculating the 'h' of these symbols. By theory or by observation, the symbols (microstates) will occur with different probabilities and this will determine _h_. If there are N moles, kilograms, volumes, or particles of the unit substance, the relationship between _h_ (in bits per unit substance) and physical extensive entropy in nats is:       S = k B ln ⁡ ( 2 ) N h {\displaystyle S=k_{\mathrm {B} }\ln(2)Nh} ![{\\displaystyle S=k_{\\mathrm {B} }\\ln\(2\)Nh}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/c10cc3654b0e1169e7d70ae1f36f5a55e60d6953)
where ln(2) is the conversion factor from base 2 of Shannon entropy to the natural base e of physical entropy. _N h_ is the amount of information in bits needed to describe the state of a physical system with entropy _S_. [Landauer's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landauer%27s_principle "Landauer's principle") demonstrates the reality of this by stating the minimum energy _E_ required (and therefore heat _Q_ generated) by an ideally efficient memory change or logic operation by irreversibly erasing or merging _N h_ bits of information will be _S_ times the temperature which is       E = Q = T k B ln ⁡ ( 2 ) N h , {\displaystyle E=Q=Tk_{\mathrm {B} }\ln(2)Nh,} ![{\\displaystyle E=Q=Tk_{\\mathrm {B} }\\ln\(2\)Nh,}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/92eef002636b73ded52eb29ebf68905df5cb62f4)
where _h_ is in informational bits and _E_ and _Q_ are in physical Joules. This has been experimentally confirmed.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-berut-4)
Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy per particle in an ideal gas (kelvins = ⁠2/3⁠ joules/_k_ B) so the J/K units of _k_ B is dimensionless (joule/joule). _k_ b is the conversion factor from energy in ⁠3/2⁠ kelvins to joules for an ideal gas. If kinetic energy measurements per particle of an ideal gas were expressed as joules instead of kelvins, _k_ b in the above equations would be replaced by 3/2. This shows that _S_ is a true statistical measure of microstates that does not have a fundamental physical unit other than the units of information, in this case nats, which is just a statement of which logarithm base was chosen by convention.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/NAtomVacuumPumpMemory.png/250px-NAtomVacuumPumpMemory.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:NAtomVacuumPumpMemory.png)N-atom engine schematic
A physical [thought experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought_experiment "Thought experiment") demonstrating how just the possession of information might in principle have thermodynamic consequences was established in 1929 by [Leó Szilárd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3_Szil%C3%A1rd "Leó Szilárd"), in a refinement of the famous [Maxwell's demon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell%27s_demon "Maxwell's demon") scenario[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-5) (and a reversal of the [Joule expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_expansion "Joule expansion") thought experiment). 
Consider Maxwell's set-up, but with only a single gas particle in a box. If the demon knows which half of the box the particle is in (equivalent to a single bit of information), it can close a shutter between the two halves of the box, close a piston unopposed into the empty half of the box, and then extract  k B T ln ⁡ 2 {\displaystyle k_{\text{B}}T\ln 2} ![{\\displaystyle k_{\\text{B}}T\\ln 2}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/f9db00924c50f0fb849b68ad39afaded430279e7) joules of useful work if the shutter is opened again. The particle can then be left to isothermally expand back to its original equilibrium occupied volume. In just the right circumstances therefore, the possession of a single bit of Shannon information (a single bit of [negentropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negentropy "Negentropy") in Brillouin's term) really does correspond to a reduction in the entropy of the physical system. The global entropy is not decreased, but information to free energy conversion is possible. 
This thought experiment has been physically demonstrated, using a [phase-contrast microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase-contrast_microscopy "Phase-contrast microscopy") equipped with a high speed camera connected to a computer, acting as the _demon_.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-6) In this experiment, information to energy conversion is performed on a [Brownian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownian_motion "Brownian motion") particle by means of _feedback control_ ; that is, synchronizing the work given to the particle with the information obtained on its position. Computing energy balances for different feedback protocols, has confirmed that the [Jarzynski equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarzynski_equality "Jarzynski equality") requires a generalization that accounts for the amount of information involved in the feedback. 
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Landauer's principle")]
Main article: [Landauer's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landauer%27s_principle "Landauer's principle")
In fact one can generalise: any information that has a physical representation must somehow be embedded in the statistical mechanical degrees of freedom of a physical system. 
Thus, [Rolf Landauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Landauer "Rolf Landauer") argued in 1961, if one were to imagine starting with those degrees of freedom in a thermalised state, there would be a real reduction in thermodynamic entropy if they were then re-set to a known state. This can only be achieved under information-preserving microscopically deterministic dynamics if the uncertainty is somehow dumped somewhere else – i.e. if the entropy of the environment (or the non information-bearing degrees of freedom) is increased by at least an equivalent amount, as required by the Second Law, by gaining an appropriate quantity of heat: specifically _kT_ ln(2) of heat for every 1 bit of randomness erased. 
On the other hand, Landauer argued, there is no thermodynamic objection to a logically reversible operation potentially being achieved in a physically reversible way in the system. It is only logically irreversible operations – for example, the erasing of a bit to a known state, or the merging of two computation paths – which must be accompanied by a corresponding entropy increase. When information is physical, all processing of its representations, i.e. generation, encoding, transmission, decoding and interpretation, are natural processes where entropy increases by consumption of free energy.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-7)
Applied to the Maxwell's demon/Szilard engine scenario, this suggests that it might be possible to "read" the state of the particle into a computing apparatus with no entropy cost; but _only_ if the apparatus has already been SET into a known state, rather than being in a thermalised state of uncertainty. To SET (or RESET) the apparatus into this state will cost all the entropy that can be saved by knowing the state of Szilard's particle. 
In 2008 and 2009, researchers showed that Landauer's principle can be derived from the [second law of thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_law_of_thermodynamics "Second law of thermodynamics") and the entropy change associated with information gain, developing the thermodynamics of quantum and classical feedback-controlled systems.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-8)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-9)
## Negentropy
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Negentropy")]
Main article: [Negentropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negentropy "Negentropy")
Shannon entropy has been related by physicist [Léon Brillouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9on_Brillouin "Léon Brillouin") to a concept sometimes called [negentropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negentropy "Negentropy"). In 1953, Brillouin derived a general equation[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-10) stating that the changing of an information bit value requires at least _kT_ ln(2) energy. This is the same energy as the work [Leo Szilard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Szilard "Leo Szilard")'s engine produces in the idealistic case, which in turn equals the same quantity found by [Landauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Landauer "Rolf Landauer"). In his book,[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-11) he further explored this problem concluding that any cause of a bit value change (measurement, decision about a yes/no question, erasure, display, etc.) will require the same amount, _kT_ ln(2), of energy. Consequently, acquiring information about a system's microstates is associated with an [entropy production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_production "Entropy production"), while erasure yields entropy production only when the bit value is changing. Setting up a bit of information in a sub-system originally in thermal equilibrium results in a local entropy reduction. However, there is no violation of the second law of thermodynamics, according to Brillouin, since a reduction in any local system's thermodynamic entropy results in an increase in thermodynamic entropy elsewhere. In this way, Brillouin clarified the meaning of negentropy which was considered as controversial because its earlier understanding can yield Carnot efficiency higher than one. Additionally, the relationship between energy and information formulated by Brillouin has been proposed as a connection between the amount of bits that the brain processes and the energy it consumes: Collell and Fauquet [[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-12) argued that De Castro [[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-13) analytically found the Landauer limit as the thermodynamic lower bound for brain computations. However, even though evolution is supposed to have "selected" the most energetically efficient processes, the physical lower bounds are not realistic quantities in the brain. Firstly, because the minimum processing unit considered in physics is the atom/molecule, which is distant from the actual way that brain operates; and, secondly, because neural networks incorporate important redundancy and noise factors that greatly reduce their efficiency.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-14) Laughlin et al.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-15) was the first to provide explicit quantities for the energetic cost of processing sensory information. Their findings in blowflies revealed that for visual sensory data, the cost of transmitting one bit of information is around 5 × 10−14 Joules, or equivalently 104 ATP molecules. Thus, neural processing efficiency is still far from Landauer's limit of _kT_ ln(2) J, but as a curious fact, it is still much more efficient than modern computers. 
In 2009, Mahulikar & Herwig redefined thermodynamic negentropy as the specific entropy deficit of the dynamically ordered sub-system relative to its surroundings.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-16) This definition enabled the formulation of the _Negentropy Principle_ , which is mathematically shown to follow from the 2nd Law of Thermodynamics, during order existence. 
## Quantum theory
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Quantum theory")]
See also: [Holographic principle § Energy, matter, and information equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holographic_principle#Energy,_matter,_and_information_equivalence "Holographic principle"); and [Quantum relative entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_relative_entropy "Quantum relative entropy")
Hirschman showed,[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-Hirschman-17) cf. [Hirschman uncertainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirschman_uncertainty "Hirschman uncertainty"), that [Heisenberg's uncertainty principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg%27s_uncertainty_principle "Heisenberg's uncertainty principle") can be expressed as a particular lower bound on the sum of the classical distribution entropies of the _quantum observable_ probability distributions of a quantum mechanical state, the square of the wave-function, in coordinate, and also momentum space, when expressed in [Planck units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_units "Planck units"). The resulting inequalities provide a tighter bound on the uncertainty relations of Heisenberg. 
It is meaningful to assign a "[joint entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_entropy "Joint entropy")", because positions and momenta are quantum conjugate variables and are therefore not jointly observable. Mathematically, they have to be treated as [joint distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_distribution "Joint distribution"). Note that this joint entropy is not equivalent to the [Von Neumann entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_entropy "Von Neumann entropy"), −Tr _ρ_ ln _ρ_ = −⟨ln _ρ_ ⟩. Hirschman's entropy is said to account for the _full information content of a mixture of quantum states_.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-18)
(Dissatisfaction with the Von Neumann entropy from quantum information points of view has been expressed by Stotland, Pomeransky, Bachmat and Cohen, who have introduced a yet different definition of entropy that reflects the inherent uncertainty of quantum mechanical states. This definition allows distinction between the minimum uncertainty entropy of pure states, and the excess statistical entropy of mixtures.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_in_thermodynamics_and_information_theory#cite_note-19))
