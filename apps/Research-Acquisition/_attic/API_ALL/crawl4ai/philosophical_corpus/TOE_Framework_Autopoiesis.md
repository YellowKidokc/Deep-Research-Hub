System capable of producing itself
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/3D-SIM-4_Anaphase_3_color.jpg/250px-3D-SIM-4_Anaphase_3_color.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:3D-SIM-4_Anaphase_3_color.jpg)3D representation of a living cell during the process of [mitosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitosis "Mitosis"), example of an autopoietic system
The term **autopoiesis** (from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") _(auto)_ 'self' and _([poiesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poiesis "Poiesis"))_ 'creation, production'), one of several current theories of life, refers to a [system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System "System") capable of producing and maintaining itself by creating its own parts.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-1) The term was introduced in the 1972 publication _[Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis_and_Cognition:_The_Realization_of_the_Living "Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living")_ by Chilean biologists [Humberto Maturana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humberto_Maturana "Humberto Maturana") and [Francisco Varela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Varela "Francisco Varela") to define the self-maintaining [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") of living [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cells_\(biology\) "Cells \(biology\)").[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Maturana_Varela_1972-2)
The concept has since been applied to the fields of [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition"), [neurobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience "Neuroscience"), [systems theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_theory "Systems theory"), [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture "Architecture") and [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology "Sociology"). [Niklas Luhmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niklas_Luhmann "Niklas Luhmann") briefly introduced the concept of autopoiesis to [organizational theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizational_theory "Organizational theory").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-3)
## Overview
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autopoiesis&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Overview")]
Maturana describes how he invented the word _autopoiesis_ :[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Maturana_Varela_1980-4): xvii  [[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-5)
> "... one day, while talking with a friend (José Bulnes) about an essay of his on Don Quixote de la Mancha, in which he analyzed Don Quixote's dilemma of whether to follow the path of arms (_praxis_ , action) or the path of letters (_poiesis_ , creation, production), and his eventual choice of the path of _praxis_ deferring any attempt at _poiesis_ , I understood for the first time the power of the word 'poiesis' and invented the word that we needed: _autopoiesis_. This was a word without a history, a word that could directly mean what takes place in the dynamics of the autonomy proper to living systems." 
— Humberto Maturana, _[Autopoiesis and Cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis_and_Cognition:_The_Realization_of_the_Living "Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living")_ , _Introduction_ , [page xvii](https://www.google.com/books/edition/Autopoiesis_and_Cognition/nVmcN9Ja68kC?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=don%20quixote&pg=PR17&printsec=frontcover)
Maturana and Varela explain that:[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Maturana_Varela_1980-4): 78 
> "An autopoietic machine is a machine organized (defined as a unity) as a network of processes of production (transformation and destruction) of components which: (i) through their interactions and transformations continuously regenerate and realize the network of processes (relations) that produced them; and (ii) constitute it (the machine) as a concrete unity in space in which they (the components) exist by specifying the topological domain of its realization as such a network."
They describe the "space defined by an autopoietic system" as "self-contained", a space that "cannot be described by using dimensions that define another space. When we refer to our interactions with a concrete autopoietic system, however, we project this system on the space of our manipulations and make a description of this projection."[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Maturana_Varela_1980-4): 89 
## Meaning
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autopoiesis&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Meaning")]
Autopoiesis was originally presented as a system description that was said to define and explain the nature of [living systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_systems "Living systems"). A canonical example of an autopoietic system is the [biological cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_cell "Biological cell"). The [eukaryotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote") cell, for example, is made of various [biochemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemical "Biochemical") components such as [nucleic acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleic_acid "Nucleic acid") and [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"), and is organized into bounded structures such as the [cell nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus"), various [organelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organelle "Organelle"), a [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane") and [cytoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoskeleton "Cytoskeleton"). These structures, based on an internal flow of molecules and energy, _produce_ the components which, in turn, continue to maintain the organized bounded structure that gives rise to these components. 
An autopoietic system is to be contrasted with an [allopoietic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allopoietic "Allopoietic") system, such as a car factory, which uses raw materials (components) to generate a car (an organized structure) which is something _other_ than itself (the factory). However, if the system is extended from the factory to include components in the factory's "environment", such as supply chains, plant / equipment, workers, dealerships, customers, contracts, competitors, cars, spare parts, and so on, then as a total viable system it could be considered to be autopoietic.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-6)
Autopoiesis in biological systems can be viewed as a network of constraints that work to maintain themselves. This concept has been called organizational closure[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-7) or constraint closure[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-8) and is closely related to the study of [autocatalytic chemical networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocatalysis "Autocatalysis") where constraints are reactions required to sustain life. 
Though others have often used the term as a synonym for [self-organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organization "Self-organization"), Maturana himself stated he would "[n]ever use the notion of self-organization ... Operationally it is impossible. That is, if the organization of a thing changes, the thing changes".[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-9) Moreover, an autopoietic system is autonomous and operationally closed, in the sense that there are sufficient processes within it to maintain the whole. Autopoietic systems are "structurally coupled" with their medium, embedded in a dynamic of changes that can be recalled as [sensory-motor coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensory-motor_coupling "Sensory-motor coupling").[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-10) This continuous dynamic is considered as a rudimentary form of [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge "Knowledge") or [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition") and can be observed throughout life-forms. 
An application of the concept of autopoiesis to [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology "Sociology") can be found in Niklas Luhmann's [Systems Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_theory#Sociology_and_Sociocybernetics "Systems theory"), which was subsequently adapted by [Bob Jessop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Jessop "Bob Jessop") in his studies of the capitalist state system. [Marjatta Maula](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Marjatta_Maula&action=edit&redlink=1 "Marjatta Maula \(page does not exist\)") adapted the concept of autopoiesis in a business context.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-11) The theory of autopoiesis has also been applied in the context of legal systems by not only Niklas Luhmann, but also Gunther Teubner.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-13) [Patrik Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrik_Schumacher "Patrik Schumacher") has applied the term to refer to the 'discursive self-referential making of architecture.' [[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-14)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-15) Varela eventually further applied autopoesis to develop models of mind, brain, and behavior called non-[representationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representationalist "Representationalist"), [enactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enactivism "Enactivism"), [embodied cognitive neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embodied_cognitive_science "Embodied cognitive science"), culminating in [neurophenomenology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurophenomenology "Neurophenomenology"). 
In the context of textual studies, [Jerome McGann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_McGann "Jerome McGann") argues that texts are "autopoietic mechanisms operating as self-generating feedback systems that cannot be separated from those who manipulate and use them".[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-16) Citing Maturana and Varela, he defines an autopoietic system as "a closed topological space that 'continuously generates and specifies its own organization through its operation as a system of production of its own components, and does this in an endless turnover of components'", concluding that "Autopoietic systems are thus distinguished from allopoietic systems, which are Cartesian and which 'have as the product of their functioning something different from themselves'". Coding and markup appear [allopoietic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allopoietic "Allopoietic")", McGann argues, but are generative parts of the system they serve to maintain, and thus language and print or electronic technology are autopoietic systems.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-17): 200–1 
The philosopher [Slavoj Žižek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavoj_%C5%BDi%C5%BEek "Slavoj Žižek"), in his discussion of [Hegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Wilhelm_Friedrich_Hegel "Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel"), argues: 
> "Hegel is – to use today's terms – the ultimate thinker of autopoiesis, of the process of the emergence of necessary features out of chaotic contingency, the thinker of contingency's gradual self-organisation, of the gradual rise of order out of chaos."[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-18)
## Relation to complexity
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autopoiesis&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Relation to complexity")]
Autopoiesis can be defined as the ratio between the complexity of a system and the complexity of its environment.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Fern%C3%A1ndez-19)
> This generalized view of autopoiesis considers systems as self-producing not in terms of their physical components, but in terms of its organization, which can be measured in terms of information and complexity. In other words, we can describe autopoietic systems as those producing more of their own complexity than the one produced by their environment.
— Carlos Gershenson, "Requisite Variety, Autopoiesis, and Self-organization" [[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Gershenson-20)
Autopoiesis has been proposed as a potential mechanism of [abiogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abiogenesis "Abiogenesis"), by which molecules evolved into more complex cells that could support the development of life.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-frontiers-21)
### Comparison with other theories of life
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autopoiesis&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Comparison with other theories of life")]
Autopoiesis is just one of several current theories of life, including the [chemoton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemoton "Chemoton")[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-gantibook-22) of [Tibor Gánti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibor_G%C3%A1nti "Tibor Gánti"), the [hypercycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercycle_\(chemistry\) "Hypercycle \(chemistry\)") of [Manfred Eigen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Eigen "Manfred Eigen") and [Peter Schuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Schuster_\(theoretical_chemist\) "Peter Schuster \(theoretical chemist\)"),[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-23) [[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-24) [[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-25) the [ (_M,R_) systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rosen_\(theoretical_biologist\)#Complexity_and_complex_scientific_models:_\(M,R\)_systems "Robert Rosen \(theoretical biologist\)")[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-26)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-27) of [Robert Rosen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rosen_\(theoretical_biologist\) "Robert Rosen \(theoretical biologist\)"), and the [autocatalytic sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocatalytic_set "Autocatalytic set")[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-28) of [Stuart Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Kauffman "Stuart Kauffman"), similar to an earlier proposal by [Freeman Dyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeman_Dyson "Freeman Dyson").[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-29) All of these (including autopoiesis) found their original inspiration in Erwin Schrödinger's book _[What is Life?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Is_Life%3F "What Is Life?")_[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-30) but at first they appear to have little in common with one another, largely because the authors did not communicate with one another, and none of them made any reference in their principal publications to any of the other theories. Nonetheless, there are more similarities than may be obvious at first sight, for example between Gánti and Rosen.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-31) Until recently[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-32)[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-33)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-34) there have been almost no attempts to compare the different theories and discuss them together. 
## Relation to cognition
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autopoiesis&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Relation to cognition")]
An extensive discussion of the connection of autopoiesis to [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition") is provided by Evan Thompson in his 2007 publication, _Mind in Life_.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Thompson_2007_auto-35) The basic notion of autopoiesis as involving constructive interaction with the environment is extended to include cognition. Initially, Maturana defined cognition as behavior of an organism "with relevance to the maintenance of itself".[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Maturana_Varela_1980_chapter-36): 13  However, computer models that are self-maintaining but non-cognitive have been devised, so some additional restrictions are needed, and the suggestion is that the maintenance process, to be cognitive, involves readjustment of the internal workings of the system in some [metabolic process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism "Metabolism"). On this basis it is claimed that autopoiesis is a necessary but not a sufficient condition for cognition.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Bitbol-37) Thompson wrote that this distinction may or may not be fruitful, but what matters is that living systems involve autopoiesis and (if it is necessary to add this point) cognition as well.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Thompson_2007-38): 127  It can be noted that this definition of 'cognition' is restricted, and does not necessarily entail any awareness or [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness "Consciousness") by the living system. With the publication of The Embodied Mind in 1991, Varela, Thompson and Rosch applied autopoesis to make non-[representationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representationalist "Representationalist"), and [enactive models of mind, brain and behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enactivism "Enactivism"), which further developed [embodied cognitive neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embodied_cognitive_science "Embodied cognitive science"), later culminating in [neurophenomenology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurophenomenology "Neurophenomenology"). 
## Relation to consciousness
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autopoiesis&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Relation to consciousness")]
The connection of autopoiesis to cognition, or if necessary, of living systems to cognition, is an objective assessment ascertainable by observation of a living system. 
One question that arises is about the connection between cognition seen in this manner and consciousness. The separation of cognition and consciousness recognizes that the organism may be unaware of the substratum where decisions are made. What is the connection between these realms? Thompson refers to this issue as the "[explanatory gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explanatory_gap "Explanatory gap")", and one aspect of it is the [hard problem of consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_problem_of_consciousness "Hard problem of consciousness"), how and why we have [qualia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualia "Qualia").[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Thompson_2007_cog-39)
A second question is whether autopoiesis can provide a bridge between these concepts. Thompson discusses this issue from the standpoint of [enactivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enactivism "Enactivism"). An autopoietic cell actively relates to its environment. Its sensory responses trigger motor behavior governed by autopoiesis, and this behavior (it is claimed) is a simplified version of a nervous system behavior. The further claim is that real-time interactions like this require attention, and an implication of attention is awareness.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Thompson_2007_sensor-40)
## Criticism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autopoiesis&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Criticism")]
There are multiple criticisms of the use of the term in both its original context, as an attempt to define and explain the living, and its various expanded usages, such as applying it to self-organizing systems in general or social systems in particular.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-41) Critics have argued that the concept and its theory fail to define or explain living systems and that, because of the extreme language of [self-referentiality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-referential "Self-referential") it uses without any external reference, it is really an attempt to give substantiation to Maturana's radical [constructivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivist_epistemology "Constructivist epistemology") or [solipsistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solipsistic "Solipsistic") [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology "Epistemology"),[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-42) or what [Danilo Zolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Danilo_Zolo&action=edit&redlink=1 "Danilo Zolo \(page does not exist\)")[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-43)[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-Wolfe-44) has called instead a "desolate theology". An example is the assertion by Maturana and Varela that "We do not see what we do not see and what we do not see does not exist".[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-45)
According to Razeto-Barry, the influence of _Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living_ in mainstream biology has proven to be limited. Razeto-Barry believes that autopoiesis is not commonly used as the criterion for life.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-:2-46)
Zoologist and philosopher [Donna Haraway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Haraway "Donna Haraway") also criticizes the usage of the term, arguing that "nothing makes itself; nothing is really autopoietic or self-organizing",[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis#cite_note-47) and suggests the use of _sympoiesis_ , meaning "making-with", instead.
