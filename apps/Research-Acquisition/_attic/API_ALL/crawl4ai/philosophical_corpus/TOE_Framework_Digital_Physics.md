From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Idea that the universe is a digital computation device
Not to be confused with [Computational physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_physics "Computational physics") or [Computational philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_philosophy "Computational philosophy").
**Digital physics** is a speculative idea suggesting that the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe "Universe") can be conceived of as a vast, digital computation device, or as the output of a [deterministic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic "Deterministic") or [probabilistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic "Probabilistic") [computer program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program "Computer program").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-:0-1) The hypothesis that the universe is a [digital computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_computer "Digital computer") was proposed by [Konrad Zuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Zuse "Konrad Zuse") in his 1969 book _[Rechnender Raum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rechnender_Raum "Rechnender Raum")_[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-2) (_Calculating-space_).[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-3) The term "digital physics" was coined in 1978 by [Edward Fredkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Fredkin "Edward Fredkin"),[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-4) who later came to prefer the term "**digital philosophy** ".[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-5) Fredkin taught a graduate course called "digital physics" at MIT in 1978, and collaborated with [Tommaso Toffoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommaso_Toffoli "Tommaso Toffoli") on "conservative logic" while [Norman Margolus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Margolus "Norman Margolus") served as a graduate student in his research group.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-6)
_Digital physics_ posits that there exists, at least in principle, a [program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program "Computer program") for a [universal computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_computer "Universal computer") that computes the [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_evolution "Cosmic evolution") of the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe "Universe"). The computer could be, for example, a huge [cellular automaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automaton "Cellular automaton").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-:0-1)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-7) It is deeply connected to the concept of [information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theory "Information theory"), particularly the idea that the universe's fundamental building blocks might be bits of information rather than traditional particles or fields. 
However, extant models of digital physics face challenges, particularly in reconciling with several continuous [symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_in_physics "Symmetry in physics")[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-8) in physical laws, e.g., [rotational symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_symmetry "Rotational symmetry"), [translational symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translational_symmetry "Translational symmetry"), [Lorentz symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_symmetry "Lorentz symmetry"), and the [Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group "Lie group") gauge invariance of [Yang–Mills theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang%E2%80%93Mills_theory "Yang–Mills theory"), all of which are central to current physical theories. Moreover, existing models of digital physics violate various well-established features of [quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics "Quantum physics"), as they belong to a class of theories involving local [hidden variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_variable_theory "Hidden variable theory"). These models have so far been disqualified experimentally by physicists using [Bell's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell%27s_theorem "Bell's theorem").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics#cite_note-10)
