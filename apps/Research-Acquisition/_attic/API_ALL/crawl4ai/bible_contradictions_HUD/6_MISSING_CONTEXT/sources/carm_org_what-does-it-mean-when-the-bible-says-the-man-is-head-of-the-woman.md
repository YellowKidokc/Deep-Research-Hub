# What does it mean when the Bible says, “the man is head of the woman?”
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 10, 2008 
4 min read 
In his first letter to the church in Corinth, Paul wrote what has today become a very controversial claim, namely “the man is the head of a woman.” The full verse reads:
“But I want you to understand that Christ is the head of every man, and the man is the head of a woman, and God is the head of Christ.” ([1 Cor. 11:3](https://ref.ly/1%20Cor.%2011.3;nasb95?t=biblia)).
In the Jewish and Christian culture when the Bible was written, there was a concept called “[Federal Headship](https://carm.org/doctrine-and-theology/federal-headship/).” This means that the male is the one who represents his descendants. Proof of this can be found in [Heb. 7:8-10](https://ref.ly/Heb.%207.8-10;nasb95?t=biblia), “And in this case mortal men receive tithes, but in that case one receives them, of whom it is witnessed that he lives on. 9 And, so to speak, through Abraham even Levi, who received tithes, paid tithes, 10 for he was still in the loins of his father when Melchizedek met him.” Notice that it says that Levi paid tithes while still in the loins of his father Abraham. How did he do this? By proxy since Abraham, his distant “father” represented him.
