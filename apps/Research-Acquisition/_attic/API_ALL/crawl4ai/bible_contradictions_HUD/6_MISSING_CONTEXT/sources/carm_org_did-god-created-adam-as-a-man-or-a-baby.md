# Did God create Adam as a man or a baby?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
October 15, 2015 
4 min read 
There is nothing in Scripture that suggests that Adam was created as an infant. If anything, the contrary is taught. Let’s take a look.
The first mention of ‘man’ in the Bible refers to man generically, as both male and female ([Gen. 1:26](https://ref.ly/Gen.%201.26;nasb95?t=biblia)). The command of God to them is to be fruitful and multiply (v. 1:28), which is not something a baby could do. Later we see that God placed the man in the garden of Eden ([Gen. 2:8](https://ref.ly/Gen.%202.8;nasb95?t=biblia)). So the Scriptures call him a man, not a baby. Furthermore, in [Gen. 2:16-17](https://ref.ly/Gen.%202.16-17;nasb95?t=biblia) the man is given a commandment to not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. This certainly does not fit admonitions given to a baby. And, we see in [Gen. 2:24](https://ref.ly/Gen.%202.24;nasb95?t=biblia) that marriage is introduced where “the man” is again mentioned. This, of course, implies maturity, not infancy.
Nothing in Genesis 1, 2, or 3 suggests that Adam was created as an infant. The pattern of God is to create things in maturity as we see in the created order where God sequentially makes the heavens and earth over six days. Each thing in the created order seems to be created in maturity, i.e., the sun, the moon, the stars, etc. The earth, however, is formless and void and God places things on it. The reason the development occurs over six days is probably to demonstrate God’s creative order, logic, and to demonstrate how we are to rest, even as God did on the seventh day. That rest, ultimately speaks of Christ’s redemptive work.
