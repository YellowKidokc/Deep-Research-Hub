# Was Acts 24:7 removed from modern Bibles?
by [Luke Wayne](https://carm.org/luke-wayne)
October 31, 2018 
5 min read 
Did modern translators delete [Acts 24:7](https://ref.ly/Acts%2024.7;nasb95?t=biblia)? Did they also erase material from the surrounding verses? King James Only advocates would certainly have us think so. It is true that many modern translations do not have verse 6 in the main body of the text and have shorter forms of verses 6 and 8 (though they do contain the additional material in a footnote). The question is, however, was this material originally part of the book of Acts? Did modern scholars _delete_ it? Or did later scribes add to what Luke originally wrote? To answer this, we need to look at the manuscripts and try to understand why each group of translators did what they did.
The passage under discussion here reads:
“And he even tried to desecrate the temple; and then we arrested him. [We wanted to judge him according to our own Law. But Lysias the commander came along, and with much violence took him out of our hands, ordering his accusers to come before you.] By examining him yourself concerning all these matters you will be able to ascertain the things of which we accuse him,” ([Acts 24:6-8, NASB](https://ref.ly/Acts%2024.6-8;nasb95?t=biblia)).
