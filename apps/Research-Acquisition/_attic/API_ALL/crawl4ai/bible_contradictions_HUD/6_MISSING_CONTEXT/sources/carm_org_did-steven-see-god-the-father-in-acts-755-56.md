# Did Stephen see God the Father in Acts 7:55-56?
by [Luke Wayne](https://carm.org/luke-wayne)
December 08, 2019 
9 min read 
Mormonism claims that their founder, Joseph Smith, saw both God the Father and Jesus Christ in visible, human forms when they allegedly appeared to him and called him to be a prophet. The Bible teaches, however, that [God the Father cannot be seen](https://carm.org/mormonism/can-the-father-be-seen/). Thus, God’s word denies even the possibility that Joseph Smith’s experience, and thus his prophetic calling, was genuine. Faced with the biblical evidence of this (Such as [1 Timothy 6:16](https://ref.ly/1%20Tim%206.16;nasb95?t=biblia), [John 1:18](https://ref.ly/John%201.18;nasb95?t=biblia), [1 John 4:10-12](https://ref.ly/1%20John%204.10-12;nasb95?t=biblia), etc.), Mormons often try to get around it by pointing to passages in the Bible which they believe are exceptions to this rule. One especially common example is Stephen’s vision in [Acts 7:55-56](https://ref.ly/Acts%207.55-56;nasb95?t=biblia), but this passage does not claim what Mormons think it does, nor does it contradict the plain teaching of the rest of Scripture.
The Mormon argument is based on the account of Stephen’s trial and execution in Acts 7. At the climax of the trial, we read of Steven:
“But being full of the Holy Spirit, he gazed intently into heaven and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing at the right hand of God; and he said, ‘Behold, I see the heavens opened up and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God,’” ([Acts 7:55-56](https://ref.ly/Acts%207.55-56;nasb95?t=biblia)).
