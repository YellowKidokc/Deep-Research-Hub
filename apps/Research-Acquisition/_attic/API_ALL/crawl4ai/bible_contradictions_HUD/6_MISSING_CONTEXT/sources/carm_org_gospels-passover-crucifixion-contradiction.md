# Do the gospels present a contradictory account of Jesus' crucifixion as it relates to the Passover
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
November 10, 2025 
4 min read 
There is a Biblical difficulty related to the four Gospels, the Passover meal, Jesus' crucifixion, and the chronology that harmonizes them. The Synoptic Gospels (Matthew, Mark, and Luke), show Jesus eating the Passover meal (Matt. 26:17–19; Mark 14:12–16; Luke 22:7–15) on the first day of the Feast of Unleavened Bread (Nisan 15), **before** He was arrested and crucified. However, the Gospel of John depicts Jesus as being crucified on the Day of Preparation for the Passover (John 19:14, 18), suggesting that the official Passover meal had not yet occurred. How do we reconcile this problem?
The most widely accepted solution centers on the fact that the Jewish people often used "Passover" and "Feast of Unleavened Bread" interchangeably to refer to the entire week-long festival (Nisan 15–21), not just the Seder meal.
John may have intentionally presented a slightly different timeline to emphasize a theological objective that Jesus is the Lamb of God. John emphasizes that the Crucifixion occurred on Nisan 14, the exact time the Passover lambs were being slaughtered in the Temple. This shows Jesus as the Passover Lamb (John 1:29) whose sacrifice fulfills the Old Testament type.
