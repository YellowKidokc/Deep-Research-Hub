# Annihilationism and Matthew 25:46 and eternal punishment
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
October 11, 2018 
6 min read 
“These will go away into eternal punishment, but the righteous into eternal life” (Matthew 25:46).
In the context of conditionalism, “eternal punishment” means eternal nonexistence. (See article [Conditionalism and conflating eternal punishment with non-existence](https://carm.org/annihilationism/conditionalism-and-conflating-eternal-punishment-with-non-existence/) for an explanation of why eternal punishment is not eternal nonexistence.) They contrast eternal punishment with eternal life and say that since eternal life means to have a living relationship with God in a physical body forever, then “eternal punishment” must mean the punishment that leads to nonexistence otherwise, they too would have eternal life. ![eternal punishment](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2009/10/hell-05.jpg)
The phrase “eternal life” is never defined in Scripture. It is however associated with many good things and contrasted with bad things. For example, it is associated with abiding with God (1 John 2:25), generic blessing (Matt. 19:16; Luke 10:25; John 3:15, etc.), grace and righteousness (Rom. 5:21), justification (Titus 3:7), never perishing (John 10:28), resurrection (John 6:40, 54), and sanctification (Rom. 6:22). It is contrasted with death (Rom. 6:23), eternal punishment (Matt. 25:46), God’s wrath (John 3:36), and perishing (John 3:16; 6:27). So, we naturally conclude that eternal life is good and eternal punishment is bad. (See [Word Study on eternal life](https://carm.org/annihilationism/word-study-on-eternal-life/))
