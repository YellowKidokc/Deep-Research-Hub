# What does the Eastern Orthodox Church say about baptism?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
April 09, 2025 
5 min read 
The Eastern Orthodox Church’s view of baptism is very similar to that of Roman Catholicism. It brings a person into the church, regenerates, removes original sin, and is necessary for salvation (normally speaking). This is typical of groups who deny justification by faith alone in Christ alone – something the Bible clearly teaches is true ([Rom. 3:20](https://ref.ly/Rom.%203.20;nasb95?t=biblia), [28](https://ref.ly/Rom%203.28;nasb95?t=biblia); [4:1-5](https://ref.ly/Rom%204.1-5;nasb95?t=biblia); [5:1](https://ref.ly/Rom%205.1;nasb95?t=biblia); [Gal. 2:16](https://ref.ly/Gal.%202.16;nasb95?t=biblia); [Phil. 3:9](https://ref.ly/Phil.%203.9;nasb95?t=biblia)). The Eastern Orthodox Church teaches that salvation is through faith and works (_Confession of Dositheus, 1672,_ Decree 13 _, crivoice.org/creeddositheus.html_). Therefore, baptism is a part of the system of achieving salvation, a system of works, rituals, sacraments, repentance, confession, prayer to saints, etc. The Eastern Orthodox Church violates the essentials of the Christian faith. It is not a true Christian Church. ![What does the Eastern Orthodox Church say about baptism?](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/baptism-icon-01.jpg)
