# Annihilationism and Ezekiel 18:4, the soul that sins will die
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
October 11, 2018 
4 min read 
[Ezekiel 18:4](https://ref.ly/Ezek%2018.4;nasb95?t=biblia), “Behold, all souls are Mine; the soul of the father as well as the soul of the son is Mine. The soul who sins will die.”
Conditionalists often use [Ezekiel 18:4](https://ref.ly/Ezek%2018.4;nasb95?t=biblia) to support their position of annihilationism. However, the text does not say that the soul that sins will cease to exist. Instead, it says the soul that sins will die. The question is, what is meant by this death? Is it nonexistence, or is it a judgment by God upon the sinner? Of course, this is what the debate is all about.
The first mention of death occurs in [Genesis 2:17](https://ref.ly/Gen%202.17;nasb95?t=biblia), where God tells Adam that in the day he eats of the fruit, he will die. Of course, the conditionalists will say that such death means a future, physical death for him. But, contextually, that does work. Furthermore, I can make the case that such death was immediate, even though Adam and Eve were still physically alive after they sinned by eating the fruit. (See the article [Annihilationism and Genesis 2:17 and “in the day”](https://carm.org/annihilationism/annihilationism-and-genesis-217-and-in-the-day/))
