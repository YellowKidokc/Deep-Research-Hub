# If Job was blameless, why did God allow Satan to afflict him?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 01, 2008 
2 min read 
If Job was blameless, why did God allow Satan to afflicted him? [Job 1:1](https://ref.ly/Job%201.1;nasb95?t=biblia) and [Job 1:12](https://ref.ly/Job%201.12;nasb95?t=biblia). When God said he was blameless, He was speaking in metaphor, in an exaggeration. We know that no one, except Jesus, is blameless in all ways. So, God was speaking of Job in a human sense that, for the most part, he was blameless. ![Job blameless why afflicted](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/12/affliction-02.jpg)
To review the first paragraph, when the Bible says that Job was blameless, it does not mean he was sinless. It means he was a God-fearing man who sought to do what was right before the Lord. Job’s awareness of his sins is acknowledged by the fact that he sacrificed animals to the Lord as atonement for his sins in chapter 1.
As the story goes, the “sons of God,” angels, presented themselves before God. Satan was there, and a conversation ensued about Job’s goodness. Satan challenges God by stating that Job will denounce God if afflicted. God then permits Satan to afflict Job. Of course, Job doesn’t denounce God. So, the question is, why would God allow Satan to do this? God, in His sovereignty, allowed it for a greater reason that was instantly apparent.
