# Did Jesus ever refer to God as YHWH, Yahweh?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 23, 2025 
3 min read 
In the Greek New Testament, the word "YHWH/Yahweh" does not occur. This is because it is Hebrew. Therefore, according to the New Testament Greek, Jesus never referred to God as Yahweh. However, Jesus would have spoken Aramaic and most probably knew Hebrew as well. In light of this, the Aramaic equivalent of Yahweh would be the same as the Hebrew one, since the two languages are related. So, when Jesus quoted the Old Testament, He did so in Aramaic or Hebrew. This means He may have referred to God as YHWH/Yahweh in those original languages. The reason I say "may" is that, at the time, it was customary to substitute God's name, YHWH, for Adonai, which is Hebrew. This was to avoid using God's name in vain (Exodus 20:7). So, if Jesus quoted Scripture in Hebrew and if He did not substitute Adonai for YHWH, then He would have referred to God as Yahweh. But we cannot say for sure that Jesus did or did not substitute YHWH for adonai. Still, if Jesus did do it, He would never have used God's name in vain.
Nevertheless, here are a few scriptures where Jesus quotes the Old Testament that include YHWH/Yahweh in the original.
So, though there is no exact reference in the Greek New Testament where Jesus uses the Hebrew tetragrammaton (YHWH/Yahweh), there are many places where Jesus quotes the Old Testament. Those verses refer to God as Yahweh. Therefore, we can say that Jesus called God Yahweh.
