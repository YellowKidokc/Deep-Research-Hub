# Who is James in the New Testament, Bible?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
July 04, 2022 
5 min read 
There appear to be five different people named James who are mentioned in the New Testament. They are listed below. The Greek word Ἰάκωβος Iákōbos; or Iakṓb, or [Jacob](https://www.blueletterbible.org/search/dictionary/viewtopic.cfm?topic=HT0001256), occurs 42 times. Of those five people, the author of the book of James, who appears to be the Lord’s brother (Gal. 1:19), occupies an important ecclesiastical position in the Jerusalem church, along with the elders there. It is possible that James took Peter’s place when Peter was jailed which would explain his influence. ![James new testament](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/bible-book-james.png)
Acts 12:17, “But motioning to them with his hand to be silent, he described to them how the Lord had led him out of the prison. And he said, ‘Report these things to James and the brethren.’ Then he left and went to another place.”
So, James was considered an important figure, especially when you look at the Jerusalem council in Acts 15:13-20. He states that the people should not trouble those Gentiles are turning to God (v. 19) and that the Gentiles should “…abstain from things contaminated by idols and from fornication and from what is strangled and from blood,” (v. 20).
