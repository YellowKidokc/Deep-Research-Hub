# Psalm 118:8 and a particularly silly King James Only argument
by [Luke Wayne](https://carm.org/luke-wayne)
January 20, 2020 
7 min read 
One of the silliest King James Only arguments I have seen, one I am sure that even some of my King James Only brothers shake their heads at, involves alleged hidden evidence in [Psalm 118:8](https://ref.ly/Ps%20118.8;nasb95?t=biblia) which is supposed to prove that the KJV is the word of God and modern translations are not. The basic idea is that the number of words in the verse and its central location in the Bible are proof that the KJV is the true word of God. Let me say clearly upfront, no; the placement and word count of [Psalm 118:8](https://ref.ly/Ps%20118.8;nasb95?t=biblia) does _not_ prove the KJV to be miraculous nor superior to any other translation. Those who raise this argument have their data wrong, and it wouldn’t prove anything even if they had the data right.
The claim is typically laid out as follows. If you count all the verses in the KJV, the middle verse of the whole Bible is [Psalm 118:8](https://ref.ly/Ps%20118.8;nasb95?t=biblia). This verse reads:
“It is better to trust in the Lord than to put confidence in man,” ([Psalm 118:8, KJV](https://ref.ly/Ps%20118.8;kjv1900?t=biblia)).
