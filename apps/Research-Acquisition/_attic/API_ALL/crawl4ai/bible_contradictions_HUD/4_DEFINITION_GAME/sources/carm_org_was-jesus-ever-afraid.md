# Was Jesus ever afraid?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
November 02, 2024 
2 min read 
There is no specific verse in the New Testament that says that Jesus was ever afraid. But, we can conclude that He _was_ afraid _w_ hen facing what He had to go through on the cross. It would be a terrifying prospect and naturally bring fear into anyone who knew he would experience it. The word “afraid” in Greek is φοβέω phobéō. It occurs 92 times in the N.T., and the NASB95 is rendered as afraid (Matt. 1:20; Mark 4:41; Luke 2:10, etc.), fear (Luke 1:50; John 12:15, etc.), frightened (1 Pet. 3:6), respect (Eph. 5:33), etc. It doesn’t occur about Jesus.[ ![Was Jesus ever afraid](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Jesus-09sqs.jpg) ](https://carm.org/attachment/jesus-09sqs/)
Jesus is God and man (John 1:1, 14; Col. 2:9). The attributes of both natures are ascribed to Him as a person. This is called the communicatio idiomatum. So, as a man, He would have naturally faced the prospect of going to the cross with great trepidation since it would involve great physical suffering. And that is exactly what we find in Scripture.
Jesus is both God and man. This is called the hypostatic union. Being a man, He would have experienced the normal emotions that a man would have experienced – otherwise, how is He a man? The range of emotions attributed to Jesus are as follows: Anger (Matt. 21:12-13), Compassion (Matt. 9:36; 14:14; Mark 1:41), Dread (Luke 22:44), Distress (Matt. 26:34; Mark 14:33), Fear (Matt. 26:38), Grief/Sadness (Matt. 23:37; 26:38), Love (John 11:5, 36), Rejoicing/Joy (Luke 10:21), and Sadness (John 11:35).
