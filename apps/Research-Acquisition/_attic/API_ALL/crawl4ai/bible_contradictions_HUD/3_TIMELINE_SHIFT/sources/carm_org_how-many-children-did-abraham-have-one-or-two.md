# How many children did Abraham have, one or two?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
December 01, 2008 
6 min read 
How many children ![Children on a white background](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/children-200x300.jpg) did Abraham have, one or two? [Genesis 22:2](https://ref.ly/Gen%2022.2;nasb95?t=biblia), [Hebrews 11:17](https://ref.ly/Heb%2011.17;nasb95?t=biblia), and [Galatians 4:22](https://ref.ly/Gal%204.22;nasb95?t=biblia) tell us different things. What about Ishmael and Isaac? Aren’t they both the children of Abraham?
The answer to this apparent contradiction is found in understanding the typological representation of Isaac, Abraham’s second-born son, as a type of Christ. Abraham had Ishmael by the handmaiden Hagar. But Isaac was the child of promise, not Ishmael: “But God said to Abraham, ‘Do not be distressed because of the lad and your maid; whatever Sarah tells you, listen to her, for through Isaac your descendants shall be named,'” ([Gen. 21:12](https://ref.ly/Gen.%2021.12;nasb95?t=biblia)).
If you look at the chart below, you will see the similarities between Isaac and Jesus. In other words, Isaac was a prophetic representation of Jesus. This is why Jesus said, “Your father Abraham rejoiced to see My day, and he saw it and was glad,” ([John 8:56](https://ref.ly/John%208.56;nasb95?t=biblia)). Abraham had, in a very real sense, seeing the gospel presentation in the offering of his son, his “only begotten.” So, we see here that the term “only begotten” is in reference to the unique son of God and Isaac was acting out the sacrifice of Christ, prophetically.
