# Jewish Scriptures and Sacred Texts
> “Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.”  — Psalm 119:105
Jews have been called the "**People of the Book** ," an appropriate title. After the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE and the subsequent exile, sacrifices became impossible and [Jewish](https://religionfacts.com/judaism) religious life turned to **study of the scriptures** and prayer in the synagogue. Study of Torah and other Jewish texts has been central to religious life ever since.
##  [](https://religionfacts.com/judaism/texts#importance-of-scripture-in-judaism "link to this section")Importance of Scripture in Judaism
**Jewish sacred texts** are considered precious sources of [Jewish history](https://religionfacts.com/judaism/history) and divine commandments (the [mitzvot](https://religionfacts.com/mitzvot)), both of which continue to play a dominant part in Judaism. To remember the great things God has done for the Jewish people in history, and what he asks of them in return, selections from the [Torah](https://religionfacts.com/torah) and the [Prophets](https://religionfacts.com/tanakh) are read in the synagogue several times a week.
Study of the Jewish scriptures (preferably in the **original Hebrew**) is an integral part of a Jewish child's education and even Jewish mysticism is focused on intensive textual study. To assist in proper interpretation and application of the Jewish scriptures, a great body of rabbinical writings has developed and continues to develop to this day.
##  [](https://religionfacts.com/judaism/texts#overview-of-jewish-sacred-texts "link to this section")Overview of Jewish Sacred Texts
The primary Jewish sacred text is the [**Tanakh**](https://religionfacts.com/tanakh), also known as the Jewish Bible or "Written Torah." It contains the same books as the Christian Old Testament, with slightly different organization. It consists of the [**Torah**](https://religionfacts.com/torah) ("Law") (five books attributed to Moses), the **Prophets** , and the **Writings**. Sometimes the term "Torah" is used to refer to the whole of the Tanakh.
The [**Septuagint**](https://religionfacts.com/septuagint) is a Greek translation of the Jewish Bible dating from 300-200 BCE, which was used by early [Christians](https://religionfacts.com/christianity). It also contains the [**Apocrypha**](https://religionfacts.com/apocrypha), a group of Jewish texts written in the 5th-1st centuries BCE that are not considered scripture in Judaism but were incorporated in the Christian Bible.
The [**Talmud**](https://religionfacts.com/talmud), also known as the "Oral Torah", is a collection of rabbinical writings that interpret, explain and apply the Jewish Bible, dating from the 2nd-5th century CE. There is a Jerusalem Talmud and a Babylonian Talmud, but the latter is the one usually meant by "the Talmud." It consists of the [**Midrash**](https://religionfacts.com/midrash), which is commentary on the Bible, and the **Gemara** , which is commentary on the Midrash.
The [**responsa**](https://religionfacts.com/responsa) is a vast collection (thousands of volumes) of answers to specific questions on Jewish law.
The [**Zohar**](https://religionfacts.com/zohar) (Book of Splendor) is the central text of [Kabbalah](https://religionfacts.com/kabbalah), the mystical branch of Judaism.
