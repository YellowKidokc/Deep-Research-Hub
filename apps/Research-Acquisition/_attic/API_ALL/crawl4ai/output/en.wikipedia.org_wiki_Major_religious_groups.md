The [world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World "World")'s principal [religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion "Religion") and spiritual traditions may be classified into a small number of major groups, though this is not a uniform practice. This theory began in the 18th century with the goal of recognizing the relative degrees of [civility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civility "Civility") in different societies,[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-2) but this concept of a ranking order has since fallen into disrepute in many contemporary cultures. 
## Religious demographics
Further information: [List of religious populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_religious_populations "List of religious populations")
Main category: [Religious demographics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Religious_demographics "Category:Religious demographics")
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Prevailing_religious_population_by_country_percentage.svg/500px-Prevailing_religious_population_by_country_percentage.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Prevailing_religious_population_by_country_percentage.svg)A map of major denominations and religions according to the Pew Research Center's 2010 study _The Future of World Religions: Population Growth Projections, 2010-2050_
One way to define a major religion is by the number of current adherents. The population numbers by religion are computed by a combination of census reports and population surveys, in countries where religion data is not collected in census, for example the United States or France. Results can vary widely depending on the way questions are phrased, the definitions of religion used and the bias of the agencies or organizations conducting the survey. Informal or unorganized religions are especially difficult to count. 
There is no consensus among researchers as to the best methodology for determining the religiosity profile of the world's population. A number of fundamental aspects are unresolved: 
  * Whether to count "historically predominant religious culture[s]".[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-3)
  * Whether to count only those who actively "practice" a particular religion.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-4)
  * Whether to count based on a concept of "self-identification as adherents".[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-5)
  * Whether to count only those who expressly self-identify with a particular denomination.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-6)
  * Whether to count only adults, or to include children as well.
  * Whether to rely on official government-provided statistics.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-7)[_[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_]
  * Whether to use multiple sources and ranges or single "best source(s)".

Religious traditions fall into super-groups in [comparative religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_religion "Comparative religion"), arranged by historical origin and mutual influence. Abrahamic religions originate in the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East"),[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-73)[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-74) [Indian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_religions "Indian religions") in the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") (South Asia) and [East Asian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_religions "East Asian religions") in [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia").[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-75) Another group with supra-regional influence are [Afro-American religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-American_religion "Afro-American religion"),[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-76) which have their origins in Central and West Africa. In contemporary scholarship, [Modern Paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Paganism "Modern Paganism") is increasingly classified as a distinct supra-regional group, encompassing both eclectic traditions and [polytheistic reconstructionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytheistic_reconstructionism "Polytheistic reconstructionism"), the latter of which often functions as a contemporary [ethnic religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_religion "Ethnic religion") focused on reviving pre-Christian European traditions.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-Strmiska2005-77)[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-York2003-78)
  * Middle Eastern religions:[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-Britannica-79)
    * [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions "Abrahamic religions") are the largest group, and these consist mainly of [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), and the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith "Baháʼí Faith"). They are named for the [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrews "Hebrews") patriarch [Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham "Abraham"), and are unified by the practice of monotheism. Today, at least 3.8 billion people are followers of Abrahamic religions[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-80) and are spread widely around the world apart from the regions around East and Southeast Asia. Several Abrahamic organizations are vigorous [proselytizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proselytism "Proselytism").[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-81) Abrahamic religions with fewer adherents include the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith "Baháʼí Faith"),[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-Abulafia-2019-82) the [Druze faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_faith "Druze faith"),[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-Abulafia-2019-82) [Samaritanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritanism "Samaritanism"),[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-Abulafia-2019-82) and [Rastafari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastafari "Rastafari").[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-Abulafia-2019-82)
    * [Iranian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_religions "Iranian religions"), partly of [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_religion "Proto-Indo-European religion") origins,[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-FOOTNOTESamuel2010-83)[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnthony2007-84) include [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"), [Yazdânism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazd%C3%A2nism "Yazdânism"), [Uatsdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uatsdin "Uatsdin"), [Yarsanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarsanism "Yarsanism"), [Manichaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manichaeism "Manichaeism"), and [Yazidism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidism "Yazidism").[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-85)
    * [Gnosticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnosticism "Gnosticism"), including historical traditions of [Mandaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeism "Mandaeism"), which is still alive in the Middle East and diaspora.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-86)
  * Eastern religions: 
    * [Indian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_religions "Indian religions"), originated in [Greater India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India "Greater India") and they tend to share a number of key concepts, such as [dharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma "Dharma"), [karma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma "Karma"), [reincarnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reincarnation "Reincarnation") among others. They are of the most influence across the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"), East Asia, Southeast Asia, as well as isolated parts of Russia. The main Indian religions are [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") and [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"). In 2010 [Ravidassia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravidassia "Ravidassia") religion was separated from Sikhism.
    * [East Asian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_religions "East Asian religions") consist of several East Asian religions which make use of the concept of _[Tao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao "Tao")_ (in Chinese), _[Đạo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao "Tao")_ (in Vietnamese) or _[Dō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8D_\(philosophy\) "Dō \(philosophy\)")_ (in Japanese or Korean). They include many [Chinese folk religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_religion "Chinese folk religion"), [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism "Taoism") and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism"), as well as [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_folk_religion "Vietnamese folk religion"), [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_shamanism "Korean shamanism") and [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto "Shinto") religions, which are influenced by Chinese religious thought.
  * Indigenous [ethnic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_religion "Ethnic religion"), found on every continent, now marginalized by the major organized faiths in many parts of the world or persisting as undercurrents ([folk religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_religion "Folk religion")) of major religions. Includes [traditional African religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religion "Traditional African religion"), Asian [shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamanism "Shamanism"), [Native American religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_religion "Native American religion"), [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples#Religion "Austronesian peoples") and [Australian Aboriginal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Aboriginal_mythology "Australian Aboriginal mythology") traditions, [Chinese folk religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_religion "Chinese folk religion"), and postwar [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto "Shinto"). Under more traditional listings, this has been referred to as "[paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism "Paganism")" along with [historical polytheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytheism#Historical_polytheism "Polytheism"). 
    * African religions:[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-Britannica-79)
      * The religions of the tribal peoples of Sub-Saharan Africa, but excluding [ancient Egyptian religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_religion "Ancient Egyptian religion"), which is considered to belong to the ancient Middle East;[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-Britannica-79)
      * [African diasporic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-American_religion "Afro-American religion") practiced in the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas"), imported as a result of the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade") of the 16th to 18th centuries, building on [traditional religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religion "Traditional African religion") of Central and West Africa.
  * [Modern Paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Paganism "Modern Paganism") and [Polytheistic reconstructionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytheistic_reconstructionism "Polytheistic reconstructionism") is a broad group of religions that seek to revive or recreate pre-Christian spiritualities. Scholars categorize these along a continuum between eclecticism (blending traditions) and reconstructionism (seeking historical and ethnic accuracy).[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-Strmiska2005-77)
    * [Reconstructionist Roman religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstructionist_Roman_religion "Reconstructionist Roman religion") (also known as _Religio Romana_ or _Cultus Deorum_) is a specific form of ethnic reconstructionism that seeks to re-establish the traditional cults and customs of ancient Rome as a culturally-rooted spiritual framework for the modern era.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-87) These movements are often affiliated with the [European Congress of Ethnic Religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Congress_of_Ethnic_Religions "European Congress of Ethnic Religions"), emphasizing a "Native Faith" identity tied to European cultural heritage.[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-88)
  * [New religious movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_religious_movement "New religious movement") is the term applied to any religious faith which has emerged since the 19th century, often [syncretizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretism "Syncretism"), re-interpreting or reviving aspects of older traditions such as [Ayyavazhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyavazhi "Ayyavazhi"), [Mormonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormonism "Mormonism"), [Ahmadiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadiyya "Ahmadiyya"), [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah%27s_Witnesses "Jehovah's Witnesses"), [polytheistic reconstructionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytheistic_reconstructionism "Polytheistic reconstructionism"), and so forth.

### Christian categorizations
Initially, Christians had a simple dichotomy of world beliefs: Christian civility versus foreign heresy or barbarity. In the 18th century, "[heresy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heresy "Heresy")" was clarified to mean [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism") and [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam");[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-89) along with [paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism "Paganism"), this created a fourfold classification which spawned such works as [John Toland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Toland "John Toland")'s _Nazarenus, or Jewish, Gentile, and Mahometan Christianity_ ,[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-90) which represented the three [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions "Abrahamic religions") as different "nations" or sects within _religion_ itself, the "true [monotheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotheism "Monotheism")." 
[Daniel Defoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Defoe "Daniel Defoe") described the original definition as follows: "Religion is properly the Worship given to God, but 'tis also applied to the Worship of Idols and false Deities."[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-91) At the turn of the 19th century, in between 1780 and 1810, the language dramatically changed: instead of "religion" being synonymous with spirituality, authors began using the plural, "religions", to refer to both Christianity and other forms of worship. Therefore, [Hannah Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Adams "Hannah Adams")'s early encyclopedia, for example, had its name changed from _An Alphabetical Compendium of the Various Sects..._ to _[A Dictionary of All Religions and Religious Denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Dictionary_of_All_Religions_and_Religious_Denominations "A Dictionary of All Religions and Religious Denominations")_.[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasuzawa200549%E2%80%9361-92)[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-93)
In 1838, the four-way division of Christianity, Judaism, [Mahommedanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammedan "Mohammedan") (archaic terminology for [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam")) and paganism was multiplied considerably by [Josiah Conder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josiah_Conder_\(editor_and_author\) "Josiah Conder \(editor and author\)")'s _Analytical and Comparative View of All Religions Now Extant among Mankind_. Conder's work still adhered to the four-way classification, but in his eye for detail he puts together much historical work to create something resembling the modern Western image: he includes [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze "Druze"), [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis "Yazidis"), [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeism "Mandaeism"), and [Elamites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elam "Elam")[_[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_][[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-94) under a list of possibly monotheistic groups, and under the final category, of "polytheism and pantheism", he listed [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"), "Vedas, Puranas, Tantras, Reformed sects" of India as well as "Brahminical idolatry", [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism"), [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"), [Lamaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism "Tibetan Buddhism"), "religion of China and Japan", and "illiterate superstitions" as others.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasuzawa200565%E2%80%9366-95)[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-96)
The modern meaning of the phrase "world religion", putting non-Christians at the same level as Christians, began with the 1893 [Parliament of the World's Religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_World%27s_Religions "Parliament of the World's Religions") in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"). The Parliament spurred the creation of a dozen privately funded lectures with the intent of informing people of the diversity of religious experience: these lectures funded researchers such as [William James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_James "William James"), [D. T. Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._T._Suzuki "D. T. Suzuki"), and [Alan Watts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Watts "Alan Watts"), who greatly influenced the public conception of world religions.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasuzawa2005270%E2%80%93281-97)
In the latter half of the 20th century, the category of "world religion" fell into serious question, especially for drawing parallels between vastly different cultures, and thereby creating an arbitrary separation between the religious and the secular.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_note-98)
### Islam categorizations
In [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran "Quran") mentions three categories: [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims"), the [People of the Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_of_the_Book "People of the Book"), and [idol worshipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idolatry "Idolatry").

## Notes
  1. **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups#cite_ref-32 "Jump up")** Historically, the Baháʼí Faith arose in 19th-century Persia, in the context of [Shia Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam "Shia Islam"), and thus may be classed on this basis as a divergent strand of Islam, placing it in the Abrahamic tradition. However, the Baháʼí Faith considers itself an independent religious tradition, which draws from Islam but also other traditions. The Baháʼí Faith may also be classed as a new religious movement, due to its comparatively recent origin, or may be considered sufficiently old and established for such classification to not be applicable.
