# Does Luke 4:18 misquote Isaiah 61:1?
by [Luke Wayne](https://carm.org/luke-wayne)
September 09, 2019 
6 min read 
An accusation frequently trotted out against the New Testament is that it allegedly “misquotes” or even “deceitfully alters” the Old Testament. A common example used is the citation of [Isaiah 61:1](https://ref.ly/Isa%2061.1;nasb95?t=biblia) found in [Luke 4:18](https://ref.ly/Luke%204.18;nasb95?t=biblia). Jesus quotes the passage as containing a reference to giving sight to the blind, but if you look the verse up in your own English Old Testament, you won’t find any such reference. This seems, at first glance, like an error in the New Testament. The fact of the matter is, however, that there is no mistake or deception going on here. Jesus simply cited an interpretive paraphrase familiar to His audience.
The key passages involved here read as follows:
“The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me, Because the Lord has anointed me To bring good news to the afflicted; He has sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, To proclaim liberty to captives And freedom to prisoners,” ([Isaiah 61:1](https://ref.ly/Isa%2061.1;nasb95?t=biblia)).
