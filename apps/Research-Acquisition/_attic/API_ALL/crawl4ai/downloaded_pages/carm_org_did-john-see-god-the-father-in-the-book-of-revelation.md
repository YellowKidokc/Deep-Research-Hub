# Did John see God the Father in the Book of Revelation?
by [Luke Wayne](https://carm.org/luke-wayne)
January 19, 2020 
7 min read 
A central claim in the Mormon religion is that their founder, Joseph Smith, saw Jesus Christ and also God the Father in visible, human form when both allegedly appeared to him and called him to “restore the Church.” The plain, biblical truth, however, is that [no one can see God the Father](https://carm.org/uncategorized/can-the-father-be-seen/). Thus, God’s word denies even the possibility that Joseph Smith’s claims here were genuine. Passages such as [1 Timothy 6:16](https://ref.ly/1%20Tim%206.16;nasb95?t=biblia), [John 1:18](https://ref.ly/John%201.18;nasb95?t=biblia), and [1 John 4:10-12](https://ref.ly/1%20John%204.10-12;nasb95?t=biblia) (among others) plainly state that no one has seen nor can see the Father. [Colossians 1:15](https://ref.ly/Col%201.15;nasb95?t=biblia) likewise states that Jesus is the image of the invisible God. The Son may be seen, but one cannot see God the Father. The Son makes the Father known.
Yet, many Mormons try to get around these facts by pointing to passages in the Bible which they believe are exceptions to this rule. One important example is John’s vision of a heavenly throne room in the Book of Revelation. Considered in context, however, this passage is not in any way contrary to the straightforward teaching that God the Father has not and can not be seen by men.
