# What was John the Baptist’s diet?
by [Luke Wayne](https://carm.org/luke-wayne)
February 22, 2016 
2 min read 
John the Baptist lived on a diet of locusts and wild honey, which was indicative of his wilderness ministry. In Matthew 3, it tells us twice that John was preaching “in the wilderness” (3:1, 3:3) and explains that “his food was locusts and wild honey.” (3:4) Mark’s gospel does precisely the same (1:2-6). Luke’s Gospel never defines a particular diet. Luke does, however, contain a prophecy from an angel to John’s father before his birth that he would “drink no wine or liquor” ([Luke 1:15](https://ref.ly/Luke%201.15;nasb95?t=biblia)) and later records Jesus saying:[ ![who John the Baptist](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/grasshopper-honey-01.jpg) ](https://carm.org/other-questions/what-was-john-the-baptists-diet/attachment/grasshopper-honey-01/)
“For John the Baptist has come eating no bread and drinking no wine” ([Luke 7:33](https://ref.ly/Luke%207.33;nasb95?t=biblia), see also [Matt. 11:18](https://ref.ly/Matt.%2011.18;nasb95?t=biblia)).
Luke also records that John “lived in the deserts until the day of his public appearance to Israel” ([Luke 1:80](https://ref.ly/Luke%201.80;nasb95?t=biblia)) and that the word of the Lord came to him “in the wilderness” ([Luke 3:2](https://ref.ly/Luke%203.2;nasb95?t=biblia)). Both Luke and Matthew record Jesus asking the crowd regarding John, “What did you go out into the wilderness to see?” ([Luke 7:24](https://ref.ly/Luke%207.24;nasb95?t=biblia), [Matt. 11:7](https://ref.ly/Matt.%2011.7;nasb95?t=biblia)).
