# Facts and intelligibility must be grounded in the Trinitarian God
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
August 05, 2025 
7 min read 
All worldviews must face foundational philosophical questions dealing with ethics, reason, knowledge, meaning, existence, and reality. In the latter category, a persistent question arises: What is the ultimate nature of reality? Is it one thing (unity) or many things (diversity)? The answer to the question relates to how we can account for the laws of logic, morality, and the relationship between universals (i.e., treeness, chairness) and particulars (i.e., individual trees and chairs) and how they relate to each other. This is the classic Problem of the One and the Many. Here it is explained.
Solving this issue has been a core question in secular metaphysics that eludes solution. But not so in Christian Theology because the Trinity solves the problem by proposing an ultimate (YHWH) who is equally both one and many. “God is a Trinity.” “God is one being in three simultaneous and distinct persons. This reconciles unity and diversity, providing the foundation for truth, logic, and intelligibility.
All other worldviews, besides the Christian worldview, affirm that the ultimate reality of the universe is either one or many. None of those worldviews has an ultimate that is one and many, since that is unique to Christian theology.
