# If God is unchangeable, how could He become a man?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
May 21, 2010 
3 min read 
The Bible teaches that God is unchangeable ([Mal. 3:6](https://ref.ly/Mal.%203.6;nasb95?t=biblia)). This means His divine nature, holiness, goodness, justice, knowledge, etc., do not change in any way. God is the eternal being ([Psalm 90:2](https://ref.ly/Ps%2090.2;nasb95?t=biblia)). Regarding the incarnation, God’s nature did not change when the Word became flesh ([John 1:1](https://ref.ly/John%201.1;nasb95?t=biblia), [14](https://ref.ly/John%201.14;nasb95?t=biblia)) in the person of Jesus. Part of the reason for this is found in the doctrine of the Trinity, where God exists as three distinct and simultaneous persons: The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. God has eternally been a Trinity and still is a Trinity. ![If God is unchangeable, how could He become a man?](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/jesus-in-the-clouds-01.jpg)
Before the Son became flesh, He was known as “The Word,” the second person of the Trinity. [John 1:1](https://ref.ly/John%201.1;nasb95?t=biblia), [14](https://ref.ly/John%201.14;nasb95?t=biblia), “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God . . . 14 And the Word became flesh, and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, glory as of the only begotten from the Father, full of grace and truth.” When the Word became flesh, He was born of the Virgin Mary and was named Jesus. So, it was not the Father or the Holy Spirit who became flesh but the Word alone.
So, the divine Word became in union with the human nature. This union of the two natures is not an alteration of either nature. It is a union. Therefore, they remain distinct, and the divine nature is unchanged while it is in union with the human nature of Jesus.
