How to become a Nazarite  
(and what to do if you are defiled)

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When either man or woman shall separate themselves to vow a vow of a Nazarite, to separate themselves unto the LORD:
3 He shall separate himself from wine and strong drink, and shall drink no vinegar of wine, or vinegar of strong drink, neither shall he drink any liquor of grapes, nor eat moist grapes, or dried.
4 All the days of his separation shall he eat nothing that is made of the vine tree, from the kernels even to the husk.
God said to Moses,

> > The Nazarite will then shave his head and burn his hair as a peace offering.

God likes offerings. Plenty of them have been presented to him within the first four books of Moses. Is there any indication as to what God does with the offerings? ...or are they merely checkpoints for people to symbolically prove their loyalty to God?  
  
According to some, rather than looking at offerings as legally binding rites that must be performed, they are just means for earning God's grace so that the link between God and humanity can be restored. The funny thing is that the system for sacrifice throughout the Old Testament (burnt, trespass, guilt, peace, etc.) was inadequate, so here comes Jesus to be the ultimate end-all sacrifice instead of a bunch of animals over a long period of time. From this, it'd make sense if a believer would say, "God is doing us all a favor by no longer requiring various types of offerings and instead offering to US his only begotten son to pay for all of our sins. See? God's great and merciful!" However, God's actions leave us with more questions than answers.  
  
If this is the case, and God's doing us a solid by sacrificing his son, then what's the point of the resurrection? Is it really a sacrifice of something (or someone...) if it's bound to return in a few days?

### Notes
  1. _When either man or woman shall separate themselves to vow a vow of a Nazarite...._ ([vv.1-27](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/num/6.html#nazarite)) 
If you want to become a Nazarite, here's what you must do - or rather not do: Don't drink alcohol (or vinegar), eat grapes, cut your hair, come near any dead bodies, or make yourself unclean for dead relatives.
[Is it OK to drink alcohol?](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/contra/alcohol.html)
[Is it OK for men to have long hair?](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/contra/hair.html)
  2. _He shall come at no dead body._ ([v.6](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/num/6.html#6)) 
I don't know what "coming at a dead body" might be, but I guess it means to stay away from them.
If so, then it must have been a problem for the Bible's most famous Nazarite -- Samson, since he was a mass murderer and was around lots of dead bodies.
  3. _If any man die very suddenly by him, and he hath defiled the head of his consecration; then he shall shave his head._ ([v.9](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/num/6.html#9)) 
If, after becoming a Nazarite, you defile yourself somehow (like if someone dies right next to you), here's what you must do to become a Nazarite again: Shave your head and sacrfice a couple of pigeons, two unblemished lambs and an unblemished ram, a basket of unleavened bread, cakes and wafers mixed with oil, and some meat and drink offerings.
After you're done with all that, burn the hair that you cut off in the same fire in which you burned the above-mentioned sacrifices.
Finally have a priest take the sodden shoulder of the ram, along with one of the cakes and wafers, put them on your hands, and then wave whole mess before the Lord.
After that you can drink wine and be a Nazarite once again (and then stop drinking wine again).
  4. _The LORD bless thee, and keep thee_ ([v.24](https://www.skepticsannotatedbible.com/num/6.html#24)) 
This blessing is often used by both Christians and Jews in their ceremonies. And it does sound nice. It's the best passage in the book of Numbers, and I would put it in the "good stuff" if it weren't coming from an imaginary (and cruel) God.
