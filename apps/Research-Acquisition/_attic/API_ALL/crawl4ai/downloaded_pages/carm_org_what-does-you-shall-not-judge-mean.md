# What does “You shall not judge” mean?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
August 09, 2014 
5 min read 
Jesus said in [Matthew 7:1](https://ref.ly/Matt%207.1;nasb95?t=biblia), “Do not judge so that you will not be judged.” Many unbelievers quote this verse when Christians say that [homosexuality](https://carm.org/homosexuality-secular-movements/christianity-and-homosexuality/) is a sin, or that adultery is wrong, or tell someone that a person might not be a Christian if, for example, he denies the resurrection of [Christ](https://carm.org/2009/10/15/christ/) ([1 Cor. 15:14](https://ref.ly/1%20Cor.%2015.14;nasb95?t=biblia)). But what do Jesus’ words actually mean? Is He saying that we can’t say anyone is wrong about anything or that someone is not a Christian or that a person is in sin? Considering that the Bible has many verses condemning sinful behavior, we have to consider Jesus’ words more carefully. Before we do that, take a look at these verses that show it is okay to make spiritual judgments.
In [1 Cor. 2:15](https://ref.ly/1%20Cor.%202.15;nasb95?t=biblia) the word “appraises” is ἀνακρίνω, _anakrino_. It means, “This word means ‘to investigate,’ and is used of judicial investigation, especially prior to the hearing proper.” _(Kittel, Gerhard, Geoffrey W. Bromiley, and Gerhard Friedrich, eds. Theological Dictionary of the New Testament. Grand Rapids, MI: Eerdmans, 1964–.)_ The verse immediately preceding it says, “But a natural man does not accept the things of the Spirit of God, for they are foolishness to him; and he cannot understand them, because they are spiritually appraised,” ([1 Cor. 2:14](https://ref.ly/1%20Cor.%202.14;nasb95?t=biblia)). So, spiritual things can be judged by Christians.
We see this when Paul talks about various sins in [Galatians 5: 19-21](https://ref.ly/Gal%205.%2019-21;nasb95?t=biblia) and makes a judgment that those who practice these things will not be saved.
