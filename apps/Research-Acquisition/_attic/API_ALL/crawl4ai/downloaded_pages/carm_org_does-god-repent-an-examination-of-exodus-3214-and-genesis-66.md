# Does God repent? An examination of Exodus 32:14 and Genesis 6:6
by [Matt Halsted](https://carm.org/matt-halsted)
November 22, 2010 
14 min read 
**Does God repent? An examination of[Exodus 32:14](https://ref.ly/Exod%2032.14;nasb95?t=biblia) and [Genesis 6:6](https://ref.ly/Gen%206.6;nasb95?t=biblia)**
Christianity’s most important doctrine is, of course, the doctrine of God. Who God is, what He has done, and what He will do in the future is the foundation for all of Christian hope. Therefore, for one to diminish the nature and character of God is to diminish all of Christian hope. To be sure, God _is_ our hope. In today’s theologically diversified climate, to lay a truth claim upon a particular doctrine (especially the “doctrine of God”) is tantamount to being “intolerant” toward other theistic positions. It carries within it the idea of exclusivism, where no other views are allowed to be considered. Despite these charges, it is the responsibility of the evangelical Christian to be committed to the teaching of Holy Scripture concerning the nature and character of God. As is obvious, to compromise on this issue would be to weaken our Christian hope and faith. Therefore, the stakes are high. ![Does God repent](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/bible-jpg-01-300x270.jpg)
The main thesis of this article is to examine, as well as refute, the charge from many non-traditional theists that God, indeed, changes His mind (i.e., “repents”). Using such passages as [Exodus 32:14](https://ref.ly/Exod%2032.14;nasb95?t=biblia) and [Genesis 6:6](https://ref.ly/Gen%206.6;nasb95?t=biblia) to defend their beliefs, many have fallen into error concerning the nature of God and His ways of working throughout redemptive history.
