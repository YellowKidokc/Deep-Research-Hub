# What does the Bible say about gambling?
by [Matt Slick](https://carm.org/matt-slick)
October 16, 2010 
3 min read 
![What does the Bible say about gambling?](https://carm.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/gambling.jpg) The Bible does not directly mention gambling for money. Neither does it provide an exhaustive list of forbidden behaviors in several areas. That is not the Bible’s purpose. Its purpose is to draw people to God. Anyone who finds himself asking, “Is it okay to…” ought to first question his motives, especially since the Bible says “…whatever is not from faith is sin” ([Rom. 14:23](https://ref.ly/Rom.%2014.23;nasb95?t=biblia)).
Gambling is leaving to chance what you should trust in our Lord. Christians should trust in the providence of God and not in “chance” to provide for them. This is why gambling cannot be endorsed biblically.
[Matt. 6:19-21](https://ref.ly/Matt.%206.19-21;nasb95?t=biblia) says, “Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy, and where thieves break in and steal. But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where moth and rust do not destroy, and where thieves do not break in and steal. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.” Why do you want to gamble? To provide for your needs? Jesus continues, saying, “seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well” ([Matt. 6:33](https://ref.ly/Matt.%206.33;nasb95?t=biblia)). Gambling can, at times, betray a lack of trust in God, that he will provide. Or is it merely that you want more money for the pursuit of pleasure? [Ecc. 5:10](https://ref.ly/Eccles.%205.10;nasb95?t=biblia) observes that “Whoever loves money never has money enough.” But he who loves God first receives what he truly wishes, joy ([Psalm 37:4](https://ref.ly/Ps%2037.4;nasb95?t=biblia)), which outlasts fleeting pleasures, the treasures that Jesus warned us about storing. Ultimately, one must ask himself, who am I serving, God or money ([Matthew 6:24](https://ref.ly/Matt%206.24;nasb95?t=biblia))? We cannot serve both.
