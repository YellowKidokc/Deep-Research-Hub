Pseudoscholarship that attempts to distort historical record
Not to be confused with [Counterfactual history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterfactual_history "Counterfactual history") or [Alternate history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternate_history "Alternate history").
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Members_of_a_Sonderkommando_1005_unit_pose_next_to_a_bone_crushing_machine_in_the_Janowska_concentration_camp.png/250px-Members_of_a_Sonderkommando_1005_unit_pose_next_to_a_bone_crushing_machine_in_the_Janowska_concentration_camp.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Members_of_a_Sonderkommando_1005_unit_pose_next_to_a_bone_crushing_machine_in_the_Janowska_concentration_camp.png)_[Sonderaktion 1005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonderaktion_1005 "Sonderaktion 1005")_ was a [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") project with the explicit goal of hiding or destroying any evidence of the [mass murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_murder "Mass murder") committed under [Operation Reinhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Reinhard "Operation Reinhard"). This was one of the earliest attempts at [Holocaust denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_denial "Holocaust denial"), taking place while the [genocide of the Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Solution "Final Solution") was still ongoing. Scholars consider [denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_denial "Genocide denial") to be an integral part of [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide "Genocide") itself.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-1) [![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Battle_flag_of_the_Confederate_States_of_America_%283-5%29.svg/250px-Battle_flag_of_the_Confederate_States_of_America_%283-5%29.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Battle_flag_of_the_Confederate_States_of_America_\(3-5\).svg)The [Lost Cause of the Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Cause_of_the_Confederacy "Lost Cause of the Confederacy") is a [negationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_negationism "Historical negationism") ideology which falsely claims that the spread of [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States "Slavery in the United States") was not the [central cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origins_of_the_American_Civil_War "Origins of the American Civil War") of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"). [![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Ermeniler_Taraf%C4%B1ndan_Katledilen_%C5%9Eehit_T%C3%BCrkler_AM.jpg/250px-Ermeniler_Taraf%C4%B1ndan_Katledilen_%C5%9Eehit_T%C3%BCrkler_AM.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ermeniler_Taraf%C4%B1ndan_Katledilen_%C5%9Eehit_T%C3%BCrkler_AM.jpg)The [Iğdır Genocide Memorial and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C4%9Fd%C4%B1r_Genocide_Memorial_and_Museum "Iğdır Genocide Memorial and Museum") in [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey") promotes the false narrative that [Armenians committed genocide against Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide_denial "Armenian genocide denial"), rather than vice versa.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Igdir-2)
**Pseudohistory** is a form of [pseudoscholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscholarship "Pseudoscholarship") that attempts to distort or misrepresent the [historical record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorded_history "Recorded history"), often by employing methods resembling those used in scholarly [historical research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History "History"). The related term **cryptohistory** is applied to pseudohistory derived from the [superstitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstition "Superstition") intrinsic to [occultism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occult "Occult"). Pseudohistory is related to [pseudoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience "Pseudoscience") and [pseudoarchaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoarchaeology "Pseudoarchaeology"), and usage of the terms may occasionally overlap. 
Although pseudohistory comes in many forms, scholars have identified common features in pseudohistorical works. Pseudohistory is almost always motivated by a contemporary [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_agenda "Political agenda"), religious, or personal agenda. It frequently presents sensational claims or a [big lie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_lie "Big lie") about historical facts which would require unwarranted [revision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_revisionism "Historical revisionism") of the historical record.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-3) Another hallmark is an underlying premise that powerful groups have a [furtive agenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furtive_fallacy "Furtive fallacy") to suppress the promoter's thesis—a premise commonly corroborated by elaborate [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory "Conspiracy theory"). Works of pseudohistory often point exclusively to unreliable sources—including [myths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myth "Myth") and [legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend "Legend"), often treated as literal historical truth—to support the thesis being promoted while [ignoring valid sources that contradict it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_pleading "Special pleading"). Some works adopt a position of historical [relativism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativism "Relativism"), insisting that there is no such thing as historical truth and that any hypothesis is equal to any other. Many works conflate mere possibility with actuality, assuming that if something _could_ have happened, then it did. 
Notable examples of pseudohistory include [British Israelism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Israelism "British Israelism"), the [Lost Cause of the Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Cause_of_the_Confederacy "Lost Cause of the Confederacy"), the [Irish slaves myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_slaves_myth "Irish slaves myth"), the [witch-cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witch-cult_hypothesis "Witch-cult hypothesis"), [Armenian genocide denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide_denial "Armenian genocide denial"), [Holocaust denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_denial "Holocaust denial"), the [clean Wehrmacht myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myth_of_the_clean_Wehrmacht "Myth of the clean Wehrmacht"), and the claim that the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre "Katyn massacre") was not committed by the Soviet [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD "NKVD"). 
## Definition and etymology
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Definition and etymology")]
The term _pseudohistory_ was coined in the early nineteenth century, which makes the word older than the related terms _[pseudo-scholarship](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pseudo-scholarship "wikt:pseudo-scholarship")_ and _[pseudoscience](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pseudo-science "wikt:pseudo-science")_.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-4) In an attestation from 1815, it is used to refer to the _[Contest of Homer and Hesiod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contest_of_Homer_and_Hesiod "Contest of Homer and Hesiod")_ , a purportedly historical narrative describing an entirely fictional contest between the Greek poets [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer "Homer") and [Hesiod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesiod "Hesiod").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-5) The pejorative sense of the term, labelling a flawed or disingenuous work of historiography, is found in another 1815 attestation.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-6) Pseudohistory is akin to pseudoscience in that both forms of falsification are achieved using the methodology that purports to, but does not, adhere to the established standards of research for the given field of intellectual enquiry of which the pseudoscience claims to be a part, and which offers little or no supporting evidence for its plausibility.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7): 7–18 
Writers [Michael Shermer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Shermer "Michael Shermer") and [Alex Grobman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Grobman "Alex Grobman") define pseudohistory as "the rewriting of the past for present personal or political purposes".[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Shermer-8): 2  Other writers take a broader definition; Douglas Allchin, a historian of science, contends that when the history of scientific discovery is presented in a simplified way, with drama exaggerated and scientists romanticized, this creates wrong stereotypes about how science works, and in fact constitutes pseudohistory, despite being based on real facts.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-9)
## Characteristics
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Characteristics")]
[Robert Todd Carroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Todd_Carroll "Robert Todd Carroll") has developed a list of criteria to identify pseudo-historic works. He states that: 
> Pseudohistory is purported history which: 
>   * Treats myths, legends, sagas and similar literature as literal truth
>   * Is neither critical nor skeptical in its reading of ancient historians, taking their claims at face value and ignoring empirical or logical evidence contrary to the claims of the ancients
>   * Is on a mission, not a quest, seeking to support some contemporary political or religious agenda rather than find out the truth about the past
>   * Often denies that there is such a thing as historical truth, clinging to the extreme skeptical notion that only what is absolutely certain can be called 'true' and nothing is absolutely certain, so nothing is true
>   * Often maintains that history is nothing but mythmaking and that different histories are not to be compared on such traditional academic standards as accuracy, empirical probability, logical consistency, relevancy, completeness, fairness or honesty, but on moral or political grounds
>   * Is selective in its use of ancient documents, citing favorably those that fit with its agenda, and ignoring or interpreting away those documents which do not fit
>   * Considers the possibility of something being true as sufficient to believe it is true if it fits with one's agenda
>   * Often maintains that there is a conspiracy to suppress its claims because of racism, atheism or ethnocentrism, or because of opposition to its political or religious agenda[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-10)
> 

[Nicholas Goodrick-Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Goodrick-Clarke "Nicholas Goodrick-Clarke") prefers the term "cryptohistory". He identifies two necessary elements as "a complete ignorance of the primary sources" and the repetition of "inaccuracies and wild claims".[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-12)
Other common characteristics of pseudohistory are: 
  * The arbitrary linking of disparate events so as to form – in the theorist's opinion – a pattern. This is typically then developed into a [conspiracy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory "Conspiracy theory") postulating a hidden agent responsible for creating and maintaining the pattern. For example, the pseudohistorical _[The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holy_Blood_and_the_Holy_Grail "The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail")_ links the [Knights Templar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Templar "Knights Templar"), the medieval [Grail Romances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Grail "Holy Grail"), the [Merovingian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merovingian "Merovingian") Frankish dynasty and the artist [Nicolas Poussin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Poussin "Nicolas Poussin") in an attempt to identify lineal descendants of Jesus.
  * Hypothesising the consequences of unlikely events that "could" have happened, thereby assuming tacitly that they did.
  * [Sensationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensationalism "Sensationalism"), or [shock value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_value "Shock value")
  * [Cherry picking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_picking "Cherry picking"), or "law office history", evidence that helps the historical argument being made and suppressing evidence that hurts it.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-13)


## Categories and examples
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Categories and examples")]
Further information: [Historical negationism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_negationism "Historical negationism") and [Pseudoarchaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoarchaeology "Pseudoarchaeology")
The following are some common categories of pseudohistorical theory, with examples. Not all theories in a listed category are necessarily pseudohistorical; they are rather categories that seem to attract pseudohistorians. 
### Main categories
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Main categories")]
#### Alternative chronologies
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Alternative chronologies")]
An alternative [chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology "Chronology") is a revised sequence of events that deviates from the standard timeline of world history accepted by mainstream scholars. An example of an "alternative chronology" is [Anatoly Fomenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Fomenko "Anatoly Fomenko")'s [New Chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Chronology_\(Fomenko\) "New Chronology \(Fomenko\)"), which claims that recorded history actually began around AD 800 and all events that allegedly occurred prior to that point either never really happened at all or are simply inaccurate retellings of events that happened later.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-14) One of its outgrowths is the [Tartary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire_\(conspiracy_theory\) "Tartarian Empire \(conspiracy theory\)") conspiracy theory. Other, less extreme examples, are the [phantom time hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_hypothesis "Phantom time hypothesis"), which asserts that the years AD 614–911 never took place; and the [New Chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Chronology_\(Rohl\) "New Chronology \(Rohl\)") of [David Rohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rohl "David Rohl"), which claims that the accepted timelines for ancient Egyptian and Israelite history are wrong.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-15)
#### Historical falsification
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Historical falsification")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Vortigern-Dragons.jpg/330px-Vortigern-Dragons.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Vortigern-Dragons.jpg)[Geoffrey of Monmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Monmouth "Geoffrey of Monmouth")'s _[History of the Kings of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historia_Regum_Britanniae "Historia Regum Britanniae")_ , a scene from which is shown in this fifteenth-century illumination, was a popular work of pseudohistory during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages").
In the eighth century, a forged document known as [Donation of Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donation_of_Constantine "Donation of Constantine"), which supposedly transferred authority over Rome and the western part of the Roman Empire to the [Pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope "Pope"), became widely circulated.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-16) In the twelfth century, [Geoffrey of Monmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Monmouth "Geoffrey of Monmouth") published the _[History of the Kings of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historia_Regum_Britanniae "Historia Regum Britanniae")_ , a pseudohistorical work purporting to describe the ancient history and origins of the British people. The book synthesises earlier Celtic mythical traditions to inflate the deeds of the mythical [King Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Arthur "King Arthur"). The contemporary historian [William of Newburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_of_Newburgh "William of Newburgh") wrote around 1190 that "it is quite clear that everything this man wrote about Arthur and his successors, or indeed about his predecessors from [Vortigern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vortigern "Vortigern") onwards, was made up, partly by himself and partly by others".[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-17)
#### Historical revisionism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Historical revisionism")]
The [Shakespeare authorship question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare_authorship_question "Shakespeare authorship question") is a [fringe theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fringe_theory "Fringe theory") that claims that the works attributed to [William Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare "William Shakespeare") were actually written by someone other than William Shakespeare of [Stratford-upon-Avon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratford-upon-Avon "Stratford-upon-Avon").[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-18)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-19)[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-20)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-21)
Another example of historical revisionism is the thesis, found in the writings of [David Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Barton_\(author\) "David Barton \(author\)") and others, asserting that the United States was founded as an exclusively [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") nation.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-22)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-23)[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Pierard-24) Mainstream historians instead support the traditional position, which holds that the American founding fathers [intended for church and state to be kept separate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state_in_the_United_States "Separation of church and state in the United States").[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-:1-25)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-:2-26)
Confederate revisionists (a.k.a. Civil War revisionists), "[Lost Cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Cause_of_the_Confederacy "Lost Cause of the Confederacy")" advocates, and [Neo-Confederates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confederate "Neo-Confederate") argue that the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America "Confederate States of America")'s prime motivation was the maintenance of [states' rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States%27_rights "States' rights") and limited government, rather than the preservation and expansion of [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States "Slavery in the United States").[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-27)[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-28)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-29)
Connected to the Lost Cause is the [Irish slaves myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_slaves_myth "Irish slaves myth"), a pseudo-historical narrative which conflates the experiences of [Irish indentured servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_indentured_servants "Irish indentured servants") and [enslaved Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade") in the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas"). This myth, which was historically promoted by [Irish nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_nationalism "Irish nationalism") such as [John Mitchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mitchel "John Mitchel"), has in the modern-day been promoted by [white supremacists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_supremacy "White supremacy") in the United States to minimize the mistreatment experienced by [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans") (such as [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism_in_the_United_States "Racism in the United States") and [segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States "Racial segregation in the United States")) and oppose demands for [slavery reparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reparations_for_slavery_in_the_United_States "Reparations for slavery in the United States"). The myth has also been used to obscure and downplay Irish involvement in the [transatlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade").[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-30)[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-31)
#### Historical negationism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Historical negationism")]
While closely related to previous categories, [historical negationism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_negationism "Historical negationism"), or denialism, specifically aims to outright deny the existence of confirmed events, often including various massacres, genocides, and [national histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism "Nationalism"). 
Some examples include [Holocaust denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_denial "Holocaust denial"), [Armenian genocide denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide_denial "Armenian genocide denial"),[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-32) as well as [Nanjing Massacre denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Massacre_denial "Nanjing Massacre denial") and [Nakba denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba_denial "Nakba denial") in the 1984 work _[From Time Immemorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_Time_Immemorial "From Time Immemorial")_ by [Joan Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Peters "Joan Peters").[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-33)
#### Psychohistory
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Psychohistory")]
Main article: [Psychohistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychohistory "Psychohistory")
Mainstream historians have categorized psychohistory as pseudohistory.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-34)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-35) Psychohistory is an amalgam of psychology, history, and related social sciences and the humanities.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-36) Its stated goal is to examine the "why" of history, especially the difference between stated intention and actual behavior. It also states as its goal the combination of the insights of psychology, especially [psychoanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoanalysis "Psychoanalysis"), with the research methodology of the [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_sciences "Social sciences") and humanities to understand the emotional origin of the behavior of individuals, groups and nations, past and present. 
#### Pseudoarchaeology
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Pseudoarchaeology")]
[Pseudoarchaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoarchaeology "Pseudoarchaeology") refers to a false interpretation of records, namely physical ones, often by unqualified or otherwise amateur archeologists. These interpretations are often baseless and seldom align with established consensus. Nazi archaeology is a prominent example of this technique.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-37) Frequently, people who engage in pseudoarchaeology have a very strict interpretation of evidence and are unwilling to alter their stance, resulting in interpretations that often appear overly simplistic and fail to capture the complexity and nuance of the complete narrative.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-38)
### Various examples of pseudohistory
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Various examples of pseudohistory")]
(These following examples can belong to a variety of the above mentioned categories, or ones not mentioned as well). 
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Ancient aliens, ancient technologies, and lost lands")]
Main article: [Ancient astronauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_astronauts "Ancient astronauts")
[Immanuel Velikovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Velikovsky "Immanuel Velikovsky")'s books _[Worlds in Collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worlds_in_Collision "Worlds in Collision")_ (1950), _[Ages in Chaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ages_in_Chaos "Ages in Chaos")_ (1952), and _Earth in Upheaval_ (1955), which became "instant bestsellers",[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7) demonstrated that pseudohistory based on ancient mythology held potential for tremendous financial success[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7) and became models of success for future works in the genre.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7)
In 1968, [Erich von Däniken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_von_D%C3%A4niken "Erich von Däniken") published _[Chariots of the Gods?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariots_of_the_Gods%3F "Chariots of the Gods?")_ , which claims that ancient visitors from outer space constructed the pyramids and other monuments. He has since published other books in which he makes similar claims. These claims have all been categorized as pseudohistory.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7): 201  Similarly, [Zechariah Sitchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zechariah_Sitchin "Zechariah Sitchin") has published numerous books claiming that a race of extraterrestrial beings from the [Planet Nibiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nibiru_cataclysm#Nancy_Lieder_and_ZetaTalk "Nibiru cataclysm") known as the [Anunnaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anunnaki "Anunnaki") visited Earth in ancient times in search of gold, and that they genetically engineered humans to serve as their slaves. He claims that memories of these occurrences are recorded in [Sumerian mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumerian_religion "Sumerian religion"), as well as other mythologies all across the globe. These speculations have likewise been categorized as pseudohistory.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-heiser-39)[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Skepdic-40)
The ancient astronaut hypothesis was further popularized in the United States by the [History Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_\(U.S._TV_network\) "History \(U.S. TV network\)") television series _[Ancient Aliens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Aliens_\(TV_series\) "Ancient Aliens \(TV series\)")_.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze2009-41) History professor [Ronald H. Fritze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_H._Fritze "Ronald H. Fritze") observed that the pseudohistorical claims promoted by von Däniken and the _Ancient Aliens_ program have a periodic popularity in the US:[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7)[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Rorotoko-42) "In a pop culture with a short memory and a voracious appetite, aliens and pyramids and lost civilizations are recycled like fashions."[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7): 201 [[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Rorotoko-42)
The author [Graham Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Hancock "Graham Hancock") has sold over four million copies of books promoting the pseudohistorical thesis that all the major monuments of the ancient world, including [Stonehenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehenge "Stonehenge"), the [Egyptian pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramids "Egyptian pyramids"), and the [moai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moai "Moai") of [Easter Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Island "Easter Island"), were built by a single ancient supercivilization,[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-43) which Hancock claims thrived from 15,000 to 10,000 BC and possessed technological and scientific knowledge equal to or surpassing that of modern civilization.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7) He first advanced the full form of this argument in his 1995 bestseller _[Fingerprints of the Gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerprints_of_the_Gods "Fingerprints of the Gods")_ ,[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7) which won popular acclaim, but scholarly disdain.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7) [Christopher Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Knight_\(author\) "Christopher Knight \(author\)") has published numerous books, including _[Uriel's Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uriel%27s_Machine "Uriel's Machine")_ (2000), expounding pseudohistorical assertions that ancient civilizations possessed technology far more advanced than the technology of today.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-44)[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-45)[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-46)[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-47)
The claim that a lost continent known as [Lemuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria_\(continent\) "Lemuria \(continent\)") once existed in the Pacific Ocean has likewise been categorized as pseudohistory.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7): 11 
Furthermore, similar conspiracy theories promote the idea of embellished, fabricated accounts of historical civilizations, namely [Khazaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazar_hypothesis_of_Ashkenazi_ancestry "Khazar hypothesis of Ashkenazi ancestry") and [Tartaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire "Tartarian Empire"). 
#### Antisemitic pseudohistory
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Antisemitic pseudohistory")]
See also: [Blood libel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_libel "Blood libel")
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/1934_Protocols_Patriotic_Pub.jpg/250px-1934_Protocols_Patriotic_Pub.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1934_Protocols_Patriotic_Pub.jpg)American edition of _[The Protocols of the Elders of Zion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Protocols_of_the_Elders_of_Zion "The Protocols of the Elders of Zion")_ from 1934
_[The Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Protocols_of_the_Learned_Elders_of_Zion "The Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion")_ is a fraudulent work purporting to show a historical conspiracy for world domination by Jews.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-ushmm.org-48) The work was conclusively proven to be a forgery in August 1921, when _[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")_ revealed that extensive portions of the document were directly plagiarized from [Maurice Joly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Joly "Maurice Joly")'s 1864 satirical dialogue _[The Dialogue in Hell Between Machiavelli and Montesquieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dialogue_in_Hell_Between_Machiavelli_and_Montesquieu "The Dialogue in Hell Between Machiavelli and Montesquieu")_ ,[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-49) as well as [Hermann Goedsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Goedsche "Hermann Goedsche")'s 1868 anti-Semitic novel _Biarritz_.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-translated97-50)
The [Khazar theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazar_hypothesis_of_Ashkenazi_ancestry "Khazar hypothesis of Ashkenazi ancestry") is an academic [fringe theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fringe_theory "Fringe theory") that postulates the belief that the bulk of [European Jewry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Europe "History of the Jews in Europe") is of [Central Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") ([Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples "Turkic peoples")) origin. In spite of the [mainstream academic consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_studies_on_Jews "Genetic studies on Jews") which conclusively rejects it, this theory has been promoted in [Anti-Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism") and some [Anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism "Anti-Zionism") circles, they argue that Jews are an alien element in both Europe and [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_and_Judaism_in_the_Land_of_Israel "History of the Jews and Judaism in the Land of Israel"). 
[Holocaust denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_denial "Holocaust denial") in particular and [genocide denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_denial "Genocide denial") in general are widely categorized as pseudohistory.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Shermer-8): 237 [[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Lipstadt-51) Major proponents of Holocaust denial include [David Irving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Irving "David Irving") and others, who argue that the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust"), the [Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor "Holodomor"), the [Armenian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide "Armenian genocide"), the [Assyrian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayfo "Sayfo"), the [Greek genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_genocide "Greek genocide") and [other genocides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocides_in_history "Genocides in history") did not occur, or accounts of them were greatly exaggerated.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Lipstadt-51)
#### Ethnocentric or nationalist revisionism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Ethnocentric or nationalist revisionism")]
See also: [Historiography and nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography_and_nationalism "Historiography and nationalism") and [National mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_mysticism "National mysticism")
Most [Afrocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrocentrism "Afrocentrism") (i.e. [Pre-Columbian Africa-Americas contact theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_Africa-Americas_contact_theories "Pre-Columbian Africa-Americas contact theories"), see [Ancient Egyptian race controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_race_controversy "Ancient Egyptian race controversy")) ideas have been identified as pseudohistorical,[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-52)[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Ortiz1997-53) alongside the "[Indigenous Aryans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Aryans#Pseudoscience_and_postmodernism "Indigenous Aryans")" theories published by [Hindu nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_nationalists "Hindu nationalists") during the 1990s and 2000s.[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-54) The "crypto-history" developed within [Germanic mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariosophy "Ariosophy") and [Nazi occultism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_occultism "Nazi occultism") has likewise been placed under this categorization.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-55)[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-56)
The [Sun Language Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Language_Theory "Sun Language Theory") is a pseudohistorical ideology which argues that all languages are descended from a form of proto-Turkish.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-57) The theory may have been partially devised in order to legitimize Arabic and Semitic loanwords occurring in the Turkish language by instead asserting that the Arabic and Semitic words were derived from the Turkish ones rather than vice versa.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-58)
A large number of nationalist pseudohistorical theories deal with the legendary [Ten Lost Tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Lost_Tribes "Ten Lost Tribes") of ancient Israel. [British-Israelism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Israelism#Claims_and_criticism "British Israelism"), also known as Anglo-Israelism, the most famous example of this type, has been conclusively refuted by mainstream historians using evidence from a vast array of different fields of study.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-59)[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-60)[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-61)
[Antiquization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquization "Antiquization") or Ancient Macedonism is a nationalistic pseudohistorical theory which postulates direct demographic, cultural and linguistic continuity between [ancient Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Macedonians "Ancient Macedonians") and the [main ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonians_\(ethnic_group\) "Macedonians \(ethnic group\)") in present-day [North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Macedonia "North Macedonia").[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-62)[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-63) The [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarians "Bulgarians") medieval dynasty of [the Komitopules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cometopuli_dynasty "Cometopuli dynasty"), which ruled the [First Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire "First Bulgarian Empire") in late 10th and early 11th centuries AD, is presented as "Macedonian", ruling a "medieval Macedonian state", because its capitals were located in what was previously the [ancient kingdom of Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(ancient_kingdom\) "Macedonia \(ancient kingdom\)").[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-64) [North Macedonian historians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography_in_North_Macedonia "Historiography in North Macedonia") often replace the ethnonym "Bulgarians" with "Macedonians", or avoid it.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-65)[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-66) North Macedonian scholars say the theory is intended to forge a national identity distinct from modern [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria "Bulgaria"), which regards North Macedonia as an artificial nation.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-nyt-macedonia-67) The theory is controversial in [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece") and sparked mass protests there in 2018.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-nyt-prespa-68) A particular item of dispute is North Macedonian veneration of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great"); mainstream scholarship holds that Alexander had Greek ancestry, he was born in an area of ancient Macedonia that is now Greece, and he ruled over North Macedonia but never lived there and did not speak the local language.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-nyt-macedonia-67)[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-69) To placate Greece and thereby facilitate Macedonian entry into the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") and [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO"), the Macedonian government formally renounced claims of ancient Macedonian heritage with the 2018 [Prespa Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prespa_Agreement "Prespa Agreement").[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-nyt-macedonia-67)[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-nyt-prespa-68)
[Dacianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacianism "Dacianism") is a Romanian pseudohistorical current that attempts to attribute far more influence over European and world history to the [Dacians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacians "Dacians") than that which they actually enjoyed.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-70) Dacianist historiography claims that the Dacians held primacy over all other civilizations, including the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_people "Roman people");[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-71) that the [Dacian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacian_language "Dacian language") was the origin of [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") and all other languages, such as [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi") and [Babylonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language "Akkadian language");[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-George_Pruteanu-72) and sometimes that the [Zalmoxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalmoxis "Zalmoxis") cult has structural links to Christianity.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-73) Dacianism was most prevalent in [National Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Communism_in_Romania "National Communism in Romania") [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania "Socialist Republic of Romania"), as the [Ceaușescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ceau%C8%99escu "Nicolae Ceaușescu") regime portrayed the Dacians as insurgents defying an "imperialist" Rome; the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Communist_Party "Romanian Communist Party") had formally attached "protochronism", as Dacianism was known, to [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism "Marxism") ideology by 1974.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-74)
#### Matriarchy
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Matriarchy")]
Main article: [Matriarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriarchy "Matriarchy")
The consensus among academics is that no unambiguously and strictly matriarchal society is known to have existed. Many societies are known, however, to have or have had some matriarchal features, in particular [matrilineality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrilineality "Matrilineality"), [matrilocality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrilocality "Matrilocality"), and/or [matrifocality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrifocality "Matrifocality").[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-75)[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-76) Anthropologist [Donald Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Brown_\(anthropologist\) "Donald Brown \(anthropologist\)")'s list of [human cultural universals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_universal "Cultural universal") (_viz._ , features shared by nearly all current human societies) includes men being the "dominant element" in public political affairs,[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-77) which is the contemporary opinion of mainstream [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology").[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-:0-78) Some societies that are matrilineal or matrifocal may in fact have [patriarchal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchal "Patriarchal") power structures, and thus be misidentified as matriarchal. The idea that matriarchal societies existed and they preceded patriarchal societies was first raised in the 19th-century among Western academics, but it has since been discredited.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-:0-78)
Despite this however, some [second-wave feminists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-wave_feminist "Second-wave feminist") assert that a matriarchy preceded the patriarchy. The [Goddess Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddess_Movement "Goddess Movement") and Riane Eisler's 1987 book _[The Chalice and the Blade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chalice_and_the_Blade "The Chalice and the Blade")_ cite [Venus figurines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_figurines "Venus figurines") as evidence that societies of [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic "Paleolithic") and [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") Europe were matriarchies that worshipped a goddess. This belief is not supported by mainstream academics.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Nelson-79)
#### Pre-Columbian trans-oceanic contact theories
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Pre-Columbian trans-oceanic contact theories")]
Main article: [Pre-Columbian trans-oceanic contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_trans-oceanic_contact "Pre-Columbian trans-oceanic contact")
Excluding the [Norse colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_colonization_of_the_Americas "Norse colonization of the Americas"), most theories of [pre-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era "Pre-Columbian era") trans-oceanic contact have been classified as pseudohistory, including claims that the Americas were actually discovered by Arabs or Muslims.[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-80) [Gavin Menzies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Menzies "Gavin Menzies")' book _[1421: The Year China Discovered the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1421:_The_Year_China_Discovered_the_World "1421: The Year China Discovered the World")_ , which argues for the idea that Chinese sailors discovered America, has also been categorized as a work of pseudohistory.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7): 11 
#### Racist pseudohistory
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Racist pseudohistory")]
[Josiah Priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josiah_Priest "Josiah Priest") and other nineteenth-century American writers wrote pseudohistorical narratives that portrayed [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans") and [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States "Native Americans in the United States") in an extremely negative light.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Williams-81) Priest's first book was _The Wonders of Nature and Providence, Displayed_ (1826).[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-82)[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Williams-81) The book is regarded by modern critics as one of the earliest works of modern American pseudohistory.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Williams-81) Priest attacked Native Americans in _American Antiquities and Discoveries of the West_ (1833)[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-83)[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Williams-81) and African-Americans in _Slavery, As It Relates to the Negro_ (1843).[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-84)[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Williams-81) Other nineteenth-century writers, such as [Thomas Gold Appleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gold_Appleton "Thomas Gold Appleton"), in his _A Sheaf of Papers_ (1875), and [George Perkins Marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Perkins_Marsh "George Perkins Marsh"), in his _The Goths in New England_ , seized upon false notions of [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings "Vikings") history to promote the superiority of [white people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people "White people") (as well as to oppose the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church")). Such misuse of Viking history and imagery reemerged in the twentieth century among some groups promoting [white supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_supremacy "White supremacy").[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-85)
#### Stalinist pseudohistory
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Stalinist pseudohistory")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Example_of_pseudohistory.gif/250px-Example_of_pseudohistory.gif)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Example_of_pseudohistory.gif)[League of Struggle for the Emancipation of the Working Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Struggle_for_the_Emancipation_of_the_Working_Class "League of Struggle for the Emancipation of the Working Class"), 1897, shortly before the arrest by the Russian secret service. Lenin is sitting in the center. After [Aleksandr Malchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Aleksandr_Malchenko&action=edit&redlink=1 "Aleksandr Malchenko \(page does not exist\)") [[ru](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%87%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%BA%D0%BE,_%D0%90%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B4%D1%80_%D0%9B%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%8C%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87 "ru:Малченко, Александр Леонтьевич")] fell out of favor with Stalin in 1930, he was removed from the picture.
Supporters of [Stalinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinism "Stalinism") pseudohistory claim, among other things, that [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin "Joseph Stalin") and other top Soviet leaders did not realize the scope of mass killings [perpetrated under the Stalin regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinism#Purges_and_executions "Stalinism"), that executions of prisoners were legally justifiable, and that prisoners in Soviet [gulags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulag "Gulag") performed important construction work that helped the Soviet Union economically, particularly during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). Scholars point to overwhelming evidence that Stalin directly helped plan mass killings, that many prisoners were sent to gulags or executed extrajudicially, and that many prisoners did no productive work, often being isolated in remote camps or given pointless and menial tasks.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-86)
#### Anti-religious pseudohistory
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Anti-religious pseudohistory")]
See also: [Bible conspiracy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_conspiracy_theory "Bible conspiracy theory") and [Christ myth theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_myth_theory "Christ myth theory")
The [Christ myth theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_myth_theory "Christ myth theory") claims that [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus") of Nazareth never existed as a historical figure and was imagined by early Christians or arose from earlier beliefs such as [star worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worship_of_heavenly_bodies "Worship of heavenly bodies"). This argument currently finds very little support among scholars and historians of all faiths and has been described as pseudohistorical.[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Ehrman285-87)[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-88)[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Powell1998-89)
Likewise, some minority historian views assert that [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad "Muhammad") either did not exist or was [not central to founding Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historicity_of_Muhammad#Minority_views_\(Muhammad_as_mythical_figure\) "Historicity of Muhammad").[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-90)
#### Religious pseudohistory
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pseudohistory&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Religious pseudohistory")]
_[The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Blood,_Holy_Grail "Holy Blood, Holy Grail")_ (1982) by [Michael Baigent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Baigent "Michael Baigent"), [Richard Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Leigh_\(author\) "Richard Leigh \(author\)"), and [Henry Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Lincoln "Henry Lincoln") is a book that purports to show that certain historical figures, such as [Godfrey of Bouillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godfrey_of_Bouillon "Godfrey of Bouillon"), and contemporary aristocrats are the lineal descendants of [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus"). Mainstream historians have widely panned the book, categorizing it as pseudohistory,[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-91)[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-92)[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Miller2004-93) and pointing out that the genealogical tables used in it are now known to be spurious.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-94) Nonetheless, the book was an international best-seller[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Miller2004-93) and inspired [Dan Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Brown "Dan Brown")'s bestselling mystery [thriller novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_\(genre\) "Thriller \(genre\)") _[The Da Vinci Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Da_Vinci_Code "The Da Vinci Code")_.[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Miller2004-93)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Fritze-7): 2–3 
Although historians and archaeologists consider the [Book of Mormon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Mormon "Book of Mormon") to be an anachronistic invention of Joseph Smith, many members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS Church) believe that it describes [ancient historical events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historicity_of_the_Book_of_Mormon "Historicity of the Book of Mormon") in the Americas. 
[Searches for Noah's Ark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Searches_for_Noah%27s_Ark "Searches for Noah's Ark") have also been categorized as pseudohistory.[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-95)[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Rough_Guides-96)[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-97)
In her books, starting with _[The Witch-Cult in Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witch-Cult_in_Western_Europe "The Witch-Cult in Western Europe")_ (1921), English author [Margaret Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Murray "Margaret Murray") claimed that the [witch trials in the early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witch_trials_in_the_early_modern_period "Witch trials in the early modern period") were actually an attempt by chauvinistic Christians to annihilate a [secret, pagan religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witch-cult_hypothesis "Witch-cult hypothesis"),[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Purkiss1996-98) which she claimed worshipped a [Horned God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horned_God "Horned God").[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Purkiss1996-98) Murray's claims have now been widely rejected by respected historians.[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-99)[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Simpson1994-100)[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Purkiss1996-98) Nonetheless, her ideas have become the [foundation myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_myth "Origin myth") for modern [Wicca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicca "Wicca"), a contemporary [Neopagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_paganism "Modern paganism") religion.[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Simpson1994-100)[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Rabinovitch2002-101) Belief in Murray's alleged witch-cult is still prevalent among Wiccans,[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Rabinovitch2002-101) but is gradually declining.[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Rabinovitch2002-101)
The belief that [ancient India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_India "Ancient India") was technologically advanced to the extent of being a nuclear power has been popularized by [Hindu nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindutva "Hindutva") on the premise that "fantastical" scientific and medical achievements described in [Hindu mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_mythology "Hindu mythology") are historically accurate.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-undark-102) In 2014, [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India "Prime Minister of India") [Narendra Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_Modi "Narendra Modi") told doctors and medical staff at a Mumbai hospital that the story of the [Hindu god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_god "Hindu god") [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha "Ganesha")—described as having the head of an elephant and the body of a human—shows [genetic science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_science "Genetic science") and [cosmetic surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmetic_surgery "Cosmetic surgery") existed in ancient India.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-undark-102)[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-103) Another example was the [2015 Indian Science Congress ancient aircraft controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Indian_Science_Congress_ancient_aircraft_controversy "2015 Indian Science Congress ancient aircraft controversy"), when Capt. Anand J. Bodas, retired principal of a pilot training facility, claimed at the [Indian Science Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Science_Congress "Indian Science Congress") that [mythical aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimana "Vimana") more advanced than today's aircraft flew in ancient India.[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-104) Nationalists have proposed that these aircraft and other ancient mythical technology should be presented as authentic in school textbooks.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-undark-102) Aniket Sule, an [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysicist "Astrophysicist") at the Homi Bhabha Center for Science Education, said that "people close to the current [Modi] government... feel that the present curriculum for science and history is too Western-centric" and that they may "brainwash a generation" of Indian scholars with such claims.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-undark-102)
[Baptist successionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist_successionism "Baptist successionism") posits that the [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist "Baptist") church did not originate with 17th-century [Puritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puritan "Puritan") [English Dissenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Dissenters "English Dissenters"), and that it instead represents an unbroken church lineage reaching back to [John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist "John the Baptist") and the [Book of Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Acts "Book of Acts") via a familial relationship between historic Christian churches with beliefs similar to modern Baptists. Historians point to a lack of evidence linking the disparate sects that comprise the lineage and note that some of them held beliefs antithetical to Baptist doctrine.[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-McBeth-105)[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-BaptistNews-106)[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Longenecker-107) Historian H. Leon McBeth wrote that "This view is based on inadequate sources, was more polemic than historical, and made large assumptions where evidence was lacking."[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-McBeth-105) Successionism implies that the Baptist church predates the Catholic Church, calling into question whether Baptists are indeed [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants "Protestants") and downplaying the influence of the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation "Reformation"), contrary to evidence that the 17th-century founders of the Baptist movement viewed themselves as participants in the Reformation.[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-McBeth-105)[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Priest2-108) Some successionists claim that persecution by the Catholic Church explains the lack of evidence for the successionist lineage.[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-BaptistNews-106)[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-Longenecker-107)
In South Asia, pseudohistorical narratives are sometimes used to relocate or reinterpret religious history in order to support religious identity claims.[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory#cite_note-109)
