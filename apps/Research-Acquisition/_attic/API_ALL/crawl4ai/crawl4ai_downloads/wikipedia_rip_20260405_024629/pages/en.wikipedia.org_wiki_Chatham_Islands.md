The **Chatham Islands** ([/ˈtʃætəm/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [_CHAT -əm_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); [Moriori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moriori_language "Moriori language"): **_Rēkohu_** , lit. 'Misty Sun'; [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language "Māori language"): _**Wharekauri**_) are an [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago "Archipelago") in the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean") about 800 km (430 nmi) east of New Zealand's [South Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Island "South Island"), administered as part of [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand"),[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-CIC_Act-5) and consisting of about 10 islands within an approximate 60 km (30 nmi) radius, the largest of which are [Chatham Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Island "Chatham Island") and [Pitt Island (_Rangiauria_)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitt_Island "Pitt Island"). They include New Zealand's easternmost point, the [Forty-Fours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forty-Fours "Forty-Fours"). Some of the islands, formerly [cleared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearing_\(geography\) "Clearing \(geography\)") for farming, are now preserved as [nature reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_areas_of_New_Zealand "Protected areas of New Zealand") to [conserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_in_New_Zealand "Conservation in New Zealand") some of the unique flora and fauna. 
The first human inhabitants of the Chatham Islands were the [Moriori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moriori "Moriori"). They are descended from the [Polynesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesians "Polynesians") who settled New Zealand and from whom the [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maori_people "Maori people") also descended. A group of the Polynesians migrated from mainland New Zealand to the Chatham Islands, probably in the 15th century. 
In 1835, members of the [Ngāti Mutunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81ti_Mutunga "Ngāti Mutunga") and [Ngāti Tama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81ti_Tama "Ngāti Tama") Māori [iwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwi "Iwi") from the [North Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Island "North Island") invaded the islands and [nearly exterminated the Moriori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moriori_genocide "Moriori genocide"), [enslaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery "Slavery") the survivors. In 1863 the Moriori were officially released from slavery through a proclamation by the [resident magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_magistrate "Resident magistrate").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Davis_&_Solomon-6)
In the period of European colonisation, the [New Zealand Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Company "New Zealand Company") claimed that the British Crown had never included the Chatham Islands as being under its control, and proposed selling them to the Germans to be a German colony. In 1841, a contract was drawn up for the sale of the islands for £10,000 (equivalent to approximately £860,000 in 2023),[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-7) but the sale fell through and the Chatham Islands officially became part of the [Colony of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_Zealand "Colony of New Zealand") in 1842. 
The Chatham Islands had a resident population of 620 in June 2025.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-NZ_population_data_2023_SA2-4) [Waitangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waitangi,_Chatham_Islands "Waitangi, Chatham Islands") is the main port and settlement. The local economy depends largely on conservation, tourism, farming, and fishing. The Chatham Islands Council provides local administration – its powers resemble those of New Zealand's [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities "Unitary authorities"). The Chatham Islands have [their own time zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Standard_Time_Zone "Chatham Standard Time Zone"), which is 45 minutes ahead of mainland New Zealand. 
## History
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]
### Moriori
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Moriori")]
Main article: [Moriori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moriori "Moriori")
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Chatham_Island_Tree_Carving.JPG/250px-Chatham_Island_Tree_Carving.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Chatham_Island_Tree_Carving.JPG)[Moriori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moriori_people "Moriori people") tree carving, or [dendroglyph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendroglyph "Dendroglyph"), in the Chatham Islands
The first human inhabitants of the Chatham Islands are the Moriori. They are descended from the East [Polynesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesians "Polynesians") who settled New Zealand and from whom the [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maori_people "Maori people") also descended. A group of New Zealand Polynesians migrated from mainland New Zealand to the Chatham Islands,[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKing200020-8) probably in the 15th century.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson201436,_73-10) Traditions of Moriori genealogy and some features of artefacts suggest that some arrivals may have come directly to the Chathams Islands from tropical East Polynesia. The Chathams are no further from [Rarotonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarotonga "Rarotonga") than the [Coromandel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coromandel_Peninsula "Coromandel Peninsula") coast is, and it is possible that they were settled separately during the Polynesian exploration of the South Pacific, with most of the immigrants coming from New Zealand later.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson201436-11) It is clear from artefacts and linguistic evidence that the final migration was from New Zealand.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKing200020-8)
The plants cultivated on mainland New Zealand were ill-suited for the colder Chathams, so the Moriori lived as [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer") and fishermen. While the islands lacked suitable trees for building ocean-going craft for long voyages, the Moriori invented the _[waka kōrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Waka_k%C5%8Drari&action=edit&redlink=1 "Waka kōrari \(page does not exist\)")_ , a semi-submerged craft constructed of flax and lined with air bladders from kelp. This craft was used to travel to the outer islands on 'birding' missions.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKing200030%E2%80%9331-12) After generations of warfare, bloodshed was outlawed by the chief [Nunuku-whenua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunuku-whenua "Nunuku-whenua") and Moriori society became peaceful. Disputes were resolved by consensus or by duels in which, at the first sign of bloodshed, the fight was deemed over. The population before European contact was about 2,000.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Richards-13)
Parts of a carved and decorated traditional ocean-going canoe ([waka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waka_\(canoe\) "Waka \(canoe\)")) were [discovered in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Island_waka "Chatham Island waka") in a creek on the northern coast of the main island. Approximately 450 pieces, including rare examples of braided fibre lashed to timber, have been removed, catalogued and stored.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-14) Maui Solomon, chair of the Moriori Imi Settlement Trust, has no doubt that it is a "Moriori ancestral waka" that brought some of his ancestors to the islands hundreds of years ago.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-15) The question of ownership of the waka is before the [Māori Land Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_Land_Court "Māori Land Court"), with the [Ministry for Culture and Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_for_Culture_and_Heritage "Ministry for Culture and Heritage") working with all stakeholders on "their future aspirations for the waka".[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-16) The report _[He Waka Tipua](https://www.mch.govt.nz/publications/he-waka-tipua)_ , issued by an expert panel after visiting the site in April 2025, concluded that the waka was of pre-European construction and likely to originate in a period before there came to be significant cultural separation between New Zealand and inhabitants of the wider Pacific. However, more detailed conclusions about the exact age and size of the waka depend on the recovery of the 90–95 per cent that remains buried.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-17)
### Early European arrival
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Early European arrival")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Torotoro_Monument_Kaingaroa.jpg/250px-Torotoro_Monument_Kaingaroa.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Torotoro_Monument_Kaingaroa.jpg)Monument to Torotoro above Kaingaroa Beach on Chatham Island
The name "Chatham Islands" comes from the name for the main island, which itself gets its name from [John Pitt, 2nd Earl of Chatham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pitt,_2nd_Earl_of_Chatham "John Pitt, 2nd Earl of Chatham"), who was the [First Lord of the Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Admiralty "First Lord of the Admiralty") in 1791, when [HMS  _Chatham_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Chatham_\(1788\) "HMS Chatham \(1788\)") reached the island.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-G.G._&_J._Robinson,_and_J._Edwards-18)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-19) The ship, whose captain was [William R. Broughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_R._Broughton "William R. Broughton"), was part of the [Vancouver Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Expedition "Vancouver Expedition"). The crew landed on the island on 29 November 1791 and claimed possession for Great Britain. Following a misunderstanding, Broughton's men shot and killed a Moriori resident of Kaingaroa, named [Torotoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torotoro_\(Moriori\) "Torotoro \(Moriori\)") (or Tamakororo). Chatham Islands date their anniversary on 29 November, and observe it on the nearest Monday to 30 November.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Richards-13)
[Sealers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_hunting "Seal hunting") and [whalers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whaling "Whaling") soon started hunting in the surrounding ocean with the islands as their base. It is estimated that 10 to 20 per cent of the indigenous Moriori soon died from diseases introduced by foreigners. The sealing and whaling industries ceased activities about 1861, while fishing remained as a major economic activity.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Richards-13)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Māori settlement in 19th century")]
Main article: [Moriori genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moriori_genocide "Moriori genocide")
On 19 November and 5 December 1835, about 900 [Māori people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people "Māori people") from the [iwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwi "Iwi") of [Ngāti Mutunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81ti_Mutunga "Ngāti Mutunga") and [Ngāti Tama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81ti_Tama "Ngāti Tama") – men, women and children – previously resident in Te Whanganui-a-Tara ([Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington "Wellington") in mainland New Zealand) and led by the chief [Pōmare Ngātata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiremu_Piti_Pomare "Wiremu Piti Pomare"), arrived on the brig _Lord Rodney_. The group brought with them 78 tonnes of seed potatoes, 20 pigs and seven large _[waka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waka_\(canoe\) "Waka \(canoe\)")_.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-being_Pakeha-20)
The local [Moriori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moriori "Moriori") received and initially cared for the incoming Māori.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-being_Pakeha-20) When it became clear that the visitors intended to stay, the Moriori withdrew to their [marae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marae "Marae") at Te Awapatiki to meet and to debate what to do about the Māori [settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settler "Settler"). The Moriori decided to keep with their policy of non-aggression. Soon, Ngāti Mutunga and Ngāti Tama began to _takahi_ (walk the land) to lay claim to it.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-being_Pakeha-20)
Ngāti Mutunga and Ngāti Tama saw the Moriori meeting as a precursor to warfare on the part of Moriori and decided to act against them. The Māori attacked: in the ensuing violence more than 260 Moriori were killed. A Moriori survivor recalled: "[The Māori] commenced to kill us like sheep... [We] were terrified, fled to the bush, concealed ourselves in holes underground, and in any place to escape our enemies. It was of no avail; we were discovered and killed—men, women and children—indiscriminately".[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-21) A Māori chief, Te Rakatau Katihe, said in the [Native Land Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Land_Court "Native Land Court") in 1870: "We took possession ... in accordance with our custom, and we caught all the people. Not one escaped. Some ran away from us, these we killed; and others also we killed – but what of that? It was in accordance with our custom. I am not aware of any of our people being killed by them."[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-22)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-23)[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDiamond199753-24)
After the killings, Moriori were forbidden to marry Moriori, or to have children with each other. Māori kept Moriori [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave "Slave") until 1863, when slavery was abolished by proclamation of the [resident magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_magistrate "Resident magistrate").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Davis_&_Solomon-6) Many Moriori women [had children by their Māori masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_slavery "Sexual slavery"). A number of Moriori women eventually married either Māori or European men. Some were taken away from the Chathams and never returned. [Ernst Dieffenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Dieffenbach "Ernst Dieffenbach"), who visited the Chathams on a [New Zealand Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Company "New Zealand Company") ship in 1840, reported that the Moriori were the virtual slaves of Māori and were severely mistreated, with death being a blessing. By the time the slaves were released in 1863, only 160 Moriori remained, hardly 10% of the 1835 population.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-being_Pakeha-20)
### Further European arrivals
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Further European arrivals")]
In early May 1838 (some reports say 1839, but this is contradicted by ship records[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-whaling-days-25)) the French whaling vessel _Jean Bart_ anchored off Waitangi to trade with the Māori. The number of Māori boarding frightened the French, escalating into a confrontation in which the French crew were killed and the _Jean Bart_ was run aground at Ocean Bay, to be ransacked and burned by Ngāti Mutunga. When word of the incident reached the French naval corvette _Heroine_ in the Bay of Islands in September 1838, it set sail for the Chathams, accompanied by the whalers _Adele_ and _Rebecca Sims_. The French arrived on 13 October and, after unsuccessfully attempting to entice some Ngāti Tama aboard, proceeded to bombard Waitangi. The next morning about a hundred armed Frenchmen went ashore, burning buildings, destroying _waka_ , and seizing pigs and potatoes. The attacks mostly affected Ngāti Tama, weakening their position relative to Ngāti Mutunga.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-whaling-days-25)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-26)
In 1840, Ngāti Mutunga decided to [attack Ngāti Tama at their pā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81ti_Mutunga%E2%80%93Ng%C4%81ti_Tama_conflict "Ngāti Mutunga–Ngāti Tama conflict"). They built a high staging next to the [pā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81 "Pā") so they could fire down on their former allies. Fighting was still in progress when the New Zealand Company ship _[Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Company_ships#Cuba "New Zealand Company ships")_ arrived as part of a scheme to buy land for settlement. The [Treaty of Waitangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Waitangi "Treaty of Waitangi"), at that stage, did not apply to the islands. The company negotiated a truce between the two warring tribes. In 1841, the New Zealand Company had proposed to establish a German colony on the Chathams. The proposal was discussed by the directors, and the secretary of the company John Ward signed an agreement with [Karl Sieveking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Sieveking "Karl Sieveking") of [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg") on 12 September 1841. The price was set at £10,000. However, when the British [Colonial Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Office "Colonial Office") stated that the islands were to be part of the [Colony of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_Zealand "Colony of New Zealand") and any Germans settling there would be treated as [aliens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_\(law\) "Alien \(law\)"), [Joseph Somes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Somes "Joseph Somes") claimed that Ward had been acting on his own initiative. The proposed leader John Beit and the expedition went to [Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson,_New_Zealand "Nelson, New Zealand") instead.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-27)[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-28)
The company was then able to acquire large areas of land at Port Hutt (which the Māori called _Whangaroa_) and [Waitangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waitangi,_Chatham_Islands "Waitangi, Chatham Islands") from Ngāti Mutunga and also large areas of land from Ngāti Tama. This did not stop Ngāti Mutunga from trying to get revenge upon Ngāti Tama for the earlier death of one of their chiefs. They were satisfied after they killed the brother of a Ngāti Tama chief. The tribes agreed to an uneasy peace, which was formally confirmed in 1842.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-29)
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Moriori_people.jpg/250px-Moriori_people.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Moriori_people.jpg)Moriori people in the late 19th century
Reluctant to give up slavery, Matioro and his people chartered a brig in late 1842 and sailed to [Auckland Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_Island "Auckland Island"). While Matioro was surveying the island, two of the chiefs who had accompanied him decided the island was too inhospitable for settlement, and set sail before he had returned, stranding him and his 50 followers. [Pākehā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81keh%C4%81 "Pākehā") settlers arrived in 1849 and Matioro and most of his people moved to [Stewart Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Island "Stewart Island") in 1854.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-30)
An all-male group of German [Moravian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Church "Moravian Church") missionaries arrived in 1843.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-31) When a group of women were sent out to join them three years later, several marriages ensued; a few members of the present-day population can trace their ancestry back to those missionary families.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
In 1865, the Māori leader [Te Kooti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Kooti "Te Kooti") was exiled on the Chatham Islands along with a large group of Māori rebels called the Hauhau, followers of [Pai Mārire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pai_M%C4%81rire "Pai Mārire") who had murdered missionaries and fought against government forces mainly on the East Coast of the North Island of New Zealand. The rebel prisoners were paid one shilling a day to work on sheep farms owned by the few European settlers. Sometimes they worked on road and track improvements. They were initially guarded by 26 guards, half of whom were Māori. They lived in _[whare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whare "Whare")_ along with their families. The prisoners helped build a redoubt of stone surrounded by a ditch and wall. Later, they built three stone prison cells. In 1868 Te Kooti and the other prisoners commandeered a schooner and escaped back to the North Island. 
Almost all the Māori returned to Taranaki in the 1860s, some after a [tsunami in 1868](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1868_Arica_earthquake "1868 Arica earthquake").[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Ara_4-32) In 1868 Percy Smith undertook the first detailed survey of the Chatham Islands. As well as laying out block boundaries he added "[paper roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_street "Paper street")".[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33)
### 1880s to today
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: 1880s to today")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Waitangi_settlement_-_1907.jpg/250px-Waitangi_settlement_-_1907.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Waitangi_settlement_-_1907.jpg)Waitangi settlement – 1907
The economy of the Chatham Islands, then dominated by the export of wool, suffered under the [international depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Depression "Long Depression") of the 1880s, only rebounding with the building of fish freezing plants at the island villages of [Ōwenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cwenga "Ōwenga") and [Kaingaroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kaingaroa,_Chatham_Island&action=edit&redlink=1 "Kaingaroa, Chatham Island \(page does not exist\)") in 1910. Construction of the first wharf at Waitangi began in 1931 with completion in 1934. On 25 November 1940, during the Second World War, the German auxiliary cruisers _[Komet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_auxiliary_cruiser_Komet "German auxiliary cruiser Komet")_ and _[Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_auxiliary_cruiser_Orion "German auxiliary cruiser Orion")_ captured and then sank the Chatham Islands supply ship the _[Holmwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ships_named_Holmwood "List of ships named Holmwood")_ , so the wharf saw little use by ships. A flying-boat facility was built at [Te Whanga Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Whanga_Lagoon "Te Whanga Lagoon") soon after; a flying boat service to and from the Chathams continued till 1966 when it was replaced with conventional aircraft.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-34)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-35)
After the Second World War, the island economy suffered again from its isolation and government subsidies became necessary. This led to many young Chatham Islanders leaving for the mainland. There was a brief crayfish boom, which helped stabilise the economy in the late 1960s and early 1970s. From the early 2000s cattle became a major component of the local economy.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Ara_4-32)
### Moriori community
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Moriori community")]
The Moriori community is organised as the [Hokotehi Moriori Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hokotehi_Moriori_Trust&action=edit&redlink=1 "Hokotehi Moriori Trust \(page does not exist\)").[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-36) The Moriori have received recognition from the [Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown "The Crown") and the New Zealand government and some of their claims against those institutions for the generations of neglect and oppression have been accepted and acted on. Moriori are recognised as the original people of [Rekohu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rekohu "Rekohu"). The Crown also recognised the [Ngāti Mutunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81ti_Mutunga "Ngāti Mutunga") Māori[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-37) as having indigenous status in the Chathams by right of around 160 years of occupation. 
The total population of the islands is around 600, including members of both ethnic groups. In January 2005, the Moriori celebrated the opening of the new [Kopinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopinga "Kopinga") [Marae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marae "Marae") (meeting house).[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Teara_Kopinga-38)
Modern descendants of the 1835 Māori conquerors claimed a share in ancestral Māori fishing rights. This claim was granted. Now that the primordial population, the Moriori, have been recognised to be former Māori—over the objections of some of the Ngāti Mutunga—they too share in the ancestral Māori fishing rights. Both groups have been granted fishing quotas.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-39)
## Geography
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Geography")]
See also: [Chatham Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Rise "Chatham Rise")
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Chatham-Islands_map_topo_en.svg/500px-Chatham-Islands_map_topo_en.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Chatham-Islands_map_topo_en.svg)[Topographic map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_map "Topographic map") of the Chatham Islands
The Chatham Islands lie roughly 840 km (455 nmi) east of [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch "Christchurch") in the South Island. The nearest New Zealand mainland point to the islands is [Cape Turnagain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Turnagain "Cape Turnagain"), in the [North Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Island "North Island"), 650 km (350 nmi) distant. The islands sit on the [Chatham Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Rise "Chatham Rise"), a large, relatively shallowly submerged (no more than 1,000 m or 3,281 ft deep at any point) plateau that stretches east from near the South Island. The Chatham Rise is part of the now largely submerged continent of [Zealandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealandia "Zealandia"). The islands, which emerged only within the last 4 million years, are the only part of the Chatham Rise showing above sea level.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-40)
The two largest islands, [Chatham Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Island "Chatham Island") and [Pitt Island (Rangiaotea)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitt_Island "Pitt Island"), constitute most of the total area of 793.87 km2 (307 sq mi), with 12 scattered islets making up the rest.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] The islands are hilly, with the coastal areas being a mix of cliffs, [dunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune "Dune"), beaches, and lagoons. Pitt is more rugged than Chatham. 
The highest point (299 m (981 ft)) is on a plateau near the southernmost tip of Chatham Island, 7⁄8 mi) south of [Lake Te Rangatapu](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lake_Te_Rangatapu&action=edit&redlink=1 "Lake Te Rangatapu \(page does not exist\)").[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-41) The plateau is dotted with numerous lakes and lagoons, flowing mainly from the island's nearby second-highest point, [Maungatere Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maungatere_Hill "Maungatere Hill"), at 294 m (965 ft).[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-42) Notable are the large [Te Whanga Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Whanga_Lagoon "Te Whanga Lagoon"), and Huro and Rangitahi. Chatham has a number of streams, including Te Awainanga and Tuku. 
Chatham and Pitt are the only inhabited islands; the other islands are conservation reserves with restricted or prohibited access. The livelihoods of the inhabitants depend on agriculture – the islands export coldwater [crayfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasus_edwardsii "Jasus edwardsii") – and, increasingly, on [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_New_Zealand "Tourism in New Zealand").

### Geology
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Geology")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Schist_formations%2C_Kaingaroa%2C_Chatham_Islands.jpg/250px-Schist_formations%2C_Kaingaroa%2C_Chatham_Islands.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Schist_formations,_Kaingaroa,_Chatham_Islands.jpg)Schist rocks, Kaingaroa beach
The Chatham Islands are far from the Australian-Pacific plate boundary that dominates the [geology of mainland New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_New_Zealand "Geology of New Zealand"). The [islands' stratigraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratigraphy_of_New_Zealand "Stratigraphy of New Zealand") consists of a [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic "Mesozoic") [schist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schist "Schist") basement, typically covered by marine sedimentary rocks.[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-45) Both these sequences are intruded by a series of [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt "Basalt") eruptions. Volcanic activity has occurred multiple times since the [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous "Cretaceous"),[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-46) but currently there is no active volcanism near any part of the Chatham Rise. Prominent [columnar basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columnar_basalt "Columnar basalt") can be seen at [Ohira Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohira_Bay "Ohira Bay") (one of the indentations in the north coast of [Petre Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petre_Bay "Petre Bay")) between [Te Roto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Roto "Te Roto") and [Port Hutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Hutt "Port Hutt").[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-47)
### Climate
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Climate")]
The Chatham Islands have an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate "Oceanic climate") ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification "Köppen climate classification"): _Cfb_)[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-48) characterised by a narrow temperature range and relatively frequent rainfall. Their isolated position far from any sizeable landmass renders the record high temperature for the main settlement (Waitangi) just 23.8 °C (74.8 °F).[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-49) The climate is cool, wet and windy, with average high temperatures between 16 and 20 °C (61 and 68 °F) in summer, and typically around 11°C in July (in the Southern Hemisphere winter). Snowfall is extremely rare, the fall recorded near sea level in July 2015 marking the first such reading for several decades.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-50) Under the [Trewartha climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification "Trewartha climate classification"), the Chatham Islands have a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate "Humid subtropical climate") (Cf) for the lack of cold weather during the winter and a daily mean temperature above 10 °C (50 °F) for 8 months or more.

#### Plants
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Plants")]
Main articles: [Flora of the Chatham Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_of_the_Chatham_Islands "Flora of the Chatham Islands") and [Endemic flora of the Chatham Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_flora_of_the_Chatham_Islands "Endemic flora of the Chatham Islands")
The natural vegetation of the islands was a mixture of forest, scrubby heath, and swamp, but today most of the land is fern or pasture-covered, although there are some areas of dense forest and areas of peat bogs and other habitats. Of interest are the [akeake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olearia_traversiorum "Olearia traversiorum") trees, with branches trailing almost horizontally in the lee of the wind.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-53) The ferns in the forest [understory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Understory "Understory") include _[Blechnum discolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blechnum_discolor "Blechnum discolor")_. 
The islands are home to a rich bio-diversity including about 50 [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism "Endemism") plants adapted to the cold and the wind, such as the Chatham Islands forget-me-not (_[Myosotidium hortensia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myosotidium "Myosotidium")_),[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-54) the Chatham Islands sow-thistle _([Embergeria grandifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Embergeria_grandifolia&action=edit&redlink=1 "Embergeria grandifolia \(page does not exist\)"))_, rautini (_[Brachyglottis huntii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachyglottis_huntii "Brachyglottis huntii")_), the Chatham Islands kakaha (_[Astelia chathamica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astelia_chathamica "Astelia chathamica")_), soft speargrass (_[Aciphylla dieffenbachii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aciphylla_dieffenbachii "Aciphylla dieffenbachii")_), and the Chatham Island akeake or Chatham Island tree daisy (_[Olearia traversiorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olearia_traversiorum "Olearia traversiorum")_). 
#### Birds
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Birds")]
The islands are a breeding ground for huge flocks of seabirds and are home to a number of endemic birds, some of which are seabirds and others which live on the islands. The best known species are the [critically endangered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN_Red_List#Categories "IUCN Red List") [magenta petrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magenta_petrel "Magenta petrel") and the vulnerable [black robin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_robin "Black robin"), both of which came perilously close to [extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction "Extinction") before drawing the attention of [conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_conservation "Bird conservation") efforts. Other endemic species are the [Chatham oystercatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_oystercatcher "Chatham oystercatcher"), the [Chatham gerygone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_gerygone "Chatham gerygone"), the [Chatham pigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_pigeon "Chatham pigeon"), [Forbes' parakeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes%27_parakeet "Forbes' parakeet"), the [Chatham snipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_snipe "Chatham snipe") and the [shore plover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shore_plover "Shore plover"). The endemic [Chatham shag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_shag "Chatham shag")[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-55) (critically endangered), the [Pitt shag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitt_shag "Pitt shag")[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-56) (endangered) and the [Chatham albatross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_albatross "Chatham albatross")[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-57) (vulnerable) are at risk of capture by a variety of fishing gear, including fishing lines, [trawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trawl "Trawl"), [gillnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillnet "Gillnet"), and pots.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-58)
A number of species have gone [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct "Extinct") since human settlement, including the [Chatham raven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_raven "Chatham raven"), [Chatham fernbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_fernbird "Chatham fernbird"), [Chatham Islands penguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eudyptes_warhami "Eudyptes warhami"), [Rēkohu shelduck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%93kohu_shelduck "Rēkohu shelduck") and the three endemic species of [flightless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flightless "Flightless") [rails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rallidae "Rallidae"), the [Chatham rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_rail "Chatham rail"), [Dieffenbach's rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieffenbach%27s_rail "Dieffenbach's rail"), and [Hawkins's rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawkins%27s_rail "Hawkins's rail"). 
#### Mammals
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Mammals")]
Marine mammals found in the waters of the Chathams include [New Zealand sea lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_sea_lion "New Zealand sea lion"), [New Zealand fur seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_fur_seal "New Zealand fur seal"), [leopard seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard_seal "Leopard seal"), and [southern elephant seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_elephant_seal "Southern elephant seal"). Many [whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale "Whale") species are attracted to the rich food sources of the [Chatham Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Rise "Chatham Rise").[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-59)

According to Marinov & McHugh (2010), the poor diversity is linked to harsh environmental conditions, such as generally low annual temperatures, constant strong winds and high acidity in the habitats where their larvae develop.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-:2-60)
_Xanthocnemis tuanuii_ is endemic to the Chatham Islands, but close to _Xanthocnemis zealandica_ (McLachlan, 1873) from mainland New Zealand and genetic studies suggest that the two species cohabitate on the Chatham Islands[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-61) Nolan & al (2007). 
According to Marinov & McHugh (2010),[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-:2-60) the Chatham Island population of _Austrolestes colensonis_ differs genetically from the populations on New Zealand’s main islands, but the differences seem too weak to separate them into two species. 
#### Threats
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Threats")]
Much of the natural forest of the islands has been cleared for farming. [Introduced species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduced_species "Introduced species") that prey on the indigenous birds and reptiles are a threat. On Mangere and Rangatira Islands, which are now preserved as nature reserves to conserve some of the unique flora and fauna, livestock has been removed and native wildlife is recovering. 
Most lakes have been affected by agricultural run-off, but water quality has improved and river quality is generally classed as 'A'.[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-62)
In February 2025, the Department of Conservation (DOC) announced a pest eradication project on the island. The project, part of the [Island-Ocean Connection Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Island-Ocean_Connection_Challenge&action=edit&redlink=1 "Island-Ocean Connection Challenge \(page does not exist\)") (IOCC), targets three islands up to 15 times larger than any previously cleared of pests in New Zealand. The goal is to remove invasive species, restore ecosystems, and protect native wildlife, including [kākāpō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%81k%C4%81p%C5%8D "Kākāpō"), seabirds, and rare plants. The total project cost is estimated at $202 million, with $54 million from the government and $11.5 million raised through philanthropy, leaving $137 million still needed.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-stuff2025-63)

Chatham Islands covers 793.88 km2 (306.52 sq mi)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Area-1) and had an estimated population of 620 as of June 2025,[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-NZ_population_data_2023_SA2-4) with a population density of 0.8 people per km2.

Chatham and Pitt Islands are inhabited and had a population of 612 in the [2023 New Zealand census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_New_Zealand_census "2023 New Zealand census"), a decrease of 51 people (−7.7%) since the [2018 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_New_Zealand_census "2018 New Zealand census"), and an increase of 12 people (2.0%) since the [2013 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_New_Zealand_census "2013 New Zealand census"). There were 390 dwellings. The median age was 44.0 years (compared with 38.1 years nationally). There were 99 people (16.2%) aged under 15 years, 96 (15.7%) aged 15 to 29, 318 (52.0%) aged 30 to 64, and 102 (16.7%) aged 65 or older.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Census_2023-67)
Ethnicities were 72.5% European/[Pākehā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81keh%C4%81 "Pākehā"), 68.6% [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people "Māori people"), 3.9% [Pasifika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasifika_New_Zealanders "Pasifika New Zealanders"), 2.0% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_New_Zealanders "Asian New Zealanders"), 1.0% Middle Eastern, Latin American and African New Zealanders, and 1.5% other. People may identify with more than one ethnicity.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Census_2023-67)
The Chatham Islands had a population of 663 at the [2018 New Zealand census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_New_Zealand_census "2018 New Zealand census"). There were 276 households, comprising 354 males and 312 females, giving a sex:sex ratio of 1.13 males per female. 
The percentage of people born overseas was 5.9, compared with 27.1% nationally. 
Although some people chose not to answer the census's question about religious affiliation, 48.4% had no religion, 33.5% were [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_New_Zealand "Christianity in New Zealand"), 5.9% had [Māori religious beliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_of_M%C4%81ori_people "Religion of Māori people"), 0.5% were [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_New_Zealand "Buddhism in New Zealand") and 1.4% had other religions. 
Of those at least 15 years old, 51 (9.2%) people had a bachelor's or higher degree, and 147 (26.6%) people had no formal qualifications. The median income was $36,000, compared with $31,800 nationally. 108 people (19.6%) earned over $70,000 compared to 17.2% nationally. The employment status of those at least 15 was that 318 (57.6%) people were employed full-time, 108 (19.6%) were part-time, and 9 (1.6%) were unemployed.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Census_2018-68)
Most residents live on Chatham Island, with only a few dozen on Pitt Island. The main settlement is the town of [Waitangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waitangi,_Chatham_Islands "Waitangi, Chatham Islands"), centrally located on the west coast, with 140 residents as of June 2025.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-NZ_population_data_2018-69) Its facilities include a hospital with resident doctor, bank, several stores, engineering and marine services, and the main shipping wharf. Other villages include [Te One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_One "Te One"), near Waitangi, [Ōwenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cwenga "Ōwenga") in the south-east, Kaingaroa in the north-east, and Port Hutt[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Quickstats-64) in the north-west. 
## Government
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Government")]
### Local government
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Local government")]
Main article: [Chatham Islands Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands_Council "Chatham Islands Council")
For local government purposes, the Chatham Islands and the adjoining sea is known as the Chatham Islands Territory and is administered by the Chatham Islands Council, which was established by the Chatham Islands Council Act 1995 (Statute No 041, Commenced: 1 November 1995).[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-CIC_Act-5) These succeeded, respectively, the [Chatham Islands County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_New_Zealand "Counties of New Zealand"), which was established in 1901,[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-70)[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-71) and the Chatham Islands County Council, which was established in 1926.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-72)[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-73) The Council is a [territorial authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_authorities_of_New_Zealand "Territorial authorities of New Zealand") that has many of the functions, duties and powers of a [district council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_New_Zealand "Districts of New Zealand") and of a [regional council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_New_Zealand "Regions of New Zealand"),[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-CIC_Act-5) making it in effect a [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority "Unitary authority") with slightly fewer responsibilities than other unitary authorities. The Council comprises a directly-elected [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Chatham_Islands "Mayor of Chatham Islands") and eight councillors, one of whom is also deputy mayor.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-myc-3) Certain regional council functions are being administered by [Environment Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_Canterbury "Environment Canterbury"), the Canterbury Regional Council.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
In the 2010 local government elections, Chatham Islands had New Zealand's highest rate of returned votes, with 71.3 per cent voting.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarper2010-74)
### Parliamentary electorates
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Parliamentary electorates")]
The Chatham Islands are within a single [electorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_electorates "New Zealand electorates") which sends one member to [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Parliament "New Zealand Parliament"). Until the 1990s, the Chatham Islands were in the [Lyttelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyttelton_\(New_Zealand_electorate\) "Lyttelton \(New Zealand electorate\)") electorate, but since then they have formed part of the [Rongotai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongotai_\(New_Zealand_electorate\) "Rongotai \(New Zealand electorate\)") general electorate, which otherwise lies in south [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington "Wellington"). [Julie Anne Genter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Anne_Genter "Julie Anne Genter") is the MP for Rongotai. The [Te Tai Tonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Tai_Tonga "Te Tai Tonga") [Māori electorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_electorates "Māori electorates") (currently held by [Tākuta Ferris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%81kuta_Ferris "Tākuta Ferris")) includes the Chatham Islands; before boundaries of Māori seats were reformed in 1996 the archipelago was part of the Māori electorate of [Western Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_M%C4%81ori "Western Māori").[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-75)
### State services
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: State services")]
[Policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Police "New Zealand Police") is carried out by a sole-charge [constable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constable "Constable") appointed by the [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington "Wellington") police district, who has often doubled as an official for many [government departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_sector_organisations_in_New_Zealand "State sector organisations in New Zealand"), including court registrar (Department for Courts), customs officer (New Zealand Customs Service) and immigration officer (Department of Labour – New Zealand Immigration Service). 
A District Court judge sent from either the North Island or the South Island presides over court sittings, but urgent sittings may take place at the Wellington District Court. 
Because of the isolation and small population, some of the rules governing daily activities undergo a certain relaxation. For example, every transport service operated solely on [Great Barrier Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Barrier_Island "Great Barrier Island"), the Chatham Islands or [Stewart Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Island "Stewart Island") need not comply with section 70C of the Transport Act 1962 (the requirements for drivers to maintain driving-hours logbooks). Drivers subject to section 70B must nevertheless keep record of their driving hours in some form.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-76)
The [Canterbury District Health Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_District_Health_Board "Canterbury District Health Board") is responsible for providing publicly funded health services for the island. Prior to July 2015, this was the responsibility of the [Hawke's Bay District Health Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawke%27s_Bay_District_Health_Board "Hawke's Bay District Health Board").[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-77)
## Economy and infrastructure
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Economy and infrastructure")]
Most of the Chatham Island economy is based on fishing and crayfishing, with only a fragment of the economic activity in adventure tourism. This economic mix has been stable for the past 50 years, as little infrastructure or population is present to engage in higher levels of industrial or telecommunications activity.[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-78)
Two 225 kW wind turbines and diesel generators provide mains power on Chatham Island, at costs of five to ten times that of electricity on the main islands of New Zealand.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-79) During 2014, 65% of the electricity was generated from diesel generators, the balance from wind.[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-80) For heating, electricity comes second to wood. In 2013, solar power contributed about a third as much as mains-generated electricity.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-81)
### External transport
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: External transport")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Landing_approach_to_Tuuta_Airport%2C_Chatham_Island.jpg/250px-Landing_approach_to_Tuuta_Airport%2C_Chatham_Island.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Landing_approach_to_Tuuta_Airport,_Chatham_Island.jpg)Visitors to the Chathams generally arrive in the islands at [Tuuta Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuuta_Airport "Tuuta Airport").
Visitors to the Chathams usually arrive by air from [Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland "Auckland"), [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch "Christchurch") or [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington "Wellington") (around two hours from Christchurch on an [ATR 72–500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72#Variants "ATR 72") aircraft) to [Tuuta Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuuta_Airport "Tuuta Airport") on Chatham Island. While freight generally arrives by ship (two days sailing time), the sea journey takes too long for many passengers, and is not always available.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-82)[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-CISL-83)
[Tasman Empire Airways Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasman_Empire_Airways_Limited "Tasman Empire Airways Limited") (TEAL) initially serviced the Chathams by air using [flying boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_boats "Flying boats"). With the withdrawal of TEAL, the RNZAF maintained an infrequent service with [Short Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Sunderland "Short Sunderland") flying boats. NZ4111 was damaged on takeoff from Te Whanga Lagoon on 4 November 1959. Its engines, radar and radio were salvaged in 1960 and the rest of the wreck was repurposed by the population around the island.[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-84) The last flight by RNZAF flying boats was on 22 March 1967.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-85) For many years [Bristol Freighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Freighter "Bristol Freighter") aircraft served the islands, a slow and noisy freight aircraft converted for carrying passengers by installing a removable passenger compartment equipped with [airline seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_seat "Airline seat") and a toilet in part of the cargo hold. The air service primarily served to ship out high-value export [crayfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crayfish "Crayfish") products. 
The grass landing field at Hapupu, at the northern end of the Island, proved a limiting factor, as few aircraft apart from the Bristol Freighter had both the range to fly to the islands and the ruggedness to land on the grass airstrip. Although other aircraft did use the landing field occasionally, they would often require repairs to fix damage resulting from the rough landing. Hapupu is also the site of the [Hāpūpū / J M Barker Historic Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C4%81p%C5%ABp%C5%AB_/_J_M_Barker_Historic_Reserve "Hāpūpū / J M Barker Historic Reserve") (one of only two national historic reserves in New Zealand), where there are momori rakau (Moriori tree carvings). 
In 1981, after many years of requests by locals and the imminent demise of the ageing Bristol Freighters, the construction of a sealed runway at Karewa, Tuuta Airport, allowed more modern aircraft to land safely. The Chathams' own airline, [Air Chathams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Chathams "Air Chathams"), with its head office in Te One,[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-86) operates services to Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch. The timetable varies seasonally, but generally planes depart the Chathams around 09:30 am (Chathams Time) and arrive in the mainland around 11am and depart again at around 2 pm (NZ time) back to the Chathams. Air Chathams operates twin turboprop [ATR 72–500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72#Variants "ATR 72") aircraft (freight and passenger) and dedicated freighter Saab 340 aircraft. The freighter is mainly used to supplement the ATR capacity during crayfish season for the transport of live export crayfish. 
The ship _Rangatira_ provided a freight service from [Timaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timaru "Timaru") to the Chatham Islands from March 2000 to August 2015.[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-87) The MV _Southern Tiare_ provides a freight service between [Napier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napier,_New_Zealand "Napier, New Zealand"), Timaru and the Chathams.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-CISL-83)
### Internal transport
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Internal transport")]
Boats are used to transport people between the islands. In 1902 four people lost their lives when the whaling boat they were sailing on from Pitt Island to Ōwenga.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33) On 17 July 1931, 11 people drowned when the 36 feet (11 m) long cutter-rigged launch _Te Aroha_ sunk during a storm while sailing the 24 miles (39 km) from Kaingaroa to Ōwenga while transporting people to play in a football match. No bodies were ever found, only a severed hand.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33) To date this has been the greatest single maritime loss of people on the islands.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33) Lost were Bishop Ashton, Taaka Ngata, B. Remi, H. Stone, brothers Joseph and William Paynter, brothers Edward, Michael and Waiti Thompson, James Whaitirl and Ririmu Wiki.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Dominion_04_09_1931-88) Up until the late 1960s boats were also regularly used to connect the settlements on the edges of the Te Whanga Lagoon. 
By the 1920s a number of formal tracks had been created around the ports.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33) After the end of the Second World War the [Ministry of Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Works_and_Development "Ministry of Works and Development") constructed a gravel road and bridges to Ōwenga. Tracks continued to be created but often did not conform with the "paper roads" on Smith's survey. Most islanders continued to travel by horses. To transport a number of people or freight, islanders would use a cart pulled by a single horse called a "konake" or "Chatham islands truck", which had a pair of wheels at the rear and sled-type runners at the front which assisted it in travelling over the soft soil covering the island.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33) Another option was a "jogger" which was pulled by two horses and which used pneumatic tires.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33) The tracks, or lack of them, were so bad that [Norman Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Kirk "Norman Kirk") (who was the local MP) on a visit to the islands in the late 1950s described a trip by Land Rover that took four hours to cover 31 miles. The Land Rover was accompanied by a tractor whose task it was to pull the other vehicle out when it became bogged.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33)
Using funds provided by the New Zealand government, a project was undertaken in 1969–70 to turn these tracks into formal roads to connect the airport at Hapapu, Kaingaroa, Manganui, Port Hutt, the Tuku and roads around Waitangi.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33) The contract was awarded to Roger Mahon from Canterbury who shipped construction equipment to the islands. With the agreements of the islanders, he and the project engineer Brian Pease chose the best route, rather than follow the paper roads laid out in the 1870s and 1880s. The islanders assisted in the work by providing quarried rock and other hardfill at little or no cost to the project.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33) Due to the benefits they were receiving from the improvement in access to their properties, landowners opted to receive no compensation for the value of the land taken for the roads. As a road was constructed, the surveyor Kenneth Wynne followed behind pegging out the legal road boundaries of what in many cases were wider roads and more roads than specified in the contract.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Rennie-33)
There is a small section of tar-sealed road between Waitangi and Te One, but the majority of the roads are gravel. 
### Telecommunications
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Telecommunications")]
A 1.5 kW wireless link[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-89) opened in 1913,[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-90) a public radio link to the mainland was built in 1953 and an island phone system in 1965.[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-91) The Chatham Islands were the last place in New Zealand to have a manual telephone exchange, switching to an automatic exchange on 18 July 1990.[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-92) In 2003 a digital microwave system was installed for 110 phones in Ōwenga.[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-93)
The islands were linked as part of the [Rural Broadband Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_in_New_Zealand#Rural_Broadband_Initiative "Internet in New Zealand") in 2014, when [satellite bandwidth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Internet_access#Bandwidth "Satellite Internet access") was increased,[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-94) and broadband is now provided by [Wireless Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_in_New_Zealand#Satellite "Internet in New Zealand"),[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-:0-95) though [Farmside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_in_New_Zealand#Satellite "Internet in New Zealand") provide some coverage.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-:1-96)
As late as 2019 there was no mobile phone [coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coverage_\(telecommunication\) "Coverage \(telecommunication\)") on the islands.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-:1-96) In December 2021, five [4G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4G "4G") cellular towers were turned on to enable mobile phone coverage on Chatham and Pitt Island and deliver faster broadband. The main tower is positioned on Target Hill, which transmits to the other four towers through a microwave radio link; it is backhauled by Eutelsat 172B to a network in Wellington. The upgraded network delivers greater bandwidth than the previous link, and provides reliable broadband.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-97)
## Education
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chatham_Islands&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: Education")]
See also: [List of schools in the Chatham Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_the_Chatham_Islands "List of schools in the Chatham Islands")
There are three schools on the Chathams, at Kaingaroa, Te One, and Pitt Island. Pitt Island and Kaingaroa Schools are staffed by sole charge principals, while Te One has three teachers and a principal. The schools cater for children from year 1 to 8. There is no secondary school. The majority of secondary school-aged students leave the island for boarding schools in mainland New Zealand. A small number remain on the island and obtain their secondary education by correspondence.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] There is no tertiary or university level institution on the islands.

The unofficial flag of the Chatham Islands is a blue field with a map of the island in the centre, the [Te Whanga Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Whanga_Lagoon "Te Whanga Lagoon") depicted in white. Behind this device map is a depiction of the rising sun, an allusion to its local name _Rēkohu_ , meaning 'rising sun'. The flag was designed in 1989 by Logan Alderson, a former [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand") police officer.[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-98)
At the 2005 opening of a new _[marae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marae "Marae")_ on the islands (which included a rare visit by the [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_New_Zealand "Prime Minister of New Zealand")), the Chathams flag was flown on a flagpole over the _marae_.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Islands#cite_note-Teara_Kopinga-38)
