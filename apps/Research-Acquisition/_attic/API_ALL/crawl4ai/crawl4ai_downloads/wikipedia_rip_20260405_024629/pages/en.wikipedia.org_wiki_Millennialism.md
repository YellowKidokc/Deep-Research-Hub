Belief that a Golden Age or Paradise will occur prior to the final judgment
Not to be confused with [Millenarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millenarianism "Millenarianism").
For the Christian doctrine regarding Jesus Christ's reign in heaven, see [Session of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_of_Christ "Session of Christ") and [Intercession of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercession_of_Christ "Intercession of Christ"). **Millennialism** (from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language "Latin language") _mille_ 'thousand'; _annus_ 'year' and _[-ism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-ism "-ism")_) or **[chiliasm](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/chiliasm "wikt:chiliasm")** (from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") equivalent) is a belief which is held by some [religious denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_denomination "Religious denomination"). According to this belief, a [Messianic Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messianic_Age "Messianic Age") (the so-called _Christian Millennium_) will be established on [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth") prior to the [Last Judgment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Judgment "Last Judgment") and the future permanent state of "eternity".[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-1)
[Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") and [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism") have both produced messianic movements which featured millennialist teachings—such as the notion that an earthly kingdom of God was at hand. These [millenarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millenarian "Millenarian") movements often led to considerable social unrest.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-2)
Similarities to millennialism also exist in [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"), which identified successive thousand-year periods, each of which will end in a cataclysm of [heresy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heresy "Heresy") and destruction, until the final destruction of [evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil "Evil") and the final destruction of the spirit of evil by a triumphant king of peace at the end of the final millennial age. Jewish and then Christian interpretations built on Zoroastrianism and on [Babylonian astrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_astrology "Babylonian astrology"), resulting in the construction of a schema of a sequence of seven successive thousand-year periods ("[millennia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennia "Millennia")") of earthly human existence.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-3)
Scholars have linked various social and political movements, both religious and [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularity "Secularity"), to millennialist metaphors. 
## Christianity
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Christianity")]
Most Christian millennialist thinking is based upon the [Book of Revelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Revelation "Book of Revelation"), specifically Revelation 20,[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-4) which describes the vision of an angel who descends from heaven with a large chain and a key to a bottomless pit, and captures Satan, imprisoning him for a thousand years: 
> He seized the dragon, that ancient serpent, who is the Devil and Satan, and bound him for a thousand years and threw him into the pit and locked and sealed it over him, so that he would deceive the nations no more, until the thousand years were ended. After that, he must be let out for a little while.
— Revelation 20:2–3[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-5)
The Book of Revelation then describes a series of judges who are seated on thrones, as well as John's vision of the souls of those who were beheaded for their testimony in favor of Jesus and their rejection of the mark of the beast. These souls: 
> came to life and reigned with Christ a thousand years. (The rest of the dead did not come to life until the thousand years were ended.) This is the first resurrection. Blessed and holy are those who share in the first resurrection. Over these the second death has no power, but they will be priests of God and of Christ, and they will reign with him a thousand years
— Revelation 20:4–6[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-6)

#### Amillennialism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Amillennialism")]
In the 2nd century, the [Alogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alogi "Alogi") (those who rejected all of John's writings) were [amillennial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amillennial "Amillennial"), as was [Caius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caius_\(presbyter\) "Caius \(presbyter\)") in the first quarter of the 3rd century.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-22) With the influence of [Platonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism "Platonism"), [Clement of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_of_Alexandria "Clement of Alexandria") and [Origen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origen "Origen") denied premillennialism.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-23) Likewise, [Dionysius of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysius_of_Alexandria "Dionysius of Alexandria") (died 264) argued that Revelation was not written by John and could not be interpreted literally; he was amillennial.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-24)
[Justin Martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Martyr "Justin Martyr") (died 165), who had chiliastic tendencies in his theology, mentions differing views in his _[Dialogue with Trypho the Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialogue_with_Trypho_the_Jew "Dialogue with Trypho the Jew")_ , chapter 80:[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-:3-25)
> "I and many others are of this opinion [premillennialism], and [believe] that such will take place, as you assuredly are aware; but, on the other hand, I signified to you that many who belong to the pure and pious faith, and are true Christians, think otherwise."[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-:3-25)
Augustine in his early days affirmed [premillennialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premillennialism "Premillennialism"), but later changed to amillennialism, causing the view to become popularized together with [Pope Gregory the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I "Pope Gregory I").[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-26)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-27)
The _[Catholic Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia "Catholic Encyclopedia")_ notes that the 2nd-century proponents of various [Gnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnosticism "Gnosticism") beliefs (themselves considered heresies) also rejected millenarianism.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-28)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Millennial_views.svg/500px-Millennial_views.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Millennial_views.svg)Comparison of Christian millennial interpretations
Christian views on the future order of events diversified after the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation "Protestant Reformation") (c. 1517). In particular, new emphasis was placed on the passages in the [Book of Revelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Revelation "Book of Revelation") which seemed to say that as Christ would return to judge the living and the dead, [Satan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satan "Satan") would be locked away for 1000 years, but then released on the world to instigate [a final battle against God and his Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armageddon "Armageddon").[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-29) Previous [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic") and [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church "Eastern Orthodox Church") theologians had no clear or consensus view on what this actually meant (only the concept of the end of the world coming unexpectedly, "like a thief in the night", and the concept of "the [Antichrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antichrist "Antichrist")" were almost universally held). Millennialist theories try to explain what this "1000 years of Satan bound in chains" would be like. 
Various types of millennialism exist with regard to [Christian eschatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_eschatology "Christian eschatology"), especially within [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism "Protestantism"), such as [Premillennialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premillennialism "Premillennialism"), [Postmillennialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmillennialism "Postmillennialism"), and [Amillennialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amillennialism "Amillennialism"). The first two refer to different views of the relationship between the "millennial Kingdom" and Christ's second coming. 
Premillennialism sees Christ's second advent as preceding the millennium, thereby separating the [Second Coming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Coming "Second Coming") from the [Final Judgment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Judgment "Final Judgment"). In this view, "Christ's reign" will be physically on the earth. 
Postmillennialism sees Christ's second coming as subsequent to the millennium and concurrent with the final judgment. In this view "Christ's reign" (during the millennium) will be spiritual in and through the [church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_church "Christian church"). 
Amillennialism sees the 1000 year kingdom as being metaphorically described in Rev. 20:1–6 in which "Christ's reign" is current in and through the church. Thus, while this view does not hold to a future millennial reign, it does hold that the New Heavens and New Earth will appear upon the return of Christ.

#### Catholic Church
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Catholic Church")]
The [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") strongly condemns millennialism as the following shows: 
> The Antichrist's deception already begins to take shape in the world every time the claim is made to realize within history that messianic hope which can only be realized beyond history through the eschatological judgment. The Church has rejected even modified forms of this falsification of the kingdom to come under the name of millenarianism, especially the "intrinsically perverse" political form of a secular messianism.
— [Joseph Cardinal Ratzinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_XVI "Benedict XVI"), Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1995[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-30)
#### Bible Student movement
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Bible Student movement")]
The [Bible Student movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_Student_movement "Bible Student movement") is a millennialist movement based on views expressed in "The Divine Plan of the Ages," in 1886, in Volume One of the _[Studies in the Scriptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studies_in_the_Scriptures "Studies in the Scriptures")_ series, by Pastor [Charles Taze Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Taze_Russell "Charles Taze Russell"). (This series is still being published, since 1927, by the [Dawn Bible Students Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_Bible_Students_Association "Dawn Bible Students Association").) Bible Students believe that there will be a universal opportunity for every person, past and present, not previously recipients of a heavenly calling, to gain everlasting life on Earth during the Millennium.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-31)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Jehovah's Witnesses")]
[Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah%27s_Witnesses "Jehovah's Witnesses") believe that Christ will rule from heaven for 1,000 years as king over the earth, assisted by the [144,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah%27s_Witnesses_and_salvation#The_.27anointed.27 "Jehovah's Witnesses and salvation") ascended humans. According to them during this 1,000 year reign the earth will become a paradise, like the Garden of Eden, and humans will themselves return to the perfection lost by Adam and Eve.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-32)
#### The Church of Almighty God
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: The Church of Almighty God")]
Also known as [Eastern Lightning, The Church of Almighty God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Lightning "Eastern Lightning") mentions in its teachings the Age of Millennial Kingdom, which will follow the catastrophes prophesied in the Book of Revelation in the Bible.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-33)
#### New Apostolic Reformation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: New Apostolic Reformation")]
Counter to much of the [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism "Pentecostalism") movement, which tends towards belief in premillennialism, the rise of the Antichrist, and the decay of the world prior to the Second Coming, the [New Apostolic Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Apostolic_Reformation "New Apostolic Reformation")'s focus is instead on an "optimistic" eschatology. It holds that most end-time prophecies have long since been fulfilled and that modern-day [prophets and apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic-Prophetic_Movement "Apostolic-Prophetic Movement") have divine authority; the end times will be an era in which obedient Christians, through using [spiritual warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_warfare "Spiritual warfare") and shaping all aspects of society into aligning with their Christian beliefs ([Seven Mountain Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Mountain_Mandate "Seven Mountain Mandate")), will bring about the Second Coming.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-34)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-GeivettPivec2014-35)
#### Alleged partial underpinning of US-Israel attack on Iran 2026
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Alleged partial underpinning of US-Israel attack on Iran 2026")]
Following the [2026 Iran conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_conflict "2026 Iran conflict"), multiple sources reported figures in the [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army") were framing the conflict in millenialist terms. [Military Religious Freedom Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Religious_Freedom_Foundation "Military Religious Freedom Foundation") received anonymous complaints from soldiers detailing claiming that they were told the conflict in Iran was meant to cause Armageddon. Another complaint mentioned officers telling soldiers that the conflict was "all part of God’s divine plan", citing the Book of Revelation.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-36) According to one [NCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer "Non-commissioned officer"), he was told "President Trump has been anointed by Jesus to light the signal fire in Iran to cause Armageddon and mark his return to Earth". In total, the Military Religious Freedom Foundation received more than 200 such complaints, which came from every branch of the military.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-37)[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-38)

## Islam
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Islam")]
See also: [Islamic eschatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_eschatology "Islamic eschatology")
The [Prophet Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad "Muhammad") has stated[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-41) that a man from his Household will come and rid the world of all injustice and tyranny. He will be known as the [Mahdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdi "Mahdi"). 
[Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims") also believe that [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus") will come alongside the Mahdi and will fight together with him against oppression and injustice, where the Mahdi will rule for a period of time before the [Day of Judgement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judgement_Day_in_Islam "Judgement Day in Islam"). The Mahdi is noted in the Sunni books, [Sunan Abi Dawud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunan_Abi_Dawud "Sunan Abi Dawud") 4285, [Sunan Ibn Majah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunan_Ibn_Majah "Sunan Ibn Majah") 4083, and [Sahih Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahih_Muslim "Sahih Muslim") 2913. 
Shia and Sunni Muslims differ on who exactly the Mahdi is. While they both agree that he will come alongside Jesus to save mankind from injustice and oppression; Sunnis believe he is yet to be born, while Shias believe that he is currently alive and in [occultation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occultation_\(Islam\) "Occultation \(Islam\)").

## Social movements
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Social movements")]
Millennial social movements, a specific form of [millenarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millenarianism "Millenarianism"), have as their basis some concept of a cycle of one-thousand years. Sometimes[_[quantify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_] the two terms[_[which?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Avoid_weasel_words "Wikipedia:Avoid weasel words")_] are used[_[by whom?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch")_] as synonyms, but purists regard this as not entirely accurate.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] Millennial social movements need not have a religious foundation, but they must[_[need quotation to verify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_] have a vision of an [apocalypse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalypse "Apocalypse") that can be [utopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopia "Utopia") or [dystopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopia "Dystopia"). Those associated with millennial social movements are "prone to [be violent]",[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-47) with certain types of millennialism connected to violence.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-48)
In progressive millennialism, the "transformation of the social order is gradual and humans play a role in fostering that transformation".[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-49)
Catastrophic millennialism "deems the current social order as irrevocably corrupt, and total destruction of this order is necessary as the precursor to the building of a new, godly order".[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-50)
However, the link between millennialism and violence may be problematic, as new religious movements may stray from the catastrophic view as time progresses.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-51)[_[need quotation to verify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_]
### Nazism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Nazism")]
The most controversial interpretation of the [three-age system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Eras "Three Eras") and of millennialism in general involves [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler")'s "[Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany")" (_Drittes Reich_), which in his vision would last for a thousand years to come (_Tausendjähriges Reich_) but ultimately lasted for only 12 years (1933–1945). 
The German thinker [Arthur Moeller van den Bruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Moeller_van_den_Bruck "Arthur Moeller van den Bruck") coined the phrase "Third Reich" and in 1923 published a book titled _[Das Dritte Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Dritte_Reich "Das Dritte Reich")_. Looking back at [German history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_history "German history"), he distinguished two separate periods, and identified them with [the ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Eras "Three Eras") of the 12th-century Italian theologian [Joachim of Fiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_of_Fiore "Joachim of Fiore"): 
  * the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire") (beginning with [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne") in AD 800): the "First Reich", _The Age of the[Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Father "God the Father")_ and
  * the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire"), under the [House of Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern "House of Hohenzollern") (1871–1918): the "Second Reich", _The Age of the[Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Son "God the Son")_.


After the interval of the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic") (1918 onwards), during which [constitutionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution "Constitution"), [parliamentarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system") and even [pacifism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifism "Pacifism") dominated, these were then to be followed by: 
  * the "Third Reich", _The Age of the[Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit "Holy Spirit")_.


Although van den Bruck was unimpressed by Hitler when he met him in 1922 and did not join the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party "Nazi Party"), nevertheless the Nazis adopted the term "Third Reich" to label the [totalitarian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarian_state "Totalitarian state") they wanted to set up when they gained power, which they [succeeded in doing in 1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabling_Act_of_1933 "Enabling Act of 1933"). Later, however, the Nazi authorities banned the informal use of "Third Reich" throughout the German press in the summer of 1939, instructing it to use more official terms such as "German Reich", "Greater German Reich", and "National Socialist Germany" exclusively.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-52)
During the early part of the Third Reich many [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans "Germans") also referred to Hitler as being the _German Messiah_ , especially when he conducted the [Nuremberg rallies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_rallies "Nuremberg rallies"),[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] which came to be held annually (1933–1938) at a date somewhat before the [September equinox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_equinox "September equinox") in [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg "Nuremberg"). 
In a speech held on 27 November 1937, Hitler commented on [his plans to have major parts of Berlin torn down and rebuilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania_\(city\) "Germania \(city\)"): 
> _[...] einem tausendjährigen Volk mit tausendjähriger geschichtlicher und kultureller Vergangenheit für die vor ihm liegende unabsehbare Zukunft eine ebenbürtige tausendjährige Stadt zu bauen [...]._
> [...] to build a millennial city adequate [in splendour] to a thousand-year-old people with a thousand-year-old historical and cultural past, for its never-ending [glorious] future [...]
After Adolf Hitler's unsuccessful attempt to implement a thousand-year reign, [the Vatican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_See "Holy See") issued an official statement that millennial claims could not be safely taught and that the related scriptures in Revelation (also called the Apocalypse) should be understood spiritually. Catholic author Bernard LeFrois wrote: 
> Millenium [_[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic "Sic")_]: [...] Since the Holy Office decreed (July 21, 1944) that it cannot be safely taught that Christ at His Second Coming will reign visibly with only some of His saints (risen from the dead) for a period of time before the final and universal judgment, a spiritual millenium is to be seen in Apoc. 20:4–6. St. John gives a recapitulation of the activity of Satan, and the spiritual reign of the saints with Christ in heaven and in His Church on earth.
— [[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-53)
### Utopianism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Millennialism&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Utopianism")]
See also: [Three Eras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Eras "Three Eras")
The early Christian concepts of millennialism had ramifications far beyond strictly religious concerns during the centuries to come, as various theorists blended and enhanced them with ideas of [utopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopia "Utopia"). 
In the wake of early millennial thinking, the [Three Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Eras "Three Eras") philosophy developed. The Italian monk and [theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology "Theology") [Joachim of Fiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_of_Fiore "Joachim of Fiore") (died 1202) saw all of human history as a succession of three ages: 
  1. the Age of the [Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Father "God the Father") (the [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament "Old Testament"))
  2. the Age of the [Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Son "God the Son") (the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament "New Testament"))
  3. the Age of the [Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit_in_Christianity "Holy Spirit in Christianity") (the age begun when Christ ascended into heaven, leaving the [Paraclete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraclete "Paraclete"), the third person of the Holy Trinity, to guide the faithful)


It was believed[_[by whom?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch")_] that the Age of the Holy Spirit would begin at c. 1260, and that from then on all believers would live as monks, mystically transfigured and full of praise for God, for a thousand years until [Judgment Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judgment_Day "Judgment Day") would put an end to the history of our planet. 
[Joachim of Fiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_of_Fiore "Joachim of Fiore")'s divisions of historical time also highly influenced the [New Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age "New Age") movement, which transformed the Three Ages philosophy into astrological terminology, relating the [Northern-hemisphere vernal equinox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_equinox "March equinox") to different [constellations of the zodiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellations_of_the_zodiac "Constellations of the zodiac"). In this scenario the Age of the Father was recast[_[by whom?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch")_] as the Age of Aries, the Age of the Son became the Age of Pisces, and the Age of the Holy Spirit was called the Aquarian New Age. The current so-called "[Age of Aquarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Aquarius "Age of Aquarius")" will supposedly witness the development of a number of great changes for humankind,[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-54) reflecting the typical features of some manifestations of millennialism.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennialism#cite_note-55)
