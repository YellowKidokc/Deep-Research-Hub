1991 pseudohistorical conspiracy theory
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Charlemagne_denier_Mayence_812_814.jpg/250px-Charlemagne_denier_Mayence_812_814.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Charlemagne_denier_Mayence_812_814.jpg)The phantom time hypothesis claims [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne") did not exist.
The **phantom time conspiracy theory** is a [pseudohistorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistorical "Pseudohistorical") [conspiracy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory "Conspiracy theory") first asserted by Heribert Illig in 1991. It hypothesizes a conspiracy by the [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor "Holy Roman Emperor") [Otto III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_III "Otto III") and [Pope Sylvester II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_II "Pope Sylvester II") to fabricate the [Anno Domini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Domini "Anno Domini") dating system retroactively, in order to place them at the special year of AD 1000, and to rewrite history[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-1) to legitimize Otto's claim to the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire"). Illig believed that this was achieved through the alteration, misrepresentation and forgery of documentary and physical evidence.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-fomenko1-2) According to this scenario, the entire [Carolingian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_period "Carolingian period"), including the figure of [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne"), is a fabrication, with a "phantom time" of 297 years (AD 614–911) added to the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages "Early Middle Ages"). 
Significant evidence—such as calendars in other European countries, most of Asia and parts of pre-Columbian America—contradicts the hypothesis and it failed to gain the support of historians and archaeologists.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Dutch-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Foessel-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Muetz-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Adams-6)
Another version of phantom time with a longer time loss is suggested in [Florin Diacu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florin_Diacu "Florin Diacu")'s 2006 book _The Lost Millennium: History's Timetables Under Siege_. 
## Heribert Illig
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Heribert Illig")]
Illig was born in 1947 in [Vohenstrauß](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vohenstrau%C3%9F "Vohenstrauß"), [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria "Bavaria"). He was active in an association dedicated to [Immanuel Velikovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Velikovsky "Immanuel Velikovsky"), [catastrophism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catastrophism "Catastrophism") and [historical revisionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_revisionism "Historical revisionism"), the _Gesellschaft zur Rekonstruktion der Menschheits- und Naturgeschichte_ (English: _Society for the Reconstruction of Human and Natural History_). From 1989 to 1994, he acted as editor of the journal _Vorzeit-Frühzeit-Gegenwart_ (English: _Prehistory-Proto-History-Present_). Since 1995, he has worked as a publisher and author under his own publishing company, _Mantis-Verlag_ , and publishing his own journal, _Zeitensprünge_ (English: _Leaps in Time_). Outside of his publications related to revised chronology, he has edited the works of [Egon Friedell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egon_Friedell "Egon Friedell"). 
Before focusing on the early medieval period, Illig published various proposals for revised chronologies of prehistory and of Ancient Egypt. His proposals received prominent coverage in German popular media in the 1990s. His 1996 _Das erfundene Mittelalter_ (English: _The Invented Middle Ages_) also received scholarly reviews, but was universally rejected as fundamentally flawed by historians.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-7) In 1997, the journal _Ethik und Sozialwissenschaften_ (English: _Ethics and Social Sciences_) offered a platform for critical discussion to Illig's proposal, with a number of historians commenting on its various aspects.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-8) After 1997, there has been little scholarly reception of Illig's ideas, although they continued to be discussed as [pseudohistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory "Pseudohistory") in German popular media.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-9)
## Claims
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Claims")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/LessayAbbaye3.JPG/250px-LessayAbbaye3.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:LessayAbbaye3.JPG)[Lessay Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lessay_Abbey "Lessay Abbey"), an example of [Romanesque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture "Romanesque architecture")
Illig's claims include:[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Illig_1-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Illig_2-11)
  * That there is a scarcity of [archaeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology") evidence that can be reliably dated to the period AD 614–911.
  * That the dating methods used for such recent periods, [radiometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiometry "Radiometry") and [dendrochronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrochronology "Dendrochronology"), are inaccurate.
  * That medieval historians rely too much on written sources.
  * That the presence of [Romanesque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture "Romanesque architecture") in tenth-century Western Europe suggests that the Roman era was not as long ago as conventionally thought.
  * That at the time of the introduction of the [Gregorian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar "Gregorian calendar") in AD 1582, there should have been a discrepancy of thirteen days between the [Julian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_calendar "Julian calendar") and the real (or tropical) calendar, when the astronomers and mathematicians working for [Pope Gregory XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XIII "Pope Gregory XIII") had found that the civil calendar needed to be adjusted by only ten days. From this, Illig concludes that the [AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Domini "Anno Domini") era had counted roughly three centuries which never existed.


## Refutation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Refutation")]
  * Observations in [ancient astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeoastronomy "Archaeoastronomy"), especially those of [solar eclipses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse "Solar eclipse") cited by European sources prior to 600 AD (when phantom time would have distorted the chronology), agree with the usual chronology and not with Illig's. Besides several others that are perhaps too vague to disprove the phantom time hypothesis, two in particular are dated with enough precision to question the hypothesis. One is reported by [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder "Pliny the Elder") in 59 AD.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Pliny-12) This date has a confirmed [eclipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_solar_eclipses "Lists of solar eclipses"). In addition, observations during the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") in China, and [Halley's Comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halley%27s_Comet "Halley's Comet"), for example, are consistent with current astronomy with no "phantom time" added.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Hermann-13)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Dutch-3)
  * Archaeological remains and dating methods such as [dendrochronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrochronology "Dendrochronology") (tree-ring dating) refute, rather than support, "phantom time".[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Foessel-4)
  * The Gregorian reform was never purported to bring the calendar in line with the Julian calendar as it had existed at the time of its institution in 45 BC, but as it had existed in 325 AD, the time of the [Council of Nicaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea "First Council of Nicaea"), which had established a method for [determining the date](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computus "Computus") of [Easter Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Sunday "Easter Sunday") by fixing the [vernal equinox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_equinox "March equinox") on March 21 in the Julian calendar. By 1582, the astronomical equinox was occurring on March 10 in the Julian calendar, but Easter was still being calculated from a nominal equinox on March 21. In 45 BC, the astronomical vernal equinox took place around March 23. Illig's "three missing centuries" thus correspond to the 369 years between the institution of the Julian calendar in 45 BC, and the fixing of the Easter Date at the Council of Nicaea in 325 AD.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Muetz-5)
  * If [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne") and the [Carolingian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_dynasty "Carolingian dynasty") were fabricated, there would have to be a corresponding fabrication of the history of the rest of Europe during the same era, including [Anglo-Saxon England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_England "Anglo-Saxon England"), the [Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_papacy "History of the papacy"), and the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire"). The "phantom time" period also encompasses the life of [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad "Muhammad") and the [Islamic expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests "Early Muslim conquests") into the areas of the former [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire "Western Roman Empire"), including the conquest of [Visigothic Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom "Visigothic Kingdom"). This history too would have to be forged or drastically misdated. It would also have to be reconciled with the history of the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") of China and its contact with the Islamic world, such as at the [Battle of Talas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Talas "Battle of Talas").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Dutch-3)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_time_conspiracy_theory#cite_note-Adams-6)
