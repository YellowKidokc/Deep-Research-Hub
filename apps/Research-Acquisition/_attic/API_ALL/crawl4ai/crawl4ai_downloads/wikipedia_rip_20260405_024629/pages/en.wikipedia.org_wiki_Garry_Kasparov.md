**Garry Kimovich Kasparov**[[a]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-2) (born **Garik Kimovich Weinstein**[[b]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-3) on 13 April 1963) is a Russian [chess grandmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmaster_\(chess\) "Grandmaster \(chess\)"), political activist and [writer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writer "Writer"), who was the [World Chess Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Champion "World Chess Champion") from 1985 to 2000. His peak [FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE "FIDE") chess [rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elo_rating_system "Elo rating system") of 2851,[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-4) achieved in 1999, was the highest recorded until being surpassed by [Magnus Carlsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Carlsen "Magnus Carlsen") in 2013. From 1984 until his retirement from regular competitive chess in 2005, Kasparov was ranked the world's No. 1 player for a record 255 months overall. Kasparov also [holds records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#Assessment_and_legacy) for the most consecutive professional tournament victories (15) and [Chess Oscars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Oscar "Chess Oscar") (11). 
Kasparov became the youngest undisputed world champion in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1985 "World Chess Championship 1985") at age 22 by defeating then-champion [Anatoly Karpov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Karpov "Anatoly Karpov"), a record he held until 2024. He defended the title against Karpov three times, in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1986 "World Chess Championship 1986"), [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1987 "World Chess Championship 1987") and [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1990 "World Chess Championship 1990"). Kasparov held the official FIDE world title until 1993, when a dispute with FIDE led him to set up a rival organisation, the [Professional Chess Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Chess_Association "Professional Chess Association").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-5) In 1997, he became the first world champion to lose a match to a computer under standard [time controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_control "Time control") when he was defeated by the [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM "IBM") supercomputer [Deep Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Blue_\(chess_computer\) "Deep Blue \(chess computer\)") in a [highly publicised match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Blue_versus_Garry_Kasparov "Deep Blue versus Garry Kasparov"). He continued to hold the "Classical" world title until his defeat by [Vladimir Kramnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Kramnik "Vladimir Kramnik") in 2000. Despite losing the PCA title, he continued winning tournaments and was the world's highest-rated player at the time of his official retirement. Kasparov coached Carlsen in 2009–2010, during which time Carlsen rose to world No. 1. Kasparov stood unsuccessfully for FIDE president in 2013–2014. 
Since retiring from chess, Kasparov has devoted his time to writing and politics. His book series _My Great Predecessors_ , first published in 2003, details the history and games of the world champion chess players who preceded him. He formed the [United Civil Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Civil_Front "United Civil Front") movement and was a member of [The Other Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Other_Russia_\(coalition\) "The Other Russia \(coalition\)"), a coalition opposing the administration and [policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putinism "Putinism") of [Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin "Vladimir Putin"). In 2008, he announced an intention to run as a candidate in [that year's Russian presidential race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Russian_presidential_election "2008 Russian presidential election"), but after encountering logistical problems in his campaign, for which he blamed "official obstruction", he withdrew.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-heir-6)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-7)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-8) Following the [Russian mass protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%932013_Russian_protests "2011–2013 Russian protests") that began in 2011, he announced in June 2013 that he had left Russia for the immediate future out of fear of persecution.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-ftfear-9) Following his flight from Russia, he lived in New York City with his family.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Incorrigible-10)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-11) In 2014, he obtained [Croatian citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_nationality_law "Croatian nationality law") and has maintained a residence in [Podstrana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podstrana "Podstrana") near [Split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split,_Croatia "Split, Croatia").[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-vecernjidrz-12)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-13)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-slobdalm-14)
Kasparov was chairman of the [Human Rights Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Foundation "Human Rights Foundation") from 2011 to 2024. In 2017, he founded the [Renew Democracy Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renew_Democracy_Initiative "Renew Democracy Initiative") (RDI), an American political organisation promoting and defending [liberal democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy "Liberal democracy") in the U.S. and abroad. He serves as chairman of the group. 
## Early life
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")]
[![refer to caption](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Kasparov-2.jpg/250px-Kasparov-2.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kasparov-2.jpg)Kasparov at age 11, Vilnius, 1974
Kasparov was born Garik Kimovich Weinstein ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Гарик Кимович Вайнштейн, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian "Romanization of Russian"):  _Garik Kimovich Vainshtein_) in [Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku "Baku"), [Azerbaijan SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Soviet_Socialist_Republic "Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic") (now [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan "Azerbaijan")), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"). His father, Kim Moiseyevich Weinstein, was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Azerbaijan "History of the Jews in Azerbaijan") and his mother, Klara Shagenovna Kasparova, was [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians "Armenians").[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-15)[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-16)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-biography-17)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-18) Both of his mother's parents were Armenians from [Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh "Karabakh").[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-19) According to Kasparov himself, he was named after United States President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman"),[[c]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-21)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-22) "whom my father admired for taking a strong stand against communism. It was a rare name in Russia, until _[Harry Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Potter "Harry Potter")_ came along."[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-23) Kasparov has described himself as a "self-appointed Christian", although "very indifferent"[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-24) and identifying as [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians "Russians"): "[A]lthough I'm half-Armenian, half-Jewish, I consider myself Russian because Russian is my native tongue, and I grew up with Russian culture."[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-25)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-26)
When he was seven years old, his father died of [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia").[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Azerbaijan-27) At the age of twelve, Kasparov, upon the request of his mother Klara and with the consent of the family, adopted Klara's surname Kasparov.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-28) Kasparov and his family had to flee [anti-Armenian pogroms in Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_pogrom "Baku pogrom") in January 1990.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-29)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-30)
### Introduction to chess
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Introduction to chess")]
Kasparov began the serious study of [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess "Chess") after he came across a problem set up by his parents and proposed a solution.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-31) From age seven, he attended the [Young Pioneer Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneers_Palace "Pioneers Palace") in [Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku "Baku") and, at ten, began training at [Mikhail Botvinnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Botvinnik "Mikhail Botvinnik")'s chess school under coach [Vladimir Makogonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Makogonov "Vladimir Makogonov"). When Kasparov was 11, Botvinnik wrote, "_The future of chess lies in the hands of this young man._ "[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-32) Makogonov helped develop Kasparov's positional skills and taught him to play the [Caro–Kann Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caro%E2%80%93Kann_Defence "Caro–Kann Defence") and the [Tartakower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savielly_Tartakower "Savielly Tartakower") System of the [Queen's Gambit Declined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen%27s_Gambit_Declined "Queen's Gambit Declined").[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-33) Kasparov won the Soviet Junior Championship in [Tbilisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tbilisi "Tbilisi") in 1976, scoring 7/9 points, at age 13. He repeated the feat the following year, winning with a score of 8.5/9. He was being coached by [Alexander Shakarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Shakarov "Alexander Shakarov") during this time.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-34)
## Chess career
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Chess career")]
### Rising up the ranks
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Rising up the ranks")]
In early 1978, Kasparov participated in the [Sokolsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Sokolsky "Alexey Sokolsky") Memorial tournament in [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk "Minsk"). Normally only established masters and local players were invited, but he received a special invitation,[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-35) and took first place. Kasparov said that after the victory, he thought he had a "very good shot" at the world championship.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-36) He first qualified for the [USSR Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Chess_Championship "USSR Chess Championship") at age 15 in 1978, the youngest-ever player at that level, by winning a 64-player Swiss system tournament at [Daugavpils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daugavpils "Daugavpils").[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-37) He scored 8.5/17 in [that Soviet championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_USSR_Chess_Championship "1978 USSR Chess Championship"). 
Kasparov's first international tournament was in [Banja Luka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banja_Luka "Banja Luka"), [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia "Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia"), in April 1979 while he was still [unrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elo_rating_system "Elo rating system"). Kasparov won this high-class tournament by 2 points,[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-38) emerging with a provisional rating of 2545,[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-39) enough to rank him equal 40th in the world.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-40) The next year, 1980, he won the [World Junior Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Junior_Chess_Championship "World Junior Chess Championship") in [Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund "Dortmund"), West Germany. Later that year, he made his debut as the second reserve for the Soviet Union at the [Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Olympiad "Chess Olympiad") at [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta "Valletta"), Malta, and became a Grandmaster.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-41) He quickly rose up the world rankings: equal 15th in January 1980,[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-42) sixth in January 1981,[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-43) equal fourth in July 1981,[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-44) fourth in January 1982[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-45) and second behind Karpov in July 1982.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-46)
As a teenager, Kasparov shared the [USSR Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Chess_Championship "USSR Chess Championship") in 1981 with [Lev Psakhis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Psakhis "Lev Psakhis") (12.5/17), although Psakhis won their game.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-47) His first win in a superclass-level international tournament was scored at [Bugojno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugojno "Bugojno"), Yugoslavia, in 1982. He earned a place in the 1982 Moscow [Interzonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interzonal "Interzonal") tournament, which he won, to qualify for the [Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_Tournament "Candidates Tournament").[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-chessmetrics-48) At age 19, he was the youngest Candidate since [Bobby Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Fischer "Bobby Fischer"), who was 15 when he qualified in 1958.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
[![refer to caption](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a5/Garry_Kasparov_1980_Dortmund.jpg/250px-Garry_Kasparov_1980_Dortmund.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Garry_Kasparov_1980_Dortmund.jpg)Kasparov at World Junior Championship victory ceremony, Dortmund, 1980
Kasparov's first (quarter-final) Candidates match was against [Alexander Beliavsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Beliavsky "Alexander Beliavsky"), whom he defeated 6–3 (four wins, one loss).[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-49) Politics threatened Kasparov's semi-final against [Victor Korchnoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Korchnoi "Victor Korchnoi"), which was scheduled to be played in [Pasadena, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_California "Pasadena, California"). The Soviet authorities would not allow Kasparov to travel to the United States, meaning that Korchnoi could have had a walkover.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-50) This decision was met with disapproval by the chess world, and Korchnoi agreed to the match to being played in London instead, along with the previously scheduled match between [Vasily Smyslov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Smyslov "Vasily Smyslov") and [Zoltán Ribli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zolt%C3%A1n_Ribli "Zoltán Ribli"). The Kasparov-Korchnoi match was put together on short notice by [Raymond Keene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Keene "Raymond Keene"). Kasparov lost the first game but won the match 7–4 (four wins, one loss).[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-51)
In January 1984, Kasparov became the [No. 1 ranked player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIDE_chess_world_number_ones "List of FIDE chess world number ones") in the world, with a FIDE rating of 2710. He became the youngest-ever world No. 1, a record that lasted 12 years until being broken by Kramnik in January 1996.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-52) That same year, he won the Candidates' final 8½–4½ (four wins, no losses) against former world champion Smyslov at [Vilnius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius "Vilnius"), thus qualifying to play Karpov for the world championship.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-53)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: 1984–1985 world championship")]
Main article: [World Chess Championship 1984–1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1984%E2%80%931985 "World Chess Championship 1984–1985")
The [World Chess Championship 1984–1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1984%E2%80%931985 "World Chess Championship 1984–1985") match between Kasparov and Karpov had many ups and downs and a controversial finish. Karpov started in very good form, and after nine games Kasparov was down 4–0 in a "first to six wins" match.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Karpov1984-54) In an unexpected turn of events, there followed a series of 17 successive draws, some relatively short, others drawn in unsettled positions. Kasparov lost game 27 (5–0), then fought back with another series of draws until game 32, earning his first-ever win against the world champion and bringing the score to 5–1. Another 14 successive draws followed, through game 46; the previous record length for a world title match had been 34 games ([José Raúl Capablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Ra%C3%BAl_Capablanca "José Raúl Capablanca") vs. [Alexander Alekhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Alekhine "Alexander Alekhine") in 1927).[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-55)
Kasparov won games 47 and 48 to bring the score to 5–3 in Karpov's favour. Then the match was ended without result by [FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_Internationale_des_%C3%89checs "Fédération Internationale des Échecs") President [Florencio Campomanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florencio_Campomanes "Florencio Campomanes"), and a new match was announced to start a few months later. The termination was controversial, as both players stated that they preferred the match to continue.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-56)[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-57) Announcing his decision, Campomanes cited the health of the players, which had been strained by the length of the match.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-58) According to grandmasters [Boris Gulko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Gulko "Boris Gulko") and Korchnoi, and historians Vladimir Popow and [Yuri Felshtinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Felshtinsky "Yuri Felshtinsky") in their _The KGB Plays Chess_ book, Campomanes had been a [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB "KGB") agent and was tasked with preventing Karpov's defeat at all costs; and the match was terminated while Karpov was still ahead to avoid the impression that the decision had been made for his benefit.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-chessbase-25-years-ago-59) US Grandmaster [Andy Soltis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Soltis "Andy Soltis") said it was "absurd" to suggest that Campomones was a KGB agent, but thought that his decisions in the match favoured Karpov.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-60)
The match became the first, and so far only, world championship match to be abandoned without a result. Kasparov's relations with Campomanes and FIDE became strained,[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-chessbase-25-years-ago-59)[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-61) and matters came to a head in 1993 with Kasparov's complete break-away from FIDE.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-62)
### World champion
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: World champion")]
[![refer to caption](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Kasparov-1.jpg/250px-Kasparov-1.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kasparov-1.jpg)Kasparov after winning the FIDE World Championship title in 1985
The [second Karpov–Kasparov match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1985 "World Chess Championship 1985") in 1985 was organised in Moscow as the best of 24 games, where the first player to win 12½ points would claim the title. The scores from the terminated match would not carry over; however, in the event of a 12–12 draw, the title would remain with Karpov. On 9 November 1985, Kasparov secured the world crown by a score of 13–11. Karpov, with White, needed to win the 24th game to retain the title but Kasparov won it with the [Sicilian Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Defence "Sicilian Defence").[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-63) He was 22 years old at the time, making him the youngest-ever world champion,[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-64) a record held by [Mikhail Tal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Tal "Mikhail Tal") for over 20 years.[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-65) Kasparov's win with Black in the 16th game has been recognised as one of the all-time chess masterpieces, including being voted the best game played during the first 64 issues of the magazine _[Chess Informant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Informant "Chess Informant")._[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-66)[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-67)
At the time, the champion still had the right to a rematch after losing the title.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-68) [This rematch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1986 "World Chess Championship 1986") took place in 1986, hosted jointly in London and [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg "Saint Petersburg"),[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-69)[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-chessgames_55017-70) with each city hosting 12 games. At one point in the match, Kasparov opened a three-point lead and looked well on his way to a decisive victory. But Karpov fought back by winning three consecutive games to level the score late in the match. At this point, Kasparov dismissed one of his seconds, grandmaster [Evgeny Vladimirov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Vladimirov "Evgeny Vladimirov"), accusing him of selling his [opening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_opening "Chess opening") preparation to the Karpov team.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-71)[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-72) Kasparov scored one more win and kept his title by a score of 12½–11½.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-chessgames_55017-70)
[A fourth match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1987 "World Chess Championship 1987") for the world title took place in 1987 in [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville "Seville"),[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-73) as Karpov had qualified through the Candidates' Matches to become the official challenger once again. This match was also very close, with neither player holding more than a one-point lead at any time. With one game left, Kasparov was down a point and needed a win to draw the match and retain his title. A long, tense game ensued, in which Karpov [blundered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blunder_\(chess\) "Blunder \(chess\)") away a [pawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawn_\(chess\) "Pawn \(chess\)") just before the first [time control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_control "Time control"). Kasparov then won a long ending to retain the title on a 12–12 scoreline.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-74)
Kasparov and Karpov [met for a fifth time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1990 "World Chess Championship 1990"), on this occasion in New York City and [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon "Lyon") in 1990, with each city hosting 12 games. Again, the result was a close one, with Kasparov winning by a margin of 12½–11½. In their five world championship matches, Kasparov had 21 wins, 19 losses and 104 draws in 144 games.[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-75)
### Break with and ejection from FIDE
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Break with and ejection from FIDE")]
[![refer to caption](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Kasparov-10.jpg/250px-Kasparov-10.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kasparov-10.jpg)Kasparov and Anand in a publicity photo on top of the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\) "World Trade Center \(1973–2001\)") in New York, 1995
In November 1986, Kasparov had created the Grandmasters Association (GMA) to represent professional players and give them more say in FIDE's activities.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-GMA-76) Kasparov assumed a leadership role. GMA's major achievement was in organising a series of [six World Cup tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Cup_1988%E2%80%931989 "Chess World Cup 1988–1989") for the world's top players.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-77) This caused an uneasy relationship to develop between Kasparov and FIDE.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-78) The previous month, Kasparov had made his feelings clear to fellow grandmaster Keene: "Campomanes must go. It is war to the death with him as far as I am concerned. I will do everything I can to remove him."[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-79)
This stand-off lasted until 1993, by which time a new challenger had qualified through the [Candidates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_Tournament "Candidates Tournament") cycle: [Nigel Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Short "Nigel Short"), a British grandmaster who had defeated Karpov in a qualifying match and then [Jan Timman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Timman "Jan Timman") in the finals held in early 1993.[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-80) After a confusing and compressed bidding process produced lower financial estimates than expected,[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-81) the world champion and his challenger both rejected FIDE's bid for an August match in Manchester and decided to play outside FIDE's jurisdiction.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-82)[[80]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-83) Their match took place under the auspices of the [Professional Chess Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Chess_Association "Professional Chess Association") (PCA), an organisation established by Kasparov and Short.[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-GMA-76)[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-84) At this point, a fracture occurred in the lineage of the FIDE World Championship. In an interview in 2007, Kasparov called the break with FIDE in 1993 the worst mistake of his career, as it hurt the game in the long run.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-85)
Kasparov and Short were ejected from FIDE and [played their well-sponsored match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1993 "World Chess Championship 1993") in London in September 1993.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-86) Kasparov won convincingly by a score of 12½–7½. The match considerably raised the profile of chess in the UK, with a substantial level of coverage on [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4 "Channel 4").[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-87)[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-88) Meanwhile, FIDE organised its world championship match between Timman (the defeated Candidates finalist) and former world champion Karpov (a defeated Candidates semi-finalist), which Karpov won.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-89)[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-90) FIDE removed Kasparov and Short from its rating list. Subsequently, the PCA created a rating list of its own, which featured all the world's top players regardless of their relation to FIDE. There were now two world champions: PCA champion Kasparov and FIDE champion Karpov.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-91) The title remained split for 13 years.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
Kasparov defended his PCA title in a [1995 match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCA_World_Chess_Championship_1995 "PCA World Chess Championship 1995") against [Viswanathan Anand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viswanathan_Anand "Viswanathan Anand") at the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\) "World Trade Center \(1973–2001\)") in New York City. Kasparov won the match by four wins to one, with thirteen draws.[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-92) He tried to organise another world championship match under a different organisation, the World Chess Association (WCA), with [Linares International Chess Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linares_International_Chess_Tournament "Linares International Chess Tournament") organiser [Luis Rentero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Rentero "Luis Rentero"). [Alexei Shirov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Shirov "Alexei Shirov") and Kramnik played a candidates match to decide the challenger, which Shirov won in an upset. But when Rentero admitted that the funds required and promised had never materialised, the WCA collapsed. Yet another body stepped in, BrainGames.com, headed by [Raymond Keene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Keene "Raymond Keene"). After a match with Shirov could not be agreed by BrainGames.com and talks with Anand collapsed, a match was instead arranged against Kramnik.[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-93)
During this period, Kasparov was approached by [Oakham School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakham_School "Oakham School") in the United Kingdom, at the time the only school in the country with a full-time chess coach,[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-94) and developed an interest in the use of chess in education. In 1997, Kasparov supported a scholarship programme at the school.[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-95)
### Losing the title and aftermath
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Losing the title and aftermath")]
[![refer to caption](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/Kasparov-11.jpg/250px-Kasparov-11.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kasparov-11.jpg)Kasparov playing against Kramnik in the Botvinnik Memorial match in Moscow, 2001
The [Kasparov–Kramnik match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_World_Chess_Championship_2000 "Classical World Chess Championship 2000") took place in London during the latter half of 2000. Kramnik had been a student of Kasparov's at the famous Botvinnik/Kasparov chess school in Russia and had served on Kasparov's team for the 1995 match with Anand.[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-96) Kasparov found that Kramnik was better prepared. As White, Kasparov could not crack the passive but solid [Berlin Defence in the Ruy Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_Lopez#Berlin_Defence "Ruy Lopez"), and Kramnik managed to draw all his games as Black. As black, Kasparov lost two (games 2 and 10), meaning Kramnik won the match 8½–6½,[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-97) and Kramnik succeeded Kasparov as the Classical World Champion.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
After losing the title, Kasparov won a series of major tournaments and remained the top-rated player in the world, ahead of both Kramnik and the FIDE World Champion. In 2001, he refused an invitation to the [2002 Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Candidates_Tournament "2002 Candidates Tournament") to choose Kramnik's first challenger, claiming his results had earned him a rematch with Kramnik.[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-98) Kasparov and Karpov played a four-game match with rapid time controls over two days in December 2002 in New York City. Kasparov suffered a surprise loss (1½–2½).[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-99)
Because of Kasparov's continuing strong results and status as FIDE world No. 1, he was included in the so-called "Prague Agreement", masterminded by [Yasser Seirawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Seirawan "Yasser Seirawan") and intended to reunite the two world championships. Kasparov was to play a match against the FIDE World Champion Ponomariov in September 2003.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-100) But this match was called off after Ponomariov refused to sign his contract for it without reservation. In its place, there were plans for a match against [Rustam Kasimdzhanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustam_Kasimdzhanov "Rustam Kasimdzhanov"), winner of the [FIDE World Chess Championship 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_World_Chess_Championship_2004 "FIDE World Chess Championship 2004"), to be held in January 2005 in the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates"). These also fell through owing to a lack of funding. Plans to hold the match in Turkey instead came too late. Kasparov announced in January 2005 that he was tired of waiting for FIDE to arrange a match and had decided to stop all efforts to become undisputed world champion once more.[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-chessbase1-101)
### Retirement from regular competitive chess
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Retirement from regular competitive chess")]
[![refer to caption](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Kasparov-34.jpg/250px-Kasparov-34.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kasparov-34.jpg)Kasparov in 2007
After winning the prestigious [Linares tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linares_International_Chess_Tournament "Linares International Chess Tournament") for the ninth time, Kasparov announced on 10 March 2005 that he would retire from regular competitive chess. He cited as the reason a lack of personal goals in the chess world. When winning the [Russian championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Chess_Championship "Russian Chess Championship") in 2004, he commented that it had been the last major title he had never won outright. He also expressed frustration at the failure to reunify the world championship.[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-102)[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-chessbase1-101) Kasparov said he might play in some rapid chess events for fun, but he intended to spend more time on his books, including the _My Great Predecessors_ series, and work on the links between decision-making in chess and other areas of life. He also stated that he would continue to involve himself in [Russian politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Russia "Politics of Russia"), which he viewed as "headed down the wrong path".[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-103)[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-104)
### Post-retirement chess
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Post-retirement chess")]
On 22 August 2006, in his first public chess games since his retirement, Kasparov played in the Lichthof Chess Champions Tournament, a [blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_chess "Fast chess") event played at the time control of five minutes per side and three-second increments per move. Kasparov tied for first with Karpov, scoring 4½/6.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-105) The pair played a 12-game match from 21 to 24 September 2009, in [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia "Valencia"), Spain. It consisted of four rapid (or semi rapid) games, in which Kasparov won 3–1, and eight blitz games, in which Kasparov won 6–2, winning the match with a final result of 9–3. The event took place exactly 25 years after the two players' unfinished encounter at [World Chess Championship 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1984 "World Chess Championship 1984").[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-106)
Kasparov coached Carlsen for approximately one year, beginning in February 2009. The collaboration remained secret until September 2009.[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-107) Under Kasparov's tutelage, in October 2009 Carlsen became the youngest ever to achieve a FIDE rating higher than 2800, and he rose from world number four to world number one. While the pair initially planned to work together throughout 2010,[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-carlsen-108) in March of that year it was announced that Carlsen had split from Kasparov and would no longer be using him as a trainer.[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-109) According to an interview with the German magazine _[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel "Der Spiegel")_ , Carlsen indicated that he would remain in contact and that he would continue to attend training sessions with Kasparov;[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-110) however, no further training sessions were held, and the cooperation fizzled out over the course of the spring.[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-111) In 2011, Carlsen said: "Thanks to [Kasparov] I began to understand a whole class of positions better. ... Kasparov gave me a great deal of practical help."[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-a-112) In 2012, when asked what he learnt from working with Kasparov, Carlsen answered: "Complex positions. That was the most important thing."[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-113)
In May 2010, Kasparov played and won 30 games simultaneously against players at [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University "Tel Aviv University") in Israel.[[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-114) In the same month, it was revealed that he had aided Anand in his preparation for the [World Chess Championship 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2010 "World Chess Championship 2010") against challenger [Veselin Topalov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veselin_Topalov "Veselin Topalov"). Anand won the match 6½–5½ to retain the title.[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-115) Kasparov began training the U.S. grandmaster [Hikaru Nakamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hikaru_Nakamura "Hikaru Nakamura") in January 2011. The first of several training sessions was held in New York just before Nakamura participated in the Tata Steel Chess tournament in Wijk aan Zee, the Netherlands.[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-116) In December 2011, it was announced that their cooperation had come to an end.[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-117)

Kasparov won again by a score of 4½–3½.[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-119)[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-120) A little after that, in October 2011, Kasparov played and defeated fourteen opponents in a [simultaneous exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_exhibition "Simultaneous exhibition") that took place in [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava "Bratislava").[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-121)
On 25 and 26 April 2015, Kasparov played a mini-match against Short. The match consisted of two rapid games and eight blitz games and was contested over the course of two days. Commentators GM [Maurice Ashley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Ashley "Maurice Ashley") and [Alejandro Ramírez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Ram%C3%ADrez_\(chess_player\) "Alejandro Ramírez \(chess player\)") remarked how Kasparov was an 'initiative hog' throughout the match, consistently not allowing Short to gain any foothold in the games. He won all games.[[124]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-127)

## Olympiads and major team events
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Olympiads and major team events")]
[![refer to caption](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Garry_Kasparov_1980_Malta.jpg/250px-Garry_Kasparov_1980_Malta.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Garry_Kasparov_1980_Malta.jpg)Kasparov at Valletta in 1980
Kasparov played in a total of eight [Chess Olympiads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Olympiad "Chess Olympiad"). He represented the Soviet Union four times and Russia four times, following the break-up of the Soviet Union in 1991. In his 1980 Olympiad debut, he became, at age 17, the youngest player to represent the Soviet Union or Russia at that level, a record which was broken by Kramnik in 1992. In 82 games, he scored (+50−3=29), for 78.7%, and won a total of nineteen medals, including team gold medals all eight times he competed.[[134]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-137)
For the 1994 Moscow Olympiad, he had a significant organisational role in helping to put together the event on short notice, after [Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaloniki "Thessaloniki") cancelled its offer to host only a few weeks before the scheduled dates. Kasparov's detailed Olympiad record follows:[[135]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-138)
  * [Valletta 1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24th_Chess_Olympiad "24th Chess Olympiad"), USSR 2nd reserve, 9½/12 (+8−1=3), team gold, board bronze;
  * [Lucerne 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Chess_Olympiad "25th Chess Olympiad"), USSR 2nd board, 8½/11 (+6−0=5), team gold, board bronze;
  * [Dubai 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/27th_Chess_Olympiad "27th Chess Olympiad"), USSR 1st board, 8½/11 (+7−1=3), team gold, board gold, performance gold;
  * [Thessaloniki 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28th_Chess_Olympiad "28th Chess Olympiad"), USSR 1st board, 8½/10 (+7−0=3), team gold, board gold, performance gold;
  * [Manila 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30th_Chess_Olympiad "30th Chess Olympiad"), Russia board 1, 8½/10 (+7−0=3), team gold, board gold, performance silver;
  * [Moscow 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_Chess_Olympiad "31st Chess Olympiad"), Russia board 1, 6½/10 (+4−1=5), team gold;
  * [Yerevan 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Chess_Olympiad "32nd Chess Olympiad"), Russia board 1, 7/9 (+5−0=4), team gold, board silver, performance gold;
  * [Bled 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35th_Chess_Olympiad "35th Chess Olympiad"), Russia board 1, 7½/9 (+6−0=3), team gold, performance gold.


Kasparov made his international debut for the USSR at age 16 in the 1980 European Team Championship and played for Russia in the 1992 edition of that championship. He won a total of five medals. His detailed record in this event follows:[[136]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-139)
  * [Skara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skara "Skara") 1980, USSR 2nd reserve, 5½/6 (+5−0=1), team gold, board gold;
  * [Debrecen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debrecen "Debrecen") 1992, Russia board 1, 6/8 (+4−0=4), team gold, board gold, performance silver.


Kasparov also represented the USSR once at the Youth Olympiad in Austria (1981). He scored 9/10 (+8–0=2) on the top board and his team lifted the title.[[137]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-140)
## Assessment and legacy
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Assessment and legacy")]
Kasparov received a [Chess Oscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Oscar "Chess Oscar") eleven times as the best chess player of the year, in 1982–1983, 1985–1988, 1995–1996, 1999, and 2001–2002.[[138]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-141) He was also awarded the [Marca Leyenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marca_Leyenda "Marca Leyenda") trophy in 1997.[[139]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-142) Between 1981 and 1991, he won or tied for first place in every tournament he entered.[[140]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-143) In 1999, Kasparov reached an Elo rating of 2851, a record that stood for over thirteen years, until Carlsen achieved 2861 in January 2013.[[141]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-144)[[142]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-145) With the exception of the PCA period and sharing first place with Kramnik in 1996, Kasparov led the rating list from 1985 to 2006 – a total of 255 months.[[143]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-146) On 1 January 2006, Kasparov ranked first with 2812. However, he was excluded from the FIDE rating list of 1 April 2006 because he had not participated in tournaments for the previous twelve months.[[144]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-147)
The [rivalry between Kasparov and Karpov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karpov%E2%80%93Kasparov_rivalry "Karpov–Kasparov rivalry") (often referred to as the "two Ks")[[145]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-148) is one of the greatest in the history of chess. In six years they played five matches comprising 144 games.[[146]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-149)[[147]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Weaver-Guardian-150) For a long time there was personal enmity between Karpov and Kasparov.[[148]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-151) The conflict between the two men also had a political connotation. Karpov was considered a representative of the Soviet [nomenklatura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomenklatura "Nomenklatura"), while Kasparov was young and popular, positioned himself as a "child of change", willingly gave candid interviews and (especially in [the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world "Western world")) had an aura of a rebel, although he was never a dissident.[[147]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Weaver-Guardian-150) Kasparov's 1985 victory coincided with the start of [perestroika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika "Perestroika") in the Soviet Union.[[149]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-152)
Carlsen said of Kasparov: "I've never seen someone with such a feel for [dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_chess#Dynamism "Glossary of chess") in complex positions."[[150]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-153) Kramnik has opined that Kasparov's "capacity for study is second to none", adding "There is nothing in chess he has been unable to deal with."[[151]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-154) In 2007, the international consulting company Synectics published a rating of 100 living geniuses in science, politics, art and entrepreneurship, in which Kasparov ranked 25th.[[152]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-155) Less known about Kasparov is his emphasis on physical fitness, including taking a month off each year to work out strenuously.[[153]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-156)
### Playing style
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Playing style")]
Kramnik called Kasparov a chess player with virtually no weaknesses.[[154]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-157) His games are characterised by a dynamic style of play with a focus on tactics, depth of strategy, subtle calculation and original [opening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_opening "Chess opening") ideas.[[155]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Rocher-BBC-158) Kasparov was known for his extensive opening preparation and aggressive play in it.[[156]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-159)[[157]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-160) Sergey Shipov considered Kasparov's moral and volitional qualities (impulsiveness and psychological instability) and excessive reliance on options, which can lead to overwork and mistakes, as amongst his few shortcomings.[[158]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-161)
Kasparov's attacking style of play has been compared by many to Alekhine,[[159]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-162)[[160]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-163) his chess idol since childhood. Kasparov has described his style as being influenced chiefly by Alekhine, Tal and Fischer.[[161]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-164) Other influences on Kasparov were his early coaches. At a young age, he met with experienced teachers [Alexander Nikitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Nikitin_\(chess_player\) "Aleksandr Nikitin \(chess player\)") and [Alexander Shakarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Shakarov "Alexander Shakarov"). Shakarov collected and systematised materials, and then became the keeper of Kasparov's "information bank". A revolutionary step at that time was the involvement of computer programs in analysing games, and it was Kasparov and his team who took the first steps in this direction.[[162]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-165)[[163]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-166) In 1973, Kasparov entered the Botvinnik school and immediately attracted attention. Botvinnik commented on the young schoolboy: "Garry's speed and memory capacity are amazing. He counts deep variations and finds unexpected moves. The power of combinational vision makes him similar to Alekhine himself."[[164]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-nkj1-167)
### Contributions to opening theory
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Contributions to opening theory")]
Kasparov has made many contributions to opening theory, including co-authoring several books on the subject.[[165]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Modern-Chess-Part-One-168) In the 1990s, he systematically developed new variants with computer programs. He also "reanimated" the [Scotch Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch_Game "Scotch Game") in top-level competitions. Kasparov successfully used this opening, which was considered outdated, in the 1990 match against Karpov and in matches with Short and Anand.[[166]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-169) One of the offshoots of the Sicilian in the [Szén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zsef_Sz%C3%A9n "József Szén") Variation is called the Kasparov Gambit.[[167]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-gameknot-170) Kasparov used this variation in the 12th and 16th games of the match with Karpov in 1985; in the second of these games, he scored a victory.[[167]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-gameknot-170) Another well-known case of winning an important game thanks to a novelty in the opening is Kasparov's 10th game of the 1995 match against Anand. On the 14th move, in a well-known position of the open variation of the Spanish Game (Ruy Lopez), Kasparov discovered a new idea with a rook sacrifice, which brought a decisive attack.[[168]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-FOOTNOTELinder2009400-171)
### Chess rating
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Chess rating")]
Kasparov holds the record for the longest time as the [No. 1 rated player in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIDE_chess_world_number_ones "List of FIDE chess world number ones")—from 1984 to 2005 (Kramnik shared the No. 1 ranking with him once, in the January 1996 FIDE rating list).[[169]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-172) He headed the [PCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Chess_Association "Professional Chess Association") rating list during the split from FIDE. At the time of his retirement, he was still ranked No. 1 in the world, with a rating of 2812. His rating has fallen inactive since the January 2006 rating list.[[170]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-173) In January 1990, Kasparov achieved the (then) highest FIDE rating ever, passing 2800 and breaking Fischer's old record of 2785. By the July 1999 and January 2000 FIDE rating lists, Kasparov had reached a 2851 [Elo rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elo_rating_system "Elo rating system"), at that time the highest rating ever achieved.[[171]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-174) He held that record until Carlsen attained a new record high rating of 2861 in January 2013.

  * [Linares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linares,_Ja%C3%A9n "Linares, Jaén") 2002, 8/12, 1st.


In these tournament victories, Kasparov had a score of 53 wins, 61 draws and 1 loss in 115 games, his only defeat coming against [Ivan Sokolov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Sokolov_\(chess_player\) "Ivan Sokolov \(chess player\)") in Wijk aan Zee 1999.

## Chess and computers
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Chess and computers")]
[Acorn Computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn_Computers "Acorn Computers") acted as one of the sponsors for Kasparov's Candidates semi-final match against Korchnoi in 1983. This was Kasparov's first introduction to computers. Kasparov was awarded a [BBC Micro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Micro "BBC Micro"), which he took back with him to Baku, making it perhaps one of the first Western-made microcomputers to reach the Soviet Union at that time.[[177]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-180) Computer chess magazine editor Frederic Friedel consulted with Kasparov in 1985 on how a chess database program would be useful preparation for competition. Friedel founded [Chessbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chessbase "Chessbase") two years later, and he gave a copy of the program to Kasparov, who started using it in his preparation.[[178]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-181) That same year, Kasparov played against thirty-two chess computers in Hamburg, winning all games.[[179]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-182) Several commercially available Kasparov computers were made in the 1980s, the Saitek Kasparov Turbo King models.[[180]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-183)[[181]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-184)[[182]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-185) On 22 October 1989, Kasparov defeated the chess computer [Deep Thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Thought_\(chess_computer\) "Deep Thought \(chess computer\)") in both games of a two-game match.[[183]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-186) In December 1992, Kasparov played thirty-seven blitz games against [Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_\(chess\) "Fritz \(chess\)") 2 in Cologne, winning 24, drawing 4 and losing 9.[[184]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-187)
Kasparov cooperated in producing video material for the computer game _[Kasparov's Gambit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasparov%27s_Gambit "Kasparov's Gambit")_ released by [Electronic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Arts "Electronic Arts") in November 1993. In April 1994, [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel "Intel") acted as a sponsor for the first [Professional Chess Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Chess_Association "Professional Chess Association") Grand Prix event in Moscow, played at a time control of twenty-five minutes per game. In May, [Chessbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chessbase "Chessbase")'s [Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_\(chess\) "Fritz \(chess\)") 3 running on an [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel "Intel") [Pentium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentium "Pentium") PC defeated Kasparov in their first game in the Intel Express blitz tournament in Munich, but Kasparov managed to tie it for first and won the play-off (+3=2). The next day, Kasparov lost to Fritz 3 again in a game on ZDF TV.[[185]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-188) In August, Kasparov was knocked out of the London Intel Grand Prix by Richard Lang's [ChessGenius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChessGenius "ChessGenius") 2 program in the first round.[[186]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-189) In 1995, during Kasparov's world title match with Anand, he unveiled an opening novelty that had been checked with a [chess engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_engine "Chess engine"), an approach that would become increasingly common in subsequent years.[[187]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-190)
Kasparov played in [a pair of six-game chess matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Blue_versus_Garry_Kasparov "Deep Blue versus Garry Kasparov") with IBM supercomputer [Deep Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Blue_\(chess_computer\) "Deep Blue \(chess computer\)"). The first match took place in Philadelphia in February 1996 and was won by Kasparov (4–2).[[188]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-191) The second was played in New York City in May 1997 and won by Deep Blue (3½–2½). The 1997 match was the first defeat of a reigning world champion by a computer under tournament conditions.[[189]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-192) The match was even after five games but Kasparov lost quickly in [Game 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Blue_-_Kasparov,_1997,_Game_6 "Deep Blue - Kasparov, 1997, Game 6"). Kasparov said that he was "not well prepared" to face Deep Blue in 1997. He said that based on his "objective strengths" his play was stronger than that of Deep Blue.[[190]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Kristol2-193) Kasparov claimed that several factors weighed against him in this match. In particular, he was denied access to Deep Blue's recent games, in contrast to the computer's team, which could study hundreds of Kasparov's.[[191]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Gimbel-194)
After the loss, Kasparov said that he sometimes saw deep intelligence and creativity in the machine's moves, suggesting that during the second game chess players had intervened in contravention of the rules. IBM denied that it had cheated, stating the only human intervention occurred between games. The rules provided for the developers to modify the program between games, an opportunity they said they used to shore up weaknesses in the computer's play revealed during the course of the match.[[191]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Gimbel-194) Kasparov requested printouts of the machine's log files but IBM refused, although the company later published them on the Internet.[[192]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-195) Much later, it was suggested that the behaviour Kasparov noted had resulted from a glitch in the computer program.[[193]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Roberts-196) Plans for further engagement between Kasparov and IBM, including a rematch, did not come to fruition, due to the accusations of cheating.[[194]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-197)
[![refer to caption](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Kasparov-18.jpg/250px-Kasparov-18.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kasparov-18.jpg)Kasparov wearing 3D glasses in his match against the program [X3D Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X3D_Fritz "X3D Fritz")
[Kasparov versus the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasparov_versus_the_World "Kasparov versus the World") was a game that took place in 1999. Kasparov conducted the white moves while more than 50,000 people from all over the globe played against him. The game was a huge mixture of tactical and strategical ideas, with Kasparov saying: "It is the greatest game in the history of chess. The sheer number of ideas, the complexity, and the contribution it has made to chess make it the most important game ever played."[[195]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-198) After 62 moves, Kasparov won the game. 
In January 2003, he engaged in a six-game classical time control match, with a $1 million prize fund, against [Deep Junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_\(chess_program\) "Junior \(chess program\)"). It was billed as the FIDE "Man vs. Machine" world championship.[[196]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-199) The engine evaluated three million positions per second.[[197]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-200) After one win each and three draws, it was all up to the final game. After reaching a decent position, Kasparov offered a draw, which was accepted by the Deep Junior team. Asked why he had offered the draw, Kasparov said he feared making a blunder.[[198]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-201) Deep Junior was the first machine to beat Kasparov with Black and at a standard time control.[[199]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-202)
In June 2003, [Mindscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindscape_\(company\) "Mindscape \(company\)") released the computer game _[Kasparov Chessmate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasparov_Chessmate "Kasparov Chessmate")_ , with Kasparov himself listed as a co-designer.[[200]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-203) In November 2003, he engaged in a four-game match against the computer program [X3D Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X3D_Fritz "X3D Fritz"), using a virtual board, [3D glasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_glasses "3D glasses") and a [speech recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_recognition "Speech recognition") system. After two draws and one win apiece, the X3D Man–Machine match ended in a draw. Kasparov received $175,000 and took home a golden trophy. He continued to regret the blunder in the second game that cost him a crucial point. He felt that he had outplayed the machine overall and performed well: "I only made one mistake but unfortunately that one mistake lost the game."[[201]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-204)
In 2021, Kasparov promoted a series of 32 [Non-fungible tokens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-fungible_token "Non-fungible token") (NFTs) that detailed important moments in his career. The top four sold for more than $11,000.[[202]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-205)[[203]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-206)

## Candidate for FIDE presidency
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Candidate for FIDE presidency")]
On 7 October 2013, Kasparov announced his candidacy for World Chess Federation president during a reception in [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn "Tallinn"), Estonia, where the 84th FIDE Congress took place.[[205]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-208) He was supported by reigning world champion and FIDE No. 1 ranked player Carlsen.[[206]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-209) At the FIDE General Assembly in August 2014, Kasparov lost the presidential election to the incumbent [Kirsan Ilyumzhinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsan_Ilyumzhinov "Kirsan Ilyumzhinov"), with a vote of 110–61.[[207]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-210) A few days before the election took place, _The New York Times Magazine_ had published a report on the viciously fought campaign. Included was information about a leaked contract between Kasparov and former FIDE Secretary General [Ignatius Leong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignatius_Leong "Ignatius Leong") from Singapore, in which the Kasparov campaign reportedly "offered to pay Leong US$500,000 and to pay $250,000 a year for four years to the ASEAN Chess Academy, an organisation Leong helped create to teach the game, specifying that Leong would be responsible for delivering 11 votes from his region [...]".[[208]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-211) In September 2015, the FIDE Ethics Commission found Kasparov and Leong guilty of violating its Code of Ethics[[209]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-212) and later suspended them for two years from all FIDE functions and meetings.[[210]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-213)

### Croatian citizenship
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Croatian citizenship")]
Kasparov had maintained a summer home in the Croatian city of [Makarska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makarska "Makarska"). In February 2014, he applied for [citizenship by naturalisation in Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_nationality_law#Citizenship_by_naturalisation "Croatian nationality law"), according to media reports, claiming he was finding it increasingly difficult to live in Russia. According to an article in _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ , Kasparov was "widely perceived" as having been a vocal supporter of [Croatian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Croatia "Independence of Croatia") during the early 1990s.[[211]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-CroatianCitizen-214) Later in February 2014, his application for naturalisation was approved and he had a meeting with Croatian prime minister [Zoran Milanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoran_Milanovi%C4%87 "Zoran Milanović") on 27 February.[[212]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-dnevnik-215) Croatian press cited his "lobbying for Croatia in 1991" as grounds for the expedited naturalisation.[[212]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-dnevnik-215) In an interview for a Croatian daily published in February 2022, Kasparov said he was "very grateful" to Milanović for the help rendered by him (then as prime minister) in obtaining Croatian citizenship.[[213]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-216)
## Books and other writings
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Books and other writings")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Autografo_Garri_Kasparov.jpg/250px-Autografo_Garri_Kasparov.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Autografo_Garri_Kasparov.jpg)Kasparov autograph
### Early writings
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Early writings")]
Kasparov has written books on chess. He published a controversial[[214]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-217) autobiography when still in his early 20s. Originally titled _Child of Change_ , it was later published as _Unlimited Challenge_. This book was updated several times after he became world champion. Its content is mainly literary, with a small chess component of key unannotated games. He published an annotated games collection in 1983, _Fighting Chess: My Games and Career_ ,[[215]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-218) which has been updated in further editions. He also wrote a book annotating the games from his [World Chess Championship 1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1985 "World Chess Championship 1985") victory, _World Chess Championship Match: Moscow, 1985_.[[216]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-219)
He has annotated his own games extensively for the Yugoslav _[Chess Informant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Informant "Chess Informant")_ series.[[217]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-220) In 1982, he co-authored _Batsford Chess Openings_ with British grandmaster Keene. That book sold well and was updated in a second edition in 1989.[[218]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-221) He also co-authored two opening books with his trainer Alexander Nikitin in the 1980s for British publisher [Batsford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavilion_Books "Pavilion Books") – on the [Classical Variation of the Caro–Kann Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caro%E2%80%93Kann_Defence#Classical_/_Capablanca_Variation "Caro–Kann Defence") and on the [Scheveningen Variation of the Sicilian Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Defence,_Scheveningen_Variation "Sicilian Defence, Scheveningen Variation").[[219]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-222) Kasparov also contributed extensively to the five-volume openings series _[Encyclopedia of Chess Openings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia_of_Chess_Openings "Encyclopedia of Chess Openings")_ from [Chess Informant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Informant "Chess Informant"), for which Kasparov also wrote personal columns called _Garry's Choice_.[[220]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-223)[[221]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-224)
In 2000, Kasparov co-authored _Kasparov Against the World: The Story of the Greatest Online Challenge_[[222]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-225) with grandmaster [Daniel King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_J._King "Daniel J. King"). The 202-page book analyses the 1999 [Kasparov versus the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasparov_versus_the_World "Kasparov versus the World") game, and holds the record for the longest analysis devoted to a single chess game.[[223]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-226)
###  _My Great Predecessors_ series
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: My Great Predecessors series")]
Main article: [_My Great Predecessors_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Great_Predecessors "My Great Predecessors")
In 2003, the first volume of his five-volume work _Garry Kasparov on My Great Predecessors_ was published. This volume deals with world champions [Wilhelm Steinitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Steinitz "Wilhelm Steinitz"), [Emanuel Lasker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Lasker "Emanuel Lasker"), Capablanca and Alekhine, and some of their strong contemporaries. It won the [British Chess Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Chess_Federation "British Chess Federation")'s Book of the Year award in 2003. Volume two, covering [Max Euwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Euwe "Max Euwe"), Botvinnik, Smyslov and Tal, appeared later in 2003. Volume three, featuring [Tigran Petrosian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigran_Petrosian "Tigran Petrosian") and [Boris Spassky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Spassky "Boris Spassky"), was published in early 2004. In December 2004, Kasparov released volume four, which covers [Samuel Reshevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Reshevsky "Samuel Reshevsky"), [Miguel Najdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Najdorf "Miguel Najdorf") and [Bent Larsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bent_Larsen "Bent Larsen") (none of whom was world champion), but focuses on Fischer. The fifth volume, devoted to the chess careers of world champion Karpov and challenger Korchnoi, was published in March 2006.[[224]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-227)
###  _Modern Chess_ series
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Modern Chess series")]
Main article: [_My Great Predecessors_ § _Modern Chess_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Great_Predecessors#Modern_Chess "My Great Predecessors")
His _Revolution in the 70s_ (published in March 2007) covers "the openings revolution of the 1970s–1980s" and was the first work in a new venture, "Modern Chess Series", which recounted his matches with Karpov and selected games. _Revolution in the 70s_ is about the development of opening theory witnessed in that decade. Systems like the novel ["Hedgehog"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedgehog_Defense "Hedgehog Defense") opening plan of passively developing the pieces no further than the first three ranks were examined in great detail. Kasparov also analysed some of the most notable games played in that period. In a section at the end of the book, top opening theoreticians provided their opinion on progress made in opening theory in the 1980s.[[165]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Modern-Chess-Part-One-168)
###  _Garry Kasparov on Garry Kasparov_ series

From 2011 to 2014, Kasparov published a three-volume series of his games, spanning his career in three eras until he stopped playing full-time in 2005.[[225]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-228)
### _Winter Is Coming_
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: Winter Is Coming")]
In October 2015, Kasparov published a book titled _Winter Is Coming: Why Vladimir Putin and the Enemies of the Free World Must Be Stopped_. The title is a reference to the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Box_Office "Home Box Office") television series _[Game of Thrones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_of_Thrones "Game of Thrones")_. In the book, Kasparov writes about the need for an organisation composed solely of democratic countries to replace the United Nations. In an interview, he called the United Nations a "catwalk for dictators".[[226]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Kristol-229)
### Historical revisionism
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=30 "Edit section: Historical revisionism")]
Kasparov believes that the conventional history of civilisation is incorrect. Specifically, he contends that the history of ancient civilisations is based on misdating of events and achievements that occurred in the medieval period.[[227]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-taylorinterview-230)[[228]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-taylortime-231) He has cited several aspects of ancient history that, he argues, are likely to be anachronisms.[[229]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-ualberta-232) Kasparov has written in support of the pseudohistorical [new chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_chronology_\(Fomenko\) "New chronology \(Fomenko\)") theory proposed by [Anatoly Fomenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Fomenko "Anatoly Fomenko"), although with some reservations.[[230]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-233) In 2001, he expressed a desire to devote his time to promoting the new chronology after his chess career. "New Chronology is a great area for investing my intellect ... My analytical abilities are well placed to figure out what was right and what was wrong."[[231]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-warren-234) "When I stop playing chess, it may well be that I concentrate on promoting these ideas... I believe they can improve our lives."[[231]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-warren-234) Later, Kasparov renounced his support of Fomenko's theories but reaffirmed his belief that mainstream historical knowledge is inconsistent.[[232]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-235)[[233]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-236)
### Other post-retirement writing
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=31 "Edit section: Other post-retirement writing")]
Kasparov wrote _[How Life Imitates Chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_Life_Imitates_Chess "How Life Imitates Chess")_ , an examination of the parallels between decision-making in chess and in the business world, in 2007.[[234]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-237) In 2008, he published a sympathetic obituary for Fischer: "I am often asked if I ever met or played Bobby Fischer. The answer is no, I never had that opportunity. But even though he saw me as a member of the evil chess establishment that he felt had robbed and cheated him, I am sorry I never had a chance to thank him personally for what he did for our sport."[[235]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-238)
Kasparov is the chief advisor for the book publisher [Everyman Chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everyman_Chess "Everyman Chess").[[236]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-239) He works closely with [Mig Greengard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mig_Greengard "Mig Greengard") and his comments can often be found on Greengard's blog.[[237]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-240)[[238]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-241) Kasparov collaborated with [Max Levchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Levchin "Max Levchin") and [Peter Thiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thiel "Peter Thiel") on _The Blueprint_ , a book calling for a revival of world innovation, planned for release in March 2013 but cancelled after the authors disagreed on its contents.[[239]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-242) In an editorial comment on Google's [AlphaZero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlphaZero "AlphaZero") chess-playing system, Kasparov argued that chess has become the model for reasoning in the same way that the fruit fly _[Drosophila melanogaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosophila_melanogaster "Drosophila melanogaster")_ became a model organism for geneticists: "I was pleased to see that AlphaZero had a dynamic, open style like my own", he wrote in late 2018.[[240]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-243)
Kasparov served as a consultant for the 2020 [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") miniseries _[The Queen's Gambit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Queen%27s_Gambit_\(miniseries\) "The Queen's Gambit \(miniseries\)")_ and gave an interview to _[Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_\(magazine\) "Slate \(magazine\)")_ on his contributions.[[241]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-244) That same year, Kasparov collaborated with [Matt Calkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Calkins "Matt Calkins"), founder and CEO of [Appian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appian_Corporation "Appian Corporation"), on _Hyperautomation_ , a book about [low-code development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-code_development_platform "Low-code development platform") and the future of business automation. Kasparov wrote the foreword where he discusses his experiences with human–machine relationships.[[242]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-245)[[243]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-246) _The New York Times_ published an essay by Kasparov titled "Garry Kasparov: What We Believe About Reality" in 2021.[[244]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-247) The essay is part of a series called _The Big Ideas: What Do We Believe_.[[245]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-248) This work was later published in a compendium titled _Question Everything: A Stone Reader._[[246]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-249)

## Personal life
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Garry_Kasparov&action=edit&section=34 "Edit section: Personal life")]
Kasparov has been married three times: to Masha, with whom he had a daughter, Polina, before divorcing;[[250]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-political_move-253) to Yulia, with whom he had a son, Vadim, before their 2005 divorce;[[250]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-political_move-253) and to Daria (Dasha), with whom he has two children, daughter Aida born in 2006 and son Nickolas born in 2015.[[251]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-New_Yorker-254)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Incorrigible-10)[[252]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-255) Kasparov's wife manages his business activities worldwide through Kasparov International Management Inc.[[253]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-256) He has lived in New York City since 2013.[[254]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-257)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov#cite_note-Incorrigible-10)
