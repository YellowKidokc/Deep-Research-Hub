Alleged lost empire
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Map_of_Asia_in_1700_by_Guillaume_de_L%27Isle.jpg/330px-Map_of_Asia_in_1700_by_Guillaume_de_L%27Isle.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Map_of_Asia_in_1700_by_Guillaume_de_L%27Isle.jpg)A 1700 map of Asia dividing "Great Tartary" into "Muscovite Tartary", "Chinese Tartary", and "Independent Tartary"
The **Tartarian Empire** or **Great Tartaria** is a group of [pseudohistorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudohistory "Pseudohistory") [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory "Conspiracy theory"), including ideas of a "hidden past" and "mud floods", which originated as pseudoscientific [Russian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_nationalism "Russian nationalism"). 
[Tartary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartary "Tartary"), or Tartaria, is a historical name for [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") and [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia"). Conspiracy theories assert that Tartary, or the Tartarian Empire, was a lost civilization with advanced technology and culture. This ignores well-documented accounts of Tartary within the [history of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Asia "History of Asia").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-skeptoid-1) In modern conceptions of the conspiracy theories, Tartary has been claimed to cover a region spanning central [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") to northern [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan "Kazakhstan") as well as areas in [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia "Mongolia"), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), and the [Russian Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Far_East "Russian Far East").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
## Background
The theory of Great Tartaria as a suppressed [lost land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lost_lands "List of lost lands") or civilization originated in Russia, with aspects first appearing in [Anatoly Fomenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Fomenko "Anatoly Fomenko")'s [new chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_chronology_\(Fomenko\) "New chronology \(Fomenko\)") in the mid-1970s and early 1980s, and was then popularized by the [racial occult history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levashovism#Eschatology "Levashovism") of [Nikolai Levashov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Levashov "Nikolai Levashov"). In Russian [pseudoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience "Pseudoscience"), known for its [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_nationalism_in_Russia "Ethnic nationalism in Russia"), Tartaria is presented as the "real" name for Russia, which was maliciously "ignored" in the West.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGorshenina2014462%E2%80%93463-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGorshenina201994-3) The [Russian Geographical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Geographical_Society "Russian Geographical Society") has debunked the conspiracy theory as an extremist fantasy, and far from denying the existence of the term, has used the opportunity to share numerous maps of "Tartary" in its collection.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-4) Since about 2016, conspiracy theories about the supposed lost empire of "Tartaria" have gained popularity on the internet, divorced from its original Russian nationalist frame.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-:0-5)
## Conspiracy theory
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Palace_horticulture_01.jpg/250px-Palace_horticulture_01.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Palace_horticulture_01.jpg)The Palace of Horticulture built for the [Panama–Pacific International Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama%E2%80%93Pacific_International_Exposition "Panama–Pacific International Exposition") in San Francisco in 1915
The globalized version of the conspiracy theory is based on an alternative view of [architectural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_architecture "History of architecture"). Adherents propose that demolished buildings such as the [Singer Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singer_Building "Singer Building"), [the original New York Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(1910%E2%80%931963\) "Pennsylvania Station \(1910–1963\)"), and the temporary grounds of the [1915 World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama%E2%80%93Pacific_International_Exposition "Panama–Pacific International Exposition") in San Francisco were actually the buildings of a vast empire based in Tartary that has been suppressed from history. Sumptuously styled [Gilded Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilded_Age "Gilded Age") buildings are often held out as really having been built by the supposed Tartaria. Other buildings, such as the [Great Pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramids "Great Pyramids") and the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House"), are further held out as Tartarian constructions. The conspiracy theory only vaguely describes how such a supposedly advanced civilization, which had reputedly achieved world peace, could have fallen and been hidden.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-:0-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-:1-6) Proponents of the theory also frequently claim that Tartarians have developed [unlimited wireless energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_energy_suppression_conspiracy_theory "Free energy suppression conspiracy theory").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-skeptoid-1)
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Derrumbe_del_cerro_Chitaria_en_Costa_Rica.png/250px-Derrumbe_del_cerro_Chitaria_en_Costa_Rica.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Derrumbe_del_cerro_Chitaria_en_Costa_Rica.png)The destruction of the Tartarian Empire is typically attributed to a colossal "mud flood".
In the conspiracy theory, the idea that a "mud flood" wiped out much of the world via [depopulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depopulation "Depopulation") and thus old buildings is common, supported by the fact that many buildings across the world have architectural elements like doors, windows, and archways submerged many feet below "ground level". Both [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") and [II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") are cited as a way in which Tartaria was destroyed and hidden, reflecting the reality that the extensive [bombing campaigns of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing_during_World_War_II "Strategic bombing during World War II") did destroy many historic buildings. The general evidence for the theory is that there are similar styles of building around the world, such as [capitol buildings with domes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolism_of_domes#Government "Symbolism of domes"), or [star forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_forts "Star forts"). Also, many photographs from the turn of the 20th century appear to show deserted city streets in many capital cities across the world. When people do start to appear in the photographs, there is a striking contrast between horse-and-cart users in the muddy streets and the elaborate, highly ornate stone mega-structures that tower above the inhabitants of the cities, which is seen even in modern cities where extreme poverty is contrasted with skyscrapers.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-:0-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-:1-6)
Zach Mortice, writing for _[Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_News "Bloomberg News")_ , believes that the theory reflects a cultural discontent with modernism and a supposition that traditional styles are inherently good and modern styles are bad. He describes the theory as "the [QAnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QAnon "QAnon") of architecture".[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-:1-6) Moritz Maurer, a religious scholar, links Tartarian imagery to the "giant trees" theory, in which colossal, flat [buttes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butte "Butte") are envisioned as the stumps of primordial "mother trees" cut down at some point in the past by unknown nefarious agents. Maurer attributes the lack of a clear narrative for both conspiracies to the image-based [social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media "Social media") on which they are presented, describing it as "meme culture" and also comparing it to QAnon.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarian_Empire#cite_note-7)
