# TRACK D — WORLDVIEWS AND RELIGIONS

Read the master prompt first. Schema, two axes, freeze-before-run, miss

ID range: TL-4000 to TL-4999.

---

## What this track is for

This is the baseline track. Its job is to make the other three legible.

Without it, a pattern found in the Scripture or Theophysics tracks has
nothing to be compared against — "this claim appears at this time" means
nothing until you know what else was available at that time. This track
supplies the denominator.

It is not a comparison of religions and it produces no ranking. Anything
resembling a score is a schema violation.

---

## What a row is here

One tradition's answer to one question. Not a tradition. Not a founder.

"Stoicism's account of the human problem" is a row. "Stoicism" is not.

Every tradition covered gets a row for each of the five questions below,
including a row saying the tradition does not address it. Silence is data
and must be recorded as silence, never left blank.

---

## The five questions, asked identically of every tradition

1. **Origin** — where does everything come from?
2. **Human problem** — what is wrong with us?
3. **Remedy** — what fixes it, and who acts?
4. **Authority** — what grounds the answer, and can it condemn its holder?
5. **End** — where is it going?

Question 4 is the one that maps to the reference test. Record what the
tradition itself claims grounds it, and whether that ground is internally
generated or claimed from outside. Do not evaluate the claim — record it.

---

## THE STATED-AS-THEY-STATE-IT RULE

Every answer is written as a serious adherent of that tradition would state
it. Not as a critic would summarize it, not as a comparative-religion
textbook would flatten it, not as a Christian apologist would set it up.

A row a well-informed adherent would not recognize is a defective row, and it
poisons the baseline it was written to provide.

---

## Dating, which is harder here than anywhere else

```yaml
founding_trad:      the tradition's own account of its origin
founding_schol:     scholarly consensus dating
dating_dispute:     none | minor | major | not-applicable
founder:            named person, or none
no_founder:         true where the tradition has no founding figure
```

Several traditions have no founding date and no founder — the category
itself does not apply, and forcing a date into the field is a fabrication.
Where a tradition's own account and scholarly dating diverge by centuries or
more, record both figures and mark the dispute major. Where a tradition's
own account places its origin outside history entirely, record that as
stated.

`not-applicable` is a legitimate and common entry here. Use it.

---

## Scope

Cover, at minimum: the major living religious traditions; the classical
philosophical schools; the modern secular worldviews (naturalism,
materialism, existentialism, secular humanism); and the political
totalizing systems of the last two centuries.

Secular worldviews get identical treatment and the identical five
questions. A grid containing only religions has quietly assumed that the
religious question is the only one being answered, which defeats the
baseline.

---

## Hazards specific to this track

**Insider/outsider dating collapse.** Two values, always, with the dispute
marked.

**Category imposition.** "Founder", "scripture", "conversion", and "doctrine"
are not universal categories. Where a concept does not apply, say so rather
than finding the nearest analogue.

**Straw-manning by compression.** The rule above. The fastest way to break
this track is a one-line summary that no adherent would accept.

**Comparative scoring.** No totals, no rankings, no counts of which
tradition answers the most questions. The grid is read later by a human.

**Christian exceptionalism in the schema.** The Christian rows are written
by the same rules, at the same grain, with the same fields, by a
contributor working the same way. If the Christian rows are noticeably
richer than the others, the track has failed and the baseline is worthless.

---

## First run

The classical philosophical schools — Stoic, Epicurean, Platonist, Cynic,
Skeptic. Well documented, clean dates, low dispute, and they exercise
question 4 hard because several of them ground authority in nature or reason
rather than revelation. Get the grain right there before touching the living
traditions, where a badly written row does real damage.
