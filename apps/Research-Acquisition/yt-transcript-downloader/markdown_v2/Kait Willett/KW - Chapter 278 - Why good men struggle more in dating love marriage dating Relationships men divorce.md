This is uncomfortable, but it's true. Good men tend to struggle way more than toxic men when it comes to dating. And this is not because women prefer toxicity, but because dating rewards certainty, not morality. And good men tend to ask for things like clarity. They wait for signals. They avoid pressure. And they prioritize comfort and emotional safety. And toxic men, they do the opposite. They act first. They assume access. And they create emotional intensity versus emotional safety. The dating environment does not evaluate long-term suitability. It responds to immediacy. Attraction happens before discernment and intensity is a lot easier to feel than stability. So, when men are told just be a good guy and it'll work out, they're being given advice optimized for relationships, not selection. And this creates confusion because men who are doing the most internal work often receive the least early amounts of feedback.

It doesn't mean that goodness is unattractive. It means that goodness alone doesn't communicate desire. And until men are taught how attraction actually forms, many will continue to mistake rejection for inadequacy when it's really just misaligned signaling.
