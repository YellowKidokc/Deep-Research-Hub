If you're a woman who's experienced a hot flash, you know how uncomfortable that is and how much of a [ __ ] psychopath your friends and your family think you are. Hot flashes are one of the most common symptoms when it comes to menopause and 80% of women who go through menopause experience hot flashes. But, what are they and why do they happen? When you get a hot flash during menopause, it's due to a drop in estrogen, which has an impact on the brain much like testosterone. And these two hormones help regulate a part of the brain called your hypothalamus, which acts in the body kind of like a thermostat. When estrogen levels drop, the hypothalamus is activated to think that the body's actually hotter than it is. And this activates your sweat glands, which is why you wake up drenched in your bed wondering what the hell just happened to me. This increases your heart rate, widens your blood vessels in order to try to cool the body down.

This is why you wake up completely drenched in sweat wondering what the hell happened to you.
