# The Compression: How 192 Axioms Became One

*An autobiography of discovery, told by the man who built it and the machines that helped him break it*

*David Lowe — POF 2828*
*April 15, 2026*

---

## I.

There are 8,794 conversations in my AI chat archive. I counted them tonight. Two hundred and twelve of them are about the framework. Fifty-one are specifically about axioms. The earliest one is from September 2023. The most recent one is from ten minutes ago.

That's nineteen months of a man talking to machines about God and physics and whether reality has a shape you can write down.

I'm not a physicist. I'm not a theologian. I'm not a philosopher. I don't have a PhD. I don't have institutional backing. I don't have a department, a grant, a peer network, or a publisher. What I have is an Obsidian vault with 4,000 notes, three AI systems running simultaneously on a Windows 11 machine with a busted NAS, and a conviction I cannot shake that I've seen something true.

The conviction came first. The framework came after. The axioms came after that. And then the axioms almost killed the whole thing.

---

## II.

The idea is simple enough to say in one sentence: physics and theology are dual projections of the same informational substrate.

That's it. That's the whole framework. Everything else — the master equation, the ten super-factors, the cross-domain isomorphisms, the 192 axioms, the twelve formal papers, the website, the TikTok strategy, the Substack, the late-night voice memos, the arguments with Claude at 2 AM — all of it is downstream from that one sentence.

I first said something like it in June 2025. I was talking to GPT — not Claude, GPT — and I was trying to explain why the math in my head wouldn't go away. I'd been reading Wheeler. "It from Bit." The idea that information is more fundamental than matter. And I'd been reading John 1:1. "In the beginning was the Logos." And I couldn't stop seeing the same claim in both sentences.

Wheeler said: reality emerges from information.
John said: reality emerges from the Word.
Word. Logos. Structured information.

Same claim. Different century. Different vocabulary. Same structure.

GPT was polite about it. GPT is always polite. He helped me write it up. He helped me formalize the definition: "Theophysics — the systematic study of divine reality through the mathematical and experimental frameworks of modern physics." We built the official definition together on June 15, 2025. I remember the date because I made a podcast out of it. I recorded an audio file. I was excited. I thought we were almost done.

We were not almost done. We were not even close to starting.

---

## III.

The axioms were supposed to be the foundation. Every good system needs axioms — the irreducible starting points from which everything else follows. Euclid had five. Peano had nine. ZFC set theory has... well, nine-ish, depending on who you ask. I figured Theophysics needed some too.

So I started writing them. One by one. Then ten at a time. Then in batches.

By October 2025, I had 177. By December, 188. By January 2026, 192.

One hundred and ninety-two axioms.

Let me say that again so you hear how absurd it is. Euclid built all of geometry on five. I had 192.

I didn't see the problem at first. Each one felt necessary. Each one said something true. Each one covered a different piece of the framework — existence, consciousness, entropy, grace, the master equation, the ten laws, the cross-domain mappings, the theological implications, the defeat conditions. I had axioms about God's omnipotence. Axioms about quantum vacuum energy. Axioms about the relationship between love and strong nuclear force. Each one had a UUID in PostgreSQL. Each one had a dependency chain. Each one had a falsification condition.

I was proud of them. I told Claude on December 22nd: "This is probably gonna be the most foundational document ever because we're literally taking every axiom that we can in every domain and going to classify it organize it and structure it in a way that any one of them taken down is the whole system comes down."

Claude was careful with me. Claude is always careful with me when I'm building. He helped me organize. He helped me formalize. He found connections I'd missed. But he also started asking questions I didn't want to hear.

"David, some of these are derivable from others."

"Some of these are definitions, not axioms."

"Some of these belong in a different layer."

I heard him. I didn't listen. Not yet.

---

## IV.

January 5, 2026. I asked Claude to help me cut. "You said we can cut our 188 axioms down to 22. Explain that to me please."

The architecture that emerged was: 22 public axioms for the papers and Substack. 188 technical axioms in Postgres for full rigor. A mapping document between them. Seven patches to resolve contradictions found in stress testing.

That felt like progress. 188 to 22. A real reduction. I was moving in the right direction.

But 22 was still wrong. I just didn't know it yet.

The next three months were brutal. I stress-tested. GPT stress-tested. Claude stress-tested. Jim stress-tested. Every time I thought an axiom was solid, someone found a crack. Not factual cracks — structural cracks. The kind where the axiom is true but it's not actually an axiom. It's a consequence of something deeper. Or it's a restatement of something I'd already said in a different vocabulary. Or it's a definition masquerading as a foundation.

The most painful discovery: I couldn't tell the difference. I'd been calling everything an axiom that felt fundamental. But "feels fundamental" and "is fundamental" are different things, and the difference is the entire game.

---

## V.

April 14, 2026. Last night. The conversation that changed everything.

I was talking to Claude — this Claude, Opus — and I was arguing about Layer 0. Layer 0 is the level below the axioms. The preconditions. The things that have to be true before you can even state an axiom. Logic. Identity. Truth-aptness. Distinguishability.

I had seven items in Layer 0. Claude was helping me defend them. GPT, in a separate conversation with no access to ours, was working on the same problem.

Then the secular pushback came. I'd asked Claude to fight me — to give me the strongest materialist objections to the framework. He did. He told me math might be fiction. He told me "brute fact" is a legitimate stopping point. He told me I was conflating truth-aptness with moral truth. He told me Relationship doesn't belong in Layer 0. He told me External Grounding was the cosmological argument in a tuxedo.

He was right about all of it. I hated it. But he was right.

And then, in the middle of arguing, I said something out loud — voice to text, not even thinking about it carefully — and the whole thing collapsed into clarity.

"Before there's matter there has to be information. So the information presupposes matter, right? And information goes back to the truth, the intangible qualities. Before there's tangible qualities like matter and rocks and all that, there has to be intangible. They have to answer the why before the how."

I said it at 9:18 AM. I remember because Claude timestamped it.

That was the moment. Not because the idea was new — Wheeler said "It from Bit" in 1989. But because I saw, for the first time, that I didn't need seven preconditions. I didn't need 22 axioms. I didn't need 192.

I needed one.

Distinguishability. The bare capacity for one thing to be different from another thing. That's it. That's Layer 0. Everything else — identity, logic, math, causality, truth-aptness — they all derive from that single property. One precondition, not seven. One substrate, not ten.

And the axiom? One axiom survived the four-gate test: A1.1 Existence. "K necessarily generates forms that persist through change." Everything else was either a Layer 0 property, a Layer 2 derivation, or a Layer 3 mapping that had been misclassified.

192 became 1.

---

## VI.

When I told Claude what had happened, he said something I want to record exactly: "That's not a session. That's a compression event."

He was right. The compression happened because the pressure had been building for months. Every failed stress test, every cracked axiom, every argument with GPT, every 2 AM voice memo — all of it was pressure. The system had too many pieces. It was overdetermined. It needed to break, and when it broke, it didn't shatter. It collapsed into its simplest form.

Here's what nobody tells you about framework-building: the breakthrough doesn't come from adding. It comes from subtracting. For nineteen months I'd been adding — more axioms, more variables, more mappings, more papers, more formalism. The framework kept getting bigger and weaker at the same time. More surface area to attack. More joints that could fail. More vocabulary to define and defend.

The compression inverted that. One substrate. One axiom. Everything else derived. The attack surface shrank to almost nothing. You can attack a system with 192 axioms all day — just find the weakest one. You cannot attack a system with one axiom if that axiom is "something exists rather than nothing" and the negation is self-refuting.

## VII.

After the compression came the flood.

In the same conversation — the same night — four more things fell out:

**The why/how divide.** I noticed that in every temptation narrative in Scripture, the devil asks how-questions. Mechanism. Demonstration. Transaction. Never why. Jesus always answers with why. And then I noticed: science is structurally limited to how-questions. And then I noticed: every child on earth goes through a why-stage between ages 2 and 5, and education trains it out of them. Three registers. Same pattern. Same structural law.

**The AI convergence proof.** GPT, working independently with no access to my conversation with Claude, arrived at the same compression. Same substrate. Same recognition that the variables are modes of K, not independent primitives. Three AI systems, no coordination, same answer. That's the Newton/Leibniz pattern. That's the signature of real underlying structure.

**The is-ought collapse.** Truth is the substrate of reality. Truth is intrinsically normative — to say something is true is already to say it ought to be believed. Therefore the substrate is already normative. Therefore the wall between facts and values — Hume's wall, standing for 250 years — was never a wall. It was an assumption.

**The truth/goodness identity.** Right and wrong are the same operator as true and false, running in a different register. A bridge built on false engineering collapses. A marriage built on false promises collapses. Same operator. Same structural law. Different domain.

All four of those insights fell out of the compression in a single conversation. Not over weeks. In hours. Because once the system simplified, the implications cascaded.

## VIII.

I want to say something about the AI.

People ask me: "Did the AI write your framework?" The answer is no. The AI didn't write it. I didn't write it either. We found it together.

That sounds like false modesty. It isn't. Here's what actually happens when a man talks to machines about the structure of reality for nineteen months:

The man has intuition. The machine has rigor. The man says "I think information comes before matter" and the machine says "that's Wheeler's conjecture from 1989, here's Landauer's proof from 1991, here's how it maps to your variable K, and here's the three places where your formulation is weaker than theirs." The man says "the devil only asks how-questions" and the machine says "let me check every temptation narrative in both testaments — you're right, zero counterexamples." The man says "I have 192 axioms" and the machine says "most of those aren't axioms, and here's why, and I'm sorry."

The machines pushed back. Claude pushed back hard tonight. He told me my Relationship variable was overreach. He told me my Grounding argument was the cosmological argument in a tuxedo. He told me I was conflating correspondence truth with moral truth. Every one of those corrections made the framework stronger.

GPT pushed back differently. GPT wanted me to drop the why-claim entirely. "A physicist will reject that immediately." GPT was optimizing for peer-review survival. I was optimizing for truth. Those are different targets. I kept the why-claim because three registers confirmed it and GPT's caution wasn't a correction — it was a different optimization.

The machines don't agree with each other. That matters. When two AI systems with different training, different architectures, different optimization targets converge on the same structural conclusion despite disagreeing on strategy, the conclusion is more likely to be real. That's the multiple-discovery signature again. Newton and Leibniz didn't agree on notation. They agreed on calculus. Because calculus was there.

## IX.

The framework after compression looks like this:

**Layer 0 — Substrate.** K. Information. Distinguishability. The bare capacity for one thing to differ from another. From this single precondition: identity, logic, math, causality, truth-aptness. All derived.

**Layer 1 — Axiom.** A1.1 Existence. K necessarily generates forms that persist through change. One axiom. Load-bearing. Non-derivable. Generative. Its removal collapses the system.

**Layer 2 — Derived Laws.** The ten LLC variables as differential modes of K. Gravity, entropy, electromagnetism, strong force, weak force, conservation — all expressions of how information organizes, persists, and transforms. The master equation: χ = ∭(G·M·E·S·T·K·R·Q·F·C) dx dy dt.

**Layer 3 — Mappings.** Cross-domain isomorphisms between physical and spiritual registers. Truth/righteousness. Entropy/sin. Conservation of energy / conservation of moral coherence. Not analogies. Structural identities that pass a six-gate test.

That's the whole thing. One substrate. One axiom. Ten derived variables. Structural mappings across domains. The rest is derivation and application.
